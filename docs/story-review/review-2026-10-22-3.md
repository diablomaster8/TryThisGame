## Where the writing stands

**Story.** A child can follow all ten units, and their shapes are strong: a beetle learning to say "I" (S12), a letter that waited twenty years (S13), a grieving girl and a cardboard dragon (S14), an honest egg stall (S15), a sawmill that learns to ask first (S16), a grieving owl (S17), and a museum with a fainting mole (S18). Friendship and trust carry every story, and family is strongest in S13's letter and S14's tea towels. The breaks are local. Two are hard facts. Pip spends all forty Shells and then has "ten Shells left", and Byte's "out by three point three" is the wrong maths. The rest are follow breaks. A pronoun sends the child after Hazel instead of Tilly (S14 reader). S13 returns "after the Hollow" and S18 opens "two days after the rain on the hill" with no bridge. A few names lean on the campaign: the Circuit, the hatch register, and "the we". Spouse love is absent from every unit, although Pixel and Byte share three of the stories and the S14 tea towels are already a married couple's joke.

**Humour.** S15 is reliably funny: Jackpot, who cannot lie, makes a perfect straight man for Dashwood, and Byte's deadpan does the rest. S14's "Rule four: say actually, lose a point" and "eaten by a god, that's canon now" are the best runners in the batch. S17's Nova ("I'm not stopping", "a tall badger") and S18's fainting professor work in both bands. The weak spots come where a set-up is never paid off. Nova promises to be on the cliff path "by accident, about nine" and never comes. Solo's wings "buzz whenever anyone speaks" once and then never again. Pixel's stream warning and Byte's unexplained "seven points" in the sprout band go nowhere too. The fixes are cheap: turn up, buzz, squelch, count down. The heavy stretches (S13 from the desk to the letter, S12 from the queen to the dark) need one dry beat beside the grief, never inside it.

**Learning Grove.** Both bands meet their caps. No story line is over 14 words in Sprout (means 9.0 to 9.4) or over 30 in Reader (means 13.6 to 16.6). Lessons mostly grow out of the story, and S16's consent bell and S17's grief notice are model lessons. The two recurring faults are placement and literal reading. Reader units keep putting a quiz between a set-up and its payoff: S14's "quiet setting" and "loudest thing", the metaphor lesson that cuts Aldous's letter in half, the hidden-feelings quiz that cites Nova's rock before she mentions it, and the bar chart between Lug's offcuts and the lean-to. Sprout still has a few idioms and machine metaphors a 3-year-old takes literally: "won't start again" beside a wheel that does, "don't make it a moment", "the Toad does feelings", and a Nubbin named Fossil met right after the fossils. Death is said plainly in S13, S17 and S18 sprout, but S12's Queen-Mother only "goes quiet for good". The cross-story recs set one gentle pattern for all of them.
