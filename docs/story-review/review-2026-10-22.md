## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All ten units can be followed. Every reader found scenes that say who is here, what they want and what changed, and the fun quests' one-want, one-obstacle shape is especially clean. The breaks are local and fixable: two characters both called Tam (S27 and S31), Tilly's "Gran" left unexplained in S24, Mother Brindle calling herself a Nubbin's mother, Kaito turning up before he is introduced, Moss's chapter 9 plan arriving as four rules at once, and a handful of choices whose branches all end the same way (c4-open, c8-open, S28, s10-foot). Friendship, family and trust are strong everywhere: Hazel and the Keepers, Ember and Ash, Sable, Corvin, Tobin and Linnet. The clear gap is love between spouses. No unit has a long-partnered couple at its heart; the nearest is S7's Pixel and Byte, who "bicker like an old married workshop", and that pair is this round's main cross-story proposal.

**Humour.** The comedy is reliable where it has a straight man. Sunny, Python, Bean and most of Avatar are already funny, with running gags and deadpan foils (Dabney, Mr Tallis, Mrs Inkwell, Warden Pryce, Tuk). The campaign is funny through chapter 5 (Fern's stone, "Rule four. Don't be Sparks.", Byte's shoelace, "Condensation.") and then thins badly. From c6-open to c10 the grave stretches run with no relief, especially Moss's three Stillness lectures and Corvin's garden. The same pattern shows in the long side stories (S2, S23, S29). The fix is nearly always to put a deadpan beat beside the grave one, never on it: an Ember aside, a hen, a duck, a Nubbin with a stick. Running gags exist but are rarely called back (Marina's rules, the skipping rhyme, Wisp's hoard, Tilly's note), so threading them across stories is the cheapest way to raise the laugh rate.

**Learning Grove.** Both bands follow the campaign faithfully, and the Reader band is clear and often funny in its first half. The problems are shape, not story. Every over-cap line measured is a choice-feedback bullet: 124 sprout lines exceed 14 words (52 even without the choice label), and 218 reader lines exceed 30 (139 without the label), with science replies running to 71 words. Sprout also still carries keep-stone and sell scenes its own choices never reach, which is the round's one Critical item. Other repeat issues: too many new names in one scene (c4-tale-2, c10-bramble), idioms a small child takes literally, and theme or phonics asks repeated until they read as a quiz. Splitting feedback to one idea a line, and turning repeated asks into refrains, would fix most of it.
