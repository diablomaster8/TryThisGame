## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All ten units can be followed, and the fun packs are the most self-contained writing in the game: each quest is one tale with an arc. The breaks are payoffs. Dragon Ball has two that are broken outright. Bulma's trigger word is chosen in DB2 and never said again, and the "Nubbins carry the balls" choice is ignored and then contradicted by three "capsule case" lines. Kami's "if he dies, I die" comes one storyline after Piccolo dies. Four choices are read by nothing: DB19's keeping, E1's e1-hazel, and both of E2's. The notes of E1 and E2 admit "voice only", which the house rule doesn't allow. Smaller jumps go unexplained: Valeria calling Doom "Father", Alice turning up at your fire, the four-star ball back in Goku's bag, and the aliens in the crane. Set-ups wait for a payoff: Hazel's blue cloth, the plum-jam thief, Bo's "When did your kid last pick you?". Friendship and family are strong everywhere: Goku and Krillin, Holmes and Watson, Woody's goodbye, Nova and Dandy. Spouse love is the gap again. Pixel and Byte, the Potato Heads, Bulma and Yamcha, Pepper, and Sue and Reed are all on stage, and none of them gets a couple beat.

**Humour.** Hodja and Alice are reliably funny. Hodja has the dead pan, "The dead are good listeners", and the counting donkey. Alice has the Dodo, the Caterpillar and "I am only the one holding the Nubbin". Dragon Ball's straight men (Bulma, Roshi, Korin, Krillin) carry it well until DB18. Holmes is dry and warm but wastes Byte, its built-in sceptic, who has no entrance and no payoff. The flat stretches are reported set-pieces: Toy Story's toddler room, the yard-sale dash, and E1 Civil War before the courthouse. The fixes are of one kind: play the set-piece, put a straight man beside it, and give a Nubbin one stubborn, wordless job (staff at Pell's stall, Goku's seconds with a sponge, a badge with the number eaten). The cross-story rec brings canon 45's misname gag to Dragon Ball and Toy Story. Bonnie's misspelt "NUBINS" closes it.

**Learning Grove.** Both A02 bands meet their caps. Sprout has no story line over 14 words (mean 9.6) and Reader none over 30 (mean 15.8). Reader's Monkey-and-Pigsy double act and the Nubbin's wordless commentary are steadily funny. The sprout band cut set-ups its payoffs still need. The turtle's crossing, Monkey's "by lunch" boast and the zodiac race are all gone, but their payoffs stay. Sprout also never says why Monkey leaves, or that the second Monkey is a fake, so a young child thinks a friend turned bad. Reader's trouble is tone, not length. About ten narrator "Suppose…" lines read like a worksheet, a lesson sits inside the "very long lunch" punchline, and the 206-bones quiz lands on the grave bone reveal. Moving each lesson to a character who needs the answer, or one beat later, fixes all three. Pronouns are clear apart from "his chest" and "his mum" in Sprout.
