## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All ten units can be followed, and most breaks are cheap: a line that contradicts itself (Ida's "Nobody stood in front of it. I did."), a name with no introduction (Root as your sister, "Mari Djata" becoming Sundiata, Winry), or a place jump (the Keeper at A1-18's eclipse). The two real breaks are dead choices at the end of two storylines: Logged In's "find Juno outside" and the Beetle Road's "ask Ottoline up" reach no ending, and Two As One puts Ottoline on the surface even if you left her below. Dawn & Dusk's one choice gives itself away, and its door and lamps promise a payoff the Whole Day ending never delivers. The Atlas pages are strong: A03's letters choice pays off three times, A02's choice twice, and A01's Mustang choice once, though A01's best running motif (the Nubbin inside Al) is never paid off. Three new names clash with canon (Tam, Hob, Ash), and "forty years" is worn thin across the Beetle Road and Dawn & Dusk. No unit has a couple on screen; Hob and Ottoline already bicker like one, and Pixel and Byte fit E3's bracket.

**Humour.** A02 is reliably funny (Silver Horn questioning a silent Nubbin, Monkey in a nightcap), and A03 earns real laughs from its guardians on a grave page. The four Digimon-frame storylines each have one good comic engine that stalls: Ida and Sorrel never speak to each other, the Warden and "the cat is always fine" vanish after chapter 3, Hob stops counting after chapter 4, and the Penhallow twins' quarrel is told, never shown. The fixes are all of one kind: put the two straight men in the same line, make the running gag come back once more (the kettle lid, the cat, the drips, the neat hand), and give the Nubbins one wordless bit (the night ticks, the indoor rain, the merged Nubbin walking into the door) before each grave beat, never in it. E3's tournament and A02's disguise farce are summarised when they should be played.

**Learning Grove.** Both A05 bands meet their caps: no line over 14 words in Sprout (mean 8.7) or over 30 in Reader (mean 16.5), so the reader's "over 14" claims were miscounts and are dropped. The bands handle Sogolon's death plainly and gently, with comfort straight after. What's left is cause and effect and names. Sprout never says why the family flees or why the king's refusal matters (the basket), introduces two or more names in several scenes, and has bolted-on lessons (Fakoli's thirst, "rocket or car?"). Reader leans on irony and idioms ("has ears", "chew on", "So, naturally") and ends the whole storyline on two fact notices. Balla's tune "door" needs naming in both bands. Reader's a05-10 maths is correct (4 × 8 = 32, with the right reasons on the wrong answers), and "her first sale in days" squares with Kolonkan's "nobody buys", so no rec.
