## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All six mains can be followed, and each has a strong spine: Devasena's heap of sticks and Kattappa's choice (A04), Balla's "write that down" telling (A05), the porter's seven nights (A06), Héctor's photo (A07), Pupus's twelve ticks (A08) and the comet's date (A09). The breaks are small and cheap to fix. The worst is in A04: the 25-year fire is only ever "his fire", so the pyre's payoff is unclear, and the fix has to name its target without describing anything on it. Several set-ups don't reach their payoffs. A06's player whispers about an uncle nobody has mentioned. A06's sail patch is "seen before" but never shown. A07's photo is never shown being pocketed, and A09's lantern rescue uses a lantern nobody has seen. The A09 garnet branch never says the Nubbin comes into the cave, which a child will worry about. Grave beats are handled well. A04's pyre, A06's widowhood and A07's murdered great-great-grandfather and fading Héctor all stay grave, and no rec puts a joke inside them. Spouse love is the thinnest theme again. Amarendra and Devasena, Imelda and Héctor, and Moominpappa and Moominmamma are on the page, but none of them gets a bicker until this run's recs give them one.

**Humour.** A05, A08 and A09 are reliably funny: Balla's griot asides, Cilindric's knot of rules, the professors' "forty-three", the Muskrat's "unphilosophical". A06 is funny when the porter is in the room and flat when he isn't (A6-11 to A6-14). A07 is funny up to the talent show, then runs grave with no breath from the party to the cenote. A04 is the most serious page by design, with long court flashbacks and no frame relief. The fixes are of three kinds. Escalate gags that repeat word for word (Pupus's tick line, twelve times). Hand a foil the line (the Serendib farmer, a rebel beside Avanthika, Kattappa's "No."). Give the Nubbin a prop to be stubborn with (pebbles, a maraca, a marigold hat).

**Learning Grove.** Both bands meet their caps. One A06 sprout line is over 14 words (a06-14-gate, fixed inside the "promised" rec), and the reader bands have none over 30 (means: A06 sprout 8.9, reader 16.0; A07 sprout 8.5, reader 15.9). Death is said plainly and gently with comfort straight after in every case: Sinbad's wife, Chicharrón, Héctor, Mamá Coco. The two exceptions are the A06 sprout's elephants ("paid for in bones") and the reader's Chicharrón, whose comfort line waits behind a lesson. The real problem is accuracy. Six maths and fact lessons disagree with their own scenes, and the A07 queue teaches something false: 2,409 and 2,377 don't share their hundreds. The sprout bands also cut set-ups the payoffs need: the porter who can't read, Imelda believing she was left, Miguel crossing over, and what happens if Coco forgets. A07 sprout garbles its central twist ("Mamá Coco! You're the man in the photo!"). A06 sprout still uses idioms ("had it coming", "two heads") and "swore". Most of the measurer's 241 pronoun flags and 68 new-name flags are false alarms from recurring names. The real ones are the three Sinbads, the translator's "he" and Miguel's six relatives in one line, and the recs cover them.
