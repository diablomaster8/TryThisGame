## Where the writing stands

**Story.** A child can follow all ten units, and S30 and S31 are the strongest Learning Grove side stories so far. Dimple's hidden sadness, Fidget's shed and Tam's night in the long grass are clear, warm and well paced. The two real breaks are both trust stakes. In S30, Moss's account of Hush has Nubbins who have died still seeing her and shivering, which goes against the story's own plain death line. In Avatar Q64, neither band says what Kaito did, so Nalu's "I haven't forgotten" and "One chance" have nothing behind them. The other breaks are cheap set-up gaps. Payoffs arrive before their set-ups: Tam's "if" in sprout, the "nothing strange" rule that Nalu makes and then credits to Tuk, Ma Reedy's sneeze, Mr. Tweed's TEA card, and Dusty's "under your table". Names also come early: Bao, Lumi and Trotwell are used before their reveals, Pemba walks in as "a new boy", and the counting lesson names the Q19 runners before they appear. Two pieces of canon are missing from both bands: Granny Brock's quilt and soup in S31 (canon 42) and the fun-quest wives (canon 43).

**Humour.** The fun quests are reliably funny in both bands: Sir Dentwhistle talking to a fence post, "Gorbold has eaten the menu", Mr. Tweed's sock, "Strategically", and "Biscuit says thank you. By eating your hat". So are the readers of S30 and S31: "Balls can't be wrong. They haven't got the muscles for it", "Your knees have stopped being knees and become opinions", and "he sent a deposit". The gaps are in sprout. Some jokes are promised and never told: S31's guising "joke", Pian's wobbly spoon, the teeny bear. Some hang on irony a 4-year-old takes at face value: Ivy's "opposite of you" costume, "Your witness was on your side", "I never mind!". And S30's middle, from the hole to the wall, has no silly beat. The fixes stay with what works: a wordless Nubbin beat just before the grave moment (Luna's yawn, Flinch in the bread bin), a straight man with one dry line (Mrs Inkwell, Granny Brock, Ned's careful silence), and callbacks that pay off (Clover's gate, the duck gland, the boomerang). Pixel and Byte are both on stage in S30 and S31 but never bicker; two lines fix it.

**Learning Grove.** Both bands meet their caps: no story line is over 14 words in Sprout (means 7.1 to 9.0) or over 30 in Reader (12.4 to 16.0). Lessons mostly grow from the story, and the refrains work (Fidget's counting, "Medium toys", the five rocks). One placement habit shows up across units. A lesson lands between a set-up and its payoff: S31's shape and its reveal, Gorbold's POP before we know he's fine, Tam's "This one", Mr. Tweed's conducting, Clover's thunder story, and the S30 daylight lesson before Clover speaks. Moving each lesson after the reveal fixes the comic timing without losing any lesson. A few idioms remain where they hurt most: "kick the bucket" in a story about a death, "smug as a cushion", "cry for help", "my pride is still down there". Checked and dropped: the S31 guising sum (Ivy's 4 doors against Ned's 5 matches the text), "run fruit" (a Grove item, not a typo), and the Gorbold "POP" in sprout (he is shown fine straight after).
