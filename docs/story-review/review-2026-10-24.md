## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All four main pages can be followed, and they are among the best-made in the Atlas. The Snow Queen keeps its running gags (the beam, the bed with opinions, the sulking peas), the Wooden Boy has the cleanest scene-to-scene logic in the run, Homecoming earns its bed scene, and the Humbug plays Baum's comedy straight and well. The breaks are payoffs, not plot. Two signature choices are let down at the finish. A14's "dont" route ends on a line that is false (Odysseus said his name to the Phaeacians and to Laertes), and A15's Oz secret sets no flag at all. Three set-ups never land: Kay's "Waited? For me?", the Fairy's gravestone, and Gerda's joke about Kay's feet. A few lines lean on things never shown, such as A14's lost fleet, A15's Golden Cap bargain and the default Corvin lines. Spouse love is strongest here of any run: Penelope and Odysseus carry it. What it lacks is bickering, and the A12 ravens are the only other couple.

**Humour.** The mains are reliably funny in their first halves and thin out at their grave middles. Those are A14-9 to A14-13 (five grave quests in a row), A13's circus summary and A15's Witch's kitchen. The fixes are of one kind: a dry straight man (Eurylochus, the Witch, the Tiger, Penelope) and a wordless Nubbin beat before the grave moment, never inside it. Several running gags only need a callback, among them the berry count, the bed with opinions, "eleven or twelve days" and the bucket nobody empties. One cross-story rec turns the Nubbin's habit of keeping souvenirs (A13's bottle, A15's spectacles) into a gag across all four pages.

**Learning Grove.** All six band files meet their caps: no story line is over 14 words in sprout or over 30 in reader (the A12 means are 9.3 and 14.7). The recurring fault is lesson placement. In both bands, quizzes cut into the peaks: the ice-melt ask between Gerda's tears and "Kay looks at her", the simile quiz at the forgetting kiss, echo physics over Pinocchio's night of crying, a grief notice that tells the child Anticlea is wrong, the Argos question before the beggar's last look, and counting straight after the sailors die. The maths and fact checks found one real error. A13 reader's martens sum ("each took 7 of 30") contradicts the seven-hen deal, and nine A13 reader lessons use numbers the scene never says. Sprout also drops causes the main gives: the mirror getting into Kay, the fish nibbling the donkey away, who Athena is, and why "Nobody" is whispered to Argos. Tags are the cleanup: 42 K-2 C3 codes sit in reader files that should use 3–5, and grade-4 NGSS codes turn up in sprout.
