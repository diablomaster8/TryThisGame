## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All ten units can be followed, and the four new Atlas pages are faithful and warm: Zal's exile and return in the Shahnameh, Ananse buying the stories from Nyame, Rumi's hidden marks in the Honmoon, and Rabbi Loew's clay guard in Prague. Each source's heroes keep their story. One break is serious. A27's Rudaba quotes a praise that only one of three a27-11-praise choices gives, in main and reader. The rest are local. The reader band cut Sam's "anything you ask" promise that Zal's letter calls in. A30's Shmuel and Mirele speak before anyone introduces them, and the rabbi recites a 1904 encyclopedia sentence. A28's Wisp variant never says what the spider "threw in for nothing", and its spare-hornet gourd has no set-up. Choices are the weak point again: A27's taught word ("friend", "home", "sorry") has no flag and no payoff. A29's sleeve choice is the model to copy, since it pays off three times.

**Humour.** A28 and A29 are reliably funny. Ananse is a vain schemer, Aso and Ya Nsia are the dry straight women, and the Nubbin sticks to everything. In A29, Zoey's "Same, honestly." and "Hero of the car park" both land. A27 and A30 are funny in patches: the kettle, Sindokht's "when Kabul isn't on fire", the flood, Avram's key "I've had it twenty years". Their grave middles (A27-6 to A27-8, A30-3 to A30-6, A30-11) have no relief beside them. The gap is the same everywhere: set-ups that never come back. Bobby's mug, his "never touch the cases" and Celine's dislike of him; the smallest chick; the nurse who was right; the rabbi's coat; the Nubbin on Nana Ama's stool. Every fix this run pays one off inside its own page, so no gag assumes another Atlas page. The epics also hand us straight men we don't use: the scorpion-man's wife, Zal's page, a court elder.

**Learning Grove.** Story lines meet their caps: reader means are 12.8 to 15.8 words, sprout means 8.4, and no story line is over the cap. The lesson replies are what spill over. A29 reader has 22 joined replies over 30 words, A28 reader 3, and A27 sprout 1. Standards are the systemic fault: 20 to 33 lessons in each reader band carry K-2 codes, and sprout uses grade-4 4-LS1-1. The retag must use approved codes only, or drop the tag. Lessons still land inside grave beats or punchlines: Humbaba's death, Rumi's exposure, Jinu's confession, the kettle. Several ask for facts the scene never says, such as the Akan day names, the double-hour and Sable's glinting eyes. Sprout keeps adult idioms ("want for nothing", "call it even") and shows the child putting a feather on a fire it then calls grown-ups only. The cultures are handled with care in both bands: the Akan and Jamaican tellings, Persian court life, and Korean folk belief.
