## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All four pages can be followed, and each keeps faith with its source: the Pride Lands' two paths, Kipling's jungle and seal beach, Ende's two-colour book, and the epic's wall that frames the whole page. The real breaks are small and cheap to fix. Two numbers fight their own scenes: A26 sprout's "six days" against seven loaves (main's loaf list stops at six too), and A23 reader's "noon at the thorns" on a two-day crossing. One lesson quotes a Timon line that plays after it, and the A26 sprout band cut the lick that "I licked it first" points back to. A few "who is here" gaps remain: Bastian meets you in Fantastica with no first beat, and on A23's Rafiki path Timon never meets you. Several reader suggestions were dropped once checked against the source: A23-9 requires A23-8 in main, Ed is introduced where he appears, and the Bandar-log gag already recurs. Spouse love is the thinnest theme, though A26 has two old couples (Uta-Napishtim and his wife, the scorpion pair) ready for it, and A24's Sea Catch and Matkah.

**Humour.** A24 is the funniest page. The kettle howl runs from A24-2 to A24-18, Baloo plays the weary schoolmaster and Kaa is vain. A26's dry voice works too (the third stair, "Eating grass, mostly"). The flat stretches have the same fix every time: a set-up the page already made, paid off in the same quest. Zazu's list and Rafiki's tea for nobody (A23), Engywook's "Report back", Atreyu "not a natural poet" and the lion turned to stone mid-word (A25), "half the bear I was" taken literally (A24), and "The king says he did" (A26). Every payoff stays inside its page, so none assumes the player has finished another Atlas page. Grave beats (Mufasa, Akela, Artax, Enkidu) are untouched; the laughs sit before or after them.

**Learning Grove.** Story lines meet their caps in every band (sprout means 7.7 to 8.7 words a line, reader 12.1 to 13.3), but lesson replies don't. A24 reader has 64 over 30 words, and the sprout bands have 22 over 14 between them. The other systemic faults are placement and tagging. Quiz-tone lessons land on grave or turning beats (pole sana, Mowgli's "Both", Atreyu's parents, Enkidu's last day). Three deaths lack a comfort line. The reader bands carry K-2 social-studies codes that EDU-STYLE forbids there (37 across three pages), and the sprout bands carry grade 3 to 4 science codes. Seven lessons cite something their scene never said. The bands are funniest where they keep main's physical comedy, like Simba's squeak, the Nubbin's peek and "Knock, knock!", and flattest where they cut it: A24's gull chant and A26's first bread.
