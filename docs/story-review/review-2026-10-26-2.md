## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All ten units can be followed scene by scene, and no Critical breaks were found. Campaign books 1 and 2 hold up well, with canon intact: Hazel's letter and notebook, Ember and Ash, Fossil in Dusty's lap, Moss on "safe" and "well", and the empty camp in the Hollow. The weak spots are set-ups that never pay off. Nova's owl speech, Fern's second jumper and Corvin's race question all come back to nothing. Fossil arrives in chapter 6 only to fade, unless S5 was played. The Japanese line has three real slips. Obon is "nearly" here and then "nearly over" a day later. Hana asks the player to say はい when the choices only offer konnichiwa. The reader band stops for a snow quiz on the doorstep of the lantern. Both language courses are accurate. The Hebrew reader flagged three stress "errors" (shtayim, mayim, parpar), but all three were backwards: the game prints SHTAI-yim, MY-yim and par-PAR, which is standard modern Hebrew, so all three were dropped. A fourth rec rested on a false premise: Ori's "Not my birthday" already names his first favourite day.

**Humour.** The run's funniest writing is character comedy. In the campaign it's Sparks's "Crunchy!" and "I didn't touch it", Byte's four stopwatches, Pixel's MAYBE box and Moss's "Anyone didn't". In the language lines it's Hana's competitiveness, Nova's "Two days." / "No. Three.", Dumpling's late bows, Ori as a bad loser, and the Wobbler. The extras are already reliably funny: Dr Fettle, the Wise Old King, the race-finish lines and the Wheel of Mediocrity. The recs are mostly payoffs that cost a line or two: owls on the ledge, the jumper worn as a hat, Gerald sitting with Moss, Sparks saying "FLAT" in her sleep, and the frog that turns out to mean "go home". Grave beats stay grave. The jokes sit in the scene before or after (Fossil's bun, the frost-licking Nubbins), never inside the farewell or the notebook. Spouse love is the thinnest theme again. Pixel and Byte now bicker over his shoelace in c7-close and his stopwatches in LJ3.

**Learning Grove.** Every band line is within its cap: no Hebrew or Japanese reader line over 30 words (means 12.2 and 12.7) and no sprout line over 14 (mean 7.4). The one joined reply over cap in each Japanese band is a choice plus lesson option, which isn't capped. Most flagged "new names" are the same tellers returning (Hana, Ori, Nova), so only sprout's LJ3-2 needs a fix. The real child-read problems are small: "savta" before it's taught, three idioms, sprout's unexplained "dramatic", and a letter scene with too many "she"s. Both reader bands still carry K-2 standards, including C3 codes that EDU-STYLE rules out. Several codes the readers suggested aren't on the approved list either, so the cross-story rec uses approved codes only. Humour holds up well in both bands, and the jokes are always about Hana, Ori and the Nubbins, never the language.
