## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All eight side stories can be followed, and the breaks are cheap: a pronoun at a key line. Hazel's "Should I have gone after him?" (S10), Tobin's "him and your grandmother" (S13), Ember's "till he had Ash" (S11) and "the morning she meant" (S12) each need one name. The signature flags all pay off (corvinGate, hazel and walk, millLetter, s6Done, s7Done, s8Reunited). The gap is the small local choices, which change nothing on screen: S6's stay or leave, S7's lever, S8's s8-told, S10's racer, S12's Solo and S13's rats. Each now has a one-line echo, most of them jokes. One continuity slip matters: when S1 is done, S10's Hazel says she sat in the snow and that Moss left out the fidgeting. Moss told both, and said she fell in the pond. The fix keeps both accounts and turns them into an old friends' argument. Family and friendship are the strongest themes here, and trust is close behind (Corvin's gate, Byte's stopwatch, Ember's whistle). Pixel and Byte (S7) are the only couple on the page, and they are good.

**Humour.** S6, S7, S11 and S12 are reliably funny: the duck, the plum, Byte's labels, the Thrums' meetings. S9, S10 and S13 carry their jokes in a dry elder (Hazel, Tobin) and go quiet at the end, which is right. The flat stretches are the grave walks next to the farewells (S10's lanterns and night, S9's dawn, S12's dark garden), and the fix is the usual one: a straight man says something dry beside the grave beat, never in it. Luna's slugs, Hazel's hens, Hazel's empty cup and Corvin's two coughs all do this. The running gags travel well and should travel further: Byte's capital-letter labels reach the gate in S9, and the duck takes a back-row seat at Hazel's race.

**Learning Grove.** Both S1 bands meet their caps: Sprout has no line over 14 words (mean 8.4), and Reader none over 30 (mean 15.4). Their refrains (Moss's "Every year.", the shoelace) work. Sprout still leans on irony and idiom at its turning points ("they have her wrong", "You'll do", "a kind of keeping"), and its Cinder ending points at a "four home" this band never set up. Reader hints at Hush's death instead of saying it, never shows Cinder holding on, and stacks two or three lessons in most scenes. Two of those lessons cut into a payoff: the simile in Hush's memory and the flame lesson before the laugh. "Eternal" and the buried tin arrive unexplained.
