## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All eight side stories can be followed, and they are warm, specific and mostly funny. Tilly's grief in S14, Dashwood's "the week before" in S15, Brisk's "that's the trouble with being liked" in S16 and Cinder's "Well is better" in S19 are among the best beats in the game. The breaks are small: one pronoun at S21's turn (whose long look?), the Pip egg in S15 that is told afterwards rather than shown, and a Nightjar who is never said to be *keeping* the eggs. The real gap is choices again. Five of these stories are marked FOR FUN, and in four of them no choice is read anywhere: S16's tired five, S21's shed lesson and Byte verdict, S20's press-or-wait, and S14's Lair ending all change no later line. S16 is the worst, because its notes promise the wild five are seen in the week visit and they are not. This run wires each one to a concrete later scene through choice-payoffs.ts: Hazel's first evening home (S10), Spindle's ladder (S27), Byte's incident log (S28), and Tilly's badges and reunion (S24). S17 and S19 already pay off their signature choices; S18's centre case does too.

**Humour.** S14, S15 and S20 are reliably funny: Tilly's rulings, Byte's disputed hit points, Jackpot ("A POEM IS NOT A NUMBER"), Pixel's "Ruling: nearly", and Gordon being hit by weather. S16's banner drafts and S18's fainting mole are strong running gags, and the fixes build them further rather than add new ones. Flat stretches are where the comic character goes missing: Nova during S17's four climbs, Delve at the dig, Granny Brock's bell with no soup. One rec just protects a joke: the s4-swim insert in S19 spends Marina's socks two lines early. Pixel and Byte are married in canon but never speak to each other in S14 or S21. Two short bickers fix that, and the one in S21 sits just before Clank overhears, where the comic beat belongs.

**Learning Grove.** S2's bands both keep to their caps: no line is over 14 words in Sprout (mean 7.7) or over 30 in Reader (mean 13.4). Of 46 pronoun candidates, only Moss's "She never said…" and Luna's unnamed Sable need a fix. The shared break is the clue chain: Clover says "Warm. Dry. Fed.", but nobody feeds Wisp in the main story or either band. Sprout is clear but thin on laughs, and gets one physical gag (the fruit bigger than his head). Its two Moss riddles ("only the good ones", "Sensible things are not always warm things") get plain words. Reader is funny in an adult, dry way, and two British idioms ("expecting a boot", "might go off") will be read literally by a 7-year-old. Its Nubbins finally get a wordless joke of their own. Sprout never says that "Gran" and "Hazel" are one person. Reader's mercy question opens four replies with the same words, which reads as a quiz.
