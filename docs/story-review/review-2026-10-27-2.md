## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All seven units can be followed, and the side stories are the strongest writing in the run: S30's sadness-has-a-job arc and S31's Gourd Night pay off their set-ups and their branches. The breaks are local. A few lines contradict the choice the player just made: Fitz "hedged" after you made him bet on you (Q45), Rook goes shirtless after you told him not to (Q37), and Tuk "keeps his promise" in the branch where he made none (Q58). Ned thanks you "for being the witness" after you refused (S31). A few scenes lean on things the player never saw: Q61's map, Kaito's past in Q64, Corky's identity in Q34's opening. The real gap is structural. No flag from Q1 to Q65 is read anywhere (FUN_PACK_RULES starts at Q66), so the four older fun packs break the house rule that choices are real. The cross-story rec wires them, and the per-quest recs give the lines. S30's "broke" and "sat" branches also end identically. Family is thin in Sunny, and spouse love appears only where Pixel and Byte share a scene.

**Humour.** Python, Bean and Sunny are reliably funny, and S31 is a dependable comic pair (Tam's solemn logic against Ivy's clipboard). The fixes are mostly payoffs: set-ups waiting for their laugh, like Sunny's forty crates of eggs, Python's fourth chair, Avatar's warning bell and the cabbage merchant's cry. Sunny's one tic is Mags-as-a-bird, used eight times, and the rec turns it into a running gag with an ending. The flat stretches are S30's grave middle (Moss's Hush speech) and Avatar's sled scene. Both get a wordless Nubbin or straight-man beat before the grave line, never inside it.

**Learning Grove.** Reader-2 meets its cap: no story line is over 30 words (mean 15). Death is said plainly and gently, with comfort straight after, and Moss's and Hazel's goodbyes are the best of it. Two things need fixing first. c12-foot-secret praises carrying a heavy secret for a grown-up, then teaches the child to tell one. And three Cinder-epilogue lessons ask about lines the child hasn't read yet (an index fix). The rest is placement and plain words. Hazel's last walks carry two lessons each (cut to one), a cheerful egg sum follows a sold Nubbin, and a few ironies ("worse than me", "optimistic about the timing") would be taken literally. Most flagged pronouns are safe because the two characters differ in gender. Ash and Ember in c12-open, and Sable as "it", are the real slips.
