## Where the writing stands

**Story.** All eight side stories can be followed, and they are some of the strongest in the game: Nova climbing with her Worry (S22), Corvin's three nights and his ledger (S23), the back pages of Hazel's atlas (S24), Spindle's words in the frost (S27) and Byte's PARCEL: INCIDENTS log (S28). The local breaks are cheap: S23's "the tin" line, S25's unnamed notebook (it is Hazel's, not Nova's), S24's lunge with no object, S28's "never done that for anyone" against Byte's own graph, and S23's Collector Corvin denying the partner he wrote to. The real gaps are choices and wiring. S24's cage choice sets no flag, and S26's bulb, hello and plot choices and S22's chimney choice are read nowhere. Worse, scenes written to pay off choices never play. S24's rule doesn't route the bird, cairn, lights or Collector variants, so its signature choice is invisible. No rule queues S25's spring coda, so the toy's name never comes back. S26's ramp and lift evenings have no hook, and three ambient scenes (Ember's soup, Byte's barks, Granny Brock's door) have no trigger at all. Spouse love is again the thinnest theme: Pixel and Byte meet only in S28, and even there Pixel greets her own house like a guest.

**Humour.** S22, S26, S27 and S28 are reliably funny: the Worry's "Three.", "a hole with ambitions", Byte's "Statistically above average", the eleven-second stare and Horace. S24 and S29 open funny and go quiet in their grave middles: Fairweather's cage speech has no foil, and the night row upriver is all dread. S23's best gag, "Nothing felt", never escalates. The fixes are of one kind, as before. Give a straight man something to be dry about (Tilly on "Proof", Marina on the log, Byte on the saucer), let a Nubbin do something wordless just before the grave beat (snoring on Corvin's boots, false alarms in the bow), and pay off the set-ups that are waiting: the cushion nobody sits on, the gherkins, "Bring cake", and Pixel's NOTES FROM HOME.

**Learning Grove.** Both S3 bands are within their caps (sprout mean 8.1 words a line, none over 14; reader mean 13.8, none over 30), and the reader band is reliably funny ("a GOOD idea at the TIME", "The colours are also disputed"). The reader's flagged over-cap reply in s3-moot is two speakers in the game, Sparks and then Byte, so it stands. What's left is who and why. Sprout names Ash and Bramble without saying they are Ember's and Fern's Nubbins, splits Hazel into "Hazel" and "Gran Hazel", never says why the moved flag made Bramble fall, and leaves "Moot" unexplained. Reader uses "Him" for Corvin twice where the steward is the nearer man, and leans on "efficient", "steward" and "procedurally". Sprout's last third (courier, truce) is its flattest stretch, and its lesson names Byte before the "Him!" gag can land.
