## reader:book1

- Chapter 1 (The Letter): Find Gran’s eggs in the long grass. Hatch one, then pet the Nubbin that comes out. · The eggs hum near the fourth lantern post. Hold an egg and give it a gentle shake to hurry it. Tap the Nubbin and choose Pet.
- Chapter 2 (Shells and Promises): Pick fruit from the trees and feed a Nubbin from your hand. Do it twice. · Tap a tree to shake it, then tap the fruit to pick it up. Tap a Nubbin and choose Feed. Keep your palm flat!

- Chapter 4 (Moss Remembers): Pet Moss on the hill. Then light all four lanterns. · Moss sits on the western hill. Tap a lantern post to light it. The fourth post only flickers, but it still counts.

> _Gran’s joke makes you laugh. But you miss her, too. That’s why the laugh comes out the wrong shape._  
> _Can a person feel happy and sad at the very same time?_  
> - Yes. Both at once. → Lots of people would agree with you. A funny memory of someone you miss can be warm and sore together. Feelings don’t have to take turns. Both are allowed.
> - No. One feeling has to win. → That’s a fair way to see it. Sometimes one feeling is so big it squashes the other one flat. Hazel might say the laugh won this time, and the sad waited its turn. Both are allowed.
> - I’m not sure. → Not being sure is honest. Feelings can be muddly, like a drawer of odd socks. You don’t have to sort them out right now. Both are allowed.
> _For grown-ups: Your child thought about mixed feelings: how missing someone and laughing at their joke can happen together._

_It is warm. Small and damp and warmer than anything has a right to be. Bits of shell sit on its head like a hat._

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, ask): a simple circuit needs a complete loop  
> **Sparks**: Want to know the secret of lantern wiring? Electricity only travels round a loop.  
> **Sparks**: Out of the battery, along a wire, through the lamp, and back to the battery. Which lamp lights up?  
> - ✅ The one where the wire goes all the way round. → YES. A complete loop. Round and round it goes, and the lamp glows. That loop is called a circuit.
> - The one with a gap in the wire. → Nope! A gap is like a broken bridge. The electricity can’t get across, so the lamp stays dark. Close the gap, make a full loop, and it lights. That loop is called a circuit.
> - The one with the most wire. → Ha, I wish. Extra wire doesn’t help if there’s a gap anywhere. It just needs one complete loop with no breaks. That loop is called a circuit.
> _For grown-ups: Your child learned that electricity flows only round a complete loop (a closed circuit), and that one gap stops a lamp lighting._

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.NF.A.1, ask): unit fractions: one quarter  
> **Fern**: Four lanterns, and one of them won’t behave. What fraction of the lanterns is flickering?  
> - ✅ One quarter (1/4) → Yes. One out of four is a quarter. So three quarters are lit, and one quarter is sulking.
> - One half (1/2) → Half would be two out of the four. Only one is flickering. One out of four equal parts is one quarter: 1/4.
> - One third (1/3) → Thirds are for things split into three. We’ve got four lanterns, so we need quarters. One out of four is one quarter: 1/4.
> _For grown-ups: Your child named a unit fraction (1/4) from a real group of four lanterns._

> - 100 °C (212 °F) → Ooh, other end! At 100 °C (212 °F) water boils. It freezes at 0 °C (32 °F). An early-summer morning is much warmer than that. So why is it frozen round her?
> _For grown-ups: Your child learned that water freezes at 0 °C (32 °F) and that frost melts back into water when warmed, a change that can go both ways._

### Glossary

- **champion**: The winner, or the very best at something, like a racing contest.

- **memorial**: Something that helps people remember someone who has died, like a flower, a stone or a bench.
- **trade**: Giving one thing to get another thing in return.
- **tongue**: The part of your mouth you taste with, or anything long and thin shaped like one, such as a tongue of frost.
- **nocturnal**: Awake and busy at night and asleep in the day, like owls, bats and hedgehogs.
- **personification**: Writing about a thing or an idea as if it were a person or alive, with feelings and wants.
- **shard**: A sharp, broken piece of something hard, like glass, pottery or stone.
- **well**: A word with two meanings: a deep hole you get water from, or healthy and happy in yourself.
- **calibrated**: Set up and checked so that it measures exactly right.
- **statistically**: Going by numbers and measurements collected about lots of things.
- **stray**: An animal that has no home or has got lost, and wanders about.

### Glossary
