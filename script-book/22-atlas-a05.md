## A5-1. The Hunters’ Prophecy

*The Epic of Sundiata (Mande oral epic, as told by griots; D. T. Niane’s telling, 1960): the hunter of Sangaran* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Lion of Mali page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time once Nova’s atlas is in the Grove; the first quest on the page.

**Cast:** Balla Fasséké (Balla Fasséké, griot to Sundiata, first of the Kouyaté griots (Mande oral epic)), Doua (Gnankouman Doua, griot to King Naré Maghan and father of Balla Fasséké (Niane’s telling)), The Hunter of Sangaran (The hunter-soothsayer who foretells Sundiata’s birth (Niane’s telling)), King Naré Maghan (Naré Maghan (Maghan Kon Fatta), king of Manding, Sundiata’s father)

### Step 1: Step into the red-earth page and find the young griot.

Hint: Touch the page, then follow the sound of a balafon to the town gate. · check: `explore:a05-niani:gate`

#### Scene `a05-01-page`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page the colour of red earth, patterned black and white like cloth dyed with mud._  
_Along the bottom someone has drawn a lion in one long line, and a buffalo in another, walking the same way._  
_In the corner, in Nova’s hand: “Griots have told this for eight hundred years, and no two tell it alike.”_  
_Under that: “Listen to more than one.”_  
_The red earth warms under your palm. Then it is dust, and round thatched houses, and the smell of millet smoke._  
**Balla Fasséké** _(shock)_: You came out of a picture. My father said someone would, one day. He says a lot of things.  
**Balla Fasséké**: I’m Balla. Balla Fasséké, son of Doua, who is griot to the king. One day I’ll be a griot too. Today I carry his things.  
**Balla Fasséké**: You could carry some of them. A griot needs an apprentice, and I haven’t got one yet.  
_Your Nubbin is already sitting on his balafon, a long row of wooden keys with gourds hung underneath. Balla doesn’t seem to mind._  

**Choices**

- **Ask what a griot does.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: We remember. Who married whom, who fought where, who was brave and who only said so.
    - **Balla Fasséké**: A king without a griot is a king nobody will remember. Kings know that. It makes them polite to us.
- **Offer to carry the balafon.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: Gently. It’s older than my father, and it knows it.

#### Done `a05-01-page-done`

**Balla Fasséké**: Come. The king sits under the silk-cotton tree in the mornings. Something is going to happen there today.  
_You don’t know how he knows. By the look of him, he doesn’t either._  

### Step 2: Stand at the back of the king’s court and listen to the hunter.

Hint: Stay beside Balla under the silk-cotton tree, and don’t interrupt. · check: `watch:a05-hunter:court`

#### Scene `a05-01-court`

_King Naré Maghan sits in the shade of a great silk-cotton tree, with his court around him and a bowl of kola nuts at his elbow._  
_Beside him stands a man with a calm face who seems to be listening to everything at once. That is Doua._  
_A stranger comes in off the road: a hunter, with a bow on his back and a bag of cowrie shells at his belt._  
**The Hunter of Sangaran**: I’m a hunter of Sangaran, king. I haven’t come for meat today. I’ve come because the shells told me to.  
**King Naré Maghan**: Then sit, hunter. A king should hear what the shells say, even when he doesn’t like it.  
_The hunter throws the cowries on a goatskin and looks at them for a long time._  
**The Hunter of Sangaran** _(think)_: Two hunters will come to your gate with a woman. She is not beautiful. Marry her anyway.  
**The Hunter of Sangaran**: She will give you a son, and that son will make the name of Manding last as long as the sun does.  
_Somebody in the court laughs. Doua looks at him, and he stops._  

**Choices**

- **Watch the king’s face.**
    - The king says nothing. He turns a kola nut over in his fingers, slowly, like a man deciding to remember.
- **Watch Balla.**
    - Balla’s lips are moving. He is saying the hunter’s words over to himself, so that he will have them.

#### Done `a05-01-court-done`

**Doua**: Hunter, the king thanks you. Eat before you go, and eat well.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Did you get all that? Say it back to me tonight. Then we’ll both have it.  

#### Complete `a05-01-complete`

_That night, by the fire, you say the hunter’s words back to Balla. You get most of them, and he fills in the rest._  
**Balla Fasséké**: That’s the first thing a griot learns. Hear it once, keep it always.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Some tellers leave the hunter out, and start with the two brothers and the buffalo. My father starts here.  
**Balla Fasséké** _(happy)_: He says a story should begin with a promise. Then everybody listens to find out if it’s kept.  
_Balla taps one key of the balafon with his thumb. It makes a low, round sound, like water moving in a gourd._  
_Your Nubbin taps the same key. The sound comes out flatter, and much more pleased with itself._  
**Balla Fasséké**: We’ll work on it.

## A5-2. Sogolon

*The Epic of Sundiata (Mande oral epic, as told by griots; D. T. Niane’s telling, 1960): the buffalo of Do and Sogolon’s coming to Niani* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Lion of Mali page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A5-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A5-1

**Cast:** Balla Fasséké (Balla Fasséké, griot to Sundiata, first of the Kouyaté griots (Mande oral epic)), The Elder Hunter (One of the two Traoré hunter brothers who bring Sogolon to Manding (names differ by telling)), Sogolon (Sogolon Kedjou, the “buffalo woman” of Do, Sundiata’s mother), King Naré Maghan (Naré Maghan (Maghan Kon Fatta), king of Manding, Sundiata’s father), Doua (Gnankouman Doua, griot to King Naré Maghan and father of Balla Fasséké (Niane’s telling))

### Step 1: Sit by the fire and hear Balla tell the tale of the buffalo of Do.

Hint: Find Balla at the fire behind Doua’s house and sit down. · check: `sit:a05-balla:fire`

#### Scene `a05-02-fire`

**Balla Fasséké**: Before the strangers reach the gate, you should know where they’ve been. Sit. This is a fire story.  
**Balla Fasséké**: In the land of Do there was a buffalo that nobody could hunt. It trampled the fields, and the best hunters came home with nothing.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Two brothers went after it. On the road they met an old woman, hungry and alone, and they shared their food with her.  
**Balla Fasséké**: She told them the truth: she was the buffalo. And because they’d been kind, she told them how to win.  
**Balla Fasséké** _(soft)_: She asked one thing. The king of Do would offer them any girl they liked. They were to choose the plainest. Her name was Sogolon.  
_Your Nubbin has crept so close to the fire that its feet are warm. It doesn’t move them._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Some tellers say the old woman was Sogolon’s aunt. Some say she was Sogolon’s own spirit, walking about without her.  
**Balla Fasséké**: My father says it doesn’t matter which. What matters is that she was strong, and that it came to the girl.  

**Choices**

- **Ask which one Balla believes.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: I believe whichever one I’m telling. That’s the job.
- **Say the brothers won by being kind.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: Yes. Write that one down in your head. It comes back.

#### Done `a05-02-fire-done`

_Out on the road a dog barks, and then another. Balla is on his feet before the third._  
**Balla Fasséké**: That’s them. Come on.  

### Step 2: Walk with Sogolon from the gate to the king’s court.

Hint: Meet the two hunters at the gate and walk beside the woman with them. · check: `escort:a05-sogolon:court`

#### Scene `a05-02-gate`

_Two dusty hunters come through the gate. Between them walks a young woman, stooped, with a hump on her back and very bright eyes._  
**The Elder Hunter**: We bring her to the king of Manding. We were told the king of Manding would know why.  
_People come out of their doorways to look. Someone whispers, and someone else doesn’t bother to whisper._  
**Sogolon**: I’ve been looked at before. It doesn’t wear me out the way they hope.  
**The Elder Hunter**: She’s walked from Do on her own feet. We offered her a donkey. She said the donkey looked tired.  
**Sogolon**: It did look tired.  
_She walks steadily, though the road is long and the court is a long way up it._  

**Choices**

- **Walk beside her.**
    - You fall in beside her. She glances at you, and something in her shoulders lets go a little.
    - **Sogolon**: A stranger from a picture, and a small round thing. Good. I like the company of odd things.
- **Offer to carry her bundle.**
    - **Sogolon**: No. It’s mine, and it’s light. But thank you for asking instead of taking it.
    - Your Nubbin takes hold of one corner anyway, and walks the rest of the way like that, helping.

#### Done `a05-02-gate-done`

_Under the silk-cotton tree, the court laughs out loud when it sees her. The king doesn’t._  
**King Naré Maghan**: Doua. The hunter of Sangaran. Do you remember what he said?  
**Doua**: Every word, my king.  

#### Complete `a05-02-complete`

**King Naré Maghan**: Then I know why you brought her to me. Sogolon of Do, will you be my wife?  
_The laughing stops all at once, like a pot taken off the fire._  
**Sogolon** _(angry)_: I will. And I’ll thank your court to stop staring. I haven’t grown a second head.  
_Doua is the first to laugh, warmly this time. Then the king laughs too, and the rest of the court has to._  
**Balla Fasséké** _(happy)_: Remember that. She made them laugh with her, not at her, in one breath. My father has been trying to do that for twenty years.

## A5-3. The Boy Who Couldn’t Walk

*The Epic of Sundiata (Mande oral epic, as told by griots; D. T. Niane’s telling, 1960): Sundiata’s childhood and the baobab leaves* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Lion of Mali page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A5-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A5-2

**Cast:** Balla Fasséké (Balla Fasséké, griot to Sundiata, first of the Kouyaté griots (Mande oral epic)), Sogolon (Sogolon Kedjou, the “buffalo woman” of Do, Sundiata’s mother), Sundiata (Sundiata Keita (Sogolon Djata, Mari Djata), founder of the Mali Empire), Sassouma Bérété (Sassouma Bérété, the king’s first wife, mother of Dankaran Touman), Doua (Gnankouman Doua, griot to King Naré Maghan and father of Balla Fasséké (Niane’s telling))

### Step 1: Visit Sogolon’s house and keep her son company.

Hint: Sogolon’s house is the small one at the back of the royal yard. · check: `visit:a05-sogolon:house`

#### Scene `a05-03-house`

_The atlas page turns a little, the way a griot skips ahead. A wedding, a birth and seven rainy seasons pass in a breath._  
_Sogolon’s son is seven years old. He sits in the dust outside her door, broad and solemn, and he crawls wherever he goes._  
**Balla Fasséké**: His name is Mari Djata. He doesn’t talk much. He eats like three boys and he watches everything.  
_Your Nubbin toddles over and sits down in front of him. The boy looks at it for a long time._  
_Then he puts out one hand, very slowly, and pats it on the head._  
**Sogolon** _(soft)_: He doesn’t do that for many people. Your little round friend is honoured.  
**Balla Fasséké**: The old king died in the dry season, quietly, with my father beside him. Dankaran Touman is king now. His mother rules.  
**Balla Fasséké** _(sad)_: Some people say the hunter was wrong. They say it where Sogolon can hear.  

**Choices**

- **Sit down in the dust with the boy.**
    - You sit, and nobody says anything. The boy goes on watching the yard, and you watch it with him.
    - After a while you notice he isn’t waiting for anything. He’s just looking, very hard, at all of it.
- **Ask Sogolon if he’ll walk one day.**
    - **Sogolon**: He will walk when he walks. I don’t pull up seedlings to make them grow faster.
    - **Sogolon**: And until then, he is my son, and that is already a great deal.

#### Done `a05-03-house-done`

**Balla Fasséké** _(think)_: Mark this, apprentice. He isn’t waiting to become someone. He already is someone. He’s just not finished.  

### Step 2: Go with Sogolon to ask Queen Sassouma for baobab leaves.

Hint: Walk with Sogolon across the yard to the queen mother’s house. · check: `escort:a05-sogolon:sassouma-yard`

#### Scene `a05-03-leaves`

**Sogolon**: I need a few baobab leaves for the sauce. Ours are gone. Sassouma has plenty.  
_Queen Sassouma sits in her own yard with her women, under a baobab heavy with leaves. She sees Sogolon coming a long way off._  
**Sogolon**: Sister. A handful of leaves, for my son’s supper.  
**Sassouma Bérété**: Take them, of course. My son picked them for me himself, this morning. He climbed up to fetch them. On his own two legs.  
**Sassouma Bérété**: Your son, I hear, still fetches things the way a lizard does.  
_The women laugh. Sogolon takes the leaves, and her hand doesn’t shake, quite._  

**Choices**

- **Say nothing, and walk out beside Sogolon.**
    - You walk out beside her. She keeps her back straight all the way to her own door, which is a long way, for her.
- **Look Sassouma in the eye.**
    - You look at Sassouma. She looks back, cool and pleasant, as if you were a cloud that might rain.
    - **Balla Fasséké**: Good. She’ll remember your face. Mind you, that may not be good.

#### Done `a05-03-leaves-done`

_At her own door, Sogolon sits down on the step and cries, quietly, with her face in the leaves._  
_Her son crawls over to her. Your Nubbin sits on her other side and leans._

_Sogolon looks up. So does Balla, and so, it seems, does the whole yard._  
**Sogolon** _(sad)_: Because of leaves. Because of a woman with a sharp tongue. Because of you, my son.  
**Sundiata** _(angry)_: Then dry your eyes, Mother. Today I stand up.  
**Sundiata**: Send to the forges. Ask the smiths for a rod of iron, so heavy it takes all of them to carry it.  
_Balla’s mouth is open. He shuts it, and you see him say the words over to himself, quickly, so he will have them._

## A5-4. The Iron Rod

**Cast:** Balla Fasséké (Balla Fasséké, griot to Sundiata, first of the Kouyaté griots (Mande oral epic)), The Master Smith (The master smith of Niani who forges the iron rod (named differently by different tellers)), Sogolon (Sogolon Kedjou, the “buffalo woman” of Do, Sundiata’s mother), Sundiata (Sundiata Keita (Sogolon Djata, Mari Djata), founder of the Mali Empire), Sassouma Bérété (Sassouma Bérété, the king’s first wife, mother of Dankaran Touman)

### Step 1: Run to the smiths with Sogolon’s message.

Hint: The forges are down by the river, where the smoke is blackest. · check: `deliver:a05-message:forge`

#### Scene `a05-04-forge`

_The smiths’ quarter smells of charcoal and hot water. Hammers ring on every side, each on its own beat._  
_The master smith is a big, quiet man with burn marks up both forearms. He listens to the whole message without stopping work._  
**The Master Smith**: The heaviest rod we’ve ever made. For the crawling boy.  
**The Master Smith**: My father forged a rod years ago, for a day like this. He said someone would come asking. He kept it under the bellows.  
_It takes six of his apprentices to drag it out. It is as long as two of you and dark as a wet night._  
**The Master Smith** _(think)_: Smiths keep secrets, apprentice. That’s half of what we are. The other half is patience.  

**Choices**

- **Take hold of the rod with the apprentices.**
    - You take hold, and it is so heavy your arms forget they are yours. Your Nubbin pushes from behind, which helps exactly nothing.
- **Ask if a boy can really lift that.**
    - **The Master Smith**: No. A boy can’t. That’s the point of asking for it.

#### Done `a05-04-forge-done`

_The whole quarter downs hammers and follows the rod up the hill. So do half the children in Niani._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Everyone wants to see it. Half of them want to laugh. Stay close to me and keep your ears open.  

### Step 2: Watch the boy stand.

Hint: Stand with Balla at the edge of the crowd outside Sogolon’s door. · check: `watch:a05-rod:sogolon-door`

#### Scene `a05-04-stand`

_The smiths lay the rod down in front of Sogolon’s door. The crowd goes quiet in a ring around it._  
_The boy crawls to it. He takes it in both hands, and sets one end in the dust._  
_He pulls, and his arms shake. Sweat runs down his face, and still he pulls, slowly, hand over hand, up the rod._  
_Nobody breathes. Your Nubbin has its paws over its eyes, and is looking through them._  
_He is on his knees, then on one foot._  
_Then he is on both feet, with the iron rod bent in his hands like a hunter’s bow._  
**Balla Fasséké** _(shock)_: He’s standing.  
_Then Balla is singing. It is a praise song, his own, made on the spot, and the whole hill takes it up._  
**Sogolon** _(happy)_: My son is standing.  

**Choices**

- **Cheer.**
    - You cheer. Your Nubbin cheers, too, which is a high round squeak, and runs three times round his feet.
- **Look at Sogolon.**
    - Sogolon isn’t cheering; she has her hands over her mouth. She has waited seven years, and she is still waiting, one breath more.

#### Done `a05-04-stand-done`

**Balla Fasséké**: Some tellers say the first rods snapped in his hands, and he leaned on his mother’s stick in the end. Some say it was a baobab branch.  
**Balla Fasséké**: I was there. It was iron. It bent. But I’ll tell you the stick version, too, because that one’s also true.  

### Step 3: Gather the baobab’s leaves for Sogolon.

Hint: Pick up the leaves around the baobab now standing at Sogolon’s door. · check: `collect:a05-leaves:baobab`

#### Scene `a05-04-baobab`

**Sundiata**: Mother. You wanted baobab leaves.  
_He walks, a little stiffly, to the edge of the town where a young baobab grows. He puts his arms around it._  
_In Niane’s telling, and plenty of others, he pulls it up by the roots and carries it home._  
_He sets it down at his mother’s door. The leaves shake all over the yard._  
**Sundiata**: From now on, the women of Niani will come to you for leaves.  
**Sogolon** _(happy)_: That’s enough for one day, my son. Sit down, before you fall down.  

#### Done `a05-04-baobab-done`

_You and your Nubbin gather the leaves into baskets. There are a great many._  
_Across the yard, Queen Sassouma’s door is shut. Nobody saw it close._  

#### Complete `a05-04-complete`

**Balla Fasséké**: From today they’ll call him Sogolon’s Djata. Say it quickly, and you get Sundiata.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Djata means lion. So that’s his mother and the lion, both, in one name. Some tellers make it from other words. They’re not wrong either.  
_Sogolon gives you a baobab seed, round and hard as a pebble, and folds your fingers over it._  
**Sogolon** _(soft)_: For your own yard. They grow very slowly. Don’t let that worry you.  
_Your Nubbin tries to eat it, and you take it back. Sogolon laughs out loud._

## A5-5. The Griot

*The Epic of Sundiata (Mande oral epic, as told by griots; D. T. Niane’s telling, 1960): Balla Fasséké becomes Sundiata’s griot* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Lion of Mali page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A5-4, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A5-4

**Cast:** Balla Fasséké (Balla Fasséké, griot to Sundiata, first of the Kouyaté griots (Mande oral epic)), Doua (Gnankouman Doua, griot to King Naré Maghan and father of Balla Fasséké (Niane’s telling)), Sundiata (Sundiata Keita (Sogolon Djata, Mari Djata), founder of the Mali Empire), Sogolon (Sogolon Kedjou, the “buffalo woman” of Do, Sundiata’s mother)

### Step 1: Stand witness when Balla is given to Sundiata as his griot.

Hint: Meet Doua and Balla at the hunters’ clearing outside the town. · check: `talk:a05-doua:clearing`

#### Scene `a05-05-given`

_Sundiata is ten now, and hunts with the older boys. Every hunter in Manding has already started telling stories about him._  
_In the hunters’ clearing, Doua puts his hand on his son’s shoulder and walks him forward._  
**Doua**: The old king asked for this before he died. Every king has a griot. My son will be yours.  
**Sundiata**: I don’t need someone to sing at me.  
**Doua**: No. You need someone to remember you. You’ll do things you’ll forget. He won’t.  
**Balla Fasséké** _(happy)_: And I’ll tell you when you’re being a fool. That’s in it too. Nobody mentions that part.  
**Sundiata**: Then you’d better stay close. I’ll need telling.  
**Balla Fasséké**: In my father’s telling, the old king gave me to Sundiata while he was still crawling. I tell it now, because now is when I knew.  

#### Done `a05-05-given-done`

_Sundiata and Balla shake hands, very formally, like two old men. Then Sundiata trips Balla into the grass._  
**Balla Fasséké** _(sad)_: He’s going to be a great king. Write that down.  

### Step 2: Let Balla teach your Nubbin a tune on the balafon.

Hint: Sit your Nubbin at the balafon by Balla’s house and choose the tune it learns. · check: `minigame:a05-balafon:lesson`

#### Scene `a05-05-lesson`

**Balla Fasséké**: Now. If you’re my apprentice, your round friend is too. It’s been staring at this balafon for ten years.  
_He puts a pair of beaters into your Nubbin’s paws. They are nearly as long as it is._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Wood on wood. The gourds underneath sing back. You don’t hit the key. You ask it.  
_Your Nubbin asks it, and the key answers, round and low. Your Nubbin looks up at you with enormous pride._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Good. One tune, to start. A griot has a thousand, but everyone has a first.  
**Balla Fasséké**: There’s the hunters’ tune, quick and stamping, the one they play for Sundiata when he comes home with meat.  
**Balla Fasséké** _(soft)_: Or there’s the one Sogolon hums at night, when she’s grinding millet. She doesn’t know anybody hears it. Everybody does.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Which one?

    - **Balla Fasséké**: That’s a tune for one person. Whoever hears it will think it’s for them.

_Your Nubbin sways, and the owls close their eyes. Somewhere below, a door bangs, and heavy feet climb the stairs fast._  
_Soumaoro Kanté fills the doorway, tall, in a cloak the colour of iron, his face like thunder. Then he hears the tune._  
**Soumaoro Kanté** _(soft)_: My mother hummed that. At night, at the grinding stone, when she thought I was asleep.  
**Soumaoro Kanté** _(angry)_: Nobody touches that balafon. I have killed kings for less than this.  
_Balla doesn’t stop. He plays the tune through to its end, and then, without a pause, he begins a praise song for Soumaoro._  
_It is a good one. It names Soumaoro’s forges and his fathers and the walls of Sosso, and it gets every one of them right._  
**Soumaoro Kanté**: Hm.  
**Soumaoro Kanté** _(think)_: Dankaran Touman sent you as a gift, griot. I think he gave me more than he knew. You stay here. You play for me now.  
_Balla bows. When he straightens, he looks at you, once, and you understand: he isn’t free, but he isn’t finished._  

#### Variant `a05-14-complete-lullaby`

_The ring falls quiet. Balla stands before the kings to tell, for the first time, the story of Sundiata._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Before I begin, my apprentice’s round friend will play us in. It learned the tune I’d have chosen myself.  
_Your Nubbin plays Sogolon’s lullaby on Balla’s balafon. Thousands of people go quiet to hear it._  
**Kolonkan** _(soft)_: That’s our mother’s grinding tune. She never knew anyone heard it.  
**Sundiata** _(happy)_: She’d be cross to be the first thing in the story. Then she’d be very pleased.  
**Balla Fasséké** _(happy)_: Eight hundred years from now, my children’s children will still be telling this. You’ll see. And they’ll argue about it.  
**Balla Fasséké**: That’s how you know a story is alive. Dead ones nobody argues about.  
_He puts the little balafon into your arms, and the beaters into your Nubbin’s hands._  
**Balla Fasséké** _(soft)_: Go home, apprentice. Remember us properly.  
_The red earth goes soft under your feet, then smooth, then paper._  
_You are back in the Grove with Nova’s atlas on your knee. From under the cover a tune keeps going, and your Nubbin taps it out, very nearly in time._
