# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Atlas, page A5: The Lion of Mali

## A5-1. The Hunters’ Prophecy

- `a05-01-s1`: Step into the red page and find Balla. · _Touch the page. Follow the balafon._
- `a05-01-s2`: Listen to the hunter, under the big tree. · _Stay by Balla. Listen quietly._

#### `sprout:a05-01-page` · [main](main.md#a05-01-page)

_A big book falls open. The page is red, like earth._  
_Black and white zigzags run round the edge. Zig, zag, zig, zag!_  
_You step into the page: dust, round houses, smoke!_  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, notice): a place on the map: Africa, West Africa, Manding and Mali  
> _You are in a land called Manding. It is in West Africa._  
> _Manding will grow into a great kingdom called Mali._  
> _This story is about 800 years old._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that this story happens in Manding, in West Africa, which grew into the kingdom of Mali long ago._

**Balla Fasséké**: Hello! I’m Balla. I carry my father’s things.  
**Balla Fasséké**: I’m a griot. A griot remembers stories.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Will you be my helper?  
_Your Nubbin sits on Balla’s balafon. Balla smiles._  

**Choices**

- **Ask what a griot does.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: Griots remember who was brave. Who was kind.
    - **Balla Fasséké**: We sing and tell stories, too!
- **Offer to carry the balafon.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: Gently, please. It is very old.

#### `sprout:a05-01-page-done` · [main](main.md#a05-01-page-done)

**Balla Fasséké**: Come! The king sits under a big tree.  
_Something is going to happen there today._  
_(Look at the page. Can you see round houses?)_  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 5  
> _(Count the round houses together.)_  
> _One, two, three, four, five. Five houses!_  
> Check: `edu:count:houses:5`
> _For grown-ups: Counted five round houses, one finger each._


#### `sprout:a05-01-court` · [main](main.md#a05-01-court)

_The king sits under a huge tree. People sit all around._  
_A hunter comes in with a bag of shiny shells._  
**The Hunter of Sangaran**: King, the shells have something to tell you.  
**King Naré Maghan**: Sit, hunter. I will listen.  
_The hunter throws the shells on a goatskin. Clatter!_  
**The Hunter of Sangaran**: A strong woman will come to your gate.  
**The Hunter of Sangaran**: She will have a son. He will make Manding great.  

> **Lesson** (sel, SL.K.1, ask): listening with ears and eyes  
> _The king listens to the hunter. What do good listeners do?_  
> - ✅ They listen and look at the speaker → Yes. I listen with my ears and my eyes.
> - They shout over the top → Shouting makes it hard to hear. Good listeners stay quiet.
> - They run round and round → Running is fun, but it’s hard to listen then. Sit still and listen.
> _For grown-ups: Practised listening with ears and eyes while someone is talking._


**Choices**

- **Watch the king’s face.**
    - The king says nothing. He turns a nut in his fingers.
- **Watch Balla.**
    - Balla’s lips move. He says the hunter’s words again, to keep them.

#### `sprout:a05-01-court-done` · [main](main.md#a05-01-court-done)

**Doua**: Hunter, thank you. Please eat before you go.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Did you get all that? Say it back to me tonight.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Approaches to Learning, notice): saying words again and again helps us remember  
> **Balla Fasséké**: A griot says words again and again, until they stick.  
> **Balla Fasséké**: Try it. Say “shells, son, strong” three times.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that repeating words aloud is a way to remember them._


#### `sprout:a05-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a05-01-complete)

_By the fire, you say the hunter’s words back to Balla._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Hear it once. Keep it always.  
**Balla Fasséké**: A story begins with a promise. Everyone listens to see if it comes true.  
_Balla taps one key with his thumb. A low, round sound!_  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, do): tapping makes a shaking, and shaking makes sound  
> _(Tap the table softly. Put your other hand on it.)_  
> _Can you feel it shake? Shaking makes sound._  
> Check: `edu:act:tap:hands`
> _For grown-ups: Tapped a surface and felt it shake: vibrations make sound._

_Your Nubbin taps it too. Its sound is flat and very proud._  
**Balla Fasséké**: We’ll work on it.  

## A5-2. Sogolon

- `a05-02-s1`: Sit by the fire and hear a story. · _Find Balla at the fire._
- `a05-02-s2`: Walk with Sogolon to the king. · _Meet the hunters at the gate._

#### `sprout:a05-02-fire` · [main](main.md#a05-02-fire)

**Balla Fasséké**: Sit by the fire. This is a fire story.  
**Balla Fasséké**: A huge buffalo trampled the fields. Nobody could catch it.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Two brothers went to find it. On the road they met an old woman.  
**Balla Fasséké**: She was hungry. The brothers shared their food.  
**Balla Fasséké**: That was kind. So she told them how to win.  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, ask): sharing is a kind thing to do  
> _What did the brothers do that was kind?_  
> - ✅ They shared their food → Yes! Sharing is kind. And kindness came back to them.
> - They ate it all → The brothers didn’t eat it all. They shared it with the old woman.
> - They ran away → They didn’t run. They sat down and shared.
> _For grown-ups: Picked out sharing food as the kind act in the story._

**Balla Fasséké**: She asked for one thing. “Choose Sogolon. She is strong.”  
_Your Nubbin’s feet are warm by the fire._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Some say the old woman was the buffalo. Some say she was Sogolon’s aunt.  

**Choices**

- **Ask which one Balla believes.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: I believe the one I’m telling. That’s my job.
- **Say the brothers won by being kind.**
    - **Balla Fasséké**: Yes! Keep that one.

#### `sprout:a05-02-fire-done` · [main](main.md#a05-02-fire-done)

_Out on the road, a dog barks. Woof!_  
_Another dog barks, woof! Then a third, woof!_  
**Balla Fasséké**: That’s them! Come on!  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 3  
> _(Bark three times together and count on your fingers.)_  
> _One, two, three!_  
> Check: `edu:count:barks:3`
> _For grown-ups: Counted three barks on three fingers._


#### `sprout:a05-02-gate` · [main](main.md#a05-02-gate)

_Two dusty hunters come through the gate._  
_Between them walks a young woman. She is stooped, with very bright eyes._  
**The Elder Hunter**: We bring her to the king.  
_People come to look. Some stare, and some whisper._  
**Sogolon**: People look at me. It doesn’t stop me walking.  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, ask): being kind when others stare  
> _People stare at Sogolon. What could you do that is kind?_  
> - ✅ Walk beside her → A friend beside me. Good.
> - Stare too → Staring can hurt. Walking beside her says, “I’m with you.”
> - Hide behind a wall → Hiding doesn’t help her. Walking beside her does.
> _For grown-ups: Chose to walk beside someone being stared at, rather than stare._

**The Elder Hunter**: We offered her a donkey. She said it looked tired!  
**Sogolon**: It did look tired.  

**Choices**

- **Walk beside her.**
    - You walk beside her. Her shoulders relax.
    - **Sogolon**: A small round friend. I like odd company.
- **Offer to carry her bundle.**
    - **Sogolon**: No, it’s light. But thank you for asking.
    - Your Nubbin holds one corner anyway.

#### `sprout:a05-02-gate-done` · [main](main.md#a05-02-gate-done)

_Under the big tree, the court laughs. The king does not._  
**King Naré Maghan**: Doua. Do you remember what the hunter said?  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): clap the beats in a name  
> _(Clap the parts of her name: So-go-lon.)_  
> _One, two, three claps. Sogolon has three parts!_  
> Check: `edu:clap:syllables:sogolon`
> _For grown-ups: Clapped the three syllables of “Sogolon”._

**Doua**: Every word, my king.  

#### `sprout:a05-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a05-02-complete)

**King Naré Maghan**: Sogolon of Do, will you be my wife?  
_The laughing stops all at once._  
**Sogolon**: I will. And please stop staring. I have only one head.  
_Doua laughs kindly, and the king laughs. Then everyone does._  
**Balla Fasséké**: She made them laugh with her, not at her!  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, notice): laughing with someone is different from laughing at them  
> **Balla Fasséké**: Laughing with someone is fun for everyone.  
> **Balla Fasséké**: Laughing at someone hurts. Sogolon showed the difference.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard the difference between laughing with and laughing at someone._


## A5-3. The Boy Who Couldn’t Walk

- `a05-03-s1`: Visit Sogolon’s house. Sit with her son. · _It is the small house at the back._
- `a05-03-s2`: Go with Sogolon to ask for leaves. · _Walk across the yard with her._

#### `sprout:a05-03-house` · [main](main.md#a05-03-house)

_The page turns. A wedding, a baby, and seven years go by._  
_Sogolon’s son is seven. He crawls everywhere, and he watches everything._  
**Balla Fasséké**: His name is Mari Djata. He doesn’t talk much.  
_Your Nubbin sits in front of him. He pats its head, slowly._  
**Sogolon**: He doesn’t do that for many people. Your friend is honoured.  
**Balla Fasséké**: The old king died. His body stopped working, and it won’t start again.  
**Balla Fasséké**: I’m very sad. His older son, Dankaran, is king now.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): what helps when someone is sad  
> _Balla is sad. What helps a sad friend?_  
> - ✅ Sit with them → Yes. Sitting close shows you care.
> - Tell them to stop → Sad feelings can’t be told to stop. Sitting close helps.
> - Run away → A friend stays close. Sit with them.
> _For grown-ups: Chose to sit with a sad friend. Heard plainly that the old king died._


**Choices**

- **Sit down in the dust with the boy.**
    - You sit. Nobody speaks.
    - He is not waiting. He is just looking, very hard.
- **Ask Sogolon if he’ll walk one day.**
    - **Sogolon**: He will walk when he walks.
    - **Sogolon**: I don’t pull up seedlings to make them grow.

#### `sprout:a05-03-house-done` · [main](main.md#a05-03-house-done)

**Balla Fasséké**: Mark this, helper. He isn’t waiting to become someone.  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, notice): plants need water, sun and time; pulling does not help  
> **Sogolon**: A seedling needs water, sun and time.  
> **Sogolon**: If I pull it up, it dies. So I wait.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that plants need water, sunshine and time to grow._

**Balla Fasséké**: He is someone already. He is just not finished.  

#### `sprout:a05-03-leaves` · [main](main.md#a05-03-leaves)

**Sogolon**: I need baobab leaves for supper. Sassouma has plenty.  
_Queen Sassouma sits under a baobab full of leaves._  
**Sogolon**: Sister, a handful of leaves, please.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): first sounds: b for baobab  
> _Baobab starts with a “b” sound. B, b, baobab!_  
> _Which word starts like baobab?_  
> - ✅ Ball → Yes! B, b, ball, and b, b, baobab.
> - Sun → Sun starts with “s”. Ball starts with “b”, like baobab.
> - Moon → Moon starts with “m”. Ball starts with “b”, like baobab.
> _For grown-ups: Matched the first sound “b” in baobab and ball._

**Sassouma Bérété**: Take them. My son picked them. He climbed up himself.  
**Sassouma Bérété**: Your son only crawls.  
_The women laugh. Sogolon takes the leaves, and she is hurt._  

**Choices**

- **Say nothing, and walk out beside Sogolon.**
    - You walk out beside her. She keeps her back straight.
- **Look Sassouma in the eye.**
    - You look at Sassouma. She looks back, cool and calm.
    - **Balla Fasséké**: She’ll remember your face.

#### `sprout:a05-03-leaves-done` · [main](main.md#a05-03-leaves-done)

_At her door, Sogolon sits down. She cries, with her face in the leaves._  
_Her son crawls over. Your Nubbin leans on her other side._  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, notice): crying is allowed, and a hug or company helps  
> _Sogolon is sad, and it’s all right to cry._  
> _Her son and your Nubbin stay close. Being close helps._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that crying is allowed and that staying close helps._


#### `sprout:a05-03-complete` · [main](main.md#a05-03-complete)

**Sundiata**: Mother. Why are you crying?  
**Sogolon**: Because of leaves. Because of sharp words. Because of you, my son.  
**Sundiata**: Dry your eyes, Mother. Today I stand up.  
**Sundiata**: Ask the smiths for a rod of iron. The heaviest one.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 7  
> _(Hold up seven fingers.)_  
> _Sundiata crawled for seven years. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven._  
> Check: `edu:count:years:7`
> _For grown-ups: Counted to seven for the seven years Sundiata crawled._

_Balla says the words over to himself, to keep them._  

## A5-4. The Iron Rod

- `a05-04-s1`: Run to the smiths with a message. · _The forges are by the river._
- `a05-04-s2`: Watch the boy stand. · _Stand with Balla at the edge of the crowd._
- `a05-04-s3`: Gather leaves for Sogolon. · _The leaves are round the baobab._

#### `sprout:a05-04-forge` · [main](main.md#a05-04-forge)

_The smiths’ street smells of smoke. Hammers ring: clang, clang!_  
_The master smith is big and quiet._  
**The Master Smith**: The heaviest rod we have ever made. For the crawling boy.  
**The Master Smith**: My father made it long ago. It waited for this day.  
_It is as long as two of you. It is dark as night._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-4, notice): fire makes iron hot and soft; cooling makes it hard again  
> **The Master Smith**: I put iron in the fire until it glows.  
> **The Master Smith**: Hot iron is soft, so I can shape it.  
> **The Master Smith**: When it cools, it is hard again.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that heat softens iron and cooling hardens it again._

**The Master Smith**: Smiths keep secrets, helper. And we are patient.  

**Choices**

- **Take hold of the rod with the helpers.**
    - You pull, and it is so heavy! Your Nubbin pushes from behind.
- **Ask if a boy can really lift that.**
    - **The Master Smith**: No. That is the point.

#### `sprout:a05-04-forge-done` · [main](main.md#a05-04-forge-done)

_Six helpers carry the rod up the hill._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 6  
> _(Count the six helpers on your fingers.)_  
> _Six helpers!_  
> Check: `edu:count:helpers:6`
> _For grown-ups: Counted six helpers carrying the rod._

_Half the children in town follow._  
**Balla Fasséké**: Everyone wants to see. Stay close to me.  

#### `sprout:a05-04-stand` · [main](main.md#a05-04-stand)

_The rod lies by Sogolon’s door. The crowd goes quiet._  
_The boy takes it in both hands. He pulls and pulls._  
_He is on his knees. Then on one foot._  
_Then he is standing! The rod bends like a bow._  
**Balla Fasséké**: He’s standing!  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): pushing and pulling  
> _(Put your hands together and push. Now pull them apart.)_  
> _Push, pull! Sundiata pulled to stand up._  
> Check: `edu:act:push-pull:hands`
> _For grown-ups: Tried pushing and pulling with their hands._

_Balla sings a praise song. The whole hill joins in._  
**Sogolon**: My son is standing.  

**Choices**

- **Cheer.**
    - You cheer! Your Nubbin squeaks and runs round his feet.
- **Look at Sogolon.**
    - Sogolon has her hands over her mouth. She waited seven years.

#### `sprout:a05-04-stand-done` · [main](main.md#a05-04-stand-done)

**Balla Fasséké**: Some tellers say it was a stick. Some say a branch.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Language and Communication, notice): one story can be told in different ways  
> **Balla Fasséké**: Griots tell a story in different ways.  
> **Balla Fasséké**: The same story can have more than one telling.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that one story can be told in more than one way._

**Balla Fasséké**: I was there. It was iron. But I’ll tell the stick one too.  

#### `sprout:a05-04-baobab` · [main](main.md#a05-04-baobab)

**Sundiata**: Mother, you wanted baobab leaves.  
_He walks to a young baobab. He puts his arms round it._  
_In some tellings, he pulls it up and carries it home._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): baobabs are very wide and live a very long time  
> _(Stretch your arms out as wide as you can.)_  
> _A baobab trunk is wider than that!_  
> _It grows slowly. Some live for hundreds of years._  
> Check: `edu:act:arms:wide`
> _For grown-ups: Stretched arms wide to imagine a baobab trunk; heard that baobabs live a very long time._

_He sets it down at his mother’s door._  
**Sogolon**: That’s enough for one day, my son. Sit down.  

#### `sprout:a05-04-baobab-done` · [main](main.md#a05-04-baobab-done)

_You gather the leaves. Basket one has 3 leaves._  
_Basket two has 5 leaves._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.C.6, ask): which group has more  
> _Basket one has 3 leaves, and basket two has 5. Which has more?_  
> - ✅ Basket two → Yes! Five is more than three.
> - Basket one → Let’s count again: basket one has 3, basket two has 5. Five is more.
> _For grown-ups: Compared 3 leaves with 5 leaves and picked the larger group._

_Across the yard, Sassouma’s door is shut._  

#### `sprout:a05-04-complete` · [main](main.md#a05-04-complete)

**Balla Fasséké**: Now they call him Sogolon’s Djata. Say it fast: Sundiata!  
**Balla Fasséké**: Djata means lion. His mother and a lion, in one name.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): first sounds  
> _Sundiata and Sogolon start with the same sound. Which starts like them?_  
> - ✅ Sun → Yes! Sss-un, Sss-ogolon and Sss-undiata all start with “s”.
> - Moon → Moon starts with “m”. Sundiata starts with “s”, like sun.
> - Tree → Tree starts with “t”. Sundiata starts with “s”, like sun.
> _For grown-ups: Matched the first sound “s” in Sundiata and Sogolon._

_Sogolon gives you a baobab seed. It is hard and round._  
**Sogolon**: For your yard. They grow slowly. Don’t worry.  
_Your Nubbin tries to eat it, so you take it back. Sogolon laughs._  

## A5-5. The Griot

- `a05-05-s1`: Watch Balla become Sundiata’s griot. · _Go to the hunters’ clearing._
- `a05-05-s2`: Help your Nubbin learn a tune. · _Sit your Nubbin at the balafon._

#### `sprout:a05-05-given` · [main](main.md#a05-05-given)

_Sundiata is ten now. He hunts with the older boys._  
**Doua**: Every king has a griot. My son will be yours.  
**Sundiata**: I don’t need someone to sing at me.  
**Doua**: You need someone to remember you.  
**Balla Fasséké**: And I’ll tell you when you’re being a fool!  
**Sundiata**: Then stay close. I’ll need telling.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.OA.A.2, ask): take away to find the difference  
> _Sundiata stood at 7. Now he is 10._  
> _How many years later is that?_  
> - ✅ 3 years → Yes! Hold up 7 fingers, then 3 more to make 10.
> - 7 years → Start at 7 and count up: 8, 9, 10. Three years.
> - 1 year → Count up from 7: 8, 9, 10. That’s three years.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out that from age 7 to 10 is 3 years._


#### `sprout:a05-05-given-done` · [main](main.md#a05-05-given-done)

_Sundiata and Balla shake hands, very seriously._  
_Then Sundiata gives him a playful push into the grass._  
_They both laugh._  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, notice): play is when everyone is laughing  
> _Friends can play and tease. It’s fine when both are laughing._  
> _If someone isn’t laughing, stop and say sorry._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that friendly play is when both are laughing; stop if someone isn’t._


#### `sprout:a05-05-lesson` · [main](main.md#a05-05-lesson)

**Balla Fasséké**: If you’re my helper, your round friend is too.  
_He gives your Nubbin two beaters. They are very long._  
**Balla Fasséké**: You don’t hit the key. You ask it.  
_Your Nubbin asks. The key answers, round and low!_  
**Balla Fasséké**: There’s the hunters’ tune: quick and stamping.  
**Balla Fasséké**: Or Sogolon’s humming tune: slow and rocking.  

> **Lesson** (music, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, do): fast and slow  
> _(Clap fast, like the hunters. Clap slow, like the humming tune.)_  
> _Fast, fast, fast. Slow… slow… slow._  
> Check: `edu:clap:tempo:fast-slow`
> _For grown-ups: Clapped a fast beat and a slow beat._

**Balla Fasséké**: Which one?  

**Choices**

- **The hunters’ tune.**
    - Balla plays it once: quick and stamping, like lots of feet.
    - Your Nubbin plays it back. Its feet stamp too.
    - **Balla Fasséké**: A tune for a crowd. You’ll never be lonely.
- **Sogolon’s lullaby.**
    - Balla plays it once. Slow, rocking, round and round.
    - Your Nubbin plays it back, very carefully.
    - **Balla Fasséké**: A tune for one person. They’ll think it’s just for them.

#### `sprout:a05-05-lesson-done` · [main](main.md#a05-05-lesson-done)

_Your Nubbin plays its tune all evening._  
_By night it plays the whole tune without stopping._  

> **Lesson** (maths, ELOF: Mathematics Development, ask): a pattern: what comes next  
> _The tune goes: low, high, low, high, low… What comes next?_  
> - ✅ High → Yes! Low, high, low, high, low, high is a pattern!
> - Low → Listen again: low, high, low, high, low… next is high.
> _For grown-ups: Continued a two-note pattern: low, high, low, high._

_Sogolon’s door is open. She sits just inside, listening._  

#### `sprout:a05-05-complete` · [main](main.md#a05-05-complete)

**Balla Fasséké**: Keep that tune. A tune you know by heart is a door.  
**Balla Fasséké**: I’ll tell you which door when we get there.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.K.3, do): first, next, last  
> _(Put three things in order: first, next, last.)_  
> _To learn a tune: first listen, next copy, last play it by heart._  
> Check: `edu:order:tune:first-next-last`
> _For grown-ups: Put the steps of learning a tune in order: listen, copy, play._

_Your Nubbin plays the first three notes. They’re still there._  

## A5-6. Exile

**Sundiata**: These ones are better. Those are still green.

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 9  
> _(Count the nine old women with your finger.)_  
> _One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine!_  
> Check: `edu:count:women:9`
> _For grown-ups: Counted nine old women, one finger each._


#### `sprout:a05-06-garden-done` · [main](main.md#a05-06-garden-done)

**The Eldest of the Nine**: We came to make you cross. You made us ashamed instead.
