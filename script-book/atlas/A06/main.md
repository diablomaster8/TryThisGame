## A6-1. Two Sinbads

*Sinbad the Sailor, from One Thousand and One Nights (traditional; retold, no translation used): the frame, the porter on the bench* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Seven Voyages page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time once Nova’s atlas is in the Grove; the first quest on the page. **Cast:** Sinbad the Porter (Sinbad the Porter, the listener of the frame tale; your friend), The Steward (the old sailor’s household steward (frame tale); original), Sinbad the Sailor (Sinbad the Sailor, old and rich, telling his seven voyages)

### Step 1: Help your friend the porter carry his load of paper across Baghdad.

Hint: Pick up the basket by the porter and follow him through the lanes. · check: `carry:a06-paper:house-of-wisdom`

#### <a id="a06-01-street"></a>Scene `a06-01-street`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page the colour of hot stone: a round city on a river, drawn from above._  
_You touch the river. The heat comes up off the paper, and you are standing in a lane with a basket on your back._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Steady. Steady! That basket is worth more than both of us, and it isn’t even heavy.  
_The man beside you carries three baskets to your one. He is a porter called Sinbad, and he has been your friend, it seems, for years._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Paper, from the paper-makers’ market, for the scholars at the House of Wisdom. They write on it faster than I can fetch it.  
**Sinbad the Porter** _(think)_: Every book in Baghdad has been on my back at some point. I can’t read a single one of them.  
_Your Nubbin rides on top of your basket, among the rolls, like a cat on a warm wall._  
_The lane smells of bread, cumin and river mud. A man is selling cold sherbet, and everybody wants some._  

**Choices**

- **Take one of his baskets as well.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: You’re a good friend and a terrible porter. Lift from the knees.
    - Your Nubbin shifts to balance the load, and very nearly manages it.
- **Ask what the scholars do with all this paper.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: They put books into Arabic, from Greek and Persian and the languages of India. Stars, medicine, numbers.
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: And they argue. Mostly they argue. Paper’s cheaper than it was, so they argue at length.

#### <a id="a06-01-street-done"></a>Done `a06-01-street-done`

_At the House of Wisdom a young scholar with ink to the elbow counts the rolls twice, and pays exactly._  
**Sinbad the Porter** _(think)_: Exactly. He always pays exactly. Never one copper over.  

### Step 2: Rest on the bench by the great house on the way home.

Hint: Sit down beside the porter in the shade by the gate. · check: `sit:a06-bench:gate`

#### <a id="a06-01-bench"></a>Scene `a06-01-bench`

_On the way home the porter stops in the shade of a great house, and sets his empty baskets on a stone bench._  
_Cool air comes from the gate with a smell of roast lamb and rosewater. Somewhere inside, a lute is playing._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Listen to that. Somebody in there has never carried anything heavier than a spoon.  
**Sinbad the Porter** _(angry)_: I carry from first light to the last call to prayer, and I go home to lentils. He sits still, and gets lamb.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Same God made us both. I’m not complaining. I’m saying it out loud, which is different.  
_He says it quite loudly. A window above the gate is open._  
_Your Nubbin looks up at the window, then at you, the way it does in the Grove when someone is about to be caught._  

**Choices**

- **Tell him to keep his voice down.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: Why? It’s just as true in a whisper.
- **Say it isn’t fair.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: Thank you.
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: It isn’t, is it? I feel much better. No, I feel worse.

#### <a id="a06-01-bench-done"></a>Done `a06-01-bench-done`

_The gate opens. A steward in a spotless turban looks at the porter, then at you, then at your Nubbin._  
**The Steward**: My master asks for the man who was talking. And his friend. And… that.  

### Step 3: Go in and meet the master of the house.

Hint: Follow the steward through the courtyard to the long room. · check: `visit:a06-mansion:hall`

#### <a id="a06-01-hall"></a>Scene `a06-01-hall`

_Inside there is a courtyard with a fountain, then a long room of cushions, and more food than you have ever seen at once._  
_At the head of the room sits an old man with a white beard, and a face burnt brown by a great deal of weather._  
**Sinbad the Sailor** _(happy)_: Sit, sit. Eat first. A man shouldn’t have to argue with God on an empty stomach.  
**Sinbad the Porter** _(shock)_: You heard that.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: The whole street heard it. What’s your name?  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Sinbad. Sinbad the porter.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: So is mine. Sinbad the sailor. Well, Sinbad, anyway. The sailor part I had to earn.  
**Sinbad the Sailor** _(think)_: You think I sat here and the lamb walked in. Let me tell you what this house cost.  

#### <a id="a06-01-hall-done"></a>Done `a06-01-hall-done`

_The porter sits on the very edge of a cushion, as if it might send him a bill._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a06-01-complete"></a>Complete `a06-01-complete`

**Sinbad the Sailor**: Seven times I went to sea. Seven times I swore I never would again.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: Seven voyages won’t fit in one supper. I’ll tell them one at a time, and you’ll take food home for whoever is waiting.  
**Sinbad the Porter** _(soft)_: My daughter. She’ll want to know why I’m late.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: Then you’ll have a story to tell her. That’s the price of supper: you listen properly.  
_The old man looks at you and your Nubbin, as if he always knew the porter would bring you._  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: You too. Sit closer. When I tell a thing, it has a way of happening again.

**Cast:** Sinbad the Porter (Sinbad the Porter, the listener of the frame tale; your friend), Sinbad the Sailor (Sinbad the Sailor, old and rich, telling his seven voyages), Young Sinbad (Sinbad the Sailor as a young merchant, inside his own tales), The Captain (a ship’s captain (voyages 1 and 3; a different man each time))

### Step 1: Listen to the first voyage, and step into it.

Hint: Sit on your cushion in the long room until the carpet turns to sea. · check: `sit:a06-cushion:first`

#### <a id="a06-02-telling"></a>Scene `a06-02-telling`

**Sinbad the Sailor**: I was young, and my father left me money, and I spent it the way young men do. Quickly, and on friends.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: When it was nearly gone, I sold what was left, bought goods, and went down the river to Basra to take ship.  
_As he talks, the pattern on the carpet under your hand turns blue, and begins to move._  
_The smell of lamb becomes a smell of tar and salt. The porter’s voice goes far away._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Go on, then. I’ll mind the dates.  
_You are on the deck of a dhow, a ship of sewn planks, under a great triangular sail with one square patch on it._  
_A young man with the old man’s eyes leans on the rail, grinning at the sea as if it owes him money._  
**Young Sinbad** _(happy)_: You’re new. Good. Everybody else here has heard my jokes.  

**Choices**

- **Ask to hear a joke.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: What’s the difference between a merchant and a sailor?
    - **Young Sinbad**: About three days out of Basra.
    - Behind him, the helmsman groans like a man who has heard it every day for a month.
- **Ask if he’s been to sea before.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Never. Isn’t it wonderful? Nothing can possibly go wrong.

#### <a id="a06-02-telling-done"></a>Done `a06-02-telling-done`

_Weeks of blue go by in a breath. Then the lookout shouts: land, a small green island, flat as a plate, on a sea with no wind._  

### Step 2: Gather firewood with the merchants on the little island.

Hint: Pick up sticks under the young trees and bring them to the fire. · check: `collect:a06-firewood:island`

#### <a id="a06-02-island"></a>Scene `a06-02-island`

**The Captain**: Ashore, all of you. Stretch your legs, wash your shirts. Back aboard by evening.  
_The island is strange underfoot: soft, a little springy, with sand in the hollows and young trees growing out of it._  
**Young Sinbad** _(happy)_: Firewood! We’ll cook properly tonight, on dry land, like civilised people.  
_The merchants build a fire and set a pot on it. Somebody starts singing a song you don’t know._  
_Your Nubbin sits down on the ground, then stands up again quickly and looks at its feet._  
_The ground is warm. Not sun-warm: warm from underneath, the way a sleeping animal is warm._  

**Choices**

- **Tell Sinbad the ground is warm.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: It’s the sun, friend. Have a date.
    - He hands you one. Under your feet, the island breathes out.
- **Kick sand over the fire.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Hey! That’s supper!
    - Too late, and not enough sand. The island has already noticed.

#### <a id="a06-02-island-done"></a>Done `a06-02-island-done`

_The island shudders. All the young trees lean the same way at once._  
**The Captain** _(shock)_: Back to the ship! It isn’t an island! It’s a fish! Run!  

### Step 3: Hold on to the washtub until the sea lets you go.

Hint: Keep hold of the tub and kick towards the light. · check: `swim:a06-tub:sea`

#### <a id="a06-02-tub"></a>Scene `a06-02-tub`

_The island rolls over, slowly, like somebody turning in bed. An eye the size of a cartwheel opens beside you._  
_It is a whale, very old. It has been asleep so long that trees grew on its back._  
_Then it is gone, down into the green, and everything that was on it is in the sea._  
_The ship is already sailing. The captain is sure nobody could live through that, and he doesn’t look back._  
**Young Sinbad** _(shock)_: Here! The washtub! Grab hold!  
_Sinbad has a wooden tub from the laundry, and he has you by the collar, and your Nubbin has you by the ear._  
_Far below, something huge sings one long low note. Then the sea is only the sea._  

**Choices**

- **Kick, hard.**
    - The tub spins round and round, and goes nowhere in particular, very fast.
- **Float, and let the current take you.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Good thinking. The sea knows where it’s going, even if I don’t.

#### <a id="a06-02-tub-done"></a>Done `a06-02-tub-done`

_A day, a night, another day. Then the tub bumps on sand, and Sinbad laughs, and is sick, and laughs again._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a06-02-complete"></a>Complete `a06-02-complete`

_The carpet is a carpet again. The porter is staring at you with a date halfway to his mouth._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: A fish. With trees on.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: A whale. I didn’t know the word then. Neither did the captain.  
**Sinbad the Porter** _(think)_: And you lit a fire on its back.  
**Sinbad the Sailor** _(happy)_: In fairness, it was a very good fire.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: I’d have woken up too.  
_Your Nubbin is still damp. Nobody can explain this, so nobody tries._

_You are back in the tale, on a beach, and Sinbad is sitting up and counting his fingers._  
**Young Sinbad**: Ten. Good. And you’re still here. Where’s here?  
**Young Sinbad** _(sad)_: Twenty bales lost. My uncle’s indigo on top, dyed with his own two hands.  
**Young Sinbad**: He’ll dye me next. Blue, to the elbows.

**Young Sinbad** _(shock)_: That’s our ship. That’s my ship.  
**The Captain**: Unloading! Most of it’s for sale. Those bales belonged to a merchant who drowned.  
**The Captain**: Sinbad of Baghdad, God rest him. I’ll sell them here and take the money home to his family.

## A6-4. The Roc’s Egg

*Sinbad the Sailor, from One Thousand and One Nights (traditional; retold, no translation used): the second voyage, the roc* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Seven Voyages page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A6-3, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A6-3

### Step 1: Find out where the ship has gone.

Hint: Climb the tallest tree on the island and look all round. · check: `explore:a06-island:spring`

#### <a id="a06-04-left"></a>Scene `a06-04-left`

**Sinbad the Sailor**: The second voyage. Money makes you bored, and boredom makes you stupid. So I went back to Basra.  
_The carpet turns blue again, then green: an island of fruit trees, a cold spring, merchants resting in the shade._  
**Young Sinbad**: Eat, drink, sleep for an hour. The captain said an hour.  
_Sinbad lies down under a tree with his turban over his eyes. Your Nubbin lies down on his chest._  
_You must sleep too. When you wake, the shade has moved a long way, and the sea is empty._  
**Young Sinbad** _(shock)_: An hour! He said an hour!  
**Young Sinbad** _(sad)_: Twice now. What is it about me that makes captains leave?  

**Choices**

- **Say it isn’t him, it’s captains.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: That’s kind. It’s also probably true.
- **Say you should climb a tree and look.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Yes. Doing something. Good. Better than sulking.

#### <a id="a06-04-left-done"></a>Done `a06-04-left-done`

_From the top of the tallest tree you can see the whole island. On the far side stands a great white dome, shining._  

### Step 2: Walk round the white dome and look for a way in.

Hint: Circle the dome. Put your ear to it. · check: `inspect:a06-dome:shell`

#### <a id="a06-04-dome"></a>Scene `a06-04-dome`

_The dome is smooth and white and as tall as a house, and it has no door._  
**Young Sinbad**: Fifty paces round, I counted. No door, no window, no seam. Who builds a house with no door?  
_Your Nubbin puts its ear to the white wall. Then it puts its whole face to it, and goes very still._  
_Something inside goes tap, very small. Then tap, tap._  
_The sun goes out. Not behind a cloud: out, the way a lamp does when somebody stands in front of it._  
_A bird comes down out of the sky, a bird the size of a cloud, and settles over the dome like a hen._  
**Young Sinbad** _(shock)_: It’s an egg. The dome is an egg. And that is its mother.  

#### <a id="a06-04-dome-done"></a>Done `a06-04-dome-done`

_The roc tucks her head under her wing. She snores, and the snore lifts your hair._  

### Step 3: Tie yourselves to the roc’s foot before she wakes.

Hint: Help Sinbad with his turban cloth at the roc’s foot. · check: `climb:a06-roc:foot`

#### <a id="a06-04-leg"></a>Scene `a06-04-leg`

**Young Sinbad**: She’ll fly in the morning. Birds do. Wherever she goes, it’s off this island.  
_He unwinds his turban, which turns out to be very long. Your Nubbin helps by getting wound up in it twice._  
_You tie yourselves to one great scaly toe, gently, under the feathers, where she won’t feel it._  
**Young Sinbad**: Don’t pull. Don’t tickle. Don’t breathe on her.  
_At dawn the roc stands, stretches wings that cover half the sky, and steps off the island into the air._  
_The sea drops away under your feet. Her heartbeat comes down her leg like a drum._  

**Choices**

- **Look down.**
    - The island is a coin. Then it is a crumb, and then it is gone.
- **Shut your eyes.**
    - Your Nubbin keeps its eyes open for both of you, and squeaks at every cloud.

#### <a id="a06-04-leg-done"></a>Done `a06-04-leg-done`

_At noon the roc comes down on a high bare ridge. You untie the turban faster than you have ever done anything._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a06-04-complete"></a>Complete `a06-04-complete`

_The roc doesn’t notice you at all. She shakes out her feathers and goes back up into the sun._  
_As she goes, one feather comes loose and turns over and over in the air. It is as long as you are tall._  
_Your Nubbin catches it, and falls over._  
**Young Sinbad** _(happy)_: Keep that. Nobody in Baghdad will believe a word of this, so you’ll want proof.  
_When the carpet is a carpet again, the feather is still in your hands. The porter’s mouth is open._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: That is not a chicken.  
**Sinbad the Sailor** _(happy)_: No. That is your friend’s, now. A feather a roc let go of is a gift, not a theft.  

**Rewards**

### Step 1: Climb down into the valley without waking the snakes.

Hint: Step only on the bare stone between the sleeping snakes. · check: `explore:a06-valley:floor`

#### <a id="a06-05-valley"></a>Scene `a06-05-valley`

_The ridge looks down into a deep, narrow valley with walls too steep to climb. Its floor glitters, as if someone spilt the stars._  
**Young Sinbad** _(shock)_: Diamonds. That’s a valley of diamonds. Look at the size of them!  
_Between the stones, long dark shapes lie coiled in the sun: snakes, each as thick as a well rope, all asleep._  
**Young Sinbad**: Ah. So that’s why nobody has picked them up.  
_There is no way out but down, so you go down, slowly, picking the gaps between the sleeping snakes._  
_Your Nubbin walks on tiptoe. It has never done this before, and it does it very well._  

**Choices**

- **Hold your breath past the biggest snake.**
    - The snake opens one eye, considers you, and decides it isn’t hungry yet.
- **Go the long way round it.**
    - It takes an hour. Sinbad complains, in a whisper, for the entire hour.

#### <a id="a06-05-valley-done"></a>Done `a06-05-valley-done`

_In the middle of the valley something falls out of the sky and lands with a wet slap. It is a great joint of meat._

## A6-16. The Porter’s Voyage

*Sinbad the Sailor, from One Thousand and One Nights (traditional; retold, no translation used): the frame’s close, retold; and the Nights’ own frame, mentioned* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Seven Voyages page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A6-15, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A6-15

### Step 1: Listen to the porter tell his own story.

Hint: Sit in the long room with the household. · check: `talk:a06-porter:story`

#### <a id="a06-16-telling"></a>Scene `a06-16-telling`

_The last evening. The long room is full: the steward, the cook, the porter’s daughter, and the old man, for once a listener._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: I’ve never told a story. I’ve carried a few. Bear with me.  
_He takes something from his sash and sets it on the low table. The lamplight finds it: a small clear stone, no bigger than a lentil._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: This is the first line of my story. A friend brought it out of a valley that nobody comes back from.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: I’ve carried other people’s jewels all my life. This is the only one anybody ever brought for me. So I’ll never sell it.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: My story is short. I’m a porter in Baghdad. I carry paper to the House of Wisdom, and I can’t read, so I listen.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: I listened to a rich man for seven nights, and I found out he was paid for, the same as me. Only in different coins.

### Step 3: Tell your own story to the Grove.

Hint: Sit on the storyteller’s cushion. · check: `sit:a06-cushion:tell`

#### <a id="a06-16-tell"></a>Scene `a06-16-tell`

_You sit on the cushion. All the small faces turn up to you._  
_Behind you there are footsteps on the grass. Nova sits down at the back of the ring without a word._  
_You have never told a story either. You think of the porter’s hands on the table._  
_Your Nubbin climbs off the cushion and sits on your foot instead, to help._  
_You take a breath, the way the old man did, as if the Grove were a ship and you could feel it move._  
_Then you begin._  

**Choices**

- **Begin with the island that was a whale.**
    - **You**: Once there was an island that was fast asleep…
    - Your Nubbin rolls on to its back, being the whale.
- **Begin with the porter on the bench.**
    - **You**: Once there was a man who carried every book in Baghdad, and couldn’t read a single one…
- **Begin with the elephants.**
    - **You**: Once there was a herd of elephants who only wanted to be left alone…
    - Every Nubbin in the ring goes very still.

### Complete

#### <a id="a06-16-complete"></a>Complete `a06-16-complete`

_It takes you all evening. Your Nubbin acts out every part, badly and with great feeling, especially the roc._  
**Nova** _(happy)_: That page always tells it a bit differently. I’ve never heard the porter get the last word before.  
**Nova**: Who told it first? Nobody knows. In the book, it’s a woman in Persia, telling a king one story a night.  
**Nova** _(soft)_: She kept a cruel king listening for a thousand and one nights, until he learned to be kind.  
**Nova**: Persian and Arabic, then French, then English, and now you, on a cushion. Stories travel like sailors. They come home changed.  
_Your Nubbins don’t leave the ring for a long time, even after the stars are out._
