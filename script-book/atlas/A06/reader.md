# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A6: Seven Voyages

## A6-1. Two Sinbads

- `a06-01-s1`: Help your friend the porter carry his load of paper across Baghdad. · _Pick up the basket by the porter and follow him through the lanes._
- `a06-01-s2`: Rest on the bench by the great house on the way home. · _Sit down beside the porter in the shade by the gate._
- `a06-01-s3`: Go in and meet the master of the house. · _Follow the steward through the courtyard to the long room._

#### `reader:a06-01-street` · [main](main.md#a06-01-street)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page the colour of hot stone: a round city on a river, drawn from above._  
_You touch the river. The heat comes up off the paper, and you are standing in a lane with a basket on your back._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Steady. Steady! That basket is worth more than both of us, and it isn’t even heavy.  
_The man beside you carries three baskets to your one. He is a porter called Sinbad, and he has been your friend, it seems, for years._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Paper, from the paper-makers’ market, for the scholars at the House of Wisdom. They write on it faster than I can fetch it.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): Baghdad was planned as a round city; paper travelled from China  
> **Sinbad the Porter**: This whole city was built in a perfect circle: four gates, the palace in the middle. People called it the City of Peace.  
> **Sinbad the Porter**: And paper? Somebody in China worked out how to make it. The skill walked west along the trade roads until it reached us.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Baghdad was planned as a round city and that paper-making spread from China along trade routes._

**Sinbad the Porter**: Every book in Baghdad has been on my back at some point. I can’t read a single one of them.  
_Your Nubbin rides on top of your basket, among the rolls, like a cat on a warm wall._  
_The lane smells of bread, cumin and river mud. A man is selling cold sherbet, and everybody wants some._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): multiplication as equal groups  
> **Sinbad the Porter**: Three baskets on my back, one on yours. Every basket holds six rolls of paper. How many rolls are we carrying?  
> - ✅ 24 → Yes! Four baskets, six rolls in each: 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24, or 4 × 6.
> - 18 → That’s my three baskets alone: 3 × 6 = 18. Add yours, another 6, and we get 24.
> - 10 → That’s 4 + 6. But each of the four baskets holds six, so it’s 4 groups of 6: 24.
> _For grown-ups: Solved an equal-groups problem (4 baskets of 6 rolls = 24)._


**Choices**

- **Take one of his baskets as well.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: You’re a good friend and a terrible porter. Lift from the knees.
    - Your Nubbin shifts to balance the load, and very nearly manages it.
- **Ask what the scholars do with all this paper.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: They put books into Arabic, from Greek and Persian and the languages of India. Stars, medicine, numbers.
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: And they argue. Mostly they argue. Paper’s cheaper than it was, so they argue at length.

#### `reader:a06-01-street-done` · [main](main.md#a06-01-street-done)

_At the House of Wisdom a young scholar with ink to the elbow counts the rolls twice, and pays exactly._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Exactly. He always pays exactly. Never one copper over.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, notice): what a scholar is; what a translation does  
> **Sinbad the Porter**: A scholar is somebody who studies one subject for years and years. These ones also translate.  
> **Sinbad the Porter**: To translate is to carry a book from one language into another, so people who couldn’t read it before can. It’s porter’s work, really. Only with words.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the words scholar and translate, and that translating carries a book into another language._

_Behind him, a long room of desks is full of people copying, translating and arguing in four languages at once._  

#### `reader:a06-01-bench` · [main](main.md#a06-01-bench)

_On the way home the porter stops in the shade of a great house, and sets his empty baskets on a stone bench._  
_Cool air comes from the gate with a smell of roast lamb and rosewater. Somewhere inside, a lute is playing._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Listen to that. Somebody in there has never carried anything heavier than a spoon.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: I carry from first light to the last call to prayer, and I go home to lentils. He sits still, and gets lamb.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Same God made us both. I’m not complaining. I’m saying it out loud, which is different.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): think: is it fair?  
> _The porter works from dawn and eats lentils. The man inside sits still and eats lamb. Is that fair? There’s no right answer. What do you think?_  
> - No, work this hard should earn more. → A fair answer. Lots of people have felt that, in every century. Hard work and good pay don’t always travel together.
> - It depends how each of them got what they have. → That’s a careful answer. Maybe we should find out before deciding. I suspect somebody’s about to tell us.
> - Fair or not, grumbling aloud won’t change it. → That’s a fair point too. Though I notice the window opened. Speaking up can change things after all.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about whether the porter’s situation is fair, giving their own reasons._

_The porter says it quite loudly. A window above the gate is open._  
_Your Nubbin looks up at the window, then at you, the way it does in the Grove when someone is about to be caught._  

**Choices**

- **Tell him to keep his voice down.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: Why? It’s just as true in a whisper.
- **Say it isn’t fair.**
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: Thank you.
    - **Sinbad the Porter**: It isn’t, is it? I feel much better. No, I feel worse.

#### `reader:a06-01-bench-done` · [main](main.md#a06-01-bench-done)

_The gate opens. A steward in a spotless turban looks at the porter, then at you, then at your Nubbin._  
**The Steward**: My master asks for the man who was talking. And his friend. And… that.  
_The porter’s face does something between a wince and a grin._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): feelings can be mixed: the porter is worried and curious at once  
> _The porter’s face is doing two things at once. He’s worried he’ll be told off, and curious who’s asking._  
> _Feeling two things together is normal. A mixed-up feeling isn’t wrong: it just has more than one part._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that people can feel two different things at once._


#### `reader:a06-01-hall` · [main](main.md#a06-01-hall)

_Inside there is a courtyard with a fountain, then a long room of cushions, and more food than you have ever seen at once._  
_At the head of the room sits an old man with a white beard, and a face burnt brown by a great deal of weather._  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: Sit, sit. Eat first. A man shouldn’t have to argue with God on an empty stomach.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: You heard that.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: The whole street heard. What’s your name?  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Sinbad. Sinbad the porter.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: So is mine. Sinbad the sailor. Well, Sinbad, anyway. The sailor part I had to earn.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: The young man in my stories is me, long ago. You will meet him.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: You think I sat here and the lamb walked in. Let me tell you what this house cost.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): character motive  
> _The old man heard the porter’s complaint, and instead of being cross he invited him in. Why do you think he did that?_  
> - ✅ He wanted to show the porter that things aren’t always as they look. → Quite right. That’s exactly it. A fair guess from somebody who’s been watching closely.
> - He wanted to make the porter feel small. → Hm, possible, but look: he feeds the porter first and shares his name. People who want to make you feel small don’t usually offer lamb.
> - He was lonely and wanted company. → That could be part of it, too. A house this big can be very quiet. Motives are often mixed.
> _For grown-ups: Inferred a character’s motive from what he does._


#### `reader:a06-01-hall-done` · [main](main.md#a06-01-hall-done)

_The porter sits on the very edge of a cushion, as if it might send him a bill._  
_A kitchen boy puts a plate of dates beside him, and he takes exactly one._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): helping a guest feel welcome  
> _The porter perches on the edge of the cushion and takes exactly one date. He feels out of place. What could help him feel welcome?_  
> - ✅ The host saying “eat first” and sharing his own name. → Yes. The old man has already done both. Small, warm acts tell a nervous guest they belong.
> - Leaving him alone until he relaxes. → Some people like a little space, but on his own he might never relax. A friendly word helps him settle.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how a host helps a nervous guest feel welcome._


#### `reader:a06-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a06-01-complete)

**Sinbad the Sailor**: Seven times I went to sea. Seven times I swore I never would again.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: Seven voyages won’t fit in one supper. I’ll tell them one at a time, and you’ll take food home for whoever is waiting.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: My daughter. She’ll want to know why I’m late.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: Then you’ll have a story to tell her. That’s the price of supper: you listen properly.  
_The old man looks at you and your Nubbin, as if he always knew the porter would bring you._  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: You too. Sit closer. When I tell a thing, it has a way of happening again.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.A.1, do): time: seven nights make a week  
> **Sinbad the Sailor**: One voyage a night, seven voyages. How many days is that? A whole week. Count the evenings on your fingers.  
> Check: `edu:count:nights:7`
> _For grown-ups: Connected seven voyages to seven nights, one week._


### Glossary

- **porter**: A person whose job is to carry heavy loads for other people.
- **sherbet**: A cold, sweet, fruity drink.
- **scholar**: Someone who studies a subject very carefully for many years.
- **translate**: To change writing or speech from one language into another.
- **steward**: A person who looks after a big house or its household for the owner.
- **courtyard**: An open space with walls or buildings round it.

## A6-2. The Island That Moved

- `a06-02-s1`: Listen to the first voyage, and step into it. · _Sit on your cushion in the long room until the carpet turns to sea._
- `a06-02-s2`: Gather firewood with the merchants on the little island. · _Pick up sticks under the young trees and bring them to the fire._
- `a06-02-s3`: Hold on to the washtub until the sea lets you go. · _Keep hold of the tub and kick towards the light._

#### `reader:a06-02-telling` · [main](main.md#a06-02-telling)

**Sinbad the Sailor**: I was young, and my father left me money, and I spent it the way young men do. Quickly, and on friends.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: When it was nearly gone, I sold what was left, bought goods, and went down the river to Basra to take ship.  
_As the old sailor talks, the pattern on the carpet under your hand turns blue, and begins to move._  
_The smell of lamb becomes a smell of tar and salt. The porter’s voice goes far away._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: Go on, then. I’ll mind the dates.  
_You are on the deck of a dhow, a ship of sewn planks, under a great triangular sail full of wind._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): a triangular sail turns to catch the wind; dhows were sewn, not nailed  
> **Young Sinbad**: See the sail? A triangle, slung on a long slanting pole. We can swing it round to catch wind from the side, not only from behind.  
> **Young Sinbad**: And the planks are sewn together with coconut rope, not nailed. Sewn hulls flex in the waves instead of cracking.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a triangular sail can be turned to catch the wind and that dhows were built with sewn planks._

_A young man with the old man’s eyes leans on the rail, grinning at the sea as if it owes him money._  
**Young Sinbad**: You’re new. Good. Everybody else here has heard my jokes.  

**Choices**

- **Ask to hear a joke.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: What’s the difference between a merchant and a sailor?
    - **Young Sinbad**: About three days out of Basra.
    - Behind Sinbad, the helmsman groans like a man who has heard it every day for a month.
- **Ask if he’s been to sea before.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Never. Isn’t it wonderful? Nothing can possibly go wrong.

#### `reader:a06-02-telling-done` · [main](main.md#a06-02-telling-done)

_Weeks of blue go by in a breath. Then the lookout shouts: land, a small green island, flat as a plate, on a sea with no wind._  
_Something about it looks wrong, though you can’t say what._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): similes  
> _“Flat as a plate.” That’s a simile: it compares two things using “as” or “like”. Which of these is also a simile?_  
> - ✅ “Swift as a deer.” → Yes! It uses “as… as” to compare someone’s speed to a deer’s.
> - “The wind was a whisper.” → Close. That compares without “as” or “like”, which makes it a metaphor, the simile’s cousin.
> - “The island was small.” → That’s just a description. A simile compares one thing with another using “as” or “like”.
> _For grown-ups: Identified a simile._


#### `reader:a06-02-island` · [main](main.md#a06-02-island)

**The Captain**: Ashore, all of you. Stretch your legs, wash your shirts. Back aboard by evening.  
_The island is strange underfoot: soft, a little springy, with sand in the hollows and young trees growing out of it._  
**Young Sinbad**: Firewood! We’ll cook properly tonight, on dry land, like civilised people.  
_The merchants build a fire and set a pot on it. Somebody starts singing a song you don’t know._  
_Your Nubbin sits down on the ground, then stands up again quickly and looks at its feet._  
_The ground is warm. Not sun-warm: warm from underneath, the way a sleeping animal is warm._  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): clues and evidence  
> _Your Nubbin has noticed some clues. Which of them suggests the “island” is really an animal?_  
> - ✅ The ground is warm from underneath, and springy. → Yes! Warm and springy ground is how a living thing feels. Rock and sand are only warm where the sun has touched them.
> - There are trees growing on it. → Trees grow on real islands, so that clue doesn’t help. Look for something rock wouldn’t do.
> - There is sand in the hollows. → Sand is on many beaches, so that doesn’t tell us much. A warm, springy floor does.
> _For grown-ups: Picked out the clue (warmth and springiness) that shows the island is alive._


**Choices**

- **Tell Sinbad the ground is warm.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: It’s the sun, friend. Have a date.
    - Sinbad hands you one. Under your feet, the island breathes out.
- **Kick sand over the fire.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Hey! That’s supper!
    - Too late, and not enough sand. The island has already noticed.

#### `reader:a06-02-island-done` · [main](main.md#a06-02-island-done)

_The island shudders. All the young trees lean the same way at once._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): working out a word from its context  
> _“The island shudders. All the young trees lean the same way at once.” What does “shudders” mean here?_  
> - ✅ Shakes suddenly, all over. → Yes! The trees leaning all at once is the clue: the whole island shook.
> - Goes very quiet. → Look at the next sentence: every tree leans at once. Something that quiet wouldn’t do that. It shakes.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out the meaning of “shudders” from the sentence around it._

**The Captain**: Back to the ship! It isn’t an island! It’s a fish! Run!  
_The captain is wrong about one thing, though. It isn’t a fish at all._  

#### `reader:a06-02-tub` · [main](main.md#a06-02-tub)

_The island rolls over, slowly, like somebody turning in bed. An eye the size of a cartwheel opens beside you._  
_It is a whale, very old. It has been asleep so long that trees grew on its back._  
_Then it is gone, down into the green, and everything that was on it is in the sea._  
_The ship is already sailing. The captain is sure nobody could live through that, and he doesn’t look back._  
**Young Sinbad**: Here! The washtub! Grab hold!  
_Sinbad has a wooden tub from the laundry, and he has you by the collar, and your Nubbin has you by the ear._  
_Far below, something huge sings one long low note. Then the sea is only the sea._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): think: who is to blame?  
> _The merchants lit a fire on the whale’s back, not knowing. The captain sailed off, thinking everyone had drowned. Who, if anyone, is to blame for what happened?_  
> _There’s no single answer._  
> - Nobody: they couldn’t have known it was a whale. → That’s a fair answer. It’s hard to blame someone for what they couldn’t know. Maybe the lesson is to be curious before lighting fires.
> - The captain, for sailing off without checking. → A fair answer. Even if you think no one could survive, looking is the kind and careful thing to do.
> - The merchants, for not asking why the ground was warm. → That’s a fair answer, too. Your Nubbin noticed. Noticing, and speaking up, matters.
> _For grown-ups: Reasoned about blame and not knowing, with no single right answer._


**Choices**

- **Kick, hard.**
    - The tub spins round and round, and goes nowhere in particular, very fast.
- **Float, and let the current take you.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Good thinking. The sea knows where it’s going, even if I don’t.

#### `reader:a06-02-tub-done` · [main](main.md#a06-02-tub-done)

_A day, a night, another day. Then the tub bumps on sand, and Sinbad laughs, and is sick, and laughs again._  
_Your arms ache. You have held on to a tub for most of three days._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-1, notice): whales are mammals that breathe air  
> **Nova**: Nova’s notes: whales look like fish, but they’re mammals. They breathe air through a blowhole, and mothers feed their calves milk.  
> **Nova**: A sleeping whale floats near the surface. Sailors in old times sometimes mistook one for an island, and told this very story.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that whales are air-breathing mammals, not fish._


#### `reader:a06-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a06-02-complete)

_The carpet is a carpet again. The porter is staring at you with a date halfway to his mouth._  
**Sinbad the Porter**: A fish. With trees on.  
**Sinbad the Sailor**: A whale. I didn’t know the word then. Neither did the captain.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: And you lit a fire on its back.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.6, ask): point of view: the sailor and the porter see it differently  
> _The old man laughs about the whale; the porter is horrified. Same event, different feelings. Which is closest to how YOU felt about the fire on the whale?_  
> - I laughed, like the sailor. → That’s a good way to feel about a story that ended well. Laughing about a past scare is a common way to be brave.
> - I felt sorry for the whale, like the porter. → Good. That’s a kind way to feel, too. Both views are real. A story can hold both.
> - Both at once. → Yes, and that’s very true to life. You can laugh at something and still feel for the creature it hurt.
> _For grown-ups: Compared their own point of view with two characters’ different reactions._

**Sinbad the Sailor**: In fairness, it was a very good fire.  
**Sinbad the Porter**: I’d have woken up too.  
_Your Nubbin is still damp. Nobody can explain this, so nobody tries._  

### Glossary

- **dhow**: A traditional ship from the Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean, with a triangular sail.
- **lookout**: A person whose job is to watch for danger or land from a high place.
- **shudders**: Shakes suddenly and strongly, all over.
- **mammal**: An animal that breathes air, has some hair and feeds its babies milk.

## A6-3. The King’s Stables

- `a06-03-s1`: Go with the king’s groom to meet the king. · _Follow the groom up from the beach where the horses graze._
- `a06-03-s2`: Help Sinbad check the cargo of a ship just in. · _Look at the marks painted on the bales on the quay._
- `a06-03-s3`: Help Sinbad prove who he is. · _Look inside the bale with the green cord._

#### `reader:a06-03-shore` · [main](main.md#a06-03-shore)

_You are back in the tale, on a beach, and Sinbad is sitting up and counting his fingers._  
**Young Sinbad**: Ten. Good. And you’re still here. Where’s here?  
_Down the beach, beautiful horses graze by the surf, and a groom shouts at you for frightening them._  
_The groom is kind, once he stops shouting. He feeds you both, and takes you up to his king._  
**The Island King**: A man who came out of the sea in a washtub. Sit by me. Tell me about Baghdad.  
**Young Sinbad**: It’s round, majesty. The city is perfectly round, with four gates, and the Caliph’s palace right in the middle.  
**The Island King**: Round. Why round?  

> **Lesson** (maths, ask): a circle: every point is the same distance from the centre  
> _Sinbad could say something clever here. In a circle, how far is every point on the edge from the centre?_  
> - ✅ The same distance. → Yes! That’s what makes a circle a circle. So in a round city, every gate is equally far from the palace.
> - It depends which point. → In a circle it doesn’t. Every point on the edge is the same distance from the centre. That’s the definition.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that every point on a circle is the same distance from its centre._


**Choices**

- **Say: so the palace is the same distance from every gate.**
    - **The Island King**: Fair. I like a fair city.
- **Say: so the markets never come to an end.**
    - **The Island King**: Ha! A city you can’t get out of. I know a few like that.

#### `reader:a06-03-shore-done` · [main](main.md#a06-03-shore-done)

_The king makes Sinbad keeper of his harbour. He writes down every ship that comes and goes._  
_And Sinbad asks every captain the way to Baghdad._  
_Sinbad’s notebook fills with names, dates and cargoes, in a neat hand that gets neater every month._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): records and ledgers: harbour-keepers wrote down ships and goods  
> **Nova**: Nova’s notes: harbour-keepers in old ports kept long lists of ships and cargoes, so the king knew what came in and what it was worth.  
> **Nova**: Old lists, letters and accounts like these are how we know about trade across the Indian Ocean a thousand years ago. Boring records make good history.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that written records of trade help us know about the past._


#### `reader:a06-03-harbour` · [main](main.md#a06-03-harbour)

_Months pass in a page. Sinbad gets a good coat and a bad cough, and still asks every ship the way home._  
_One morning a dhow ties up at the quay, and its sail has a patch on it you have seen before._  
**Young Sinbad**: That’s our ship. That’s my ship.  
**The Captain**: Unloading! Most of it’s for sale. Those bales belonged to a merchant who drowned.  
**The Captain**: Sinbad of Baghdad, God rest him. I’ll sell them here and take the money home to his family.  
_The bales have Sinbad’s mark painted on the sacking. Your Nubbin sniffs one, and sneezes: it smells of home._  
**Young Sinbad**: I’m Sinbad. I’m him.  
**The Captain**: Everybody’s Sinbad, the minute there’s something to be had. I met three Sinbads at the last port.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): think: who do we believe?  
> _The captain has met three false Sinbads, so he doesn’t believe this one. How should we decide whether to believe someone? There’s no one right way._  
> - Ask for proof they could only have if it’s true. → A fair answer, and it’s what the story does next. Proof is hard to fake.
> - Believe people unless there’s a reason not to. → A fair answer. Trusting first is kind, though the captain has been tricked before, so he has a reason.
> - Be careful when money is involved. → A fair answer. People do lie more when there’s something to gain, which is exactly why the captain is wary.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how we decide whom to believe._


#### `reader:a06-03-harbour-done` · [main](main.md#a06-03-harbour-done)

_The captain folds his arms. The bales sit between you, smelling of Basra._  
_Sinbad looks like a man who has just been told that he is dead._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): feelings words  
> _Sinbad is frustrated and a little scared. Which word best describes feeling that nobody will believe the truth?_  
> - ✅ Helpless. → Yes. Helpless means you want to act but nothing you say or do seems to work. It’s a heavy feeling, and a friend nearby makes it lighter.
> - Bored. → Not bored: Sinbad is fully awake and worried. The word we want is helpless.
> - Proud. → Proud is the opposite of how he looks. Helpless fits better: stuck, with no way to be believed.
> _For grown-ups: Chose a word that matches a character’s feeling._


#### `reader:a06-03-proof` · [main](main.md#a06-03-proof)

**Young Sinbad**: Ask me anything. Anything about the voyage.  
**The Captain**: All right. What did we eat the night the island sank?  
**Young Sinbad**: Nothing. You all ran off and left the pot.  
**The Captain**: Hm. Then what’s in the bale with the green cord?  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.D.8, ask): two-step problem  
> **The Captain**: One more test. A crate holds twelve jars of oil. I have two crates and five loose jars. How many jars in all?  
> - ✅ 29 → Correct. Two crates of twelve is 24, and five loose jars makes 29. You’d make a decent merchant. Your Nubbin counts the jars on its fingers too, and nods.
> - 17 → That’s one crate and the loose jars. But I said two crates: 12 + 12 = 24, and then 5 more is 29.
> - 36 → That treats the loose jars as a third crate. Two crates is 24; the loose five are only five: 29.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a two-step word problem (2 × 12 + 5 = 29)._

_Sinbad goes blank. He was a young man who never read his own lists._  
_Your Nubbin is already tugging at the green cord. A corner of something blue shows through: dyed cloth, folded the Baghdad way._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-1, notice): indigo is a dye made from a plant  
> **Nova**: Nova’s notes: indigo is a deep blue dye made from the leaves of the indigo plant, soaked, beaten and dried. It was traded across the Indian Ocean for centuries.  
> **Nova**: Indigo clings to the outside of each thread instead of soaking right in, so it rubs off bit by bit. That’s why blue jeans fade.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that indigo is a plant-based blue dye and how it sticks to cloth._


**Choices**

- **Point at the blue cloth, and let Sinbad say it.**
    - **Young Sinbad**: Indigo! Twenty lengths of indigo, from my uncle’s shop by the bridge!
    - **The Captain**: …That’s right.
- **Whisper, “Your uncle’s shop.”**

> - From the east. → A wind from the east would push the sail towards the west. A push towards the east means the wind comes from the west.
> - From above. → Wind moves sideways across the ground. A push to the east means a wind from the west.
> _For grown-ups: Reasoned that a wind that pushes a sail eastwards blows from the west._
