> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): understand a simile (as orange as tiny lamps)  
> _“As orange as tiny lamps.” What is being compared to what?_  
> - ✅ The marigolds are compared to lamps → Yes. A simile uses “as” or “like” to compare. Marigolds glow with colour, the way lamps glow with light.
> - The lamps are compared to the town → Not quite. The marigolds are the thing described, and lamps are what they are compared to.
> - The flags are compared to birds → The flags are cut into birds, but this simile is about the flowers. They are compared to lamps.
> _For grown-ups: Identified the two things compared in a simile._

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, notice): plucked strings vibrate and make sound; shorter strings sound higher  
> _When a string is plucked, it vibrates, which means it shakes back and forth very fast. The shaking passes through the air as sound._  
> _A shorter or tighter string shakes faster and sounds higher. That is why a guitarist presses a string down to change the note._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that vibrating strings make sound, and that shorter strings sound higher._

**Miguel**: There’s a talent show tonight. In the square. If I win, my family has to see I’m a musician.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): think about whether to tell a family a hard truth  
> _Miguel hasn’t told his family about his music. What might happen if he tells them, and what if he doesn’t?_  
> - They might be angry, but at least it is honest → A fair answer. Honesty can be uncomfortable, but it lets people know the real you.
> - Hiding it will keep the peace for now → A fair answer too. But secrets can grow heavy, and he may have to face it later.
> - He could tell one person he trusts first → That is a thoughtful middle path. Practising with someone safe can make a hard talk easier.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about the costs of telling or hiding something important from family._
