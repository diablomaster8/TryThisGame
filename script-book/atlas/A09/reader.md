# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A9: The Comet

## A9-1. Strange Signs

- `a09-01-s1`: Step into the valley on the atlas page and meet whoever lives in the tall blue house. · _Touch the blue house on the page, then follow the path to the garden gate._
- `a09-01-s2`: Follow Sniff’s secret path down to the shore and see what is wrong with the sea. · _Take the narrow path behind the woodshed. It goes through the bushes, not round them._
- `a09-01-s3`: Ask the Muskrat in the hammock what the grey dust means. · _He is in the hammock between the two birch trees, and he will not be getting out of it._

#### `reader:a09-01-valley` · [main](main.md#a09-01-valley)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a green valley by the sea, with a tall blue house in it like a stove that grew._  
_You touch the chimney. The page smells of wet grass, and then so does the air._  
_It is morning, but a grey one. Everything looks dusted with ash and forgotten._  
_A round white someone is standing in the garden, holding a cup and frowning at the sky._  
**Moomintroll**: Oh! Hello. Are you a visitor? Mamma will be pleased. She likes visitors best on grey days.  
_A smaller, sharper someone pops out from behind the water butt and looks straight at your Nubbin._  
**Sniff**: What’s that? Is it valuable? It looks valuable. It looks like it would fit in a pocket.  
**Moomintroll**: That’s Sniff. He means hello.  
**Sniff**: I mean hello AND is it valuable.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): what a character’s words tell us about him  
> _Sniff’s first question is whether your Nubbin is valuable. What does that tell us about Sniff?_  
> - ✅ He likes to collect and keep things safe. → Yes. He asks about value and pockets, so he is a collector who worries about losing things.
> - He wants to be rude. → He is blunt, but he says hello too. His question is really about what he cares about: keeping things close.
> - He does not like Nubbins. → Not at all. He wants to put it in his pocket, which is his way of liking something.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a character trait (a collector who wants things safe) from his first lines._


**Choices**

- **Let your Nubbin try Sniff’s pocket.**
    - Your Nubbin climbs into Sniff’s coat pocket, turns round twice and settles, as if it has been waiting all its life for this pocket.
    - **Sniff**: It fits! It fits exactly. I’ll be very careful with it. Probably.
- **Keep your Nubbin in your arms for now.**
    - **Sniff**: Suit yourself. I only offered. It’s a very good pocket.
    - Your Nubbin looks at the pocket for a long moment. Then it wriggles down and climbs in anyway.

#### `reader:a09-01-valley-done` · [main](main.md#a09-01-valley-done)

_Moomintroll holds out a paw. It is soft, and slightly sticky with jam._  
**Moomintroll**: I’m Moomintroll. Come and see the sea. Something’s wrong with it.  
_Moomintroll and Sniff come from a book. It was written in a real country: Finland, in the far north of Europe._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, notice): Finland has two national languages, Finnish and Swedish  
> _Finland has two national languages: Finnish, which most people speak, and Swedish, which some Finns speak as their first language._  
> _Tove Jansson, who created the Moomins, grew up speaking Swedish in Finland. She wrote the books in it._  
> _Hello is “hei” in Finnish and “hej” in Swedish. They look nearly the same, but the languages are very different._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Finland has two national languages, Finnish and Swedish, and that the Moomin books were written in Swedish by a Finn._


#### `reader:a09-01-shore` · [main](main.md#a09-01-shore)

**Sniff**: This is my secret path. I found it. Nobody else knows about it.  
**Moomintroll**: I know about it.  
**Sniff**: Nobody else EXCEPT you. And now them. It’s getting very crowded for a secret.  
_The path ducks under brambles and comes out on a beach of round stones. The sea is the colour of an old spoon._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-ESS2-1, notice): waves wear stones round, slowly (weathering and erosion)  
> _Why are beach stones round? Waves roll them against each other, over and over, for hundreds or thousands of years._  
> _Each knock wears away a few grains, and the sharp corners go first. Wearing away like this is called erosion._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that moving water slowly rounds off stones, which is a kind of erosion._

_A fine grey dust floats on it. It moves when the water moves, and it does not wash away._  
**Moomintroll**: It was blue yesterday. It’s always been blue.  
_Along the shore, under a lip of rock, there is a cave. The mouth is low; inside it is dry, and the floor is soft sand._  
**Sniff**: That’s our cave. Mine, mostly. You can come in, but don’t touch anything.  
_There is nothing in it to touch. Sniff looks round it proudly anyway._  

#### `reader:a09-01-shore-done` · [main](main.md#a09-01-shore-done)

_You sit in the cave mouth and watch the grey sea breathe in and out._  
**Moomintroll**: Mamma would like this cave. It feels like being kept.  
_Sniff sits a little closer to you than he needs to, and says nothing._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): figurative language: “being kept”  
> _Moomintroll says the cave “feels like being kept”. What does he mean?_  
> - ✅ It feels safe, like somebody is looking after you. → Yes. “Kept” is how you talk about something precious that someone looks after. He feels cherished and safe.
> - He wants to take the cave home. → A fair guess, but he does not mean owning it. He means how the cave makes him feel: held safe.
> _For grown-ups: Explained a metaphor (“feels like being kept”) as feeling safe and looked after._


#### `reader:a09-01-hammock` · [main](main.md#a09-01-hammock)

_Back in the garden, a Muskrat lies in a hammock with his paws folded on his stomach, thinking very hard about nothing._  
**Moomintroll**: Muskrat, the sea’s gone grey. Do you know why?  
**The Muskrat**: Of course I know why. Something is coming. Something always is.  
**The Muskrat**: A comet, very probably. A star with a tail, falling where it likes.  
**Sniff**: Falling where? Not here. Somewhere else. Somewhere a long way off with nothing valuable in it.  
**The Muskrat**: Where it falls is no concern of a philosopher. Everything ends. It is rather restful, if you think about it.  
_Sniff does think about it. You can tell, because he goes the colour of the sea._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): the same thought can feel different to different people  
> _The Muskrat finds “everything ends” restful. Sniff goes pale. Why might one thought feel so different to two people?_  
> - They care about different things. → That’s a fair answer. The Muskrat loves quiet, and Sniff loves his pockets and his friends, so there is more for Sniff to lose.
> - One of them is more frightened than the other. → That’s a fair answer. Fear is part of it, and what we are afraid of depends on what matters to us.
> - The Muskrat has not really thought about it. → That’s a fair answer. Perhaps he says it so often that it has stopped feeling real. Sniff is feeling it for the first time.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why one idea can comfort one person and frighten another._

_Moominpappa comes out on the veranda with his hat on, which is how you know he has had an idea._  
**Moominpappa**: There’s an Observatory in the Lonely Mountains, full of professors with telescopes. They’ll know.  
**Moominpappa**: Somebody should go and ask them. Somebody young, and brave, with a good raft.  

**Choices**

- **“We’ll go.”**
    - **You**: We’ll go.
    - **Moomintroll**: Yes! We’ll go. All of us. Sniff too.
    - **Sniff**: Sniff too? Who said Sniff too?
- **Ask the Muskrat if he’s ever been wrong.**
    - **The Muskrat**: Once. I thought I would get a whole afternoon without being asked questions.
    - **Moomintroll**: That means he doesn’t know. We’ll go and ask the professors.

#### `reader:a09-01-hammock-done` · [main](main.md#a09-01-hammock-done)

_That night you all stand on the veranda, and Moominmamma points._  
_Low over the mountains there is a new star. It has a tail, faint and red, like a match just blown out._  
_Over the next days, the tail will grow, and it will always point one way: away from where the Sun is._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): a comet’s tail points away from the Sun, and grows as it nears it  
> _A comet is a lump of ice and dust, a bit like a dirty snowball, often only a few kilometres across._  
> _Far from the Sun it is frozen and dark. As it gets closer, the ice turns to gas and loosens dust. Both stream away and glow._  
> _Sunlight and a stream of particles from the Sun push that gas and dust away._  
> _So the tail always points away from the Sun, even when the comet is heading out again._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned what a comet is, and why its tail grows near the Sun and always points away from it._


#### `reader:a09-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a09-01-complete)

**Moominmamma**: Well. If you’re going, you’re going properly. Pullovers. Sandwiches. Something for colds.  
**Moominmamma**: Two sandwiches each, for the three of you, and I’ll hear no arguing.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, do): multiplication as equal groups: 3 travellers, 2 sandwiches each  
> _Three travellers, two sandwiches each. Make three groups of two in the basket. How many sandwiches is that? (3 × 2)_  
> Check: `edu:multiply:sandwiches:6`
> _For grown-ups: Used equal groups to work out 3 × 2 = 6 sandwiches._

**Moominmamma**: And you’ll come home when you’ve found out. Whatever they say.  
**Moomintroll**: We’ll come home, Mamma. Of course we will.  
_Sniff pats his pocket. Your Nubbin pats back from the inside._  
**Sniff**: I’m only going because the Nubbin wants to. Somebody has to look after it.  

### Glossary

- **valuable**: Worth a lot to somebody, in money or because they love it.
- **veranda**: A roofed porch along the outside of a house.
- **philosopher**: A person who thinks long and hard about big questions.

**Notes:** Lessons: a character from his words; Finnish and Swedish, with hei and hej; erosion and round stones; a metaphor; a think question on why one thought feels different to two people; what a comet is and why its tail points away from the Sun; 3 × 2.

## A9-2. The Raft

- `a09-02-s1`: Help Moominpappa build a raft on the riverbank. · _Carry planks from the woodshed to the bank. Moominpappa will tell you where they go._
- `a09-02-s2`: Load the raft and push off into the river. · _The basket goes in the middle. Sniff’s things go wherever Sniff can be talked into putting them._
- `a09-02-s3`: Steer the raft downriver until it is time to make camp. · _Keep to the middle of the river and watch the banks._

#### `reader:a09-02-build` · [main](main.md#a09-02-build)

_Moominpappa has laid out planks on the riverbank in the shape of a raft: two metres wide, three metres long._  
**Moominpappa**: Now. A raft must be sound, and it must have a mast, and it must have a name.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): multiple-meaning words: “sound”  
> _Moominpappa says a raft must be “sound”. “Sound” can also be what your ears hear. Which meaning does he intend?_  
> - ✅ Strong and in good condition. → Yes. A sound raft has no weak planks or loose knots. The same word means what we hear, but the sentence tells us which.
> - Noisy. → That would be a funny raft! Check the rest of the sentence: it is about a raft that must not fall apart. That is “sound” meaning strong.
> _For grown-ups: Chose the right meaning of the multiple-meaning word “sound” from context._

**Moomintroll**: Does it need a name?  
**Moominpappa**: Everything that goes anywhere needs a name. Otherwise how will it know it’s gone?  
_You carry planks and Moomintroll ties knots. Sniff supervises from a stump and offers advice nobody takes._  
**Sniff**: That plank’s crooked. That one’s too short. That one is fine, I suppose, if you like that sort of plank.  
_By noon there is a raft, and it floats. Moominpappa looks as if he has built a ship of the line._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.D.8, ask): perimeter of a rectangle  
> _Moomintroll wants to tie a rope all the way round the edge of the raft: 2 m wide, 3 m long. How much rope goes round the edge?_  
> - ✅ 10 metres → Yes: 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 = 10. The distance round the edge is called the perimeter.
> - 6 metres → That is the area in square metres: 2 × 3. The rope goes round the edges: 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 = 10 metres.
> - 5 metres → That is only one width and one length. The rope goes right round: two widths and two lengths, which is 10 metres.
> _For grown-ups: Found the perimeter of a 2 m by 3 m rectangle by adding all four sides._

**Moominpappa**: What shall we call her?  

**Choices**

- **Suggest calling it after Moominmamma.**
    - **Moominpappa**: Ah. Yes. Then she’ll be sure to bring you home.
    - He paints MAMMA on the stern in careful blue letters.
- **Suggest “The Valuable”.**
    - **Sniff**: Oh. Oh, that’s a very good name. That’s the best name.
    - Moominpappa paints it on, and Sniff stands next to it for a long time with his paws behind his back.

#### `reader:a09-02-build-done` · [main](main.md#a09-02-build-done)

_Moominmamma comes down the bank with a basket so full the lid will not close._  
**Moominmamma**: Sandwiches on top. Pullovers underneath. Don’t eat the pullovers.  
_A nail drops out of Moominpappa’s pocket and sinks to the bottom of the river at once, while a plank bobs beside it._  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): why things float: density  
> _The nail sinks and the plank floats. Why does a whole wooden raft float, even loaded with a basket and three passengers?_  
> - ✅ Wood is lighter than the same amount of water, so the water holds it up. → Yes. Wood is less dense than water, so it floats. Iron is denser, so a lone nail sinks.
> - The river is calm. → A calm river helps us steer, but it is not why it floats. Wood weighs less than the same volume of water.
> - Because the raft is big. → Size alone does not decide it. A big stone still sinks. A raft floats because wood is less dense than water.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that things float when they are less dense than water._


#### `reader:a09-02-launch` · [main](main.md#a09-02-launch)

_Sniff arrives with a sack of his things: a tin box, a broken compass, a piece of red glass and a great many buttons._  
**Moomintroll**: Sniff, there isn’t room for all that.  
**Sniff**: There’s room for the Nubbin. The Nubbin’s valuable. These are valuable. It’s the same thing.  
_Your Nubbin, in his pocket, looks at the sack. Then it looks at you._  
**Moominmamma**: Sniff, dear. Things are lovely, but they don’t row.  
**Sniff**: These ones might. You don’t know. Nobody’s ever asked them.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Eco.1.3-5, ask): what makes something valuable  
> _Sniff says his buttons are “valuable”. Is a thing valuable because of what it costs, or because of what it means to someone? Think about it._  
> - Because of what it costs. → That’s a fair answer. Money is one way people measure value. Sniff’s buttons would not sell for much, though, so he must mean something else too.
> - Because of what it means to someone. → That’s a fair answer. A single button can be priceless to the person who found it. Value can be a feeling as well as a price.
> - Both, depending on who you ask. → That’s a fair answer. A jeweller and Sniff may both call something valuable, for different reasons.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about whether value comes from price or from what a thing means to a person._


**Choices**

- **Help Sniff choose the one thing he’d miss most.**
    - It takes Sniff a very long time. In the end he picks the red glass, because the sun comes through it.
    - **Sniff**: The rest can stay with Moominmamma. She’s nearly as careful as me.
- **Find room for all of it.**
    - You find room. The raft sits a little lower in the water and turns, very slowly, in a circle.
    - Halfway round, Sniff quietly takes the buttons back up the bank to Moominmamma.

#### `reader:a09-02-launch-done` · [main](main.md#a09-02-launch-done)

_Moominpappa pushes the raft off with his foot. Moominmamma waves her apron until the bend._  
_Then there is only the river, the grey sky and the red tail low over the hills._  
_Sniff takes out his broken compass. The needle swings, wobbles and sticks, pointing at nothing in particular._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): a compass needle is a magnet that points north  
> _A compass needle is a small magnet. The Earth is a giant magnet too, so the needle swings round until it points to the magnetic north pole._  
> _That is why a working compass points the same way wherever you stand. Sailors have used one for hundreds of years._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a compass needle is a magnet that lines up with the Earth’s magnetic field, pointing north._


#### `reader:a09-02-river` · [main](main.md#a09-02-river)

_The river carries you through woods and meadows. Everything you pass seems to be leaving._  
_Birds fly low and quiet, all one way. A line of ants crosses a log with their eggs held over their heads._  
**Moomintroll**: Where are they all going?  
**Sniff**: Away. Which is what sensible people do. We’re going TOWARDS it.  
**Moomintroll**: We’re going towards finding out. That’s different.  
**Sniff**: It doesn’t feel different. It feels exactly the same.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): the same action can feel different from inside  
> _Moomintroll and Sniff are walking the same way. Moomintroll feels he is going to find out._  
> _Sniff feels he is going to meet the thing he fears. Doing a brave thing can feel scary and brave at once._  
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that two people can do the same thing and feel differently about it, and that bravery can sit alongside fear._

_The river forks round a rock. One way is quick and white; the other is slow and dark under the trees._  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): animals move where their needs are met  
> _Animals often leave a place when it stops giving them what they need. Which of these is a real reason birds and ants might move away?_  
> - ✅ Food, water or shelter is running out, or the weather is changing. → Yes. Animals move to places where their needs are met. Many sense changes in weather before we do.
> - They have finished their holiday. → A funny thought, but animals move for survival. They go where food, water and shelter are.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that animals move to places where their needs for food, water and shelter are met._


**Choices**

- **Take the quick channel.**
    - The raft bucks and spins and shoots out into a pool, and everybody laughs, even Sniff, once it is over.
    - **Sniff**: I wasn’t frightened. My pocket was frightened.
- **Take the slow channel.**
    - You drift under the trees in green half-dark. A heron watches you go by and does not bother to move.
    - **Moomintroll**: This is nice. You could almost forget.

#### `reader:a09-02-river-done` · [main](main.md#a09-02-river-done)

_You pushed off at one o’clock. At dusk, at seven in the evening, you pull in to a sandy bank._  
_Moomintroll unpacks the sandwiches._  
_Upstream, a thin line of smoke rises from somebody else’s fire._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.A.1, ask): time intervals  
> _You set off at 1:00 in the afternoon and landed at 7:00 in the evening. How long were you on the river?_  
> - ✅ 6 hours → Yes. From 1 to 7 is 6 hours: 1 to 2, 2 to 3, and so on.
> - 8 hours → Count the hours from 1 to 7: that is 6. If it helps, count on your fingers one hour at a time.
> - 7 hours → 7:00 is the end time, not the length. Count from 1 up to 7 and you get 6 hours.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a time interval of 6 hours from 1:00 to 7:00._


#### `reader:a09-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a09-02-complete)

_Sniff eats his sandwich very slowly, to make it last._  
**Sniff**: When I was small, I thought the sky was a lid. Like on a tin. And nothing could get through it.  
**Moomintroll**: And now?  
**Sniff**: Now I think it’s just sky. Anything can get through sky.  
_Your Nubbin climbs out of his pocket and sits on his knee, which is as near as it can get to a lid._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): a character’s motive  
> _Why does Sniff tell Moomintroll about the lid, just now, at the end of a long day?_  
> - ✅ He is saying out loud what really frightens him. → Yes. A lid would keep the comet out. “Anything can get through sky” is his fear, spoken at last.
> - He is bored and wants to chat. → Not quite. Look at how he says it: softly, and sadly. It is a worry he has been keeping inside.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out why Sniff shares a childhood memory: to say his fear aloud._

## A9-3. Snufkin

- `a09-03-s1`: Find whoever is camping upstream. · _Follow the smoke along the bank; listen for music._
- `a09-03-s2`: Sit by Snufkin’s fire and hear how he travels. · _Sit on the log nearest the fire._
- `a09-03-s3`: Look at the comet with Snufkin before you all sleep. · _Walk down to the water’s edge, where the trees open up._

#### `reader:a09-03-camp` · [main](main.md#a09-03-camp)

_You follow the smoke along the bank. Before you see the fire, you hear a tune._  
_It is a harmonica, playing something that goes up like a question and comes down without needing an answer._  

> **Lesson** (music, ask): pitch: high and low notes  
> _Snufkin’s tune “goes up like a question and comes down without needing an answer”. When a tune goes up, are the notes getting higher or lower in pitch?_  
> - ✅ Higher → Yes. Going up in a tune means the pitch is rising. Try humming a question, such as “Really?”. Your voice goes up at the end.
> - Lower → Lower is going down. A question tends to lift at the end, like the start of Snufkin’s tune. Try saying “Really?” and listen to your voice rise.
> _For grown-ups: Identified rising pitch as the notes going higher._

_A small green tent stands by the water. In front of it, someone in an old green hat sits on a stone, fishing._  
_The tune stops. He does not turn round._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, notice): a harmonica makes sound with vibrating reeds  
> _Inside a harmonica are tiny metal strips called reeds. Breathe across one and it vibrates, wobbling to and fro very fast._  
> _That vibration shakes the air, and the shaking air is the sound you hear. Short, stiff reeds vibrate faster and make higher notes._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that vibrating reeds make the sound of a harmonica, and that faster vibration gives a higher note._

**Snufkin**: Come and sit down, if you’re coming. The fish have heard you anyway.  
**Moomintroll**: We didn’t mean to scare them.  
**Snufkin**: They don’t mind. They’re only pretending to be scared, so they don’t have to be caught.  

#### `reader:a09-03-camp-done` · [main](main.md#a09-03-camp-done)

**Snufkin**: I’m Snufkin. There’s coffee, if you don’t mind it out of the one cup.  
_He passes the cup round, and it goes round twice. Nobody minds._  
_There are four of you: Snufkin, Moomintroll, Sniff and you. Everybody takes one sip each time round._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): multiplication as equal groups: 4 people × 2 rounds  
> _The cup goes round twice, with one sip each time for each of the four of you. How many sips is that altogether?_  
> - ✅ 8 → Yes. Two rounds of four sips: 4 × 2 = 8. Or 4 + 4.
> - 6 → Close. Each round has four sips, not three. Two rounds is 4 + 4, which is 8.
> - 4 → That is only one trip round. It goes round twice: 4 + 4 = 8 sips.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out 4 × 2 = 8 using equal groups._


#### `reader:a09-03-fire` · [main](main.md#a09-03-fire)

_Sniff is looking around the camp with growing alarm._  
**Sniff**: Where’s all your stuff?  
**Snufkin**: This is all my stuff. A tent, a pipe, a cup, a harmonica. A hat.  
**Sniff**: That’s not stuff. That’s hardly anything. What do you do when you want something?  
**Snufkin**: I look at it. Then I leave it where it is, and it’s still beautiful, and I don’t have to carry it.  
_Sniff holds his pocket as if Snufkin might suggest leaving your Nubbin where it is._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Eco.1.3-5, notice): needs and wants  
> _A need is something you cannot do without, like food, water, warmth and shelter._  
> _A want is something you would like, like a collection of buttons. People often disagree about which is which._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the difference between needs and wants._

**Snufkin**: Not that. You don’t own a friend; you just walk next to them.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): does owning more make us feel safer?  
> _Snufkin owns almost nothing and seems content. Sniff owns a lot and is always worried. Does owning more make people feel safer? Think about it._  
> - Yes, more things means more protection. → That’s a fair answer. Having enough food and a roof really does help us feel safe. Snufkin has what he needs, too.
> - No, feeling safe comes from somewhere else. → That’s a fair answer. Many people find that friends, and trusting that they can cope, matter more than things.
> - It depends how much they already have. → That’s a fair answer. After our needs are met, extra things may add less and less, while worry about losing them can grow.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about whether having more possessions makes people feel safer._


**Choices**

- **Ask where he’s going.**
    - **Snufkin**: South, in the end. I always go south in the end. But I’ve no hurry.
    - **Snufkin**: Except there’s that.
    - He tips his hat back and looks at the tail over the hills.
- **Ask if he ever gets lonely.**
    - **Snufkin**: Sometimes. Then I go and find someone. Then I go away again. Both are good.
    - **Moomintroll**: You could come with us.
    - **Snufkin**: I could.

#### `reader:a09-03-fire-done` · [main](main.md#a09-03-fire-done)

_He plays again, softer. The tune goes round the fire and out over the water, and comes back sounding older._  
_Your Nubbin climbs out of Sniff’s pocket and sits by Snufkin’s boot to listen._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, notice): echoes: sound bounces off surfaces  
> _Sound travels in waves, and when a wave meets a hard surface, such as a cliff or still water, some of it bounces back. That bounce is an echo._  
> _On a quiet night by the water, the echo arrives a moment after the tune, a little fainter, which is why it “comes back older”._
