# Atlas, page A11: The Enchanted Place

## A11-1. Bump, Bump, Bump

*Winnie-the-Pooh (1926), ch. I: Pooh and some bees* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Enchanted Place page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time once Nova’s atlas is in the Grove; the first quest on the page. Not in the UK before 1 January 2027.

**Cast:** Christopher Robin (Christopher Robin as Milne wrote him (1926 and 1928); original design), Pooh (Edward Bear, called Winnie-the-Pooh (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design)

### Step 1: Step into the pencil Forest and meet the boy at its edge.

Hint: Touch the page, then walk up the path to the boy with the bear. · check: `explore:a11-forest:edge`

#### <a id="a11-01-page"></a>Scene `a11-01-page`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page drawn in soft pencil: a stream, a bridge, and a hill with a ring of trees on top._  
_Across the top there is writing, in slow, careful print._  
_Here is Edward Bear, coming downstairs now, bump, bump, bump, on the back of his head, behind Christopher Robin._  
_The pencil goes soft under your fingers, then softer. Then it is pine needles, and you and your Nubbin are standing in them._  
_A boy is coming up the path with a bear under his arm, upside down. Neither of them seems to mind._  
**Christopher Robin**: Hallo. You’re not from the Forest. I know everybody in the Forest.  
**Christopher Robin**: This is Pooh. He came down the stairs on his head, so he may be a bit muddled. He usually is anyway.  
**Pooh** _(think)_: How do you do. Is that a Heffalump?  
**Christopher Robin**: No, Pooh.  
**Pooh**: A very small one, I expect. They start small. Everything does.  
_Your Nubbin looks behind itself, in case there is a Heffalump there._  

**Choices**

- **Tell Pooh it’s a Nubbin.**
    - **Pooh**: A Nubbin. Yes. That’s just what a very small Heffalump would say.
- **Let your Nubbin decide.**
    - Your Nubbin sits on Pooh’s foot. Pooh decides that is a friendly thing for a Heffalump to do, and lets it.
- **Ask what a Heffalump is.**
    - **Christopher Robin**: Nobody knows exactly. That’s the best sort of animal.

#### <a id="a11-01-page-done"></a>Done `a11-01-page-done`

**Pooh**: I was just going to see about some honey. You can come, if you like.  
**Pooh** _(happy)_: Honey always goes better with somebody watching.  

### Step 2: Hold the balloon string while Pooh floats up to the bees.

Hint: Stand under the tall oak and keep both hands on the string. · check: `wait:a11-balloon:oak`

#### <a id="a11-01-balloon"></a>Scene `a11-01-balloon`

_High in an oak there is a buzzing. Pooh stops so suddenly that your Nubbin walks into the back of him._  
**Pooh** _(happy)_: That is a honey sort of buzz. I’d know it anywhere.  
**Pooh**: The trouble with honey is that it’s always at the top of things. Christopher Robin, have you got such a thing as a balloon?  
**Christopher Robin**: I’ve got two. A green one and a blue one. What do you want a balloon for?  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Honey.  
**Pooh**: The blue one, please. Bees won’t mind a bit of blue sky. And I shall roll in the mud, so I look like a small black cloud.  
_He does, very thoroughly. Your Nubbin rolls in the mud too, in case it’s a rule._  
**Christopher Robin**: You hold the string. Don’t let go unless he says. And don’t laugh. It hurts his feelings.  
_Up goes Pooh, past the branches, round and black and very hopeful. He stops at exactly the height of the nest._  

#### <a id="a11-01-balloon-done"></a>Done `a11-01-balloon-done`

_The string tugs in your hands like a kite that has been told about honey._  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Christopher Robin? These bees are looking at me in a very suspicious sort of way.  

### Step 3: Help get Pooh down again, without laughing.

Hint: Keep the string steady while Christopher Robin aims his popgun. · check: `watch:a11-bees:oak`

#### <a id="a11-01-bees"></a>Scene `a11-01-bees`

**Pooh**: Would you walk up and down with your umbrella, Christopher Robin, and say it looks like rain? Then they’ll believe I’m a cloud.  
_Christopher Robin walks up and down under his umbrella, saying it looks like rain. It doesn’t, and the bees know._  
**Pooh**: I think these are the wrong sort of bees. They would make the wrong sort of honey. I shall come down now.  
**Christopher Robin**: How?  
_Pooh hadn’t thought about that. He holds on, with the face of a bear doing a hard sum._  
**Pooh**: If you were to shoot the balloon with your popgun, I should come down. Gently. I hope.  
**Christopher Robin**: I might miss.  
**Pooh**: You might. Try not to.  

**Choices**

- **Bite your lip and hold steady.**
    - You bite your lip, and the string stays steady. Pooh looks down at you gratefully.
- **Laugh, a little, into your sleeve.**
    - **Pooh**: I heard that.
    - **Christopher Robin**: It’s all right. I laughed the first time too. He forgave me by lunch.

#### <a id="a11-01-bees-done"></a>Done `a11-01-bees-done`

_Pop goes the popgun. “Ow!” says Pooh._  
**Christopher Robin**: Did I miss?  
**Pooh** _(sad)_: You hit something. It wasn’t the balloon.  
_The second cork gets the balloon. Pooh floats down into your arms like a large, muddy leaf._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a11-01-complete"></a>Complete `a11-01-complete`

_Pooh’s arms have held the string so long that they stay up in the air, like a bear about to be surprised._  
**Christopher Robin**: They’ll come down in a week or so. If a fly lands on his nose, he has to blow it off. Like that. Pooh!  
_Pooh blows a fly off his nose. He looks pleased to be good at something._  
**Christopher Robin**: You can come back, you know. There’s always something on in the Forest. Or nothing, which is better.  
_He ties a blue ribbon round your Nubbin’s middle, in a careful double bow._  
**Christopher Robin**: So everyone knows it’s a Friend. Otherwise Piglet will think it’s a Woozle, and then there’s no talking to him.

**Notes:** OPENING: the atlas page is drawn in soft pencil and colours in as you step through (a slow wash, two seconds). a11-01-page#1–2 show the line of print across the top of the page; voice it as narration. ## A11-2. A Tight Place

*Winnie-the-Pooh (1926), ch. II: Pooh goes visiting and gets into a tight place* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Enchanted Place page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A11-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A11-1

**Cast:** Pooh (Edward Bear, called Winnie-the-Pooh (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Rabbit (Rabbit (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Christopher Robin (Christopher Robin as Milne wrote him (1926 and 1928); original design)

### Step 1: Go visiting with Pooh, to Rabbit’s.

Hint: Follow Pooh along the sandbank to the hole in it. · check: `visit:a11-rabbit:burrow`

#### <a id="a11-02-visit"></a>Scene `a11-02-visit`

_Pooh is humming along the sandbank, which is what he does when he isn’t doing anything else._  
**Pooh** _(happy)_: A hole. A hole means Rabbit, and Rabbit means company, and company means a little something.  
**Pooh**: Hallo? Anybody in?  
**Rabbit**: Nobody’s in. Go away.  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Then who said that?  
**Rabbit**: Nobody. I said it for him.  
_Rabbit puts his head out, sighs, and looks at your Nubbin._  
**Rabbit**: And what is that?  
**Pooh**: A very small Heffalump.  
**Rabbit**: It is not. It’s wearing a bow. Heffalumps don’t wear bows. Come in, then, both of you. Wipe your feet.  
_Rabbit gets out the honey. Pooh has a little, and then a little more, and then the rest, so it won’t feel left out._  
_Your Nubbin has one crumb. Rabbit watches it eat the crumb as if it might be the start of something._  

#### <a id="a11-02-visit-done"></a>Done `a11-02-visit-done`

**Rabbit**: Well. Must you be going? Already? What a shame.  
_Pooh starts out of the front door, and his front half gets out. The rest of him has had too much honey to follow._  
**Pooh** _(shock)_: Oh, dear. I seem to have stopped.  

### Step 2: Fetch Christopher Robin to look at the stuck bear.

Hint: Run up the path to Christopher Robin’s house, then walk him back. · check: `escort:a11-cr:rabbit-door`

#### <a id="a11-02-stuck"></a>Scene `a11-02-stuck`

_Pooh is stuck. His head and arms are out in the morning, and the rest of him is in Rabbit’s hall._  
**Pooh** _(sad)_: I can’t go forwards and I can’t go back. It’s all on account of the door being too narrow.  
**Rabbit**: It’s all on account of somebody eating too much honey. I’m only saying.  
**Rabbit**: You. Keeper. Go and get Christopher Robin. Run.  
_You run. Christopher Robin comes back at a sensible walk, because he has seen this sort of thing before._  
**Christopher Robin**: There’s only one thing for it. He’ll have to stay there until he’s thin again.  
**Pooh** _(shock)_: How long does getting thin take?  
**Christopher Robin**: About a week, I should think.  
**Pooh**: A week! What about meals?  
**Christopher Robin**: No meals. That’s rather the point.  
_A tear rolls down Pooh’s nose and drips on the sand. Your Nubbin sits in front of him, nose to nose._  
**Pooh** _(sad)_: Then would you read to me? Something comforting. A Sustaining Book, for a Bear in a Tight Place.  

#### <a id="a11-02-stuck-done"></a>Done `a11-02-stuck-done`

**Christopher Robin**: We’ll take turns. You read in the mornings, and I’ll read in the afternoons.  

### Step 3: Read to Pooh while he gets thinner.

Hint: Sit by Pooh’s head and pick something to read; the week passes as the story goes on. · check: `sit:a11-pooh:rabbit-door`

#### <a id="a11-02-week"></a>Scene `a11-02-week`

_So for a week you sit on the sandbank and read to the North end of Pooh. Christopher Robin takes the afternoons._  
_At the South end, inside, Rabbit has hung his washing on Pooh’s back legs._  
**Rabbit** _(think)_: It’s a waste of a perfectly good pair of legs otherwise. Do you mind, Pooh?  
**Pooh**: I don’t mind. I should like to know what’s on them, though.  
**Rabbit**: Tea towels.  
**Pooh**: Ah.  
_Your Nubbin brings Pooh an acorn to look at, since he isn’t allowed to eat anything._  
**Pooh**: What are we reading today?  

**Choices**

- **A story about a bear who was exactly the right size.**
    - **Pooh**: I like him already. Does he get stuck?
    - **You**: Never.
    - **Pooh**: Lucky bear.
- **A book about the sea, where there aren’t any doors.**
    - **Pooh**: No doors at all? That sounds like a very comfortable place for a bear.
- **Just sit with him. Don’t read anything.**
    - You sit by his head and say nothing at all.
    - **Pooh**: That’s the most sustaining one so far.

#### <a id="a11-02-week-done"></a>Done `a11-02-week-done`

_On the seventh day Christopher Robin takes Pooh’s front paws. Rabbit takes Christopher Robin._  
_Rabbit’s friends-and-relations take Rabbit, all of them, in a long line. You take the end, and your Nubbin takes you._  
**Christopher Robin**: Pull!  
_For a long time nothing happens. Then, with a pop like a ginger-beer cork, out comes Pooh, and everyone sits down at once._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a11-02-complete"></a>Complete `a11-02-complete`

**Pooh** _(happy)_: Thank you. All of you. Especially the one at the end, who I couldn’t see but could feel.  
_Pooh nods to his friends and walks off into the Forest, humming, a little thinner and not at all wiser._  
**Rabbit** _(think)_: He’ll be back. He knows where the honey is now. I shall have to get a wider door.  
_Rabbit looks at the hall where the legs were. He looks very nearly as if he misses them._

**Notes:** The week passes in the scene, not in real time: no daily visits (canon 22). Rabbit’s friends-and-relations are a long line of small silhouettes of all shapes (hedgehog, mouse, beetle); none speaks. The pull is a tug-of-war beat with a held pose and a pop sound.

## A11-3. The Woozle

*Winnie-the-Pooh (1926), ch. III: Pooh and Piglet go hunting* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Enchanted Place page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A11-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A11-2

**Cast:** Piglet (Piglet (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Pooh (Edward Bear, called Winnie-the-Pooh (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Christopher Robin (Christopher Robin as Milne wrote him (1926 and 1928); original design)

### Step 1: Follow Pooh round the spinney after the mysterious tracks.

Hint: Walk round the larch spinney in the snow behind Pooh and Piglet. Don’t spoil it. · check: `walk:a11-spinney:larches`

#### <a id="a11-03-tracks"></a>Scene `a11-03-tracks`

_It is a fine winter’s day. Piglet is brushing snow off his front step when Pooh goes past, round in a circle._  
**Piglet**: Pooh? Why are you going round and round?  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Following something. I don’t know what yet. That’s the exciting part.  
**Pooh**: Look. Paw-marks. Something has been round here, and round, and I have been round after it.  
**Piglet** _(shock)_: Could it be… a Woozle?  
**Pooh**: It might. Woozles are very hard to tell from the front.  
_Piglet looks at your Nubbin, hard. Your Nubbin looks back with great innocence._  
**Piglet** _(think)_: That one’s got a bow on. Christopher Robin s-said it was a Friend. But a Woozle would say that.  
_Your Nubbin trots after Pooh round the spinney of larches, putting its small feet very carefully in the snow._  

**Choices**

- **Tell them the little prints are your Nubbin’s.**
    - **Pooh**: Yes, yes. And these others are something else. Do keep up.
    - Nobody is listening. It is very hard to spoil a thing when nobody is listening.
- **Say nothing. Let it play out.**
    - You say nothing. It feels like carrying a very full cup, very still.
- **Let your Nubbin go round once more.**
    - Your Nubbin goes round once more, very pleased with itself. Now there is one more set of tracks.

#### <a id="a11-03-tracks-done"></a>Done `a11-03-tracks-done`

_Round they go. Every time they come back to the same place, there are more tracks than before._  

### Step 2: Watch from the oak with Christopher Robin until they work it out.

Hint: Climb the big oak by the spinney and keep quiet. · check: `watch:a11-spinney:oak`

#### <a id="a11-03-count"></a>Scene `a11-03-count`

**Pooh** _(shock)_: Look! Two of them now. And one very small. And a third, and something else besides.  
**Piglet** _(shock)_: Three Woozles and a Wizzle, perhaps. Pooh, I’ve just remembered something I meant to do yesterday. I’d better go and do it.  
_Up in a big oak, Christopher Robin is sitting very still. He beckons you up, and your Nubbin rides in your hood._  
**Christopher Robin**: Don’t say anything. He gets there in the end. He always does, and it’s better when he gets there himself.  
_Pooh stops and looks at the tracks. He puts his own foot into one of them, and it fits._  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Oh.  
_He puts his foot into another. It fits too._  
_Piglet, a little way off, looks at the tiny prints, then at your Nubbin’s feet, and goes pink._  

#### <a id="a11-03-count-done"></a>Done `a11-03-count-done`

_Christopher Robin climbs down from the tree._  
**Christopher Robin** _(happy)_: Silly old Bear, what were you doing?  
**Pooh** _(sad)_: I have been going round and round after my own feet. I am a Bear with no Brain worth mentioning.  
**Christopher Robin** _(soft)_: There isn’t a better bear anywhere. I’d know.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a11-03-complete"></a>Complete `a11-03-complete`

**Pooh** _(happy)_: Being silly is very hungry work. Is it lunch?  
_Pooh and Christopher Robin go home together. Piglet walks a long way round your Nubbin, then, after a while, a shorter way._  
**Piglet** _(soft)_: I knew it wasn’t a Woozle. Mostly.  
_Your Nubbin holds out a paw. Piglet shakes it quickly, and then a second time, slower._

## A11-4. Eeyore’s Tail

*Winnie-the-Pooh (1926), ch. IV: Eeyore loses a tail and Pooh finds one* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Enchanted Place page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A11-3, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A11-3

**Cast:** Pooh (Edward Bear, called Winnie-the-Pooh (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Eeyore (Eeyore, the old grey donkey (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Owl (Owl (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Christopher Robin (Christopher Robin as Milne wrote him (1926 and 1928); original design)

### Step 1: Find out what is wrong with Eeyore.

Hint: Eeyore stands in the thistly corner of the Forest. · check: `talk:a11-eeyore:thistles`

#### <a id="a11-04-gloomy"></a>Scene `a11-04-gloomy`

_Eeyore, the old grey donkey, stands by himself in a thistly corner of the Forest, with his head on one side._  
_Sometimes he thinks sadly about things. Sometimes he just thinks._  
**Pooh**: Good morning, Eeyore.  
**Eeyore** _(sad)_: Good morning, Pooh. If it is a good morning. Which I doubt.  
**Pooh**: Why, what’s the matter?  
**Eeyore**: Nothing. Nobody minds. Nobody ever does.  
_Pooh walks all the way round Eeyore. Then he walks round again, to be sure._  
**Pooh** _(shock)_: Eeyore. What has happened to your tail?  
_Eeyore turns slowly to look, and turns, and turns, and comes back to where he started._  
**Eeyore** _(sad)_: You’re right. It’s gone. That explains a great deal.  
**Eeyore**: Somebody must have taken it. How like them.  

**Choices**

- **Promise you’ll help find it.**
    - **Eeyore**: Kind of you. Don’t trouble. I expect somebody is using it for something important.
- **Sit with him for a moment first.**
    - You sit in the thistles beside him, which are very prickly. Eeyore notices, and doesn’t say so, which is a sort of thank you.
- **Look behind the thistles.**
    - Your Nubbin looks behind the thistles, under a stone and inside its own bow. No tail.

#### <a id="a11-04-gloomy-done"></a>Done `a11-04-gloomy-done`

**Pooh**: I shall find your tail for you, Eeyore. Me. Pooh.  
**Eeyore** _(soft)_: Thank you, Pooh. You’re a real friend. Not like some.  

### Step 2: Go with Pooh to ask Owl.

Hint: Owl lives at The Chestnuts, high in a tree, behind the grandest door in the Forest. · check: `visit:a11-owl:chestnuts`

#### <a id="a11-04-owl"></a>Scene `a11-04-owl`

_Owl lives at The Chestnuts, high in a tree, behind a very grand door with a knocker and a bell-pull._  
_There are two notices by the door, in Owl’s spelling: one says to ring, and the other to knock if the ringing doesn’t work._  
_Pooh knocks and rings. Your Nubbin, who can reach neither, stands on Pooh._  
**Owl**: Ah, Pooh. And company. How do you do. What can I do for you?  
**Pooh**: Eeyore has lost his tail, and he’s very sad about it. Could you tell me how to find it?  
**Owl**: Well. In cases of this sort there is a Customary Procedure. First, one Issues a Notice.  
**Pooh** _(sad)_: For I am a Bear of Very Little Brain, and long words Bother me.  
**Owl**: It means the Thing to Do. Then one offers a Reward. Then one considers the Matter in all its Aspects.  
_Owl talks for some time, in longer and longer words. Pooh nods at the places where nodding seems to be wanted._  
_Your Nubbin isn’t listening. It is looking at the bell-pull, which is grey, and hairy, and has a tuft at the end._  

#### <a id="a11-04-owl-done"></a>Done `a11-04-owl-done`

**Pooh** _(think)_: Owl. That bell-pull. Where did you get it?  
**Owl**: Came across it in the Forest, hanging on a bush. I gave it a pull to see who was in. Nobody was. It came away in my wing.  
**Pooh**: It belongs to Eeyore. He’s been missing it all morning, and the back of him hasn’t known what to do.  

### Step 3: Hold the tail straight while Christopher Robin nails it back on.

Hint: Carry the tail back to the thistles and hold it where Christopher Robin points. · check: `place:a11-tail:eeyore`

#### <a id="a11-04-nail"></a>Scene `a11-04-nail`

_You carry the tail back through the Forest, very carefully, as if it might wake up._  
_Christopher Robin meets you at the thistles with a little hammer and one very short nail._  
**Christopher Robin**: Hold it straight. Not that straight. There. Eeyore, this won’t hurt. It’s a very small nail.  
**Eeyore**: Don’t mind me. Nobody does.  
_Tap, tap: Eeyore doesn’t flinch. Your Nubbin flinches for him, both times._  
_Then Eeyore swishes his tail, carefully, like somebody trying a new door._  
**Eeyore**: Hm. It works. Not that anybody asked.  
_He swishes it again, and frisks about among the thistles. He is happy, though he would rather you didn’t mention it._  

#### <a id="a11-04-nail-done"></a>Done `a11-04-nail-done`

**Eeyore** _(soft)_: Thank you, Pooh. And the one with the bow. Thank you.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a11-04-complete"></a>Complete `a11-04-complete`

_Pooh walks home feeling that he has done a Good Deed, which he has._  
_He feels so pleased that he is ready for a little something by eleven o’clock. So he has one._  
**Pooh** _(happy)_: Owl knows ever such a lot. Long words and everything. But it was your Nubbin who looked at the bell-pull.  
_Your Nubbin looks proud. Then, to be safe, it looks for every other bell-pull in the Forest, and there aren’t any._

## A11-5. The Heffalump Trap

*Winnie-the-Pooh (1926), ch. V: Piglet meets a Heffalump* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Enchanted Place page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A11-4, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A11-4

**Cast:** Christopher Robin (Christopher Robin as Milne wrote him (1926 and 1928); original design), Piglet (Piglet (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design), Pooh (Edward Bear, called Winnie-the-Pooh (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design)

### Step 1: Help Pooh and Piglet dig a Very Deep Pit by the Six Pine Trees.

Hint: Dig where Pooh points; Piglet supervises the supervising. · check: `build:a11-pit:six-pines`

#### <a id="a11-05-dig"></a>Scene `a11-05-dig`

**Christopher Robin**: I saw a Heffalump today. I think.  
_Christopher Robin says it carelessly, the way you might mention rain, and then goes home to his lunch._  
**Piglet** _(shock)_: A Heffalump. Here. Pooh, what does one do with a Heffalump?  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Catch it. In a Very Deep Pit. A cunning one, near where Heffalumps walk. The Six Pine Trees, I should think.  
_Piglet digs and Pooh supervises. You do most of it, and your Nubbin moves one pebble a long way, very proudly._  
**Piglet**: Won’t the Heffalump see the pit?  
**Pooh**: We cover it with sticks and leaves. And at the bottom we put something it likes, so it comes.  
**Piglet**: What do Heffalumps like?  
**Pooh** _(happy)_: Honey.  
**Piglet**: Are you sure?  
**Pooh**: Everybody likes honey.  

**Choices**

- **Ask what they’ll do once it’s caught.**
    - **Pooh**: Be very polite to it, I expect. It’ll be cross otherwise.
    - **Piglet**: Oh, d-dear.
- **Dig a little step in one side, in case.**
    - **Piglet**: A step? For the Heffalump?
    - **You**: For anyone who falls in by mistake.
    - **Pooh**: Very sensible. Nobody will.
- **Check it’s deep enough.**
    - You climb in to look, and it comes up to your chin. Your Nubbin, at the top, looks down at you as if you are the Heffalump.

#### <a id="a11-05-dig-done"></a>Done `a11-05-dig-done`

_Pooh fetches his very last jar of honey and lowers it into the pit. He looks at it for some time after._  

### Step 2: Keep the lantern steady while Pooh checks on the honey in the night.

## A11-20. An Enchanted Place

*The House at Pooh Corner (1928), ch. X: Christopher Robin and Pooh come to an enchanted place* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Enchanted Place page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A11-19, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A11-19

**Cast:** Christopher Robin (Christopher Robin as Milne wrote him (1926 and 1928); original design), Pooh (Edward Bear, called Winnie-the-Pooh (A. A. Milne, 1926 and 1928); original design)

### Step 1: Walk with Christopher Robin and Pooh to the top of the Forest.

Hint: Walk a little way behind them, up through the Forest to Galleons Lap. · check: `walk:a11-galleons-lap:climb`

#### <a id="a11-20-climb"></a>Scene `a11-20-climb`

_Christopher Robin and Pooh walk up through the Forest, and you walk a little way behind, with your Nubbin._  
_Nobody is in a hurry. Christopher Robin points things out, the way you do when you want to remember the way._  
**Christopher Robin** _(think)_: Pooh. Of all the things there are to do, what do you like best?  
**Pooh**: Coming round to yours. And you saying there’s honey. And there being honey. And Piglet. Mostly that.  
**Christopher Robin**: I like that too. But what I like best of all is doing Nothing.  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Nothing? What does Nothing look like, when you’re doing it?  
**Christopher Robin**: It’s when you go out for no reason, and come back for no reason, and the whole day in between is yours.  
**Pooh** _(happy)_: Oh. I do that a lot.  
**Christopher Robin**: I know. You’re very good at it.  
_They come out at the top of the Forest. There is a ring of trees there, sixty-something of them, standing round as if they’ve been waiting._  
_Nobody has ever been able to count them: sixty-three, or sixty-four. It changes a little every time you try._  

**Choices**

- **Count the trees.**
    - You count sixty-three, then again, and get sixty-four. Your Nubbin, counting on its paws, gives up at four and is content.
- **Don’t count them. Just look.**
    - You don’t count them. It seems like the kind of place that would rather not be counted.

### Step 2: Wait at the edge of the circle while Christopher Robin and Pooh talk.

Hint: Stay at the edge. This bit is theirs. · check: `wait:a11-galleons-lap:edge`

#### <a id="a11-20-knight"></a>Scene `a11-20-knight`

_At the edge of the circle you stop. Your Nubbin stops too, and sits down without being told._  
_Christopher Robin and Pooh go into the middle, where the grass is soft, and sit, and talk about everything._  
_Kings and queens, and a thing called Factors, and a place called Brazil. Pooh listens, and doesn’t mind not knowing._  
**Christopher Robin**: Pooh. Stand up.  
_Christopher Robin takes a stick and touches Pooh on each shoulder with it, very gravely._  
**Christopher Robin**: Arise, Sir Pooh. Knight of the Forest, and my truest friend.  
_Pooh rises, and thinks about being a knight, and decides he likes it, though he isn’t sure what it involves._  
_Then Christopher Robin is quiet for a long time, looking at the trees._  
**Christopher Robin** _(soft)_: Pooh. Promise you won’t forget me. Not ever. Not even when I’m grown up and enormous.  
**Pooh** _(think)_: Will I be enormous too?  
**Christopher Robin**: No. You’ll be just the right size.  
**Pooh** _(soft)_: Then I promise.  
**Christopher Robin**: And if I’m not here so much, you’ll know it isn’t because of you. Won’t you?  
**Pooh**: I shall know. Even if I don’t know why.
