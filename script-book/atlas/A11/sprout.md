# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Atlas, page A11: The Enchanted Place

## A11-1. A Bear on the Stairs

- `a11-01-s1`: Meet the boy and his bear at the edge of the Forest. · _Touch the pencil page. Then walk up the path._
- `a11-01-s2`: Hold the balloon string while Pooh floats up to the bees. · _Stand under the big oak tree. Hold on tight._
- `a11-01-s3`: Help Pooh come down again. Try not to laugh. · _Keep the string steady._

#### `sprout:a11-01-page` · [main](main.md#a11-01-page)

_The atlas page is drawn in soft pencil._  
_Pencil, pencil, whoosh! Now it is a real, green Forest._  
_A boy comes up the path. He has a bear under his arm._  
_The bear is upside down. He does not mind a bit._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): compare two things: bigger and smaller  
> _(Point to the boy. Point to the bear.) Who is bigger?_  
> - ✅ The boy → Yes! The boy is bigger. The bear is smaller.
> - The bear → Look again. The boy is taller. The bear is smaller.
> _For grown-ups: Compared two characters by size: bigger and smaller._

**Christopher Robin**: Hello! This is Pooh. He came down the stairs on his head.  
**Pooh**: Hello. Is that a Heffalump?  
**Christopher Robin**: No, Pooh. I think it is a Friend.  
**Pooh**: A very small Heffalump. They start small.  
_Your Nubbin looks behind itself. No Heffalump there._  

**Choices**

- **Tell Pooh it is a Nubbin.**
    - **Pooh**: A Nubbin. That is just what a Heffalump would say.
- **Let your Nubbin decide.**
    - Your Nubbin sits on Pooh’s foot, and Pooh lets it. Nice!
- **Ask what a Heffalump is.**
    - **Christopher Robin**: Nobody knows. That is the best sort of animal.

#### `sprout:a11-01-page-done` · [main](main.md#a11-01-page-done)

**Pooh**: I was going to see about some honey. Come too.  
**Pooh**: Honey is better with someone to watch.  
_Pooh hums as he walks. Hum, hum, hum._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.K.3, notice): listening for the story: who and where  
> _Every story has people in it. We call them characters._  
> _Here, the characters are Pooh and the boy. The place is a Forest._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that stories have characters and a setting._


#### `sprout:a11-01-balloon` · [main](main.md#a11-01-balloon)

_Buzz, buzz! A buzzing comes from high in an oak._  
**Pooh**: A honey buzz! Honey is always at the top of things.  
**Pooh**: Christopher Robin, have you got a balloon?  
_Pooh rolls in mud. Now he is round and black, like a cloud._  
_Your Nubbin rolls in the mud too, just in case._  
**Christopher Robin**: Hold the string. Do not let go unless he says.  
_Up goes Pooh, past the branches, up to the bees._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.G.A.1, ask): position words: above and below  
> _(Point up, then down.) Is Pooh above you, or below you?_  
> - ✅ Above → Yes! Pooh is above you, up high. You are below, holding on.
> - Below → Look up! Pooh is above you, up high. You are below him.
> _For grown-ups: Used the position words above and below._


#### `sprout:a11-01-balloon-done` · [main](main.md#a11-01-balloon-done)

_The string tugs in your hands. Tug, tug._  
**Pooh**: Christopher Robin? These bees look at me very strangely.  
_Bzzz. The bees buzz round and round._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, notice): bees live in a nest and make honey  
> _Bees live together in a nest. They make honey from flower juice._  
> _The honey is their food. Pooh wants some too!_  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that bees live in a nest and make honey for food._


#### `sprout:a11-01-bees` · [main](main.md#a11-01-bees)

**Pooh**: Walk up and down with your umbrella. Say it looks like rain.  
_Christopher Robin walks up and down. Nobody is fooled._  
**Pooh**: I shall come down now. But how?  
**Pooh**: Pop the balloon with your popgun. Gently.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): staying kind when something is funny  
> _Pooh looks very serious. What is the kind thing to do?_  
> - ✅ Hold steady and stay quiet → Yes. Pooh feels braver when you stay kind.
> - Laugh at him → Funny things can still hurt feelings. A kind friend waits.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about being kind to a friend who is in a silly, scary spot._

**Christopher Robin**: I might miss.  
**Pooh**: Try not to.  

**Choices**

- **Bite your lip and hold steady.**
    - You hold steady. Pooh looks down, and he is glad.
- **Laugh, a little, into your sleeve.**
    - **Pooh**: I heard that.
    - **Christopher Robin**: It is all right. I laughed too. He forgave me by lunch.

#### `sprout:a11-01-bees-done` · [main](main.md#a11-01-bees-done)

_Pop! goes the popgun. “Ow!” says Pooh._  
**Pooh**: That was not the balloon.  
_Pop! The second one gets the balloon._  
_Pooh floats down like a big, muddy leaf. Whoosh._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, notice): sound words: pop, ow, whoosh  
> _Pop! Ow! Whoosh! These are sound words._  
> _(Say them together. Make the sounds big, then small.)_  
> _For grown-ups: Played with sound words in a story._


#### `sprout:a11-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a11-01-complete)

_Pooh’s arms stay up in the air, like a surprised bear._  
**Christopher Robin**: They will come down in a week. Pooh, blow that fly off.  
_Pooh blows the fly off his nose. He is proud._  
_Christopher Robin ties a blue ribbon on your Nubbin._  
**Christopher Robin**: So everyone knows it is a Friend.  
_A ribbon is tied in a bow. First one loop, then another._  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, notice): a friend is someone who is kind to you  
> **Christopher Robin**: A friend is someone who is kind and glad to see you.  
> **Christopher Robin**: Now you are a Friend of the Forest. A good thing to be.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard what makes a friend: being kind and glad to see someone._


## A11-2. A Tight Place

- `a11-02-s1`: Go visiting with Pooh, to Rabbit’s. · _Follow Pooh along the sandbank to the hole._
- `a11-02-s2`: Fetch Christopher Robin to see the stuck bear. · _Run to his house. Then walk back together._
- `a11-02-s3`: Read to Pooh while he gets thinner. · _Sit by Pooh’s head. Choose a story._

#### `sprout:a11-02-visit` · [main](main.md#a11-02-visit)

_Pooh hums along the sandbank. Hum, hum._  
**Pooh**: A hole! A hole means Rabbit. Rabbit means a little something.  
**Rabbit**: Nobody is in. Go away.  
**Pooh**: Then who said that?  
**Rabbit**: Nobody. I said it for him.  
**Rabbit**: Oh, all right. Come in. Wipe your feet.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): sharing: leave some for others  
> _Pooh ate all of Rabbit’s honey. Sharing means leaving some for others._  
> _A good guest asks, “Is there enough for everyone?”_  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that sharing means leaving some for others._

_Pooh has a little honey, then a little more, then the rest._  

#### `sprout:a11-02-visit-done` · [main](main.md#a11-02-visit-done)

**Rabbit**: Must you go? What a shame.  
_Pooh pushes out of the front door._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): compare: wide and narrow  
> _The door is small. Pooh is round. Is the door wide, or narrow?_  
> - ✅ Narrow → Yes, narrow. And Pooh is wide. That is why he is stuck.
> - Wide → Wide is the other way. This door is narrow, and Pooh is wide.
> _For grown-ups: Used the words wide and narrow to compare a door and a bear._

_His front half gets out. The rest is too full of honey._  
**Pooh**: Oh dear. I seem to have stopped.  

#### `sprout:a11-02-stuck` · [main](main.md#a11-02-stuck)

_Pooh is stuck. His head is out in the sun._  
**Pooh**: I cannot go forwards. I cannot go back.  
**Rabbit**: Somebody ate too much honey. I am only saying.  
_You run for Christopher Robin. He walks back calmly._  
**Christopher Robin**: He will have to stay until he is thin again. A week.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, notice): asking for help when you are stuck  
> _Pooh is stuck. He did not hide it. He told everyone._  
> _When we are stuck, asking for help is the clever thing to do._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that asking for help is a clever thing to do when you are stuck._

**Pooh**: A week! What about meals?  
**Pooh**: Please read to me. Something comforting.  

#### `sprout:a11-02-stuck-done` · [main](main.md#a11-02-stuck-done)

**Christopher Robin**: We will take turns. You read in the mornings.  
**Christopher Robin**: I will read in the afternoons.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Literacy, notice): sharing books by reading aloud  
> _Reading to someone is a lovely gift._  
> _Your voice makes the words come alive, like a friend in the room._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that reading aloud is a kind way to keep someone company._

_Pooh sniffs. Then he smiles a very small smile._  

#### `sprout:a11-02-week` · [main](main.md#a11-02-week)

_So you sit on the sandbank. You read to Pooh._  
_Inside the hole, Rabbit hangs his washing on Pooh’s back legs._  
**Rabbit**: It is a waste of good legs otherwise.  
**Pooh**: I do not mind. What is on them?  
**Rabbit**: Tea towels.  
_Your Nubbin gives Pooh an acorn to look at._  
**Pooh**: What are we reading today?  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.A.1, do): put the days of the week in order, to 7  
> _A week has seven days. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven._  
> _Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday._  
> _(Say them together. Count one on your fingers for each.)_  
> Check: `edu:order:days-of-week`
> _For grown-ups: Said the seven days of the week in order and counted them._


**Choices**

- **A story about a bear who was exactly the right size.**
    - **Pooh**: I like him. Does he get stuck?
    - **You**: Never.
    - **Pooh**: Lucky bear.
- **A book about the sea, where there are no doors.**
    - **Pooh**: No doors? That sounds very comfortable.
- **Just sit with him. Do not read.**
    - You sit by his head. You say nothing at all.
    - **Pooh**: That is the most comforting one so far.

#### `sprout:a11-02-week-done` · [main](main.md#a11-02-week-done)

_On the seventh day, Christopher Robin takes Pooh’s paws._  
_Rabbit takes Christopher Robin. Rabbit’s family takes Rabbit._  
_You are at the end. Your Nubbin takes you._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): pulling together is stronger than pulling alone  
> _One friend pulling is not enough. A long line of friends is much stronger._  
> _When we all pull the same way, big things can move._  
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that many friends pulling together can move something heavy._

**Christopher Robin**: Pull! Pull! Pull!  
_Pop! Out comes Pooh, and everybody sits down at once._  

#### `sprout:a11-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a11-02-complete)

**Pooh**: Thank you all. Especially the one at the end.  
_Pooh walks off humming. He is thinner now._  
**Rabbit**: He will be back. I need a wider door.  
_Rabbit looks at the empty hall. He almost misses the legs._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, notice): a good friend waits with you  
> _For a whole week, friends sat and waited with Pooh._  
> _Waiting with someone is a way of saying, I care about you._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that sitting and waiting with a friend shows you care._


## A11-3. The Woozle

- `a11-03-s1`: Follow Pooh round the trees after the mystery tracks. · _Walk round the spinney in the snow. Do not tell the secret yet._
- `a11-03-s2`: Watch from the oak with Christopher Robin. · _Climb the big oak and keep quiet._

#### `sprout:a11-03-tracks` · [main](main.md#a11-03-tracks)

_It is a snowy day. Piglet sweeps snow off his step._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-ESS2-3, notice): snow is frozen water  
> _Snow is water that has frozen into tiny, soft crystals._  
> _When the sun comes out, snow melts back into water. Drip, drip._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that snow is frozen water and melts when it warms up._

_Pooh goes past, round and round in a circle._  
**Piglet**: Pooh, why do you go round and round?  
**Pooh**: I am following something. Paw prints!  
**Piglet**: A Woozle?  
**Pooh**: It might be. Woozles are hard to tell from the front.  
_Your Nubbin puts its feet carefully in the snow._  

**Choices**

- **Tell them the little prints are your Nubbin’s.**
    - **Pooh**: Yes, yes. Do keep up.
    - Nobody listens. They are too busy tracking.
- **Say nothing. Let it play out.**
    - You say nothing. It feels like carrying a full cup.
- **Let your Nubbin go round once more.**
    - Round goes your Nubbin. Now there is one more set of prints.

#### `sprout:a11-03-tracks-done` · [main](main.md#a11-03-tracks-done)

_Round they go. Round and round._  
_Every time, there are more tracks._  
_(Point to the footprints. Count them: one, two, three.)_  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count footprints to 3  
> _Pooh’s prints. Piglet’s prints. Your Nubbin’s tiny prints._  
> _One, two, three sets. Each friend makes their own._  
> Check: `edu:count:footprints:3`
> _For grown-ups: Counted three sets of footprints, one for each walker._


#### `sprout:a11-03-count` · [main](main.md#a11-03-count)

**Pooh**: Look! Two Woozles now. And one little one.  
**Piglet**: I just remembered something I meant to do. Bye!  
_Up in the oak, Christopher Robin sits very still._  
**Christopher Robin**: Do not say anything. It is better when he finds out himself.  
_Pooh puts his foot in a print. It fits!_  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): testing an idea: does my foot fit the print?  
> _Pooh tries his foot in the print. How does he find out?_  
> - ✅ He tries it and sees if it fits → Yes! Trying it is a clever way to find out.
> - He guesses → Guessing is a start. Trying his foot is how he really finds out.
> _For grown-ups: Saw that testing an idea (does the foot fit?) is a way of finding out._

**Pooh**: Oh.  
_Piglet looks at your Nubbin’s feet. He goes pink._  

#### `sprout:a11-03-count-done` · [main](main.md#a11-03-count-done)

_Christopher Robin climbs down from the tree._  
**Christopher Robin**: Oh, Pooh. Whose footprints do you think those are?  
**Pooh**: My own. I have been following my own feet.  
**Christopher Robin**: There is no better bear anywhere.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): feeling silly is fine; friends are kind  
> _Pooh felt a little silly. Everybody feels silly sometimes._  
> _Christopher Robin did not laugh at him. He said something kind._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that feeling silly is ordinary and kind friends stay kind._


#### `sprout:a11-03-complete` · [main](main.md#a11-03-complete)

**Pooh**: Being silly is hungry work. Is it lunch?  
_Pooh and Christopher Robin go home._  
_Piglet walks a long way round your Nubbin. Then a shorter way._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): compare: a long way and a short way  
> _First Piglet goes a long way round. Then a short way. Which is shorter?_  
> - ✅ The second way → Yes! The second way is shorter. Piglet is less worried now.
> - The first way → The first way was long. The second way is shorter, and closer.
> _For grown-ups: Compared a long path and a short path, and saw Piglet grow less worried._

**Piglet**: I knew it was not a Woozle. Mostly.  
_Your Nubbin holds out a paw. Piglet shakes it, twice._  

## A11-4. Eeyore’s Tail

- `a11-04-s1`: Find out what is wrong with Eeyore. · _He is in the thistly corner of the Forest._
- `a11-04-s2`: Go with Pooh to ask Owl. · _Owl lives high in a tree, behind a grand door._
- `a11-04-s3`: Hold the tail straight while Christopher Robin nails it on. · _Carry the tail back. Hold it where Christopher Robin points._

#### `sprout:a11-04-gloomy` · [main](main.md#a11-04-gloomy)

_Eeyore is a grey donkey. He stands in the thistles, all by himself._  
**Pooh**: Good morning, Eeyore.  
**Eeyore**: Good morning. If it is. Which I doubt.  
**Pooh**: What is the matter?  
**Eeyore**: Nothing. Nobody minds.  
_Pooh walks round Eeyore. Then round again._  
**Pooh**: Eeyore! Where is your tail?  
**Eeyore**: It is gone. That explains a great deal.  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, ask): how a sad friend feels, and what helps  
> _Eeyore is sad. What could help him?_  
> - ✅ Sit with him and help look → Thank you. That helps a bit.
> - Tell him not to be sad → Feelings do not just go away. Sitting with him helps more.
> - Walk away → Eeyore is lonely. Staying close helps more.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about being with a sad friend and helping._


**Choices**

- **Promise you will help find it.**
    - **Eeyore**: Kind of you. Do not trouble. Somebody is using it, I expect.
- **Sit with him for a moment first.**
    - You sit in the prickly thistles beside him.
    - Eeyore notices, and does not say so. That is his thank you.
- **Look behind the thistles.**
    - Your Nubbin looks behind the thistles and under a stone. No tail.

#### `sprout:a11-04-gloomy-done` · [main](main.md#a11-04-gloomy-done)

**Pooh**: I shall find your tail, Eeyore. Me. Pooh.  
**Eeyore**: Thank you, Pooh. You are a real friend.  
_Eeyore’s ears lift, just a little._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-1, notice): thistles are prickly to keep animals off  
> _Thistles have sharp prickles all over._  
> _The prickles help the plant. Most animals do not want to nibble it._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that thistle prickles help protect the plant._


#### `sprout:a11-04-owl` · [main](main.md#a11-04-owl)

_Owl lives high in a tree, behind a grand door._  
_There are two signs by the door. They are spelled in Owl’s own way._  
**Owl**: Ah, Pooh. How do you do?  
**Pooh**: Eeyore has lost his tail. How do I find it?  
**Owl**: There is a Proper Way. First, one Puts Up a Notice.  
**Pooh**: I am only a small bear. Big words make my head go fuzzy.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.2.4, ask): asking what a word means  
> _Pooh does not know the big words. What can he do?_  
> - ✅ Ask Owl what the word means → Quite right. “Notice” just means a sign.
> - Pretend he knows → Asking is brave and clever. Owl is happy to explain.
> _For grown-ups: Saw that asking what a new word means is a good thing to do._

**Owl**: A Notice is a sign. One writes on it and pins it up.  
**Owl**: Usually I do the writing.  
_Your Nubbin is not listening. It stares at the bell-pull._  

#### `sprout:a11-04-owl-done` · [main](main.md#a11-04-owl-done)

**Pooh**: Owl, that bell-pull. Where did you get it?  
**Owl**: I found it on a bush. It came away in my wing.  
**Pooh**: It is Eeyore’s! It is his tail!  
_(Point to the grey tail with a tuft at the end.)_  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, notice): looking closely finds things  
> _Your Nubbin did not listen to the long words. It looked closely._  
> _Looking carefully is how scientists find answers._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that looking closely helps us find answers._


#### `sprout:a11-04-nail` · [main](main.md#a11-04-nail)

_You carry the tail carefully, as if it might wake._  
_Christopher Robin has a little hammer and one tiny nail._  
**Christopher Robin**: Hold it straight. There. Eeyore, this will not hurt.  
**Eeyore**: Do not mind me.  
_Tap, tap. Eeyore does not flinch, but your Nubbin does, twice._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count the taps: 2  
> _Tap, tap. (Tap the table with me.)_  
> _One tap, two taps. The tail is on tight._  
> Check: `edu:count:taps:2`
> _For grown-ups: Counted two taps of the hammer while a tail was fixed on._

**Eeyore**: Hm. It works. Not that anyone asked.  
_Swish, swish! Eeyore swishes his tail, and he is happy._  

#### `sprout:a11-04-nail-done` · [main](main.md#a11-04-nail-done)

**Eeyore**: Thank you, Pooh. And the one with the bow.  
_Eeyore swishes his tail. Swish._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, notice): sound words: swish  
> _Swish is a sound word. It sounds like what it does._  
> _(Swish your arm like a tail: swish, swish!)_  
> _For grown-ups: Heard and acted out the sound word swish._

_He is glad. He would rather you did not mention it._  

#### `sprout:a11-04-complete` · [main](main.md#a11-04-complete)

_Pooh walks home. He feels he has done a Good Deed._  
_He is ready for a little something by eleven o’clock._  
**Pooh**: Owl knows ever such a lot. But your Nubbin looked at the bell-pull.  
_Your Nubbin looks proud._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): everyone is good at something different  
> _Owl is good at long words. Your Nubbin is good at looking._  
> _Everybody is good at something. It all helps._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that everyone has different things they are good at._


## A11-5. The Very Deep Pit

- `a11-05-s1`: Help Pooh and Piglet dig a deep pit by the Six Pine Trees. · _Dig where Pooh points._
- `a11-05-s2`: Keep the lantern steady while Pooh checks the honey. · _Walk with Pooh and hold the lantern still._
- `a11-05-s3`: Let Piglet meet the “Heffalump”. Do not explain. · _Stay by the pit with the lantern._

#### `sprout:a11-05-dig` · [main](main.md#a11-05-dig)

**Christopher Robin**: I saw a Heffalump today. I think.  
**Piglet**: A Heffalump! Pooh, what do we do?  
**Pooh**: We catch it in a Very Deep Pit, by the Six Pine Trees.  
_Piglet digs and Pooh watches. You do most of it._  
_Your Nubbin moves one pebble a very long way._  
**Piglet**: What do Heffalumps like?  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count the pine trees: 6  
> _The Six Pine Trees stand in a row. (Point and count them.)_  
> _One, two, three, four, five, six. Six pine trees!_  
> Check: `edu:count:pine-trees:6`
> _For grown-ups: Counted six pine trees by pointing at each one._

**Pooh**: Honey.  

**Choices**

- **Ask what they will do once it is caught.**
    - **Pooh**: Be very polite to it, I expect.
    - **Piglet**: Oh, d-dear.
- **Dig a little step in one side, in case.**
    - **Piglet**: A step? For the Heffalump?
    - **You**: For anyone who falls in by mistake.
    - **Pooh**: Very sensible.
- **Check it is deep enough.**
    - You climb in. It comes up to your chin!

#### `sprout:a11-05-dig-done` · [main](main.md#a11-05-dig-done)

_Pooh fetches his very last jar of honey._  
_He lowers it into the pit. Down, down, down._  
_He looks at it for a long time._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, notice): honey keeps for a very long time  
> _Honey is made by bees, and it keeps for a very long time._  
> _That is why Pooh’s last jar will still be good tomorrow._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that honey keeps for a very long time._


#### `sprout:a11-05-night` · [main](main.md#a11-05-night)

_That night Pooh cannot sleep._  
**Pooh**: I will see if the honey is still there. Hold the lantern?  
_You walk with the lantern. Your Nubbin stays in the light._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.G.A.1, ask): position words: inside and outside  
> _Your Nubbin stays in the circle of light. Is it inside, or outside?_  
> - ✅ Inside → Yes! Inside the light. Outside, it is dark.
> - Outside → Look again. It stays inside the light, where it can see.
> _For grown-ups: Used the position words inside and outside._

_Pooh climbs down into the pit and licks the honey._  
**Pooh**: Mmff. Mmff mff.  
_The jar is stuck on Pooh’s head!_  

**Choices**

- **Hold the lantern steady and wait.**
    - You hold it steady. It trembles once, because you giggle.
- **Try to pull the jar off.**
    - It will not come. This will have to be a morning job.

#### `sprout:a11-05-night-done` · [main](main.md#a11-05-night-done)

_Pooh climbs out somehow._  
_He wanders off in the grey morning, with the jar on his head._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, ask): sounds are muffled inside a jar  
> _Pooh talks inside the jar. Do his words sound loud and clear?_  
> - ✅ No, they sound muffled and boomy → Yes! A jar changes how a voice sounds.
> - Yes, loud and clear → Not quite. In a jar the voice sounds muffled and boomy.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that a voice sounds different inside a jar._

**Pooh**: Mmff mmm mmff.

#### `sprout:a11-20-climb` · [main](main.md#a11-20-climb)

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count a circle: mark where you started

> _For grown-ups: Learned to count a circle by marking where you started._
