## A12-1. The Troll’s Mirror

Andersen, The Snow Queen, 1844); original design), The Troll (The wicked sprite or troll of the first story, voiced by Grandmother in a shadow-show)

### Step 1: Step into the cut-paper town and take the room under the eaves.

Hint: Touch the page, then climb the attic stairs to Grandmother. · check: `explore:a12-attic:stairs`

#### <a id="a12-01-arrive"></a>Scene `a12-01-arrive`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page of cut paper: rooftops, chimneys, and two attic windows with a gutter between them._  
_The paper is so fine it stirs when you breathe. Then it’s a steep wooden stair, and you’re on it, with a bag and your Nubbin._  
**Grandmother**: There you are. The lodger. You’ll want the room under the eaves, and the bed by the chimney.  
**Grandmother**: The other bed has opinions. Mind your head on the beam; everybody hits it, the first week.  
_Grandmother is small and quick, with her spectacles pushed up into white hair._  
_Across the gutter, in the window opposite, two faces appear: a boy and a girl, both trying to see you at once._  
**Kay**: Is that the new lodger? Has it got a dog?  
**Gerda**: That’s not a dog, Kay. Look at its ears.  
_Grandmother looks at your Nubbin over her spectacles._  
**Grandmother** _(think)_: And what’s this? It isn’t a cat. I’ve had cats.  

**Choices**

- **Tell her it’s a Nubbin.**
    - **Grandmother**: A Nubbin. Well, it can have the end of the bed and the heel of the loaf, same as a cat.
- **Say it’s your friend, and it goes where you go.**
    - **Grandmother**: Good. Things that go where you go are worth a bed.
- **Let your Nubbin bow.**
    - Your Nubbin bows, nearly falls down the stairs, and is caught by Grandmother without her looking.
    - **Grandmother**: Manners. It can stay.

#### <a id="a12-01-arrive-done"></a>Done `a12-01-arrive-done`

**Grandmother**: That’s Kay and Gerda. They live across the gutter, one each side, and they’re in here more than they’re out.  
**Grandmother**: Evenings, there’s a story. You can hold the candle. Lodgers always hold the candle.  

### Step 2: Hold the candle for Grandmother’s shadow-show.

Hint: Stand by the attic wall and keep the candle still. · check: `place:a12-candle:attic-wall`

#### <a id="a12-01-show"></a>Scene `a12-01-show`

_It’s late summer, and the light goes about nine. Kay and Gerda come across by the gutter boxes, the way they always do in summer._  
_Grandmother sits with a sheet of black paper and a long pair of scissors, snipping without looking._  
**Grandmother**: Candle a bit higher. There. Now the wall’s a stage, and you’re the sun. Don’t wobble; suns don’t.  
**Kay** _(happy)_: Do the troll one. The troll one’s best.  
**Gerda**: It’s frightening.  
**Kay**: That’s why.  
**Grandmother**: Right. Steady, all of you. This story starts badly and gets better, which is the best sort.  
_A paper troll steps onto the wall, all knees and grin. He holds a mirror, cut so thin the candle shows through it._  
**Grandmother**: He was a wicked troll, the worst there was, and he had made a mirror that was wrong in a particular way.  
**Grandmother**: Anything good or lovely, held up to it, shrank to nearly nothing. Anything mean or ugly got bigger and worse.  
**Grandmother** _(think)_: A green meadow in that glass looked like boiled spinach. A kind thought showed up as a sneer.  
**Kay**: I’d like to look in it.  
**Gerda** _(sad)_: I wouldn’t.  

#### <a id="a12-01-show-done"></a>Done `a12-01-show-done`

_Grandmother does the troll’s voice. It’s lower than you would expect, and very pleased with itself._  
**The Troll** _(happy)_: Ha! Look how small goodness goes! Look how big the rest of it gets! Isn’t it wonderful?  

### Step 3: Watch the troll’s mirror break.

Hint: Keep the candle steady to the end of the show. · check: `watch:a12-shadow-show:mirror`

#### <a id="a12-01-shards"></a>Scene `a12-01-shards`

**Grandmother**: The troll kept a school, if you can believe it, and his pupils carried the mirror all round the world.  
**Grandmother**: Soon there wasn’t a country or a person left that hadn’t been seen in it the wrong way round.  
**Grandmother**: Then they thought they’d fly up to the sky with it, and make fun of the angels too.  
_The paper mirror climbs the wall in little jerks. The higher it goes, the wider its painted grin spreads._  
**Grandmother** _(shock)_: And it grinned so hard it shook, and shook, until it slipped right out of their hands.  
_Grandmother opens her fingers. A handful of paper snips falls through the candlelight like snow._  
**Grandmother**: It broke into more pieces than there are people. Some no bigger than a grain of sand, and they blew everywhere.  
**Grandmother**: Get one in your eye and everything looks wrong. Get one in your heart and it goes hard and cold, like a stone in a frosty field.  
**Kay**: And then what?  
**Grandmother** _(soft)_: Then you need somebody to come and fetch you.  

**Choices**

- **Brush the paper snips off Gerda’s sleeve.**
    - You brush them off. Gerda checks her other sleeve herself, twice, and then Kay’s.
- **Catch a snip as it falls.**
    - You catch one on your palm, and it’s only paper. You look at it anyway, a little longer than you meant to.
- **Ask who comes to fetch you.**
    - **Grandmother**: Whoever loves you most and is too stubborn to stop. That’s usually how it goes.

#### <a id="a12-01-shards-done"></a>Done `a12-01-shards-done`

_The troll laughs on the wall until his paper sides split. Grandmother blows out the candle._  
_In the dark, the draught from the window lifts one snip that isn’t paper. It glints once, and is gone._

## A12-2. Two Windows

*The Snow Queen (H. C. Andersen, 1844), second story: a little boy and a little girl* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Mirror Shard page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A12-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A12-1

**Cast:** Gerda (Gerda (H. C. Andersen, The Snow Queen, 1844); original design), Kay (Kay (H. C. Andersen, The Snow Queen, 1844); original design), Grandmother (Kay and Gerda’s grandmother (The Snow Queen, 1844); original design)

### Step 1: Water the roses in the gutter boxes.

Hint: Climb out to the gutter with the watering can and do the top of the arch. · check: `carry:a12-watering-can:gutter`

#### <a id="a12-02-roses"></a>Scene `a12-02-roses`

_In the morning you see it all properly: two attics with roofs that nearly touch, and a lead gutter running between them._  
_Long ago the two families each put a box across the gutter. Now peas hang over the edges, and in each box grows a rose tree._  
_The roses have climbed into an arch over the gutter. Under it are two little stools, and two children who think it’s the best room in town._  
**Gerda**: You’re tallest. You can do the top of the arch. Don’t drown the peas; they sulk.  
**Kay**: Peas don’t sulk.  
**Gerda**: These ones do.  
**Kay**: Gran says the roses are older than both of us put together. She says they’ll still be here when we’re old.  
_Your Nubbin tries to help by sitting in the watering can._  

**Choices**

- **Water the top of the arch, carefully.**
    - You water the high roses. A few drops roll off the petals onto Kay’s head, and he pretends not to notice, very badly.
- **Give the peas a bit extra, in case.**
    - **Gerda**: See? They’re cheering up already.
    - **Kay**: They look exactly the same.
- **Lift your Nubbin out of the can first.**
    - Your Nubbin comes out of the can dripping and offended. Gerda dries it on her apron, and it forgives everybody.

#### <a id="a12-02-roses-done"></a>Done `a12-02-roses-done`

**Gerda**: In winter we can’t climb across. We have to go down all our stairs and up all the others.  
**Kay**: It takes years.  
**Gerda**: It takes two minutes.  

### Step 2: Warm a coin on the stove and melt a peephole in the frozen window.

Hint: Hold a copper coin on the stove, then press it to the glass. · check: `craft:a12-peephole:window`

#### <a id="a12-02-frost"></a>Scene `a12-02-frost`

_Winter comes, and the windows freeze over with ferns of ice. You can’t see across the gutter at all._  
_Kay and Gerda have a trick for this. They warm copper coins on the stove and press them to the glass._  
_Each coin melts a round peephole, as neat as a button. A bright eye looks through each one, across the snow._  
_Downstairs, Grandmother is watching the snow come down past the window._  
**Grandmother** _(soft)_: Look at the white bees swarming.  
**Kay**: Have they got a queen? Like real bees?  
**Grandmother**: They have. She flies where the swarm is thickest. She’s the biggest of them, and she never stays on the ground for long.  
**Grandmother**: Some nights she flies through the streets and looks in at the windows. That’s when the frost comes up in flowers.  
**Kay** _(happy)_: Let her try. I’d put her on the hot stove, and she’d melt.  

**Choices**

- **Tell Kay that’s no way to treat a guest.**
    - **Kay**: She isn’t a guest. She’s weather.
- **Ask Grandmother if she’s ever seen her.**
    - **Grandmother**: Once or twice. Through the glass. Not close, and that’s how I like it.
- **Look out of your own peephole.**
    - The flakes go past your peephole in their thousands. One of them seems, for a moment, to look back.

#### <a id="a12-02-frost-done"></a>Done `a12-02-frost-done`

_Grandmother smooths Kay’s hair and starts a story about a goose instead. She doesn’t mention the queen again._  

### Step 3: Look out of your window that night.

Hint: Wait at your peephole after the house has gone to bed. · check: `watch:a12-window:night`

#### <a id="a12-02-night"></a>Scene `a12-02-night`

_That night you can’t sleep. Your Nubbin can’t either; it sits on the sill with its nose to your peephole._  
_Across the gutter Kay is up too, half undressed, standing on a chair at his window._  
_A few flakes drift down. One, bigger than the rest, settles on the edge of a flower box, and stays._  
_It grows. It grows into a woman, tall and fine, dressed in white gauze made of a million starry flakes._  
_She is lovely, and she is ice, all of her, dazzling and glittering. And she is alive._  
_Her eyes are like two bright stars, but there is no rest in them at all._  
_She looks in at Kay’s window. She nods to him, and lifts one hand, and beckons._  

**Choices**

- **Knock on your window.**
    - You knock hard on the glass. Kay jumps down off his chair, and the woman turns her head towards you, slowly.
    - Then she isn’t there. Something large and white goes past the window, like a bird.
- **Call Kay’s name.**
    - You call his name across the gutter. Kay startles and jumps down from his chair.
    - When you look again there’s nobody on the box. Something white goes past, like a big bird in the dark.
- **Pull your Nubbin back from the glass.**
    - You lift your Nubbin away from the window. It’s cold right through, and it burrows into your shirt.
    - Kay jumps down off his chair. When you look again a big white bird is passing, and the box is empty.

#### <a id="a12-02-night-done"></a>Done `a12-02-night-done`

_You sit up until morning. The frost on your window has grown into flowers you have never seen in any garden._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a12-02-complete"></a>Complete `a12-02-complete`

**Kay**: It was a dream. I dreamed a lady on the box. That’s all.  
**Gerda** _(think)_: I dreamed you were standing on a chair.  
**Kay**: Well, I wasn’t.  
_Grandmother, cutting the bread, looks at Kay a moment longer than usual._  
_Then the thaw comes, and the swallows, and the roses wake up in the gutter as if nothing at all has happened._

## A12-3. The Splinter

*The Snow Queen (H. C. Andersen, 1844), second story: the splinter in Kay’s eye and heart* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Mirror Shard page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A12-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A12-2

**Cast:** Kay (Kay (H. C. Andersen, The Snow Queen, 1844); original design), Gerda (Gerda (H. C. Andersen, The Snow Queen, 1844); original design), Grandmother (Kay and Gerda’s grandmother (The Snow Queen, 1844); original design)

### Step 1: Sit under the rose arch with Kay and Gerda.

Hint: Climb out to the gutter and take the spare stool. · check: `sit:a12-roses:gutter`

#### <a id="a12-03-arch"></a>Scene `a12-03-arch`

_It’s summer, and the roses have never been so good. Gerda has learned a hymn with roses in it, and sings it all day._  
_She sings it to Kay under the arch, and he sings it back. Your Nubbin hums the tune a beat behind them both._  
_They’re looking at a picture book of animals and birds. Kay is doing the noises, and the noises are terrible._  
**Kay**: That’s an elk. It goes like this.  
**Gerda** _(happy)_: It does not.  
_Across the town, the church clock begins to strike five._  
**Kay** _(shock)_: Ow!  
**Kay** _(sad)_: Something stung me. In my heart. And my eye, something’s gone in my eye.  
_In the low sun you see it: a glint, smaller than a grain of sand, there and gone._  

**Choices**

- **Look in Kay’s eye.**
    - You hold his eyelid gently and look, and there’s nothing there. You are almost sure there’s nothing there.
- **Hold Kay’s hand till it stops.**
    - You hold his hand, and it’s warm. After a moment it isn’t quite as warm, and you tell yourself it is.
- **Call Grandmother.**
    - Grandmother comes up, peers into his eye with her spectacles on, and finds nothing at all.
    - **Grandmother**: Blink. Again. There. Gone.

#### <a id="a12-03-arch-done"></a>Done `a12-03-arch-done`

**Kay**: It’s gone. I think it’s gone.  
_It hasn’t gone._  

### Step 2: Put the rose box straight after Kay kicks it.

Hint: Lift the box back onto the gutter and press the earth round the roots. · check: `place:a12-rose-box:gutter`

#### <a id="a12-03-kick"></a>Scene `a12-03-kick`

**Gerda** _(sad)_: Kay? Are you all right?  
**Kay** _(angry)_: Why are you crying? It makes you look ugly.  
**Kay**: There’s nothing the matter with me. Ugh. That rose has a worm in it. And that one’s all crooked.  
**Kay**: They’re ugly roses. They match the ugly boxes.  
_He kicks the box. It skids along the gutter, and he snaps off two roses and drops them over the edge._  
_You reach for him, and then you understand what you saw._  
_You can’t take a splinter out of a heart. Not with your hands, and not with anything you own._  
_Kay goes in and slams his window. Gerda stands in the gutter with her picture book held against her chest._  
**Gerda** _(sad)_: He said it was a book for babies.  

**Choices**

- **Fetch the two roses back for Gerda.**
    - You lean over the gutter’s edge and fish them back. Gerda puts them in a cup of water on her sill.
- **Knock on Kay’s window.**
    - You knock. Kay opens it, looks at you, and does a perfect, horrible impression of your face.
    - Then he shuts it again.
- **Say nothing. Straighten the box together.**
    - You and Gerda lift the box back into place without a word. Your Nubbin pats the earth flat round the roots.

#### <a id="a12-03-kick-done"></a>Done `a12-03-kick-done`

_The rose tree has lost two flowers and a bit of bark. Roses forgive that sort of thing, if they’re left alone._  

### Step 3: Go out in the snow when Kay calls you to see his glass.

Hint: Follow Gerda down to the street and look where Kay shows you. · check: `watch:a12-kay:burning-glass`

#### <a id="a12-03-glass"></a>Scene `a12-03-glass`

_All that autumn Kay is clever and cruel. He puts on Grandmother’s spectacles and does her voice, and the whole street laughs._  
_He can do anybody. He does Gerda best of all, and he only does the bits that hurt._  
**Grandmother** _(sad)_: He’s a sharp lad. Too sharp, lately.  
_When the snow comes he runs out with a big burning-glass, and holds out the blue tail of his coat to catch the flakes._  
**Kay** _(happy)_: Look in the glass, Gerda. Look how perfect they are.  
_Under the glass each flake is a star, sharp and exact, every arm the same as every other._  
**Kay**: Much better than real flowers. Not a single thing wrong with them. If only they didn’t melt.  
**Gerda**: Real flowers smell of something.  
**Kay**: That’s what’s wrong with them.  

**Choices**

- **Look in the glass.**
    - You look. The flake is beautiful and perfect, and you feel nothing at all looking at it, which frightens you.
- **Throw a snowball at Kay.**
    - It gets him on the shoulder. For a second he almost laughs like he used to, and then he brushes it off and looks bored.
- **Stand by Gerda.**
    - You stand next to Gerda, and neither of you says anything. Your Nubbin leans on her boot.

#### <a id="a12-03-glass-done"></a>Done `a12-03-glass-done`

**Kay**: I’m allowed in the big square tomorrow, with the boys who go sledging. Gran said.  
**Gerda** _(think)_: Gran said maybe.

#### <a id="a12-04-square"></a>Scene `a12-04-square`

_Kay comes past with big gloves on and his sledge on his back._  
**Kay**: I’m allowed in the big square. I’m going.  
**Gerda** _(sad)_: Go with him. Please. Just keep an eye on him. He won’t let me.  
_The square is full of noise. The boldest boys tie their sledges to farmers’ carts and get pulled a good way for nothing._  
_Then a great sledge comes in, painted white. Somebody sits in it wrapped in a rough white fur, with a white cap on._  
_It drives round the square. Every boy watches it, and the white driver watches only Kay._  

**Choices**

- **Shout to Kay to stay put.**
    - **Kay**: Mind your own business, lodger!
    - He doesn’t even look round. He’s watching the white sledge.
- **Push through to get hold of his cord.**
    - You push through the crowd towards him. There are too many boys and too many sledges, and the square is suddenly very wide.
- **Pick up your Nubbin. It’s gone very still.**
    - You pick it up. It’s staring at the white sledge, and it’s cold to the touch, the way it was at the window.

#### <a id="a12-04-square-done"></a>Done `a12-04-square-done`

_Kay has his cord in his hands. He’s tying a careful knot, with his tongue between his teeth, the way he does sums._  

### Step 2: Run after the white sledge.

Hint: Run! Keep after it through the town gate. · check: `race:a12-sledge:town-gate`

#### <a id="a12-04-chase"></a>Scene `a12-04-chase`

_As the white sledge comes round again, Kay ties his little sledge to it, and off he goes._  
_Faster, into the next street, and faster. The driver turns and nods to Kay, kindly, as if they’ve known each other for years._  
_Every time Kay reaches to untie the cord, the driver nods again. Every time, Kay sits still._  
_You run. Your Nubbin runs ahead of you, and then you scoop it up and run faster._  
_The sledges go out through the town gate. The snow comes down so thick that you can’t see your own hand._  
**Kay** _(shock)_: Stop! Stop! I want to get off!  
_He tries to say his prayers, and all that comes is his times tables._  
_You lose them. Then the snow parts for a moment, and you see them from a rise: the sledge has stopped._  

#### <a id="a12-04-chase-done"></a>Done `a12-04-chase-done`

_The driver stands up. The fur and the cap are all snow, and the driver is a lady, tall and slender and glittering white._  
_It’s the woman from the window. It’s the Snow Queen._  

### Step 3: Watch from the rise.

Hint: Stay on the rise. You can’t reach them from here. · check: `watch:a12-queen:rise`

#### <a id="a12-04-kiss"></a>Scene `a12-04-kiss`

**The Snow Queen**: We’ve come a long way. But you’re shivering. Creep in under my bearskin.  
_She sits Kay beside her and wraps the fur round him. He looks as if he’s sinking into a snowdrift._  
**The Snow Queen**: Are you still cold?  
_She kisses his forehead. It’s colder than ice, and it goes straight to his heart, which is nearly ice already._  
_For a moment he looks as if it will be the end of him. Then he looks perfectly well, and he doesn’t feel the cold at all._  
**Kay**: My sledge! Don’t forget my sledge.  
_It’s tied to one of the white hens that fly behind her. She kisses his forehead once more, and you watch him forget._  
_Gerda, Grandmother, the roses, the attic: you see them go out of his face like lamps going out down a street._  
_Then the sledge rises over the forest. The wolves howl, and the moon comes out huge and white._  

**Choices**

- **Call his name, as loud as you can.**
    - You shout his name. The wind takes it the other way, back towards the town, as if it means to deliver it to Gerda.
- **Stand and watch until they’re gone.**
    - You watch until the sledge is a speck, and then a star, and then nothing.

#### <a id="a12-04-kiss-done"></a>Done `a12-04-kiss-done`

_You walk back in the dark. The town gate is still open, and nobody has noticed anything at all._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a12-04-complete"></a>Complete `a12-04-complete`

_Gerda is waiting at the top of the stairs. You tell her everything: the white sledge, the lady, the cord._
