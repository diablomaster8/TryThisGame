# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Atlas, page A12: The Mirror Shard

## A12-1. The Troll’s Mirror

- `a12-01-s1`: Climb the stairs and meet Grandmother. · _Touch the page. Then go up the attic stairs._
- `a12-01-s2`: Hold the candle for Grandmother’s shadow-show. · _Stand by the wall. Keep the candle still._
- `a12-01-s3`: Watch the troll’s mirror break. · _Keep the candle steady to the end of the show._

#### `sprout:a12-01-arrive` · [main](main.md#a12-01-arrive)

_Nova’s atlas opens at a page made of cut paper._  
_Rooftops! Chimneys! Two attic windows, side by side._  
_You climb a steep wooden stair with your Nubbin._  
**Grandmother**: There you are, lodger. Your bed is by the chimney.  
**Grandmother**: The other bed creaks. It grumbles all night.  
**Grandmother**: Mind your head on the beam. Everyone bumps it.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.G.A.1, ask): above and below  
> _Where is the beam? Above your head, or under your feet?_  
> - ✅ Above my head → Yes! Look up. The beam is above you.
> - Under my feet → Look up! The beam is above your head. Mind it.
> _For grown-ups: Practised the position words above and below._

_Across the gutter, a boy and a girl wave. They are Kay and Gerda._  
**Grandmother**: And what is this? It is not a cat.  

**Choices**

- **Tell her it’s a Nubbin.**
    - **Grandmother**: A Nubbin! It can have the end of the bed.
- **Say it’s your friend.**
    - **Grandmother**: Good. Friends are worth a bed.
- **Let your Nubbin bow.**
    - Your Nubbin bows. Boing! It nearly tumbles down.
    - **Grandmother**: Manners. It can stay.

#### `sprout:a12-01-arrive-done` · [main](main.md#a12-01-arrive-done)

**Grandmother**: That is Kay and Gerda. They live across the gutter.  
**Grandmother**: Each evening there is a story. You hold the candle.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): saying yes to helping  
> _Grandmother asks you to hold the candle. What do you say?_  
> - ✅ Yes, I will help! → Thank you. Helping is a kind thing to do.
> - No, that is boring. → It might be fun! Helping makes a story go well.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about saying yes when someone asks for help._


#### `sprout:a12-01-show` · [main](main.md#a12-01-show)

_Late summer. Kay and Gerda climb across the gutter._  
_Grandmother has black paper and long scissors. Snip, snip, snip!_  
**Grandmother**: Hold the candle high. Now the wall is a stage.  
_A paper troll steps onto the wall. He holds a thin mirror._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-2, ask): shadows need light  
> _The troll is a shadow on the wall. What makes a shadow?_  
> - ✅ Paper that blocks the candle light → Yes! The paper blocks the light. That makes a dark shape.
> - Nothing at all → Something blocks the light. Try it: hold your hand in front of a lamp.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a shadow is made when something blocks light._

**Grandmother**: His mirror made good things look small.  
**Grandmother**: And it made ugly things look big.  
**Kay**: I’d like to look in it.  
**Gerda**: I wouldn’t.  

#### `sprout:a12-01-show-done` · [main](main.md#a12-01-show-done)

_Grandmother does the troll’s voice. It is deep and pleased._  
**The Troll**: Ha! See how small good things look in my mirror!  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, ask): words for feelings  
> _How does the troll feel? Pleased, or sad?_  
> - ✅ Pleased → Yes. He is pleased with his wicked mirror.
> - Sad → Listen to his big laugh. He is pleased, not sad.
> _For grown-ups: Named a character’s feeling from the way he sounded._


#### `sprout:a12-01-shards` · [main](main.md#a12-01-shards)

**Grandmother**: The troll had a school. His pupils took the mirror everywhere.  
**Grandmother**: Then it slipped from their hands. Crash!  
_Grandmother opens her fingers. Paper snips fall like snow._  
**Grandmother**: It broke into more pieces than people. Some were as small as sand.  
**Grandmother**: A piece in your eye makes things look wrong.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): comparing sizes  
> _Which is smaller: a grain of sand, or a big snip?_  
> - ✅ A grain of sand → Yes! A grain of sand is tiny, tinier than any snip.
> - A big snip → A big snip is bigger. The grain of sand is the smaller one.
> _For grown-ups: Compared two sizes: smaller and bigger._

**Grandmother**: A piece in your heart makes it cold.  
**Kay**: And then what?  
**Grandmother**: Then you need someone to come and fetch you.  

**Choices**

- **Brush the snips off Gerda’s sleeve.**
    - You brush them off. Gerda checks her other sleeve, twice.
- **Catch a snip as it falls.**
    - You catch one. It is only paper. You look at it a long time.
- **Ask who comes to fetch you.**
    - **Grandmother**: Whoever loves you most. They are stubborn about it.

#### `sprout:a12-01-shards-done` · [main](main.md#a12-01-shards-done)

_The troll laughs on the wall. Grandmother blows out the candle._  
_In the dark, something glints once. Then it is gone._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-2, notice): no light, no shadows  
> **Grandmother**: Whoosh, the candle is out. No light means no shadows.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that we need light to see, and that shadows need light too._


#### `sprout:a12-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a12-01-complete)

_Later you lie in the bed by the chimney._  
_The other bed goes creak, creak. It grumbles, just as Grandmother said._  
_Two small rose trees bloom in the dark across the gutter._  
_Your Nubbin curls up on your feet._  
_Rain begins to fall. Pitter, patter, pitter, patter._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): clap the syllables  
> _(Clap along: pit, ter, pat, ter. Four claps!)_  
> Check: `edu:clap:syllables:pitter-patter`
> _For grown-ups: Clapped the parts of the words pitter-patter._


#### `sprout:a12-01-ember-forgiven` · [main](main.md#a12-01-ember-forgiven)

_Variant of `a12-01-complete`._

_Your Nubbin carries a little lantern. In it is Cinder’s ember, small and red._  
_It burns steady. Grandmother holds her hands out to it._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): a fire gives heat and light  
> _A little fire gives two things. Which two?_  
> - ✅ Warmth and light → Yes! A fire gives warmth and light.
> - Ice and snow → Ice and snow are cold! A fire gives warmth and light.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a fire gives warmth and light._

**Grandmother**: Now that’s a good little fire. Bring it up; the eaves get cold.  

#### `sprout:a12-01-ember-remembered` · [main](main.md#a12-01-ember-remembered)

_Variant of `a12-01-complete`._

_Your Nubbin carries a little lantern. In it is Cinder’s ember, small and red._  
_It flickers now and then, as if remembering. It never goes out._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): flames flicker when air moves  
> **Grandmother**: A flame flickers when air moves. A lantern keeps it safe.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that flames flicker when air moves._

**Grandmother**: That one has a story in it. Bring it up; the eaves get cold.  

## A12-2. Two Windows

- `a12-02-s1`: Water the roses in the gutter boxes. · _Climb out to the gutter with the watering can._
- `a12-02-s2`: Warm a coin and melt a peephole in the frosty window. · _Warm a coin on the stove. Press it on the glass._
- `a12-02-s3`: Look out of your window that night. · _Wait quietly at your peephole._

#### `sprout:a12-02-roses` · [main](main.md#a12-02-roses)

_In the morning you see two attics. Their roofs nearly touch._  
_A gutter runs between them. Peas hang over the boxes._  
_In each box grows a rose tree. Together they make an arch._  
**Gerda**: You’re tallest. Water the top of the arch.  
**Gerda**: Not too much water! The peas sulk.  
**Kay**: Peas don’t sulk.  
**Gerda**: These ones do. Look. Sulky peas.  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, ask): what plants need  
> _What do roses need to grow?_  
> - ✅ Water and sunshine → Yes! Water and sunshine make roses grow.
> - Only sweets → Roses like water and sunshine. Not sweets!
> _For grown-ups: Learned that plants need water and light to live._

**Kay**: Gran says the roses will last until we’re old.  
_Your Nubbin tries to help. It sits in the watering can._  

**Choices**

- **Water the top of the arch.**
    - Drip, drip! A drop falls on Kay’s head. He pretends not to notice.
- **Give the peas a bit extra.**
    - **Gerda**: See? They are cheering up.
    - **Kay**: They look the same.
- **Lift your Nubbin out of the can.**
    - Your Nubbin comes out dripping. Gerda dries it. All is forgiven.

#### `sprout:a12-02-roses-done` · [main](main.md#a12-02-roses-done)

**Gerda**: In winter we can’t climb across.  
**Gerda**: We go down all our stairs and up all the others.  
**Kay**: It takes years!  
**Gerda**: It takes two minutes.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): longer and shorter  
> _Which way is shorter? Across the gutter, or down all the stairs?_  
> - ✅ Across the gutter → Yes! The gutter is the short way. It takes no time.
> - Down all the stairs → The stairs are the long way. The gutter is shorter.
> _For grown-ups: Compared a short path with a long path._


#### `sprout:a12-02-frost` · [main](main.md#a12-02-frost)

_Winter comes. Ice ferns grow on the windows._  
_Kay and Gerda warm copper coins on the stove._  
_They press a coin on the glass. The ice melts into a round hole._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-4, ask): warm things melt ice  
> _What melts the ice on the window?_  
> - ✅ A warm coin → Yes! Warm things melt ice.
> - A cold spoon → A cold spoon keeps ice icy. A warm coin melts it.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that something warm melts ice._

_Through the hole, a bright eye looks across the snow._  
**Grandmother**: Look at the white bees swarming in the snow.  
**Kay**: Do they have a queen?  
**Grandmother**: They do. Some nights she looks in at windows.  
**Kay**: I’d put her on the hot stove! She would melt.  

**Choices**

- **Tell Kay that’s unkind to a guest.**
    - **Kay**: She isn’t a guest. She’s weather.
- **Ask Grandmother if she has seen her.**
    - **Grandmother**: Once or twice, through the glass. Not close.
- **Look out of your own peephole.**
    - Snowflakes whirl past by the thousand. One seems to look back.

#### `sprout:a12-02-frost-done` · [main](main.md#a12-02-frost-done)

_Grandmother smooths Kay’s hair._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): kind wishes  
> _Kay wished the queen would melt. Is that a kind wish?_  
> - ✅ No, it isn’t kind → Right. Unkind wishes make hearts cold. Kind words keep them warm.
> - Yes, it is kind → Hmm. Wishing someone harm is not kind. Kind words keep hearts warm.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about why a kind wish is better than an unkind one._

_She tells a story about a goose instead._  

#### `sprout:a12-02-night` · [main](main.md#a12-02-night)

_That night you can’t sleep. Your Nubbin looks out the peephole._  
_Across the gutter, Kay stands on a chair at his window._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-ESS2-1, ask): seasons and weather  
> _Snow is falling. Which season is it? Summer, or winter?_  
> - ✅ Winter → Yes! Snow comes in the cold winter.
> - Summer → Summer is warm. Snow comes in winter.
> _For grown-ups: Linked snow to winter weather._

_A big snowflake lands on the flower box. It grows!_  
_It grows into a tall lady made of ice and starlight._  
_She is lovely and cold. Her eyes shine like stars._  
_She looks in at Kay. She waves her hand. Come!_  

**Choices**

- **Knock on your window.**
    - You knock hard. Kay jumps down.
    - The lady is gone. A big white bird flies past.
- **Call Kay’s name.**
    - You call his name. Kay jumps down.
    - The box is empty. Something white flies by, like a big bird.
- **Pull your Nubbin back from the glass.**
    - Your Nubbin is cold right through. It snuggles into your shirt.
    - Kay jumps down. A white bird passes. The box is empty.

#### `sprout:a12-02-night-done` · [main](main.md#a12-02-night-done)

_You sit up until morning._  
_The frost on your window has grown into ferns._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-ESS2-3, notice): frost is ice  
> **Grandmother**: Frost is ice. Water from the air freezes on the cold glass.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that frost is ice that grows on very cold glass._


#### `sprout:a12-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a12-02-complete)

**Kay**: It was a dream. I dreamed a lady on the box.  
**Gerda**: I dreamed you stood on a chair.  
**Kay**: Well, I wasn’t.  
_Grandmother looks at Kay a little longer than usual._  
_Then the thaw comes. The swallows come back. The roses wake up._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-ESS2-1, ask): spring follows winter  
> _The swallows are back and the snow has melted. Which season is it?_  
> - ✅ Spring → Yes! After winter comes spring.
> - Winter → Winter is cold and snowy. Melting snow and swallows mean spring.
> _For grown-ups: Named spring as the season that follows winter._


## A12-3. The Splinter

- `a12-03-s1`: Sit under the rose arch with Kay and Gerda. · _Climb to the gutter. Take the spare stool._
- `a12-03-s2`: Put the rose box straight again. · _Lift the box. Press the earth round the roots._
- `a12-03-s3`: Go out in the snow with Kay. · _Follow Gerda down to the street._

#### `sprout:a12-03-arch` · [main](main.md#a12-03-arch)

_It is summer. The roses are the best ever._  
_Gerda sings a rose hymn. Kay sings it back._  
_They look at a picture book of animals. Kay does the noises._  
**Kay**: That is an elk. It goes like this.  
**Gerda**: It does not!  
_The church clock begins to strike five._  
**Kay**: Ow! Something stung my heart. And my eye!  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, ask): count the bongs  
> _Bong, bong, bong, bong, bong. How many bongs did the clock make?_  
> - ✅ Five → Yes! One, two, three, four, five. Five o’clock!
> - Three → Let’s count again: one, two, three, four, five. Five bongs.
> _For grown-ups: Counted five bongs of a clock._

_A tiny bit of the troll’s mirror flies into Kay’s eye._  
_Another bit goes into his heart._  

**Choices**

- **Look in Kay’s eye.**
    - You look gently. There is nothing there, you think.
- **Hold Kay’s hand.**
    - His hand is warm. Then it is not quite so warm.
- **Call Grandmother.**
    - Grandmother peers in. She finds nothing at all.
    - **Grandmother**: Blink. Again. There. Gone.

#### `sprout:a12-03-arch-done` · [main](main.md#a12-03-arch-done)

**Kay**: It’s gone. I think it’s gone.  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, ask): naming feelings  
> _Kay says “Ow!” and looks sad. How does Kay feel?_  
> - ✅ Hurt and worried → Yes. Hurt and worried. It is fine to feel that.
> - Very happy → Look at his face. He is hurt and worried, not happy.
> _For grown-ups: Named a friend’s feelings: hurt and worried._

_It hasn’t gone._  

#### `sprout:a12-03-kick` · [main](main.md#a12-03-kick)

**Gerda**: Kay? Are you all right?  
**Kay**: Stop crying! It looks silly!  
**Kay**: These roses are ugly. They have worms.  
_The mirror bit makes Kay say mean things. It is not really Kay talking._  
_He kicks the box. Two roses fall over the edge._  
_You reach for him. But you can’t pull a splinter out of a heart._  
_Kay slams his window. Gerda holds her book close._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): helping a sad friend  
> _Gerda is sad. What helps a sad friend?_  
> - ✅ Sit close and be kind → Yes. Sitting close helps a lot.
> - Tell her to stop crying → That does not help. Sitting close and listening does.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about what really helps a sad friend._

**Gerda**: He said it is a baby book.  
_Your Nubbin glares at every window in the street, just in case._  

**Choices**

- **Fetch the two roses back for Gerda.**
    - You fish them back. Gerda puts them in a cup of water.
- **Knock on Kay’s window.**
    - Kay opens it. He pulls a horrible face at you.
    - Then he shuts it.
- **Say nothing. Straighten the box together.**
    - You lift the box together. Your Nubbin pats the earth flat.

#### `sprout:a12-03-kick-done` · [main](main.md#a12-03-kick-done)

_The rose tree has lost two flowers and a bit of bark._  
_Roses forgive that, if you leave them be._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, notice): plants can recover  
> **Grandmother**: Give a rose water and sun, and it grows new flowers.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that a plant with water and light can grow back._


#### `sprout:a12-03-glass` · [main](main.md#a12-03-glass)

_All autumn, Kay is clever and unkind. He copies Grandmother’s voice._  
_Everyone laughs. But he copies Gerda too, only the parts that hurt._  
_When snow comes, he runs out with a big glass._  
_He catches snowflakes on his sleeve._  
**Kay**: Look, Gerda! Look how perfect they are!  
_Under the glass each flake is a six-pointed star._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count how many  
> _(Point to the star’s arms and count them. One, two, three, four, five, six!)_  
> Check: `edu:count:snowflake-arms:6`
> _For grown-ups: Counted the six arms of a snowflake._

**Gerda**: Real flowers smell of something.  
**Kay**: That’s what is wrong with them.  

**Choices**

- **Look in the glass.**
    - You look. The flake is lovely, but it feels cold and flat.
- **Throw a snowball at Kay.**
    - Splat! Kay almost laughs. Then he brushes it off, bored.
- **Stand by Gerda.**
    - You stand by Gerda. Your Nubbin leans on her boot.

#### `sprout:a12-03-glass-done` · [main](main.md#a12-03-glass-done)

**Kay**: I’m allowed in the big square tomorrow. Gran said.  
**Gerda**: Gran said maybe.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Language and Communication, ask): what “maybe” means  
> _Gran said “maybe”. Does maybe mean yes?_  
> - ✅ No. Maybe means not sure yet → Right. Maybe means we will see.
> - Yes, it means yes → Maybe means yes? Hmm, silly! Maybe means not sure yet.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that “maybe” means not sure yet._


#### `sprout:a12-03-complete` · [main](main.md#a12-03-complete)

_That evening Gerda sits on her stool in the cold._  
**Gerda**: He’s still in there. Something is in front of him.  
_You sit down next to her on Kay’s stool._  
_You wait with her. Waiting together is a kind of help._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): calm and wait together  
> _(Sit beside a grown-up. Take ten slow breaths together.)_  
> Check: `edu:breathe:slow:10`
> _For grown-ups: Practised sitting calmly beside someone and breathing slowly._


## A12-4. The Big Sledge

- `a12-04-s1`: Follow Kay to the great square. · _Go to the square. Keep Kay in sight._
- `a12-04-s2`: Run after the white sledge. · _Run! Keep going to the town gate._
- `a12-04-s3`: Watch from the hill. · _Stay on the hill. You can’t reach them from here._

#### `sprout:a12-04-square` · [main](main.md#a12-04-square)

_Kay comes past with big gloves and his sledge on his back._  
**Kay**: I’m going to the big square!  
**Gerda**: Go with him, please. Stay close to him.  
_The square is busy. Bold boys tie sledges to farmers’ carts._  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, ask): staying safe on sledges  
> _The boys tie their sledges to moving carts. Is that safe?_  
> - ✅ No, it isn’t safe → Right. A cart can pull you too fast. A little hill is much safer.
> - Yes, it’s safe → A moving cart can pull you too fast. A little hill is safer.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about why it is not safe to hitch a sledge to a moving cart._

_A great white sledge comes in. The driver wears white fur._  
_The white driver watches only Kay._  

**Choices**

- **Shout to Kay to stay put.**
    - **Kay**: Mind your own business, lodger!
    - He doesn’t look round. He watches the white sledge.
- **Push through to get hold of his cord.**
    - There are too many boys. The square is suddenly very wide.
- **Pick up your Nubbin. It has gone very still.**
    - It stares at the white sledge. It feels cold, like at the window.

#### `sprout:a12-04-square-done` · [main](main.md#a12-04-square-done)

_Kay holds his cord. He ties a careful knot, tongue between his teeth._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): sledges slide on snow  
> _Which is better for sledging, snow or grass?_  
> - ✅ Snow → Yes! A sledge slides well on smooth snow.
> - Grass → Grass is bumpy and stops a sledge. Smooth snow lets it slide.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a sledge slides best on smooth snow._


#### `sprout:a12-04-chase` · [main](main.md#a12-04-chase)

_The white sledge circles again. Kay ties his sledge to it. Off he goes!_  
_Faster, into the next street. Faster!_  
_The driver nods to Kay kindly, as if they are old friends._  
_You run. Your Nubbin runs ahead. You scoop it up and run faster._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.G.A.1, ask): in front and behind  
> _Your Nubbin runs ahead. Is it in front of you, or behind?_  
> - ✅ In front of me → Yes! It is in front of you.
> - Behind me → Behind is at your back. Your Nubbin is in front, running ahead.
> _For grown-ups: Practised the position words in front and behind._

_Out through the town gate. The snow comes down thick and white._  
**Kay**: Stop! I want to get off!  
_You lose them. Then, from a hill, you see the sledge has stopped._  

#### `sprout:a12-04-chase-done` · [main](main.md#a12-04-chase-done)

_The driver stands up. Under the snow is a tall lady, pale and sparkling._  
_It is the lady from the window. It is the Snow Queen._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.K.3, ask): who is in the story  
> _Who is driving the white sledge?_  
> - ✅ The Snow Queen → Yes. The lady from the window is the Snow Queen.
> - Grandmother → Grandmother is at home. The driver is the lady from the window.
> _For grown-ups: Named a character from the story._


#### `sprout:a12-04-kiss` · [main](main.md#a12-04-kiss)

**The Snow Queen**: We have come a long way. You are shivering.  
**The Snow Queen**: Creep in under my fur.  
_She wraps Kay in her white bearskin. He sinks in like a snowdrift._  
**The Snow Queen**: Are you still cold?  
_She touches his forehead. It is colder than ice._  
_Now Kay does not feel cold at all._  
_Gerda, Grandmother, the roses: Kay forgets them. But you do not._  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, ask): all feelings are allowed  
> _Kay feels nothing now. Is feeling nothing better than feeling sad?_  
> - ✅ No. Feelings come with loving people → Yes. Feelings are part of being alive. Sad is allowed.
> - Yes. Feeling nothing is better → Feelings are allowed. A hug or a friend helps with the hard ones.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about how all feelings, even sad ones, are okay._

_The sledge rises over the forest. A big white moon comes out._  

**Choices**

- **Call his name, as loud as you can.**
    - The wind blows your voice back towards the town, to Gerda.
- **Stand and watch until they’re gone.**
    - You watch till the sledge is a speck, then a star, then nothing.

#### `sprout:a12-04-kiss-done` · [main](main.md#a12-04-kiss-done)

_You walk back in the dark. The town gate is still open._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-ESS1-1, notice): the Moon in the night sky  
> _The Moon shines in the night sky. Its shape changes each month._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that the Moon is seen at night and changes shape through the month._


#### `sprout:a12-04-complete` · [main](main.md#a12-04-complete)

_Gerda waits at the top of the stairs. You tell her everything._  
**Gerda**: Then he isn’t lost. He has been taken. That is different.  
_People in the town say Kay fell in the river._  
**Gerda**: No. He hates water. He would never go near the river.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.2.3, ask): how a character responds  
> _Everyone says Kay is gone. What does Gerda think?_  
> - ✅ He can still be found → Yes. Gerda keeps hoping, and she will go and look.
> - They are right → Gerda does not agree. She believes Kay can still be found.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed how Gerda responds when others lose hope._

_Grandmother is sad all winter. Gerda is brave and quiet._  

#### `sprout:a12-04-kept` · [main](main.md#a12-04-kept)

_Variant of `a12-04-complete`._

_You have a small cold stone from the Wellspring. You kept it._  
_The white sledge doesn’t pass you. It stops right beside you._  
_The driver looks down. Her face is ice, lovely and still._
