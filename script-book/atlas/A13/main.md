## A13-2. Geppetto Carves a Puppet

*The Adventures of Pinocchio (Carlo Collodi, 1883), ch. III: the carving, the kick, and the carabineer* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Wooden Boy page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A13-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A13-1

**Cast:** Geppetto (Geppetto, called Polendina for his yellow wig (Collodi, 1883); original design), Pinocchio (Pinocchio, the wooden puppet (Collodi, 1883); original design: flowered-paper suit, bark shoes, breadcrumb cap, no strings), Carabineer (The carabineer in the street (Collodi, 1883, ch. ### Step 1: Hold the lamp while Geppetto carves.

Hint: Stand at his elbow with the lamp. Watch the nose. · check: `watch:a13-geppetto:carving`

#### <a id="a13-02-carve"></a>Scene `a13-02-carve`

_Geppetto lays the log on his bench, rolls up his sleeves and looks at it for a long time, the way you look at a letter before you open it._  
**Geppetto**: What shall I call him? Pinocchio. I knew a whole family of Pinocchios once, and they all did splendidly.  
**Geppetto** _(happy)_: The richest of them begged for a living. You see? Lucky.  
_A pinocchio is a pine nut. It suits a thing made of pine, with a hard little head on it._  
_He carves the hair, then the forehead, then the eyes. The eyes move, and stare at him._  
**Geppetto** _(shock)_: Wooden eyes, why are you looking at me?  
_Nobody answers. He carves the nose, and the nose begins to grow._  
_He cuts it shorter, and it grows longer. He cuts it again, and it grows again, until he gives up and leaves it._  
_He carves the mouth. Before he has quite finished, it laughs at him and sticks its tongue out._  
**Geppetto** _(angry)_: Stop laughing!  
_The mouth stops laughing. It goes on sticking its tongue out._  

**Choices**

- **Point out that the nose is growing again.**
    - **Geppetto**: I know. I’ve decided not to notice. You should try it.
- **Keep the lamp steady and say nothing.**
    - You keep the lamp steady. Geppetto works faster, the way people do when their work is watching them back.

#### <a id="a13-02-carve-done"></a>Done `a13-02-carve-done`

_Geppetto carves the hands. The moment they are finished, they snatch the yellow wig off his head and put it on their own._  
**Geppetto**: Pinocchio! Give that back this minute!  
_Pinocchio gives it back. His eyes are laughing._  

### Step 2: Help Geppetto teach the puppet to walk.

Hint: Hold one wooden hand; Geppetto holds the other. Mind the door. · check: `walk:a13-pin:first-steps`

#### <a id="a13-02-legs"></a>Scene `a13-02-legs`

**Geppetto** _(sad)_: You young rascal. You’re not even finished, and already you have no respect for your father.  
_He carves the legs and the feet. The first thing the feet do is kick him on the end of the nose._  
**Geppetto** _(think)_: I deserved that. I ought to have thought of it before I made them.  
_He sets Pinocchio on the floor to teach him to walk. You take one wooden hand, and he takes the other._  
_The legs are stiff, then less stiff. Pinocchio takes one step, and another, and then he is running._  
**Pinocchio** _(happy)_: Ha! Legs! I’ve got LEGS!  
_He is out of the door before either of you can shut it, and away down the street, clack-clack-clack on the cobbles._  
**Geppetto**: Catch him! Catch him!  
_People in the street watch him go by, and nobody catches him. They are laughing too hard to catch anything._  

#### <a id="a13-02-street"></a>Done `a13-02-street`

_At the bottom of the street a carabineer steps out and catches Pinocchio neatly by the nose. It is a very convenient nose for it._  
_Geppetto arrives out of breath and gives him a good shake. Pinocchio throws himself on the cobbles and refuses to walk another step._  
_A crowd gathers. The crowd decides, as crowds do, that the poor little puppet is frightened of the dreadful old man._  
**Carabineer**: Well. If that’s how he treats them, he can come along with me.

_Pinocchio picks himself up, brushes off his knees and runs home over the fields, as fast as a hare with somewhere to be._

**The Talking-cricket** _(think)_: I shan’t go until I have told you one great truth.  
**The Talking-cricket**: Boys who turn their backs on their fathers, and run off from home to please themselves, come to no good. Sooner or later. Usually sooner.  
**Pinocchio**: Sing away, Cricket. I’m off at first light. If I stay, they’ll send me to school, and I’d rather chase butterflies.

**The Talking-cricket**: At least learn a trade. Something to earn an honest crust.

### Step 1: Let Geppetto in.

#### <a id="a13-05-window"></a>Scene `a13-05-window`

**Geppetto**: Gone? Gone where? Open this door at once.  
**Pinocchio**: I can’t stand up! I’ll have to walk on my knees for the rest of my life!  
_Geppetto thinks this is another trick, so he climbs in through the window. He tries to; he is not a young man, and it is not a large window._  
_You lean out and take his arm, and he comes over the sill like a sack of meal._  
**Geppetto** _(shock)_: Now, you young— Oh. Oh, my boy. Your feet.

### Step 2: Help with breakfast: three pears, peeled.

Hint: Pinocchio wants them peeled. Don’t throw the peel away. You’ll see. · check: `feed:a13-pin:pears`

#### <a id="a13-05-pears"></a>Scene `a13-05-pears`

**Pinocchio**: Peel them for me, please. I can’t eat skins. I’m very particular.  
**Geppetto** _(think)_: Particular. At your age. With your feet in the fire. Well, well.  
_He peels them anyway, and lays the peel and the cores in a corner of the table._  
_Pinocchio eats the first pear in two bites and throws the core away. Then the second, then the third._  
**Pinocchio** _(sad)_: I’m still hungry.  
**Geppetto**: Well, I’ve nothing else. Except the peel and the cores.  
**Pinocchio**: I don’t eat peel. I don’t eat cores.  
_He looks at them for quite a long while._  

**Choices**

- **Ask Geppetto whether he has eaten.**
    - **Geppetto**: Me? Oh, yes. Ages ago. Hours.
