# Atlas, page A14: Homecoming

## A14-1. The Loom

*The Odyssey (Homer; Butler’s prose translation, 1900), Book I: Athena visits Telemachus* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Homecoming page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time once Nova’s atlas is in the Grove; the first quest on the page.

**Cast:** Telemachus (Telemachus, son of Odysseus and Penelope (Odyssey)), Mentes (Athena (Butler: Minerva), daughter of Zeus, in the likeness of Mentes, chief of the Taphians (Odyssey I)), Penelope (Penelope, wife of Odysseus (Odyssey)), Eurycleia (Eurycleia (Butler: Euryclea), nurse to Odysseus and Telemachus (Odyssey))

### Step 1: Step onto Ithaca, and wait at the gate.

Hint: Touch the page, then walk up to the courtyard gate. · check: `visit:a14-ithaca:gate`

#### <a id="a14-01-gate"></a>Scene `a14-01-gate`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page of blue: sea, then islands, then one rough island with goats on it._  
_Under your fingers the blue goes warm and salty. Then you are standing at a courtyard gate, with your Nubbin._  
_Inside, young men lounge about playing counters on the hides of oxen they never paid for. It smells of roast meat and spilt wine._  
_Nobody comes to the gate. Beside you stands an old traveller with a bronze spear, waiting, as you are._  
**Mentes** _(think)_: In a good house, a stranger isn’t left standing at the gate. This was a good house once.  
_A young man is sitting among the noise as if he were somewhere else. He sees the two of you, and goes red to the ears._  
**Telemachus**: Strangers, at the gate, and nobody… Forgive us. Come in, both of you. Eat first, then tell me what you need.  

**Choices**

- **Wait to be asked in properly.**
    - **Telemachus**: You’re asked. Properly. I’m Telemachus. It’s my house, for what that’s worth these days.
- **Step in. You’re hungry.**
    - You step in. The old traveller nods at you, as if that was the right answer too.
- **Let your Nubbin go first.**
    - Your Nubbin walks in ahead of you and waits by the hearth, as if it knows where guests go. Telemachus laughs, for the first time in a while.

#### <a id="a14-01-gate-done"></a>Done `a14-01-gate-done`

_Telemachus takes the old man’s spear and stands it in a rack with other spears, all dusty. They were his father’s._  
**Telemachus**: Over here, away from them. You won’t hear yourself think near that lot.  

### Step 2: Sit with Telemachus and the old traveller, and listen.

Hint: Take the seat by the far pillar, out of the suitors’ way. · check: `sit:a14-mentes:hearth`

#### <a id="a14-01-hearth"></a>Scene `a14-01-hearth`

_A maid pours water over your hands from a gold jug, then brings bread, then meat. The traveller eats like someone who has walked a long way._  
**Mentes**: I’m Mentes. My father and yours were guest-friends, long ago. I came hoping to find him home.  
**Telemachus**: Then you’re twenty years late, like everyone. He went to Troy when I was a baby. Troy fell, and he never came back.  
**Telemachus** _(angry)_: Those men are suitors. They want my mother to marry one of them, and until she chooses, they eat here. Every day.  
**Telemachus** _(sad)_: The pigs, the sheep, the goats, the wine. They’re eating my father’s house one dinner at a time.  
**Mentes** _(soft)_: You have his eyes. I knew them well.  
**Telemachus** _(sad)_: My mother says I’m his. I never saw him, so I can’t say.  
_The traveller looks at him for a long moment. Then at you, as if you were part of the answer._  
**Mentes**: He is not dead. He is held somewhere on the wide sea, and he is trying. He is a man who finds a way.  
**Mentes**: Call the men of Ithaca to an assembly. Then take a ship and go asking: old Nestor at Pylos, then Menelaus at Sparta.  

**Choices**

- **Ask Mentes how he knows.**
    - **Mentes**: Some things I know because I’ve seen them. Some, because I know him.
- **Tell Telemachus you’ll go with him.**
    - **Telemachus**: You would? You don’t even know us.
    - Mentes smiles into his cup. Here, it seems, a guest is part of the house for as long as they stay.
- **Eat, and listen.**
    - You eat, and listen. The bread is good: it’s the last of the good flour, and Telemachus gave it to the strangers.

#### <a id="a14-01-hearth-done"></a>Done `a14-01-hearth-done`

_Mentes stands and goes out across the yard. Then a bird goes up through the smoke-hole in the roof, and there is no Mentes._  
**Telemachus** _(shock)_: That was no traveller.  
_He stands up differently after that, as if someone had just told him whose son he was._  

### Step 3: Watch from the stair as Penelope comes down.

Hint: Stand at the foot of the stair, by the hall door. · check: `watch:a14-penelope:stair`

#### <a id="a14-01-stair"></a>Scene `a14-01-stair`

_In the hall the bard Phemius is singing for the suitors: the Greeks coming home from Troy, and all the ones who never did._  
_A woman comes down the stair with two maids, and stands in the doorway with a veil drawn across her face._  
**Penelope** _(sad)_: Phemius, you know a hundred stories. Sing them another one. Not that one. Not in this house.  
**Telemachus** _(soft)_: Mother. It isn’t his fault what happened at Troy. Let him sing. A lot of men didn’t come home.  
_Penelope looks at her son as if he has grown taller in front of her. Then she goes back up the stair._  
_Through her door at the top you see a great loom, and on it a cloth as wide as a sail, the colour of undyed wool._  
_Your Nubbin climbs two stairs to look. A maid gently turns it round and points it back down._  

#### <a id="a14-01-stair-done"></a>Done `a14-01-stair-done`

**Telemachus** _(angry)_: That cloth has been on that loom as long as the suitors have been in this hall. Ask Antinous about it. He loves telling it.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a14-01-complete"></a>Complete `a14-01-complete`

_Night. The suitors go home to sleep, promising to be back for breakfast, and they always keep that promise._  
_Old Eurycleia, who nursed Telemachus and his father before him, lights him up to bed with a torch._  
**Eurycleia** _(soft)_: I had your father on my knee, and then you. I’ll see the two of you in one room before I’m done.  
**Telemachus**: Tomorrow I’ll call the assembly. Will you stand with me? Just stand. You don’t have to say anything.  
_Eurycleia folds his tunic, hangs it on a peg, and pulls the door shut by its silver ring._  
_You and your Nubbin sleep on fleeces in the porch, as guests do here. Somewhere above, very quietly, a loom is working._  
_It sounds as if it is going backwards._

## A14-2. The Assembly

*The Odyssey (Homer; Butler’s prose translation, 1900), Book II: the assembly; Penelope’s web; Telemachus sails* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Homecoming page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A14-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A14-1

**Cast:** Telemachus (Telemachus, son of Odysseus and Penelope (Odyssey)), Antinous (Antinous, the boldest of the suitors (Odyssey)), Penelope (Penelope, wife of Odysseus (Odyssey)), Mentor (Athena, daughter of Zeus, in the likeness of Mentor, Odysseus’s old friend (Odyssey II–III)), Eurycleia (Eurycleia (Butler: Euryclea), nurse to Odysseus and Telemachus (Odyssey))

### Step 1: Stand behind Telemachus at the assembly.

Hint: Follow him to the meeting-place and stand behind his father’s seat. · check: `talk:a14-telemachus:assembly`

#### <a id="a14-02-assembly"></a>Scene `a14-02-assembly`

_At dawn the heralds call the men of Ithaca to the meeting-place. Nobody has called them since Odysseus sailed._  
_Telemachus comes in with a spear in his hand and two hounds at his heels. The old men make room for him in his father’s seat._  
_You stand behind the seat with your Nubbin. The suitors sit in a row along the front, and grin._  
**Telemachus** _(angry)_: No army is coming. I called you for my own trouble. My father is lost, and my house is being eaten.  
**Telemachus** _(angry)_: Go home, all of you, and eat your own dinners. Aren’t you ashamed? The gods see a house treated like this.  
_He throws the speaker’s staff down on the ground, and his eyes are full of tears. For a moment nobody says anything._  
_Then someone in the front row laughs, and the rest of the row joins in._  

**Choices**

- **Pick up the staff and hand it back to him.**
    - You pick up the staff and give it back to him. He takes it without looking at you, and holds it like a rope.
- **Stare at the one who laughed first.**
    - You look straight at him. He stops laughing, remembers he’s a suitor, and starts again, a bit louder.
- **Stay where you are, right behind him.**
    - You don’t move. Telemachus feels it, and his back straightens a little against the noise.

#### <a id="a14-02-assembly-done"></a>Done `a14-02-assembly-done`

_A tall man in a bright cloak stands up from the front row: Antinous, the loudest of the suitors._  
**Antinous** _(angry)_: Blame us? Blame your mother. She’s the cleverest woman in Greece, and she’s led us on for years.  

### Step 2: Listen to Antinous tell the story of the web.

Hint: Stay by Telemachus while Antinous speaks. · check: `watch:a14-antinous:assembly`

#### <a id="a14-02-antinous"></a>Scene `a14-02-antinous`

**Antinous**: Three years she promised, and three years we waited. Shall I tell you how she did it? Everyone else knows.  
**Antinous**: She set up a great tambour frame in her room, and began to work on an enormous piece of fine needlework.  
**Antinous**: “It’s a shroud for old Laertes,” she said. “Wait till it’s done, or the women will say he died with nothing to be wrapped in.”  
**Antinous**: Reasonable. We’re reasonable men. We waited.  
_As he talks, the edges of the meeting-place go soft, as if the page were listening too._  
_The stone turns to floorboards, and the daylight to torchlight. You are in an upstairs room, holding a torch, and you don’t remember picking it up._  

#### <a id="a14-02-antinous-done"></a>Done `a14-02-antinous-done`

_In front of you stands the great loom. Penelope sits at it, alone, with a small knife._  

### Step 3: Hold the torch while Penelope works.

Hint: Keep the torch high and steady by the loom. · check: `carry:a14-torch:loom`

#### <a id="a14-02-loom"></a>Scene `a14-02-loom`

**Antinous**: We saw her at her great web all day long, but at night she would unpick the stitches again by torchlight.  
_Penelope pulls a thread, and a day’s work comes undone in one long, soft sigh. She winds it back on the shuttle for tomorrow._  
**Penelope** _(soft)_: Hold it a little higher. There. Thank you.  
**Penelope**: Every row I weave is one more day he has to come home. Every row I take out gives the day back.  
**Penelope** _(think)_: The long threads stand still and wait. The short one goes back and forth, back and forth. I know which one I am.  
_Your Nubbin holds the loose end of the thread so it doesn’t tangle. It is very serious about this._  
**Penelope** _(think)_: They think I’m slow. I’m the fastest weaver on Ithaca. I have to be, to unpick it all by dawn.  
_Out in the dark passage, a floorboard creaks. Someone has been listening at the door._  

**Choices**

- **Go and look in the passage.**
    - You look, and a maid’s skirt whisks round the corner and is gone. You know where she’s going.
- **Tell Penelope someone heard.**
    - **Penelope**: I know. I’ve known for a week which one it would be. I thought I’d finish tonight’s row anyway.
- **Hold the torch steady. Keep going.**
    - You keep the torch steady, and she keeps going. Neither of you looks at the door.

#### <a id="a14-02-loom-done"></a>Done `a14-02-loom-done`

_The torchlight goes thin, and it is daylight again. You are standing in the meeting-place with empty hands._  
**Antinous** _(angry)_: A maid told us. We caught her at it, so she had to finish the thing.  
**Antinous**: Send your mother back to her father’s house, boy, and let him marry her off. Until then, we eat.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a14-02-complete"></a>Complete `a14-02-complete`

_The assembly breaks up, and nobody helps Telemachus. One old man, Mentor, tries to, and is shouted down._  
_That evening at the shore, Mentor finds him again: or someone with Mentor’s face, who walks too lightly for an old man._  
**Mentor**: A ship, and twenty to row it. I have found both. Fetch food for the voyage, and tell no one.  
_Eurycleia fills twelve jars of wine and twenty measures of barley-meal, crying the whole time._  
**Eurycleia** _(sad)_: Your father went over the sea and never came back. Now you. Who will I have left to scold?  
**Telemachus** _(soft)_: Don’t tell my mother. Not for eleven or twelve days, unless she asks.  
_At nightfall the ship slides out of the harbour. You sit in the stern with your Nubbin, and a wind comes up behind you from nowhere._  
_Mentor sits by the steering oar and looks up at the sail. The wind is exactly as strong as it needs to be._

## A14-3. Nestor and Menelaus

*The Odyssey (Homer; Butler’s prose translation, 1900), Books III–IV: Pylos and Sparta* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Homecoming page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A14-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A14-2

**Cast:** Telemachus (Telemachus, son of Odysseus and Penelope (Odyssey)), Mentor (Athena, daughter of Zeus, in the likeness of Mentor, Odysseus’s old friend (Odyssey II–III)), Nestor (Nestor, the old king of Pylos (Odyssey III)), Menelaus (Menelaus, king of Sparta (Odyssey IV)), Helen (Helen, queen of Sparta (Odyssey IV))

### Step 1: Land at Pylos and ask old Nestor for news.

Hint: Walk up the beach to the feast and sit where you’re put. · check: `visit:a14-pylos:shore`

#### <a id="a14-03-pylos"></a>Scene `a14-03-pylos`

_Sandy Pylos, at sunrise. The whole town is on the beach, at a great feast in honour of Poseidon, god of the sea._  
**Telemachus** _(sad)_: I can’t. I don’t know how to talk to an old king. What if I get it wrong?  
**Mentor** _(soft)_: Some of it you’ll think of yourself. The rest will be given you. Go on.  
_Nestor’s son runs to meet the strangers before anyone asks who they are, and sits you all on soft fleeces on the sand._  
_Nestor is so old that he was old at Troy. He has outlived nearly everyone he went there with._  
**Nestor** _(soft)_: Eat first. Then names. Then, if you like, sorrows. There’s always time for sorrows.  
**Telemachus**: I’m Odysseus’s son. I’ve come for news of my father. Any news, even bad.  
**Nestor** _(shock)_: His son! You talk just like him. I’d never have thought a young man could sound so like him.  
**Nestor** _(sad)_: But I know nothing, child. We left Troy together, and quarrelled, and parted at sea. He turned back. I came home.  

**Choices**

- **Ask who might know.**
    - **Nestor**: Menelaus. He was blown about longer than any of us, and came home last. Go to Sparta. My son will drive you.
- **Ask if there’s any hope.**
    - **Nestor**: With that one? There’s always hope. I never once saw him stuck for long.
- **Let your Nubbin sit by the old king.**
    - Your Nubbin climbs onto the fleece beside Nestor. He rests an old hand on it, as if it were a grandchild, and goes on talking.

#### <a id="a14-03-pylos-done"></a>Done `a14-03-pylos-done`

_When the feast is over, Mentor rises, and is a sea-eagle, and is gone over the water. Everyone on the beach falls silent._  
**Nestor** _(soft)_: That was Athena, daughter of Zeus. She stood by your father at Troy. If she walks beside you, you won’t go far wrong.  
_Nestor’s people honour her on the shore in the old way. You stand back with your Nubbin and keep quiet, as guests should._  

### Step 2: Ride to Sparta with Nestor’s son.

Hint: Climb into the chariot behind Telemachus. · check: `escort:a14-telemachus:sparta`

#### <a id="a14-03-sparta"></a>Scene `a14-03-sparta`

_Two days in a chariot over the hills, with Nestor’s son Pisistratus at the reins. Your Nubbin rides up front and leans into the corners._  
_Sparta lies in a hollow among the hills. Menelaus’s house gleams with bronze and gold, amber and silver and ivory._  
**Telemachus** _(shock)_: Pisistratus, look at it. Is this what the house of Zeus is like?  
**Menelaus** _(sad)_: No mortal’s house is like Zeus’s, boy. And I’d give two-thirds of this to have back the friends who died at Troy.  
_He hasn’t asked your names. In Sparta, as in Pylos, a guest is fed first and asked after._  
_Then a woman comes down from a high room with her spindle, and stops, and stares at Telemachus._  
**Helen** _(shock)_: I have never seen anyone so like another. This must be Odysseus’s son. He has his father’s feet. His hands.  
**Menelaus** _(soft)_: Now that you say it… The hair, the look about the eyes. You’re right, my dear.  
_Telemachus holds his cloak up in front of his face. Everyone weeps a little, even Pisistratus, who never met Odysseus at all._  

**Choices**

- **Tell them yes, it’s his son.**
    - You say it for him, because he can’t. Menelaus reaches across the table and grips Telemachus by the arm.
- **Let Telemachus answer.**
    - **Telemachus**: I am. I’ve come to ask whether my father is alive.

#### <a id="a14-03-sparta-done"></a>Done `a14-03-sparta-done`

**Menelaus**: Sit. Eat. In the morning I’ll tell you something I’ve told nobody since I came home.  

### Step 3: Hear what the Old Man of the Sea told Menelaus.

Hint: Sit with Menelaus by the fire in the morning. · check: `talk:a14-menelaus:proteus`

#### <a id="a14-03-proteus"></a>Scene `a14-03-proteus`

**Menelaus**: Coming home from Troy, I was stuck twenty days on an island called Pharos. No wind, and the food running out.  
**Menelaus**: A goddess of the sea took pity on us. “Catch my father, Proteus, the Old Man of the Sea,” she said. “He knows everything.”  
**Menelaus**: At noon he comes up out of the waves to sleep among his seals. You must catch him, and hold on, whatever he turns into.  
_As he tells it, the hall smells of seals. You are lying under a sealskin on a hot beach, with Menelaus and three of his men._  
_An old man comes up out of the sea, counts his seals, counts you among them, and lies down to sleep._  
**Menelaus**: Now!  
_He is a lion in your arms, then a snake, a leopard, a boar; then running water, then a tall tree. Your Nubbin holds on to one leg throughout._  
_At last he is an old man again, and tired, and he answers._  
**Menelaus** _(soft)_: Odysseus is on an island, in the house of the nymph Calypso, Proteus said. She keeps him there. He has no ship, and no crew to row one.  

**Choices**

- **Ask Menelaus: alive?**
    - **Menelaus**: Alive. Weeping, Proteus said. But alive.
- **Look at Telemachus.**
    - Telemachus is very still. Then he stands up, as if he means to swim there.

#### <a id="a14-03-proteus-done"></a>Done `a14-03-proteus-done`

**Telemachus** _(happy)_: Alive. He’s alive. Then I have to go home, now, and tell her.  
**Menelaus**: Stay eleven or twelve days! No? Then take a gift: a silver mixing-bowl with a rim of gold. A guest goes home with something.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a14-03-complete"></a>Complete `a14-03-complete`

_You ride back to Pylos and the ship. Behind you, Helen watches from the gate until the chariot is out of sight._  
_On Ithaca the suitors have found out he’s gone. Antinous takes a fast ship and twenty men, to wait for him among the rocks._  
_Telemachus doesn’t know that yet. He stands in the bow all the way, as if that will get him home sooner._  
_Then Nova’s atlas turns its own page, the way a wind turns one, and you are somewhere else entirely._

## A14-4. Calypso’s Island

*The Odyssey (Homer; Butler’s prose translation, 1900), Book V: Hermes on Ogygia; the raft* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Homecoming page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A14-3, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A14-3

**Cast:** Hermes (Hermes (Butler: Mercury), messenger of the gods (Odyssey V, X)), Calypso (Calypso, the goddess-nymph of Ogygia (Odyssey V)), Odysseus (Odysseus (Butler: Ulysses), son of Laertes, king of Ithaca (Homer, Odyssey))

### Step 1: Walk with the messenger to Calypso’s cave.

Hint: Follow the young man with the gold sandals up from the beach. · check: `explore:a14-ogygia:cave`

#### <a id="a14-04-ogygia"></a>Scene `a14-04-ogygia`

_An island at the navel of the sea, far from anywhere. Alder and poplar and sweet-smelling cypress, with owls and sea-crows nesting in them._  
_A vine heavy with grapes grows over the mouth of a cave. Four springs run out across meadows of violets and wild parsley._  
_Someone lands on the beach beside you as lightly as a gull: a young man with golden sandals and a wand._  
**Hermes**: You’re far from your own country, guest. So am I, and I don’t love all this salt water. Walk with me.  
_In the cave a cedar fire is burning, and a woman is weaving at a loom with a golden shuttle, and singing. She knows Hermes at once._  
**Calypso**: Hermes. You don’t often come. Tell me what you want, and then let me give you dinner.  
_She sets the food of the gods in front of him, ambrosia and red nectar. You are given bread and figs, which is food for people._  
**Hermes**: Zeus sends word. The man who is with you must go home. It is not his fate to die here, far from his own people.  
**Calypso** _(angry)_: You gods are hard. Whenever one of us loves a mortal, you begrudge it.  
**Calypso** _(sad)_: I found him clinging to a keel. I fed him. I told him I would make him never die and never grow old.  
**Calypso** _(sad)_: But nobody goes against the will of Zeus. Let him go, then, over the sea, if that is what he wants.  

**Choices**

- **Thank Calypso for the figs.**
    - **Calypso**: You’re welcome, little guest. You, at least, will go home glad.
- **Ask Hermes where Odysseus is.**
    - **Hermes**: On the shore. He is always on the shore.

#### <a id="a14-04-ogygia-done"></a>Done `a14-04-ogygia-done`

_Hermes goes away over the sea the way he came. Calypso puts down her shuttle and goes to find Odysseus._  

### Step 2: Find Odysseus on the shore.

Hint: Go down to the rocks above the beach. · check: `find:a14-odysseus:shore`

#### <a id="a14-04-shore"></a>Scene `a14-04-shore`

_He is sitting on a rock above the beach, looking at the sea, and crying. Not loudly: the way people cry who have been at it for years._  
_He is broad, grey at the temples, in a beautiful cloak that somebody else gave him._  
**Odysseus** _(sad)_: Another guest. She’s had none in seven years but me. Sit, if you like. The rock is wide.  
**Odysseus** _(sad)_: Every morning I come here. Every night I go back to her, because there’s nowhere else to go.  
**Odysseus** _(soft)_: Ithaca is rough. Goats, mostly. I’d give anything to see the smoke going up from its houses, just once.  
_Your Nubbin sits down on the rock beside him and looks at the sea too, in case there is something out there._  

**Choices**

- **Sit with him and say nothing.**
    - You sit, and the sea goes in and out. After a while he wipes his face on the beautiful cloak.
    - **Odysseus**: Thank you. Nobody does that.
- **Ask him about home.**
    - **Odysseus**: My son was a baby. He’ll be a man. My father will be old. My mother, I hope, is well.
- **Tell him you’ve met his son.**
    - **Odysseus**: My son? You’ve seen him? How… No. Don’t tell me how. Is he tall? He’d be tall.

#### <a id="a14-04-shore-done"></a>Done `a14-04-shore-done`

_Calypso comes down the beach. She stands beside him, and doesn’t touch him._  
**Calypso** _(sad)_: Don’t cry any more. I’ll let you go. Cut trees and build a raft, and I’ll give you bread and water and a fair wind.
