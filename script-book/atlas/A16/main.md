### Step 1: Step into the desert page and find the stranger under the plane.

Hint: Touch the little planet on the page, then walk towards the plane in the dunes. · check: `explore:a16-desert:plane`

#### <a id="a16-01-desert"></a>Scene `a16-01-desert`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page that is nearly empty: one small round planet, one chair, and a sunset going on behind it._  
_You touch the sunset. It is warm the way sand is warm, and then it is sand, and you are standing in it._  
_Dunes in every direction, and a sky so clean it looks scrubbed. Your Nubbin sneezes at the size of it._  
_A few dunes off, a small aeroplane sits nose-down in the sand. A pair of legs sticks out from under the engine._  
**The Pilot** _(think)_: Unless you’re a mechanic, don’t come any closer. If you are, come quickly.  
_He slides out on his back: a man with oil to the elbows and a face that hasn’t slept._  
**The Pilot**: You’re the last thing I expected. I’m days from anywhere, and I’ve water for about eight of them.  
**The Pilot**: Where did you come from? No. Don’t tell me. I’ve stopped expecting sensible answers out here.  

**Choices**

- **Offer to hold the spanner.**
    - **The Pilot**: Hold it, and don’t hand it to me unless I ask. I have a system. It’s mostly muttering.
- **Ask if he’s hurt.**
    - **The Pilot**: Only my pride. And the engine. The engine’s worse.
- **Sit down in the shade of the wing.**
    - You sit, and your Nubbin sits. After a moment the man sits too, because it seems rude not to.

### Step 2: Look at the pilot’s first drawing, and say what you see.

#### <a id="a16-01-drawing"></a>Scene `a16-01-drawing`

_At noon the sun is too high to work in. The pilot crawls out, wipes his hands, and takes a battered notebook from his jacket._  
**The Pilot**: When I was six, I drew this. My first drawing. I showed it to every grown-up I knew.  
_It is a brown shape like a hill, or a lump, or a hat with a very low crown._  
**The Pilot**: I asked them if it frightened them. They all said, why would anybody be frightened of a hat?  
**The Pilot**: So now I show it to everyone I meet. It’s a test. Go on. What is it?

**Choices**

- **Say it looks like something with something inside it.**
    - **The Pilot**: Go on…

## A16-2. Draw Me a Sheep

*Le Petit Prince (Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, 1943), ch. II: the sheep in the box* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Fox and the Rose page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A16-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A16-1

**Cast:** The Pilot (the aviator who tells the story in Le Petit Prince (1943); original design), The Prince (the little prince of asteroid B-612 (Le Petit Prince, 1943); original design)

### Step 1: Spend the night by the plane, and see who comes at sunrise.

Hint: Rest in the shade of the wing until the page’s dawn. · check: `wait:a16-plane:dawn`

#### <a id="a16-02-dawn"></a>Scene `a16-02-dawn`

_That night you sleep on the sand by the wing. There are so many stars that the dark between them looks crowded._  
_At sunrise a small voice wakes the pilot. It is quite a polite voice, and quite certain._  
**The Prince**: Please. Would you draw a sheep for me?  
_A boy is standing in the sand. He has a long scarf, a serious face, and not a speck of dust on him anywhere._  
**The Pilot**: A what?  
**The Prince** _(soft)_: A sheep. Please. If you’re not too busy.  
_The pilot looks at the desert, and the plane, and the boy, who is days from anywhere and doesn’t seem to mind._  
**The Pilot**: I can’t really draw. I only ever learnt the one. Here.  
_He draws the snake with the elephant inside it, from the outside, and hands it over with a shrug._  
**The Prince**: No, not an elephant in a snake. Snakes are dangerous, and elephants take up far too much room. A sheep.  
_The pilot’s mouth falls open. Your Nubbin looks very pleased, as if it has found a cousin._  

**Choices**

- **Sit down to watch.**
    - You sit cross-legged in the sand. The boy sits too, opposite, and waits for his sheep as if it were a bus.
- **Say hello to the boy.**
    - **The Prince**: Hello. Can you draw sheep?
    - **You**: No. Sorry.
    - **The Prince**: That’s all right. Nobody can, it seems.

### Step 2: Watch the pilot try to draw a sheep good enough.

Hint: Stay by the boy while the pilot draws. · check: `watch:a16-sheep:drawings`

#### <a id="a16-02-drawings"></a>Scene `a16-02-drawings`

_The pilot draws a sheep. The boy studies it with great care._  
**The Prince**: No. That one’s poorly already. Another, please.  
_He draws a second, fluffier sheep. The boy smiles kindly, the way you smile at a mistake._  
**The Prince**: That one has horns. That’s a ram. I asked for a sheep.  
_The third is a small, neat sheep with a tidy face._  
**The Prince** _(sad)_: He’s nearly worn out, that one. Mine has to last.  
_The pilot looks at his engine, which is waiting, and at the sun, which is climbing. He scribbles four quick lines._  
**The Pilot**: This is a box. The sheep you want is inside.  
_It is a plain box with three holes in the side. The boy’s whole face lights up._  
**The Prince** _(happy)_: That’s him! That’s exactly him! Will he need much grass?  
**The Pilot**: Why?  
**The Prince**: Where I live, there isn’t much room for grass. Or for anything.  
_He bends close to the holes, and whispers, so as not to wake anybody._  
**The Prince** _(soft)_: Shh. He’s asleep already.  

#### <a id="a16-02-drawings-done"></a>Done `a16-02-drawings-done`

_Your Nubbin climbs onto the box and sits on the lid, very gently, as if it might be someone’s bed._  
_Neither of you can see the sheep, but the boy can. That seems to be enough for everybody._  

### Step 3: Decide whether to ask for a muzzle for the sheep.

Hint: Talk to the pilot while the boy is busy with his box. · check: `choose:a16-muzzle:box`

#### <a id="a16-02-muzzle"></a>Scene `a16-02-muzzle`

_The boy lies on his front with one eye to the holes. The pilot has gone back under his engine._  
**The Prince**: Sheep eat bushes, don’t they?  
_From under the plane comes a muffled “Mostly.”_  
**The Prince** _(think)_: Good. That’s useful. That’s very useful.  
**The Prince**: And flowers? Do they eat flowers?  
_The voice under the plane says sheep eat whatever they find: even flowers, even flowers with thorns._  
_The boy goes quiet, the way people do when they’ve thought of something and put it away for later._  
_You look at the box, with your Nubbin on the lid. A sheep that eats everything might need something on its nose, one day._

## A16-3. Asteroid B-612

*Le Petit Prince (Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, 1943), ch. IV–V: the planet and the baobabs* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Fox and the Rose page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A16-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A16-2

**Cast:** The Pilot (the aviator who tells the story in Le Petit Prince (1943); original design), The Prince (the little prince of asteroid B-612 (Le Petit Prince, 1943); original design)

### Step 1: Listen to the boy tell where he comes from.

Hint: Sit by the plane after dark, when the boy starts talking. · check: `talk:a16-prince:plane`

#### <a id="a16-03-telling"></a>Scene `a16-03-telling`

_It takes days to get anything out of him. He asks questions all the time, and hardly hears yours._  
_But at night, by the plane, with the engine cold and the stars out, the boy begins to tell._  
**The Prince**: My planet is hardly bigger than a house. You can walk all round it before your tea gets cold.  
_The pilot murmurs that it must be an asteroid, one of the little ones with only a number for a name._  
**The Pilot**: There’s one called B-612. An astronomer saw it once, and nobody believed him till he came back in a better suit.  
**The Prince**: Grown-ups like numbers. Tell them about a new friend, and they ask how much he weighs.  
**The Prince**: You can come, if you like. When I tell it, you’ll see.  
_He tells it so well that the sand under you curves. The sky comes down close, and you are standing on a planet the size of a house._  

**Choices**

- **Ask what colour his sunsets are.**
    - **The Prince**: Every colour, one after another. You’ll see those too.
- **Ask how many steps it is round.**
    - **The Prince**: That’s a grown-up question.
    - **The Prince**: Forty-one. Not counting going round the rose.
- **Watch your Nubbin.**
    - Your Nubbin has already set off round the planet. It comes back from the other side almost at once, astonished.

### Step 2: Help with one morning’s weeding on the little planet.

Hint: Pull the baobab shoots while they’re tiny. Leave anything that might be a rose. · check: `collect:a16-baobab:shoots`

#### <a id="a16-03-weeding"></a>Scene `a16-03-weeding`

_It is morning on the little planet. There are three volcanoes, each about knee-high, and two of them are warm._  
**The Prince**: I sweep them out every day. Then they burn slow and steady, and I can heat my breakfast on them.  
**The Prince** _(think)_: That one’s asleep. I sweep it anyway. You never know with volcanoes.  
_Then he kneels and shows you the soil. Little green shoots are coming up all over it, all looking much the same._  
**The Prince**: These are baobabs. They grow into trees as big as hills. Let them, and their roots go right through the planet.  
**The Prince**: And these, the same size, might be roses. You have to tell them apart while they’re small. Then you mustn’t wait.  
_Your Nubbin takes hold of a shoot with both paws and looks up, waiting to be told._  

**Choices**

- **Pull it. It has a baobab’s fat stem.**
    - Out it comes, root and all. The boy nods once, like a teacher who has seen a sum done right.
- **Ask him which it is first.**
    - **The Prince**: Baobab. Good. Asking is better than pulling up a rose.
    - Out it comes, root and all.
- **Leave it a day, to be sure.**
    - **The Prince**: No. By tomorrow it’ll be too big to tell you anything but trouble.
    - He pulls it himself, gently and at once.

#### <a id="a16-03-weeding-done"></a>Done `a16-03-weeding-done`

_By the time the sun is properly up, the planet is clean. You have a small heap of baobab shoots, and not one rose in it._  
**The Prince** _(sad)_: I knew a planet where a lazy man left three of them. You can’t see the ground there any more.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a16-03-complete"></a>Complete `a16-03-complete`

**The Prince**: Every morning, first thing. If you leave it for later, later turns into trees.  
_The telling fades. The sand comes back under you, and the pilot is lying by the plane with his hands behind his head._  
**The Pilot**: Baobabs. All right. I’ll draw you one, so nobody forgets.  
_He draws a little planet with three enormous trees squeezing it. Your Nubbin gives the drawing a very hard look._

## A16-4. The Sunsets

*Le Petit Prince (Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, 1943), ch. VI: the sunsets* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Fox and the Rose page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A16-3, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A16-3

**Cast:** The Pilot (the aviator who tells the story in Le Petit Prince (1943); original design), The Prince (the little prince of asteroid B-612 (Le Petit Prince, 1943); original design)

### Step 1: Sit with the boy on his planet, and watch the sunsets.

Hint: Sit in the chair beside his. Move when he moves. · check: `sit:a16-chair:sunset`

#### <a id="a16-04-chairs"></a>Scene `a16-04-chairs`

_Another evening by the plane, and another telling. This time the boy says only one word: sunsets._  
_Then you are on his planet again, and he is setting out two chairs. One is a chair, and the other is a pebble, for your Nubbin._  
**The Prince**: On my planet you don’t have to wait for the sun to set. You just move your chair a few steps, and it sets again.  
_The sun goes down. He gets up, moves his chair a few steps and sits, and the sun goes down again._  
_You move your chair, and your Nubbin rolls its pebble. The sun goes down a third time, pink and then gold._  
**The Prince**: Once I watched it lots and lots of times in one day. I lost count somewhere after forty.  
_He doesn’t say why. He moves his chair again, and sits, and holds his knees._  

**Choices**

- **Ask him why.**
    - **The Prince**: You only want that many when something’s wrong.
    - He doesn’t say what.
- **Say nothing, and move your chair when he does.**
    - You move when he moves. Sunset after sunset, nobody says anything, and nobody has to.
- **Let your Nubbin lean on him.**
    - Your Nubbin leaves its pebble and leans on his ankle. He doesn’t look down, but he moves his foot so it fits better.

#### <a id="a16-04-chairs-done"></a>Done `a16-04-chairs-done`

_Pink, gold, red, violet. The planet is so small you can feel it turning under the chair legs._  
_After a long time he stops moving his chair, and lets the night come._  

### Step 2: Stay with him until the last sunset is over.

Hint: Keep sitting. Wait for the stars. · check: `wait:a16-sunset:dark`

#### <a id="a16-04-dark"></a>Scene `a16-04-dark`

_The stars come out over the little planet one by one, the way lamps come on in a street._  
**The Prince**: There’s a flower. On my planet. That’s why I couldn’t stop watching.  
**The Prince** _(soft)_: I’ll tell you about her. Not tonight.  
_The planet lets go of you. You’re back on the sand, and the pilot is turning a bolt over and over in his fingers._  
**The Pilot**: He cries sometimes, you know. I don’t know what to do about it.  
**The Pilot** _(sad)_: I’ve never known what to do about anybody crying. They don’t teach you that with engines.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a16-04-complete"></a>Complete `a16-04-complete`

_The boy has fallen asleep against the plane’s wheel, with his scarf over his nose._  
_The pilot puts his jacket over him very carefully, as if he has never done it before. He probably hasn’t._  
**The Pilot** _(think)_: A flower. All that, for a flower.  
**The Pilot** _(soft)_: Well. I suppose that’s as good a reason as any.  

**Rewards**


Sets on completion: `{"a16-04":true}`

**Notes:** The sunsets run on the page’s own clock, about ten seconds each; the player moves the chair (one tap) to get the next one, as many as they like, and the step completes once the boy stops (after five or so). Never a real-time wait. Your Nubbin’s pebble rolls with it. The boy never says how many; "lost count somewhere after forty" is ours.

## A16-5. The Rose

*Le Petit Prince (Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, 1943), ch. VIII–IX: the rose and the leaving* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Fox and the Rose page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A16-4, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A16-4

**Cast:** The Prince (the little prince of asteroid B-612 (Le Petit Prince, 1943); original design), The Rose (the prince’s rose (Le Petit Prince, 1943); original design), The Pilot (the aviator who tells the story in Le Petit Prince (1943); original design)

### Step 1: See the new flower on the boy’s planet come out.

Hint: Wait by the strange shoot until it opens at sunrise. · check: `watch:a16-rose:bud`

#### <a id="a16-05-bud"></a>Scene `a16-05-bud`

_This time he tells it as soon as the sun is down, as if he has been saving it up._  
_His planet, long ago: one morning, a shoot nobody has seen before, not a baobab, not a weed. He watches it every day._  
_It grows a bud, an enormous one, and then it takes ages. Inside the green, something is choosing its colours very carefully._  
**The Prince** _(soft)_: She wanted to come out perfect. She wasn’t going to be rushed. Not by me. Not by the sun.  
_Then one morning, exactly at sunrise, she opens, and yawns._  
**The Rose**: Oh. I’ve only just woken up. Please excuse me. I must look a sight.  
**The Prince** _(shock)_: You look like nothing I’ve ever seen.  
**The Rose**: Naturally. And I came out at sunrise, which is the proper time.  
**The Rose**: I believe it’s time for breakfast. If somebody would be so kind.  
_He runs for the watering can. Your Nubbin runs too, though nobody asked it to and it has no watering can._  

**Choices**

- **Bow to her.**
    - **The Rose**: Well. At least somebody here has manners.
- **Help him fill the watering can.**
    - You hold the can and he pours. The rose watches you both as if she is marking you out of ten.
- **Look at her thorns.**
    - **The Rose**: Four. Quite enough for anything with claws. Let them come.
    - Your Nubbin takes a step back, to be polite.

#### <a id="a16-05-bud-done"></a>Done `a16-05-bud-done`

**The Rose**: There’s a wind on this planet that comes from nowhere. A screen would be the least you could do.  
_She coughs, a small cough and possibly an invented one. He runs for the screen anyway._  

### Step 2: Help the boy carry a glass globe to the rose, for her nights.

Hint: Carry the globe round the planet to her, and don’t drop it. · check: `carry:a16-globe:rose`

#### <a id="a16-05-globe"></a>Scene `a16-05-globe`

_After that she is always wanting something: water, the screen, a better angle to the sun. He brings it all, and gets sadder._  
**The Prince** _(sad)_: I believed every word she said. That was the trouble.  
**The Prince**: She filled the whole planet with her smell, every morning, and I only noticed the complaining.  
_He has made a glass globe for her, to keep off the cold at night. It is as clear as water and nearly as heavy._  
**The Prince**: Help me carry it? It’s the only thing on my planet that can break.  
_You take one side and he takes the other. Your Nubbin walks in front, looking for stones to warn you about._  

**Choices**
