## A18-1. The Counterpane

- `a18-01-s1`: Step into the painted page and knock at the merchant’s house. · _Touch the page, then walk up to the wooden house with the carved shutters._
- `a18-01-s2`: Walk Vasilisa to her mother’s door, and wait there. · _Stand at the bedroom door with your Nubbin. Don’t go in._

#### `reader:a18-01-house` · [main](main.md#a18-01-house)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page painted in egg yellow and soot black: a wooden house, a birch wood, and far off, a fence of pale sticks._  
_The paint smells of woodsmoke. Then it is woodsmoke, and you and your Nubbin are standing on a swept step with snow piled at its edges._  
_A big man in a fur hat opens the door before you can knock, as if he has been watching the road._  
**The Merchant**: You have the traveller’s book! The woman with the map sold me needles once and bought half my ribbons, and I still don’t know who won.  
**The Merchant**: Any friend of hers may lodge here. There’s a room over the stable, and it’s warm, because the horse is.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): the meaning of a word from its context: lodge  
> _The merchant says you may lodge in the room over the stable. What does it mean to lodge somewhere?_  
> - ✅ To stay in a room in someone else’s home → Yes. A lodger stays in a room in someone’s home, often for a fee or as a favour. The stable room is your lodging.
> - To get stuck → That’s another meaning: a ball can lodge in a tree, which means it gets stuck. Here it means a bed under someone else’s roof.
> - To go for a walk → Not a walk. A lodger stays somewhere for the night, or for longer. Look at the next line: a room, and a warm horse.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out the meaning of “lodge” (to stay in a room in someone else’s home) from the sentences around it._

**The Merchant**: My wife is not well. We walk softly in this house. You’ll walk softly too, I think.  
_A girl of about eight looks round his knee. She has a plait to her waist, and she studies your Nubbin the way you would study a cake._  
**Vasilisa**: What is it? Is it a doll? Does it eat?  

**Choices**

- **Tell her it’s a Nubbin, and it eats everything.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Everything? Even porridge? Even the burnt bit?
    - Your Nubbin looks at her as if the burnt bit is the best bit, which it is.
- **Let your Nubbin answer for itself.**
    - Your Nubbin walks up and sits on her shoe. She goes very still, the way you do when a bird lands near you.
- **Ask her name first.**
    - **Vasilisa**: You are meant to say yours first. That’s manners.
    - **Vasilisa**: But I’ll tell you anyway, because you asked nicely.

#### `reader:a18-01-house-done` · [main](main.md#a18-01-house-done)

**Vasilisa**: I’m Vasilisa. Mother says I’m to be polite to lodgers, so I will be. Mostly.  
**The Merchant**: Go and wash your hands, Vasilisa. Your mother may want you later.  
_She goes, slowly, looking back at your Nubbin all the way up the stairs._  
_You run a hand along the wall. It is made of whole logs, laid one on another and stuffed between with moss._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.3-5, ask): why forest lands built with logs  
> _Many houses in forest lands were built of logs, with moss in the gaps. Why might a village near a big forest build this way?_  
> - ✅ Logs were plentiful, and thick wood keeps the cold out → Yes. People build from what is close at hand, and thick wood holds warmth well. The moss stops draughts.
> - Because stone was not allowed → Nothing in the story says that. People used the nearest building material, and in a forest land that is wood.
> - Because logs are cheaper than a tent → A tent would not last a winter. Logs are plentiful near a forest, and thick wood is a good insulator against the cold.
> _For grown-ups: Reasoned that people build from local materials: logs are plentiful in forest lands and keep homes warm._


#### `reader:a18-01-counterpane` · [main](main.md#a18-01-counterpane)

_That evening a quiet voice at the end of the passage calls Vasilisa’s name, and the whole house hears it._  
**Vasilisa**: Will you come? Only to the door. I don’t like the passage when it’s dark.  
_You walk her to the door and stop there. The room is warm and dim, and smells of beeswax._  
_Her mother sits up against the pillows under a counterpane sewn from a hundred bright scraps._  
**Vasilisa’s Mother**: Vasilisa, my darling, I am very ill, and I will not get better. Come close.  
_From under the counterpane she takes a little doll, no longer than your hand, with a painted face and a red scarf._  
**Vasilisa’s Mother**: I am leaving you this doll, and all my love in her. Keep her close, and tell no one.  
**Vasilisa’s Mother**: When you are in trouble, give her something to eat first. Then ask her. She will tell you what to do.  
**Vasilisa**: Something to eat first. Then ask. And tell nobody.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): when a secret is kind and when it should be told  
> _Vasilisa promises to tell nobody about the doll. Is keeping a secret always the right thing?_  
> _When might a secret be kind, and when should it be told to a grown-up you trust?_  
> - A secret that protects something good is fine to keep → That’s a fair answer. A surprise party, or a gift, is a happy secret. The test is whether keeping it protects somebody or hurts somebody.
> - A secret that frightens or hurts someone should be told → That’s a fair answer, and an important one. If a secret makes you scared or sad, or means someone is in danger, tell a grown-up you trust.
> - It depends on who you promised and why → That’s a fair answer. Promises matter, and so does safety. When the two pull apart, safety comes first, and you can always ask for help deciding.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about when a secret is a kind one to keep and when it should be told to a trusted grown-up._

_Her mother looks past her, to the door, to you. She doesn’t send you away._  

**Choices**

- **Stay where you are, by the door.**
    - You stay. Your Nubbin sits on your foot and does not fidget, for once.
- **Step back into the passage, to give them the room.**
    - Through the gap you see her mother lift a hand and lay it on Vasilisa’s head, and leave it there.
- **Nod to her: you’ll look after the girl.**
    - **Vasilisa’s Mother**: Thank you. She will need a friend who can keep quiet.

#### `reader:a18-01-counterpane-done` · [main](main.md#a18-01-counterpane-done)

_Vasilisa comes out with both hands under her apron, holding something very carefully._  
**Vasilisa**: I’m not to show you. So I’m not showing you.  
_She walks back down the passage with her hands still under her apron._  
_You glance back at the bed. The counterpane is made of a hundred bright patches, sewn in ten tidy rows._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): equal groups: 100 patches in 10 equal rows  
> _The counterpane has 100 patches in 10 equal rows. How many patches are in each row?_  
> - ✅ 10 → Yes: 100 ÷ 10 = 10, because 10 × 10 = 100. Ten rows of ten.
> - 100 → 100 is the total for all ten rows. Share 100 into 10 equal rows: 100 ÷ 10 = 10 in each.
> - 1 → One per row would make only 10 patches in all. Since 10 × 10 = 100, each row has ten.
> _For grown-ups: Divided 100 into 10 equal rows to find 10 per row, linking multiplication and division._


#### `reader:a18-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a18-01-complete)

_In the night, Vasilisa’s mother dies. That means her body stopped working, and it will not start again._  
_In the morning the merchant sits at the kitchen table with his hat in his hands, turning it round and round._  
_Vasilisa sits on the bottom stair, wrapped in her mother’s counterpane. Somebody has put it round her._  
_You don’t know what to say, so you don’t say anything. You sit down on the stair beside her._  
_After a while your Nubbin climbs up between you and sits too._  
**Vasilisa**: She said I’m to be good, and not cry too much. I think I’m crying the right amount.  
**You**: I think so too.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): how to support someone who is grieving  
> _Vasilisa is grieving. You don’t know what to say._  
> _What helps most?_  
> - ✅ Stay close, listen, and let them cry → Yes. You don’t need the right words. Being there, and not hurrying the sadness, is the kindest help.
> - Tell her to cheer up → It comes from a kind place, but grief doesn’t work like that. Staying close helps more than telling someone how to feel.
> - Leave her alone so she can forget → Some quiet is fine. But being left alone can feel lonely, and a grieving person doesn’t want to forget. Stay near, and let her cry.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that staying close and listening is the best support for someone who is grieving._

_Under the counterpane, her hand is closed round something small. She holds on to it all day._

## A18-2. Little Doll, Eat

- `a18-02-s1`: Follow Vasilisa down to the cellar with a candle. · _Take the candle from the kitchen shelf and go down after her._
- `a18-02-s2`: Hold the light while Vasilisa feeds the doll, and keep still. · _Keep the candle steady and don’t speak._
- `a18-02-s3`: Learn how to set the food out, and make Vasilisa a promise. · _Lay the morsels in a row in front of the doll, then answer Vasilisa._

#### `reader:a18-02-supper` · [main](main.md#a18-02-supper)

_The days after are grey and quiet. People come with pies, stand in the kitchen, and go away again._  
_At supper Vasilisa eats almost nothing. The best bits go off her plate and into her apron: the crust, the egg, the honey cake._  
**The Merchant**: Eat, little one. You’re getting thin.  
**Vasilisa**: I am eating, Father. Look.  
_She chews a bit of turnip very hard, for him to see. Then she slips off her bench and out of the room._  

> **Lesson** (health, ask): people, like all animals, need food to live and grow  
> _Vasilisa is hiding her food. People, like all animals, need food to live: it gives us energy to move, think and grow._  
> _What will happen if she keeps going without?_  
> - ✅ She will get tired and find it hard to concentrate → Yes. Without enough food the body runs low on energy, and the brain does too. Hungry people tire quickly and think less clearly.
> - She will grow stronger → The opposite. A body needs fuel to grow strong. Skipping meals leaves it weak and tired.
> - Nothing will change → Bodies notice. Without regular meals we feel weak, tired and cross. Her father is right to worry.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that food is the body’s fuel and that going without leaves us tired and unable to concentrate._

_Your Nubbin looks at you. It has noticed the honey cake too._  

**Choices**

- **Take the candle and follow her.**
    - The cellar door is open a crack. Below there is no light at all, and a very small voice.
- **Finish your supper first, then follow.**
    - You finish, because you are hungry, and take the candle down after her.
    - She hasn’t got far. She was waiting in the dark for somebody with a light.

#### `reader:a18-02-supper-done` · [main](main.md#a18-02-supper-done)

**Vasilisa**: Oh. It’s you. Shut the door. Properly, it sticks.  
_You shut it properly. It does stick, like a gate you know, a long way from here._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): multiple-meaning words: stick  
> _Vasilisa says the door “sticks”. The word has several meanings: a stick of wood, to stick a pin in, to stick with glue._  
> _Which meaning fits the door?_  
> - ✅ It doesn’t open or shut easily → Yes. A door that sticks is one that jams and needs a push. Only the sentence around the word tells you which meaning is meant.
> - It is made of sticks → A stick of wood is one meaning, but a door that “sticks” is doing something, not made of something. Look at the verb.
> - It has a pin in it → Sticking a pin is another meaning, but nobody pinned the door. The sentence says shut it properly, so it jams.
> _For grown-ups: Used the surrounding sentence to choose the right meaning of a multiple-meaning word (sticks)._


#### `reader:a18-02-cellar` · [main](main.md#a18-02-cellar)

_Vasilisa kneels on an upturned tub between the potatoes and the pickle crocks, and sets the doll in front of her._  
_From her apron come the crust, the egg and the honey cake. She lays them before the doll in a careful row._  
_Vasilisa has been going without, to save the best bits for the doll. Tonight is the first night you see it._  
**Vasilisa**: Little doll, please eat. I am so sad.  
**Vasilisa**: Mother died, and Father is sad, and everybody brings pies. I don’t want pies. I want to know what to do.  
_For a moment nothing happens. Then the doll’s painted eyes begin to shine, like two drops of lamp oil with a light behind them._  
_The crust is gone, and the egg. Your Nubbin leans forward to see where the honey cake went, and it is gone too._  
**Dollet**: Sadness is heavy, Vasilisa, but you don’t carry it alone. Go up and sleep.  
**Dollet**: Tomorrow you will be a little lighter, and the day after, a little more.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): comfort that doesn’t try to fix feelings  
> _The doll doesn’t say “cheer up”. It says she doesn’t carry the sadness alone, and she’ll feel lighter bit by bit._  
> _Why might that help more?_  
> - ✅ It says the feeling is allowed, will ease, and she has company → Yes. Comfort that accepts a feeling, promises it will ease with time, and stays beside you, is far stronger than an order to cheer up.
> - It makes the sadness go away at once → It doesn’t, and that’s the honest part. Sadness needs time. What changes at once is that Vasilisa isn’t on her own.
> - It gives her something to eat → The food is part of her kindness to the doll. The comfort comes from the words: you’re not alone, and it will get lighter.
> _For grown-ups: Understood that good comfort accepts a feeling, promises it will ease, and stays close._

_Your Nubbin is staring at the doll. The doll, as far as anyone can tell, is staring back._  
**Vasilisa**: She ate. She always eats. Mother said she would.  

**Choices**

- **Keep the candle very still.**
    - The flame doesn’t even lean. Vasilisa notices, and gives you the smallest nod.
- **Let your Nubbin go closer.**
    - Your Nubbin creeps up to the tub and sniffs the doll. The doll doesn’t move, and somehow it is clear that it allows this.

#### `reader:a18-02-cellar-done` · [main](main.md#a18-02-cellar-done)

**Vasilisa**: She won’t help on an empty stomach. Mother said so.  
**Vasilisa**: Nobody should have to.  
_The candle between you makes a small ring of gold on the floor. Beyond the ring, the crocks are only shapes._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, ask): we see because light reaches our eyes  
> _Inside the candle’s ring you can see the crocks clearly. Beyond it you see only shapes._  
> _How do we see anything at all?_  
> - ✅ Light bounces off objects and into our eyes → Yes. The candle gives out light, the light bounces off the crocks, and some of it reaches your eyes. Where little light arrives, little can be seen.
> - Our eyes send out beams to touch things → People once thought so, but it isn’t true. Eyes only receive light. That’s why we can’t see in a room with no light at all.
> - Things glow by themselves in the dark → Only a few things do, like the candle flame itself. Most things are seen by the light that bounces off them.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that we see objects because light bounces off them into our eyes._


#### `reader:a18-02-promise` · [main](main.md#a18-02-promise)

**Vasilisa**: You saw. So now you have to learn it properly, in case I’m ever ill, or away, or I forget.  
**Vasilisa**: The best bits. Not the leftovers. The best bits, and first, before I have mine.  
**Vasilisa**: In a row, so she can see them all. Then you say the words, and you wait. You mustn’t poke her.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): multiplication as equal groups: 3 × 7  
> _Each night you lay out 3 morsels in a row. How many morsels is that over a week of 7 nights?_  
> - ✅ 21 → Yes. 7 nights of 3 morsels is 7 × 3 = 21. Three, six, nine, twelve, fifteen, eighteen, twenty-one.
> - 10 → That’s 7 + 3. Each of the 7 nights has its own 3 morsels, so multiply: 7 × 3 = 21.
> - 37 → That’s the digits side by side. We need 7 equal groups of 3: 7 × 3 = 21.
> _For grown-ups: Used multiplication as equal groups to find 3 morsels × 7 nights = 21._

_She shows you twice. Your Nubbin watches as if there will be a test, which there may be._  
**Vasilisa**: Now you have to promise. Never tell. Not Father, not anybody.  
**Vasilisa**: You’re not “anybody”, you’re the lodger. That’s different. I’ve decided.  

**Choices**

- **Promise. Never, not anybody.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Good. Shake.
    - Her hand is very small and very serious.
- **Cross your heart, and your Nubbin’s too.**
    - You cross your heart, then your Nubbin’s, which tickles it. Vasilisa decides this counts double.
- **Ask why it has to be a secret.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Because people would want her. People always want things that help.
    - **Vasilisa**: And because Mother said so. That’s two reasons. Promise.
    - You promise.

#### `reader:a18-02-promise-done` · [main](main.md#a18-02-promise-done)

_Climbing the cellar steps, you think of a rule you know. It is in your gran’s handwriting, the first of three._  
_Feed them before you feed yourself._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.3-5, notice): sharing food shows respect in many cultures  
> _In many cultures around the world, offering a guest the best food first is a way of showing respect._  
> _Sharing bread, tea or soup says: you are welcome, and you matter._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that sharing food is a sign of respect and welcome in many cultures._

_Night after night, Vasilisa has gone without her own best food so that someone else could eat._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): whether to feed others before yourself  
> _Hazel’s rule says feed them before you feed yourself. Is it always right to put others first?_  
> _What if you were the one who was starving?_  
> - Feed others first, because caring means giving → That’s a fair answer. Giving to others first is a powerful kind of love. Notice that Vasilisa still eats her share of turnip: she is giving, not vanishing.
> - Look after yourself too, or you can’t help anyone → That’s a fair answer. A hungry helper soon can’t help. Many caring people share what they have and still make sure they are fed.
> - It depends on who needs it more → That’s a fair answer. A rule is a good start, and wise people check who needs the food most before they decide.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about whether caring means always putting others first, or looking after yourself too._


#### `reader:a18-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a18-02-complete)

_After that, the best bits of Vasilisa’s supper always go missing. Her father worries that she’s thin._  
_She is, a little. But she sleeps, and in the mornings she sings while she plaits her hair._  

> **Lesson** (music, ask): rhythm and repeated lines in work songs  
> _Vasilisa sings while she works. Work songs often have a steady beat and lines that come round again and again._  
> _Why might that help?_  
> - ✅ A steady beat helps people keep time together, and makes the work lighter → Yes. A beat gives everyone the same rhythm to move to, and repeated lines are easy to join in with, so rowing, weaving or hauling feels smoother.
> - It makes the work faster than it can be done → Not faster than possible. It keeps a steady pace, which helps people last longer.
> - It stops other people hearing → Songs don’t hide anything. The beat is about pace and company.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a steady beat helps people keep time together when working._

## A18-3. The Widow

- `a18-03-s1`: Meet the merchant’s new wife and her daughters. · _Wait in the yard when the cart comes in at the gate._
- `a18-03-s2`: Get up before dawn and see how the work is getting done. · _Go out to the garden before anyone else is up._
- `a18-03-s3`: Help Vasilisa look busy when her stepmother comes round. · _When the back door opens, give Vasilisa something to be doing._

#### `reader:a18-03-cart` · [main](main.md#a18-03-cart)

_The atlas turns a page by itself. When you step back in it is spring, and the birches have their small green coins on._  
**The Merchant**: A house wants a mistress, and a girl wants a mother. I’ve found a widow with two daughters near Vasilisa’s age.  
**The Merchant**: She seemed kind. Mostly she seemed a woman with a lot of trunks.  
_A cart turns in at the gate, piled with trunks. On top sit three people in good shawls, looking at the house as if pricing it._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.D.8, ask): a two-step weight problem  
> _The cart carries 3 trunks of 14 kg each and 2 trunks of 9 kg each. What do the trunks weigh altogether?_  
> - ✅ 60 kg → Yes. 3 × 14 = 42 kg, and 2 × 9 = 18 kg, and 42 + 18 = 60 kg. Two multiplications, then an addition.
> - 23 kg → That’s 14 + 9. Every trunk counts: first 3 × 14 = 42 and 2 × 9 = 18, then add them: 60 kg.
> - 42 kg → That’s only the three big trunks. Add the two small ones, 2 × 9 = 18, and you get 42 + 18 = 60 kg.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a two-step problem: 3 × 14 = 42, 2 × 9 = 18, then 42 + 18 = 60 kg._

**The Stepmother**: So this is the house. And this is the girl.  
_The stepmother looks Vasilisa up and down. Her daughters do the same, one after the other, like an echo._  
**The Elder Sister**: She’s very pretty.  
**The Younger Sister**: Too pretty. It isn’t fair.  
**The Stepmother**: Pretty fades, my loves. Sun and wind and work see to that, and there’s a great deal of work in a house like this.  
_Vasilisa curtsies and says nothing, with one hand in her apron pocket._  

**Choices**

- **Offer to carry a trunk.**
    - **The Stepmother**: The lodger. Yes. You may carry all of them.
    - You carry all of them. Your Nubbin carries a hatbox, very seriously, with its eyes shut.
- **Stand beside Vasilisa and say nothing.**
    - You stand beside her. The stepmother’s eyes rest on you a moment and move on, the way you would look past a chair.

#### `reader:a18-03-cart-done` · [main](main.md#a18-03-cart-done)

**The Stepmother**: Vasilisa will see to the garden, the water, the stove and the floors. A girl should be useful.  
**The Elder Sister**: And we’ll see to the sitting down.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): a character’s motive  
> _Why does the elder sister joke that she will “see to the sitting down”?_  
> - ✅ She wants an easy life and is pleased to see Vasilisa doing the work → Yes. Her joke shows her motive: she enjoys being idle and likes seeing the girl she envies kept busy. What a character says often shows what they want.
> - She is afraid of hard work → That may be part of it, but the way she says it is pleased, not scared. Her words show she likes the arrangement.
> - She wants to help Vasilisa → A helper offers to share. She keeps the sitting for herself, so her motive is comfort and envy.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a character’s motive from what she says: she wants comfort and enjoys the arrangement._

_Vasilisa curtsies again. In her pocket, something small is very still._  

#### `reader:a18-03-dawn` · [main](main.md#a18-03-dawn)

_Before dawn you hear the cellar door. You go down to the garden with your Nubbin, in your socks._  
_Vasilisa is sitting in the shade of the apple tree, making a crown of dandelions._  
_The beds are weeded and the water butts are full. Smoke is rising from the kitchen chimney, and the stove inside is hot._  
**Vasilisa**: Shh. She did it. I fed her last night and asked her, and she said go to sleep. So I did.  
_In the cabbage bed you find the doll, no taller than a weed, pulling up the last dandelion with both arms._  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): plants compete for water and light  
> _Weeds grow among the cabbages. Why does pulling weeds help the cabbages grow?_  
> - ✅ Weeds compete with the cabbages for water, light and space → Yes. Every plant in a bed needs water, light and room. A weed takes some of each, so a weeded bed leaves more for the crop.
> - Weeds are poisonous to cabbages → Most aren’t. The trouble is crowding: weeds use up water, sunlight and soil space the cabbages need.
> - Weeds make the soil too warm → Not warmer. Weeds simply share, or steal, the water, light and space the cabbages need.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that weeds compete with crops for water, light and space, so weeding helps the crop._

_Your Nubbin rushes to help. They fall over backwards together, and the dandelion comes out._  
**Vasilisa**: She’s made me a salve for the sun as well. Out of something. I don’t ask what.  
_Vasilisa puts her dandelion crown on your Nubbin and the doll back in her pocket, and the morning starts._  

#### `reader:a18-03-dawn-done` · [main](main.md#a18-03-dawn-done)

**Vasilisa**: Now I have to look tired. Can you look tired? I can’t. I’ve been asleep.
