# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Atlas, page A18: The Doll in the Pocket

## A18-1. The Counterpane

- `a18-01-s1`: Step into the painted page. Knock at the merchant’s house. · _Touch the page. Then walk up to the wooden house._
- `a18-01-s2`: Walk Vasilisa to her mother’s door. Wait there. · _Stand at the door with your Nubbin. Don’t go in._

#### `sprout:a18-01-house` · [main](main.md#a18-01-house)

_The atlas opens to a painted page. It is yellow and black._  
_Pop! You step inside. A wooden house, with snow at its edges._  
_A big man in a fur hat opens the door._  
**The Merchant**: Come in! A friend of Nova’s can stay with us.  
**The Merchant**: My wife is poorly. We walk softly here.  
_A little girl peeks round his knee. She has a long plait._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): first sound of a name  
> **The Merchant**: This is my girl, Vasilisa. Vvv-asilisa.  
> _(Say the first sound together: vvv. Now try: van, vase, vine.)_  
> Check: `edu:say:first-sound:v`
> _For grown-ups: Said the first sound of a character’s name (v) and thought of other words that start with it._

**Vasilisa**: What is it? Does it eat?  

**Choices**

- **Say it is a Nubbin. It eats everything.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Everything? Even burnt porridge?
    - Your Nubbin nods. Burnt bits are the best bits.
- **Let your Nubbin say hello.**
    - Your Nubbin sits on her shoe. She goes very still.
- **Ask her name first.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Manners say you go first. But I will tell you anyway.

#### `sprout:a18-01-house-done` · [main](main.md#a18-01-house-done)

**Vasilisa**: I am Vasilisa. I will be kind to guests. Mostly.  
**The Merchant**: Go and wash your hands, Vasilisa.  
_She goes up slowly. She keeps looking back at your Nubbin._  
_The house is made of big logs, laid one on another._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, ask): long ago, houses were often made of wood  
> _This house is made of logs. Where do logs come from?_  
> - ✅ Trees → Yes! Long ago, many houses were built from tree logs.
> - Clouds → Not clouds. Logs come from trees. A forest gave this house its walls.
> - The sea → Not the sea. Logs come from trees, cut from the forest.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that long ago many houses were built from logs, which come from trees._


#### `sprout:a18-01-counterpane` · [main](main.md#a18-01-counterpane)

_That evening, a soft voice calls Vasilisa._  
_You walk her to the bedroom door. You wait there._  
_Her mother sits up in bed. Her blanket is made of bright scraps._  
**Vasilisa’s Mother**: Vasilisa, I am very poorly. I will not get better.  
_She takes out a little doll with a red scarf._  
**Vasilisa’s Mother**: This doll is my love, in a small shape. Keep her close.  
**Vasilisa’s Mother**: When you need help, give her food first. Then ask her.  
**Vasilisa’s Mother**: Tell nobody about her, and she will help you.  
**Vasilisa**: Food first. Then ask. Tell nobody.  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, ask): naming a big feeling  
> _Vasilisa’s mother is very poorly. How might Vasilisa feel?_  
> - ✅ Sad and scared → Yes. Vasilisa feels sad and scared. Those feelings are allowed.
> - Silly and giggly → Not giggly. Look at her face. She is sad and scared.
> - Hungry for pie → Not pie. Her heart is heavy. She is sad and scared.
> _For grown-ups: Named the feelings (sad and scared) that someone has when a person they love is very ill._

_Her mother looks at you. She does not send you away._  

**Choices**

- **Stay by the door.**
    - You stay. Your Nubbin sits on your foot and keeps still.
- **Step back and give them room.**
    - Through the gap, her mother lays a hand on Vasilisa’s head.
- **Nod: you will look after her.**
    - **Vasilisa’s Mother**: Thank you. She will need a friend.

#### `sprout:a18-01-counterpane-done` · [main](main.md#a18-01-counterpane-done)

_Vasilisa comes out. Her hands are under her apron._  
**Vasilisa**: I am not to show you. So I am not showing you.  
_She walks away slowly, holding her hands close._  
_You look back at the bed. The blanket has six bright patches._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count patches to six  
> _(Count the patches: two red, one blue, three yellow.)_  
> _One, two, three, four, five, six. Six patches!_  
> Check: `edu:count:patches:6`
> _For grown-ups: Counted a small group of patches to find how many there are altogether (six)._


#### `sprout:a18-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a18-01-complete)

_In the night, Vasilisa’s mother dies._  
_That means her body stopped working. It will not start again._  
_In the morning, the merchant sits with his hat in his hands._  
_Vasilisa sits on the bottom stair. Someone wrapped her mother’s blanket round her._  
_You do not know what to say. So you sit beside her._  
_Your Nubbin climbs up and sits too._  
**Vasilisa**: I am sad. I think I am crying the right amount.  
**You**: I think so too.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): what helps someone who is sad  
> _Vasilisa is very sad. What helps a sad friend?_  
> - ✅ Sit beside them → Yes. Sitting close shows you care. You do not need clever words.
> - Say stop crying → Crying is allowed. Sitting beside them helps much more.
> - Walk away → A sad friend feels better when someone stays. Sit beside them.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that sitting beside a sad friend is a good way to help, and that crying is allowed._

_Her hand is closed round something small. She holds on all day._  

## A18-2. Little Doll, Eat

- `a18-02-s1`: Follow Vasilisa down to the cellar with a candle. · _Take the candle from the kitchen shelf and go after her._
- `a18-02-s2`: Hold the light while Vasilisa feeds the doll. Keep still. · _Keep the candle steady. Don’t speak._
- `a18-02-s3`: Learn how to set out the food. Make Vasilisa a promise. · _Lay the food in a row in front of the doll. Then answer Vasilisa._

#### `sprout:a18-02-supper` · [main](main.md#a18-02-supper)

_Days pass. People bring pies, then go home._  
_At supper, Vasilisa eats almost nothing._  
_The best bits go into her apron: a crust, an egg, a honey cake._  
**The Merchant**: Eat, little one. You are getting thin.  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, ask): bodies need food and water to be strong  
> _Vasilisa is not eating much. What does a body need to feel strong?_  
> - ✅ Food and water → Yes! Good food and water give a body energy to play, work and grow.
> - Only pie → Pie is yummy. But a body needs many foods, and water too.
> - Nothing at all → Everybody needs food and water. Without them we feel weak.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about what a body needs to feel strong: food and water._

**Vasilisa**: I am eating, Father. Look!  
_She chews a tiny bit of turnip. Then she slips away._  
_Your Nubbin looks at you. It saw the honey cake too._  

**Choices**

- **Take the candle and follow her.**
    - The cellar door is open a crack. Below, a very small voice.
- **Finish your supper, then follow.**
    - You finish. Then you take the candle down after her.
    - She was waiting in the dark for somebody with a light.

#### `sprout:a18-02-supper-done` · [main](main.md#a18-02-supper-done)

**Vasilisa**: Oh! It is you. Shut the door. Properly, it sticks.  
_You shut it properly. Thud! It does stick._  
_Door. Floor. They sound the same at the end._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): rhyming words  
> _(Say door and floor. Can you find another word that rhymes? More? Store?)_  
> Check: `edu:say:rhyme:door`
> _For grown-ups: Listened for the matching end sound in rhyming words (door, floor, more)._


#### `sprout:a18-02-cellar` · [main](main.md#a18-02-cellar)

_Vasilisa kneels between the potatoes and the pickle jars._  
_She sets the doll down. Before it, she lays the crust, egg and cake._  
**Vasilisa**: Little doll, please eat. I am very sad.  
**Vasilisa**: Mother died. Father is sad. What do I do?  
_The doll’s painted eyes begin to glow, soft and warm._  
_The food is gone. Your Nubbin leans in, and the cake is gone too!_  
**Dollet**: Do not be afraid, Vasilisa. You do not carry this alone.  
**Dollet**: Go up and sleep. Tomorrow you will feel a little lighter.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): how to help a sad friend  
> _Vasilisa feels sad and alone. What helps?_  
> - ✅ Stay close and listen → Yes. Staying close and listening is how the doll helps.
> - Say hurry up → Sad feelings need time. Staying close and listening helps.
> - Go away → Going away leaves a sad friend alone. Stay close and listen.
> _For grown-ups: Practised choosing to stay close and listen when a friend is sad._

_Your Nubbin stares at the doll. The doll stares back._  
**Vasilisa**: She ate! She always eats. Mother said she would.  

**Choices**

- **Keep the candle very still.**
    - The flame does not even lean. Vasilisa gives you a tiny nod.
- **Let your Nubbin go closer.**
    - Your Nubbin sniffs the doll. The doll lets it.

#### `sprout:a18-02-cellar-done` · [main](main.md#a18-02-cellar-done)

**Vasilisa**: She will not help on an empty tummy. Mother said so.  
**Vasilisa**: Nobody should have to.  
_Your candle makes a little ring of light in the dark cellar._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-2, notice): we see things when there is light  
> _A candle makes light. Light helps us see._  
> _In a dark cellar, we cannot see much._  
> _Candles are hot, so only grown-ups hold them._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that we see things because there is light, and that a candle flame is hot._


#### `sprout:a18-02-promise` · [main](main.md#a18-02-promise)

**Vasilisa**: You saw. Now you must learn it properly.  
**Vasilisa**: The best bits. First, before I have mine.  
**Vasilisa**: Put them in a row, so she can see them all.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count three things in a row  
> _(Lay three things in a row: a crust, an egg, a cake. Count them.)_  
> _One, two, three. Three morsels for the doll!_  
> Check: `edu:count:morsels:3`
> _For grown-ups: Laid out objects in a row and counted them to find how many (three)._

_She shows you twice. Your Nubbin watches as if there will be a test._  
**Vasilisa**: Now promise. Never tell. Not Father. Not anybody.  
**Vasilisa**: You are not anybody. You are my guest. I decided.  

**Choices**

- **Promise. Never, not anybody.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Good. Shake.
    - Her hand is very small and very serious.
- **Cross your heart, and your Nubbin’s too.**
    - Your Nubbin giggles. Vasilisa says that counts double.
- **Ask why it is a secret.**
    - **Vasilisa**: People want things that help. And Mother said so. Promise.
    - You promise.

#### `sprout:a18-02-promise-done` · [main](main.md#a18-02-promise-done)

_Climbing the steps, you think of a rule in Gran Hazel’s writing._  
_Feed them before you feed yourself._  
_Vasilisa gives her own best food away._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): sharing food is a way of caring  
> _Why is sharing food a kind of care?_  
> - ✅ It shows we care about them → Yes. Sharing food says: you matter to me.
> - It is only a rule → A rule is not the reason. Caring is. Sharing says: you matter.
> - It makes food bigger → Food gets smaller when it is shared. But hearts get warmer.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about how sharing food shows that we care about someone._


#### `sprout:a18-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a18-02-complete)

_After that, the best bits of supper go missing._  
_Vasilisa is a little thin. But she sleeps well._  
_In the mornings she sings while she plaits her hair._  

> **Lesson** (music, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, do): feel a steady beat  
> _Vasilisa’s song has a steady beat, like a heartbeat._  
> _(Pat your knees to the beat: pat, pat, pat, pat.)_  
> Check: `edu:clap:beat:song`
> _For grown-ups: Patted a steady beat along with a song._

_Some nights you take the napkin down yourself._  
_You lay out the food in a row. You wait, and do not poke._  
_The eyes glow. The food is gone._  
_Your Nubbin checks the empty napkin every single time._

## A18-3. The Widow

- `a18-03-s1`: Meet the merchant’s new wife and her daughters. · _Wait in the yard when the cart comes in at the gate._
- `a18-03-s2`: Get up before dawn. See how the work is getting done. · _Go out to the garden before anyone else is up._
- `a18-03-s3`: Help Vasilisa look busy when her stepmother comes round. · _When the back door opens, give Vasilisa something to be doing._

#### `sprout:a18-03-cart` · [main](main.md#a18-03-cart)

_The atlas turns a page. It is spring, and the birches are green._  
**The Merchant**: I have found a new wife. She has two daughters.  
_A cart rolls in, piled with trunks. Three people sit on top._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): heavier and lighter  
> _The trunks are huge. The hatbox is tiny._  
> _Which is heavier to carry?_  
> - ✅ The big trunk → Yes! The big trunk is heavier. The hatbox is light.
> - The tiny hatbox → Look again. The big trunk is much heavier. The hatbox is light.
> _For grown-ups: Compared two objects by weight: the big trunk is heavier, the little hatbox lighter._

**The Stepmother**: So this is the house. And this is the girl.  
**The Elder Sister**: She is very pretty.  
**The Younger Sister**: Too pretty. It is not fair!  
**The Stepmother**: Pretty fades, my loves. Sun and wind and work will see to that.  
_Vasilisa curtsies and says nothing. One hand is in her pocket._  

**Choices**

- **Offer to carry a trunk.**
    - **The Stepmother**: The guest may carry all of them.
    - You carry them all. Your Nubbin carries a hatbox, eyes shut.
- **Stand beside Vasilisa.**
    - You stand beside her. The stepmother looks past you.

#### `sprout:a18-03-cart-done` · [main](main.md#a18-03-cart-done)

**The Stepmother**: Vasilisa will do the garden, the water, the stove and the floors.  
**The Elder Sister**: And we will do the sitting down.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, ask): words and voices that show feelings  
> _The elder sister says it in a sneery voice. How does she sound?_  
> - ✅ Unkind → Yes. A sneery voice sounds unkind. Words and voices show feelings.
> - Kind → Listen again. A sneery voice is not kind. It sounds unkind.
> - Sleepy → Not sleepy. A sneery voice sounds unkind.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed how a voice and words can show a feeling, such as unkindness._

_Vasilisa curtsies again. In her pocket, something small is very still._  

#### `sprout:a18-03-dawn` · [main](main.md#a18-03-dawn)

_Before dawn you creep to the garden in your socks._  
_Vasilisa sits under the apple tree making a flower crown._  
_The beds are weeded. The water barrels are full._  
_Smoke curls from the kitchen chimney. The stove is hot._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, ask): plants need water and light  
> _The garden beds are full of plants. What do plants need to grow?_  
> - ✅ Water and sunlight → Yes! Plants drink water and use sunlight to grow.
> - Only sweets → Plants do not eat sweets. They need water and sunlight.
> - Nothing at all → Every plant needs water and sunlight to grow.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that plants need water and sunlight to grow._

**Vasilisa**: Shh! I fed the doll and asked. She said sleep, so I did.  
_In the cabbage bed, the doll pulls up the last dandelion._  
_Your Nubbin helps, and they tumble backwards. Pop! It comes out._  
_Vasilisa puts the crown on your Nubbin. The morning begins._  

#### `sprout:a18-03-dawn-done` · [main](main.md#a18-03-dawn-done)

**Vasilisa**: Now I have to look tired. Can you look tired? I cannot.  
_Vasilisa keeps a happy secret. It keeps her safe._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, notice): happy secrets and secrets that scare you  
> _A happy secret is fine. It is like a surprise party._  
> _But if a secret makes you scared or sad, tell a grown-up you trust._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that happy secrets are fine, and that a secret that feels scary should be told to a trusted grown-up._


#### `sprout:a18-03-busy` · [main](main.md#a18-03-busy)

_The back door bangs. The stepmother comes out with her daughters._  
**The Stepmother**: Is the garden done? Is the water in? I do not think so.  
_Vasilisa holds a flower crown and nothing else. She looks at you._  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, ask): sun safety  
> _Working in the sun all day can make skin hot and sore. What helps?_  
> - ✅ A hat, shade and water → Yes! A hat, a shady rest and a drink keep you safe.
> - Nothing at all → Too much sun can hurt. A hat, some shade and water help.
> - A big winter coat → Too hot! A hat, some shade and water are better.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a hat, shade and water help keep us safe when we are out in the sun._

**The Elder Sister**: She was picking flowers! She has pollen on her nose!  
**The Stepmother**: Where is your hoe, girl? Where is your bucket?  
_You have about one second._  

**Choices**

- **Hand her an empty bucket.**
    - She swings it as if it were full. A very good act.
    - **The Stepmother**: Hm. Carry on, then.
- **Splash water on her apron.**
    - Splash! She looks wet and tired. Perfect.
    - **The Younger Sister**: She is all wet! Ha!
- **Make your Nubbin stand still like a weed.**
    - Your Nubbin freezes in the cabbages. The stepmother walks right past.

#### `sprout:a18-03-busy-done` · [main](main.md#a18-03-busy-done)

_The stepmother walks round the garden. She finds nothing wrong._  
_This makes her crosser than anything._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): sharing jobs fairly  
> _In a house, who should do the jobs?_  
> _Should one person do all of them?_  
> - ✅ Everybody shares the jobs → Yes. Sharing the jobs is fair. Many hands make light work.
> - One person does everything → That is not fair. One person gets worn out. Sharing is fair.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about fairness: in a home, jobs should be shared._

**The Stepmother**: Sun and wind. Every day. We shall see.  

#### `sprout:a18-03-complete` · [main](main.md#a18-03-complete)

_Spring turns into summer. Every morning the work is done._  
_Every evening the best bits go down to the cellar._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-ESS1-2, ask): summer days are longer than winter days  
> _It is summer now. Are summer days longer or shorter than winter days?_  
> - ✅ Longer → Yes! In summer the sun stays up longer. There is more daylight.
> - Shorter → Winter days are the short ones. Summer days are longer.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that summer days have more daylight than winter days._

_The sisters sit in the shade. They grow pale and sour._  
_Vasilisa works in the sun, or seems to. She grows strong._  
**The Elder Sister**: It is not fair. She works and gets prettier.  
_Your Nubbin, napping in the cabbages, looks well too._  

## A18-4. Suitors

- `a18-04-s1`: Step back into the page, some years on. · _Turn the atlas page. Walk up the path to the gate._
- `a18-04-s2`: See what the stepmother says to the young men at the gate. · _Stand near the gate and listen._
- `a18-04-s3`: Hold the gate for the last young man. See him go. · _Open the gate when he knocks. You cannot let him in._

#### `sprout:a18-04-years` · [main](main.md#a18-04-years)

_The atlas turns many pages. Whoosh, like birds taking off._  
_The birches are twice as tall now. The swing is too small._  
_Vasilisa comes down the path. She is grown up, taller than you._  
**Vasilisa**: You have not changed at all! Not a hair!  
_Your Nubbin walks round her twice, puzzled._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): taller and shorter  
> _Vasilisa was small. Now she is tall._  
> _Who is taller now, you or Vasilisa?_  
> - ✅ Vasilisa → Yes! She is taller than you now. She has grown.
> - You → Look again. Vasilisa has grown. She is taller than you now.
> _For grown-ups: Compared two heights and saw that a person who has grown is taller._

**Vasilisa**: I am glad. Everything else changes. It is nice when something stays.  
**Vasilisa**: Stay a while? Young men keep knocking at the gate.  

**Choices**

- **Ask who is knocking.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Young men. For me.
    - **Vasilisa**: Not for my sisters.
    - **Vasilisa**: One brought a goose. He hoped the goose would do the talking.
    - Your Nubbin looks round for the goose. It has time for geese.
- **Ask quietly about her pocket.**
    - **Vasilisa**: Still there. She says hello.
    - The doll did not say it. Vasilisa is being kind.

#### `sprout:a18-04-years-done` · [main](main.md#a18-04-years-done)

_Behind her, down at the gate, somebody is knocking._  
_Knock, knock, knock! Wood makes a sound when it is tapped._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, do): tapping makes sound; hard is louder  
> _(Tap a table softly. Then tap it hard._  
> _Which sound is louder?)_  
> Check: `edu:tap:sound:soft-loud`
> _For grown-ups: Tapped softly and hard and heard that harder taps make louder sounds._


#### `sprout:a18-04-gate` · [main](main.md#a18-04-gate)

_At the gate stands a young man in a clean shirt._  
**The Stepmother**: Who is it for? Not for my girls?  
_He goes pink. He says Vasilisa’s name._  
**The Stepmother**: Go home. The youngest does not marry before the elders.  
**The Stepmother**: That is the custom in this house.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, ask): a custom is a way people always do things  
> _A custom is something a family does again and again. Which is a custom?_  
> - ✅ Saying goodnight every evening → Yes! Goodnight every evening is a custom. People keep customs for years.
> - Dropping a spoon once → Dropping a spoon was an accident. A custom happens again and again.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a custom is something a family or village does again and again._

_He goes. The next young man does not get a word in._  
**The Elder Sister**: Nobody comes for us. It is her face.  
**The Younger Sister**: It is her fault for having it.  
_Upstairs, a window closes very quietly._  

#### `sprout:a18-04-gate-done` · [main](main.md#a18-04-gate-done)

**The Stepmother**: Guest. You will keep that gate tomorrow. Nobody comes in for her.  
_Gate. Wait. Late. They all rhyme._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): rhyming words  
> _(Say gate, wait, late. Can you add one more? Plate? Skate?)_  
> Check: `edu:say:rhyme:gate`
> _For grown-ups: Said rhyming words that end like gate and added one of their own._


#### `sprout:a18-04-last` · [main](main.md#a18-04-last)

_The next evening one more comes. He is younger, with mended sleeves._  
_He holds a bunch of blue cornflowers._  
**A Young Man**: Is she in? I walked from the mill. I only wanted to ask.  
_The stepmother watches from one window. Vasilisa watches from another._  
**A Young Man**: I suppose it is no. It was always going to be no.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): being kind to someone who is disappointed  
> _The young man is sad. He walked a long way._  
> _What could you do?_  
> - ✅ Be kind to him → Yes. A kind word helps when someone is sad.
> - Laugh at him → Laughing hurts. A kind word helps a sad person.
> - Pretend not to see → He is sad, and you can see it. A kind word helps.
> _For grown-ups: Chose kindness towards someone who is disappointed._

_Your Nubbin holds the gate with both paws, as if that could help._  

**Choices**

- **Say she would like the cornflowers.**
    - **A Young Man**: Then give them to her. Do not say who.
    - He goes off lighter than he came.
- **Say nothing. Hold the gate.**
    - He nods to you like a friend. He walks away.
- **Whisper about the market on Thursdays.**
    - He grins. The window bangs open.
    - **The Stepmother**: I heard that!
    - He runs. Your Nubbin runs after him, to be kind.

#### `sprout:a18-04-last-done` · [main](main.md#a18-04-last-done)

_Later, in the cellar, Vasilisa thanks you for minding the gate._

#### `sprout:a18-16-palace` · [main](main.md#a18-16-palace)

**Vasilisa**: Sell it, Granny. Keep the money for both of us.  
**The Old Woman**: Sell cloth like this? No. It is a gift, or it is nothing.  
_You walk to the palace. The old woman carries her linen like treasure._  
_She walks under the windows. A window opens, and a man looks out._  
_He is the Tsar, in his slippers._  
**The Tsar**: What do you have there, old mother?  
**The Old Woman**: A wonder. I will show it only to you.  
_He holds the linen up. The light goes through it._  
**The Tsar**: What do you want for it?  
**The Old Woman**: Nothing. It is a gift.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): a gift is given freely  
> _The old woman gives the linen as a gift. What is a gift?_  
> - ✅ Something given freely, asking nothing back → Yes! A gift is given freely, with nothing asked back.
> - Something you swap for money → That is selling. A gift is given freely.
> _For grown-ups: Learned the difference between a gift (given freely) and something sold._


**Choices**

- **Bow, as everyone else does.**
    - You bow. Your Nubbin bows too and slides off the linen.
- **Keep your Nubbin off the linen.**
    - You catch your Nubbin as it reaches for a corner. The Tsar laughs.

#### `sprout:a18-16-tsar` · [main](main.md#a18-16-tsar)

_The Tsar puts on one shirt. He sends for the seamstress._  
**Vasilisa**: Me? At the palace? Like this?  
**The Old Woman**: Like that. Wash your face. It is a very good face.  
_On the steps she touches her pocket, then takes her hand out, steadier._  
**The Tsar**: You made these? All of them?  
**Vasilisa**: Yes, Majesty. With help.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): honesty and sharing the thanks  
> _Vasilisa says: with help. Why is that a good thing to say?_  
> - ✅ It is true, and it shares the thanks → Yes. Honest people share the thanks with those who helped.
> - It makes the shirts bigger → Not bigger. It is honest, and it shares the thanks.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about honesty: saying who helped, and sharing the thanks._

**The Tsar**: Then I do not want to let you go. Would you mind very much?  
**Vasilisa**: I would not mind at all.  

**Choices**

- **Cheer, out loud, in a palace.**
    - You cheer. Your Nubbin runs round the throne room twice.
- **Just squeeze her hand.**
    - You squeeze her hand. She squeezes back so hard it hurts, and laughs.

#### `sprout:a18-16-complete` · [main](main.md#a18-16-complete)

_In spring the merchant comes home over the hill._  
**The Merchant**: Married? My Vasilisa? To whom? To the… oh. Oh, my goodness.  
_He finds what he promised: a silver thimble. It goes in her other pocket._  
**The Merchant**: Did Nova say who won?  
**You**: She says it was a draw. Nobody won.  
**The Merchant**: That is just what a winner would say.  
_She takes him to live with her, and the old woman too._  
_Your Nubbin carries Baba Yaga’s bag of oats down the palace steps._  
_Last of all, Vasilisa gives your Nubbin a little painted doll in a pouch._  
**Vasilisa**: The real one stays with me. This one just needs looking after.  
_Your Nubbin looks at the pouch, then her pocket. It thinks that is fair._  
_The tale ends the old way: the little doll stayed in her pocket, always._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Literacy, ask): the main idea of a story  
> _What is this story mostly about?_  
> - ✅ Caring for others → Yes! Vasilisa feeds and cares for others, and care comes back to her.
> - Winning a race → There was no race. The story is about caring for others.
> - A lost hat → A lost hat? Ha ha, plop! The story is about caring for others.
> _For grown-ups: Named the main idea of the story: caring for others._

_The page settles under your hand. You are back in the Grove._  
_The doll goes in with the rest of its collection, in your coat._  
_Your coat pocket has stopped trying._
