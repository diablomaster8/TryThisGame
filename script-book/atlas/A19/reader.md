# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A19: The Dragon Who Fell

## A19-1. This Is Berk

- `a19-01-s1`: Come ashore on Berk and find somewhere to sleep. · _Touch the page, then walk up the jetty to the boy counting barrels._
- `a19-01-s2`: Keep the forge fire hot while the raid goes on outside. · _Pump the bellows in time. The fire wants long, steady pushes._
- `a19-01-s3`: Help Hiccup get his home-made launcher out of the forge. · _Take one end of the launcher and wheel it out of the forge door._

#### `reader:a19-01-arrive` · [main](main.md#a19-01-arrive)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page drawn in charcoal. You blink, and you and your Nubbin are on a rocking boat in the spray._  
_Ahead, an island of cliffs and wooden houses climbs out of the sea. There are sheep on every ledge that will hold one._  
_On the jetty, a skinny boy in a fur waistcoat is counting barrels, badly, because he keeps looking at the sky._  
**Hiccup**: Welcome to Berk. It’s far to the north, it rains nearly every day, and the wind has never once stopped.  
**Hiccup**: I’m Hiccup. Yes, it’s a real name in my story. No, I didn’t pick it.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): Berk is invented, but the Vikings were real and settled cold islands  
> _Berk is made up, so you will not find it on a map. But the Vikings were real people._  
> _They sailed from Scandinavia in long, narrow ships, from the late 700s to the 1060s._  
> _Real Norse families settled on cold, windy islands like Shetland, the Faroes and Iceland._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Berk is invented but the Vikings were real, and that Norse families settled on cold islands such as Shetland, the Faroes and Iceland._

_A big man stumps down the jetty with a hammer in one hand and a hook where the other should be._  
**Gobber**: Iron for the forge, at last! And what’s that?  
_Your Nubbin, who has been sick twice over the side, peers up at him._  
**Gobber**: A hatchling, from the south by the look of it. No, don’t tell me. If I don’t know what it is, I don’t have to do anything about it.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.2.4, ask): working out an unknown word from the words round it  
> _Gobber calls your Nubbin a “hatchling”, and an egg hatches. What do you think a hatchling is?_  
> - ✅ A young creature that has just come out of an egg → Yes. The word hides a clue: hatch, plus -ling, which means a small young one.
> - A little door in a roof → A hatch can be a door, but Gobber is talking about your Nubbin. Think of eggs: a hatchling hatches.
> - A kind of hat → It sounds like it, but look at the clue “from the south” and the egg link. A hatchling has just hatched.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out the meaning of “hatchling” from its parts and the story._


**Choices**

- **Tell him it’s a Nubbin.**
    - **Gobber**: A Nubbin. Right. Never heard of one, so it can’t be on the list of things to hit.
- **Let your Nubbin wave a wing at him.**
    - Your Nubbin waves one stubby wing. Gobber waves his hook back, gravely, as one professional to another.
- **Say nothing at all.**
    - **Hiccup**: That’s the right answer with Gobber. He likes a mystery he doesn’t have to solve.

#### `reader:a19-01-arrive-done` · [main](main.md#a19-01-arrive-done)

**Gobber**: You’ll want work. Everyone on Berk works, or gets eaten. There’s a loft over the forge, and bellows with nobody on them.  
**Hiccup**: Welcome to the forge. Mind the sword rack. And the axe rack. And the ceiling.  
_A forge is a workshop with a very hot fire, where a smith heats iron until it can be hammered into shape._  
_The loft is warm and smells of smoke and hot metal. Your Nubbin makes a nest in a pile of old sacks._  
_Down below, crates of iron bars wait to be unpacked._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): multiply equal groups: 3 × 12  
> _The boat brought 3 crates of iron bars, and each crate holds 12 bars. How many bars is that altogether?_  
> - ✅ 36 → Yes! Twelve, and twelve, and twelve: 12 + 12 + 12 = 36. Three groups of twelve.
> - 15 → That is 3 + 12. But there are three whole crates of twelve. Try 12 + 12 + 12.
> - 24 → That is two crates. We have three: 12 + 12 + 12 = 36.
> _For grown-ups: Multiplied 3 equal groups of 12 to find 36._


#### `reader:a19-01-raid` · [main](main.md#a19-01-raid)

_You wake in the dark to horns, shouting, and a red light under the loft door that is not morning._  
_Out in the lane a roof is burning. Something with wings lifts a sheep off a wall, and the sheep looks more surprised than anyone._  
**Gobber**: Keeper! Bellows! Every axe on Berk comes back blunt by midnight, and every one of them wants an edge!  
_You pump, and the fire roars white. Your Nubbin sits on the end of the anvil, flinching at every bang from outside._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-4, ask): a fire needs air to burn, and burning cannot be undone  
> _The fire roars white when you pump the bellows. What are the bellows giving the fire?_  
> - ✅ Air, which a fire needs in order to burn → Yes! Fire eats air as well as wood. Blow more in, and it burns hotter and whiter. And burnt is burnt. Ash never turns back into wood.
> - More wood → The wood is already in there. The bellows push in air, and that is what the fire is hungry for.
> - Water → Water would put the fire out. Bellows give it air.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a fire needs air to burn, which is why a forge uses bellows, and that burning cannot be undone._

**Hiccup**: Sorry I’m late. I was out there, briefly. Everybody told me to come back in here, very firmly.  
**Gobber**: Because out there you’re a danger to yourself and everyone near you. In here you’re only a danger to me.  
**Hiccup**: That’s fair. That’s actually fair.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): how kindness changes the way we hear a hard truth  
> _Gobber told Hiccup a hard truth, and Hiccup said it was fair. Is a hard truth easier to hear when someone says it kindly?_  
> - Yes, kindness helps us listen → That’s a fair answer. When we feel safe, we can take in what is true, rather than defending ourselves.
> - No, the truth is the truth, however it is said → That’s a fair answer. The words matter most, but how they are said can decide whether we are able to hear them.
> - It depends on the person → That’s a fair answer. People do differ, and a good friend learns how each person likes to be told.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about whether a hard truth is easier to hear when it is said kindly._

_Vikings run in with blunt axes and run out with sharp ones. Nobody looks at Hiccup, except to step round him._  

#### `reader:a19-01-raid-done` · [main](main.md#a19-01-raid-done)

_By the end your arms are jelly. Your Nubbin has fallen asleep on the anvil, which nobody else on Berk could do._  

> **Lesson** (health, 4-LS1-1, notice): tired muscles need rest, food and water to recover  
> _Your arms feel like jelly because your muscles have used up the energy stored in them._  
> _Rest, water and food let them recover. Each time you work them, they get a little stronger._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned why muscles feel tired after hard work and that rest, food and water help them recover._

**Gobber**: Not bad, for the help. Hiccup, stay here. Stay. Here.  
_Hiccup holds up his hands. They are black with soot to the elbow._  
**Hiccup**: I am staying. Look. Staying very hard.  
_Outside the horns stop. The raid is over, and the village begins to count what is missing._  

#### `reader:a19-01-launcher` · [main](main.md#a19-01-launcher)

_The moment Gobber is out of the door, Hiccup is under his workbench, pulling a sheet off something with wheels._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): wheels roll, so there is less rubbing than dragging  
> _Drag something heavy along the ground and friction rubs against it the whole way._  
> _A wheel rolls instead of rubbing, so the same load is much easier to move._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that wheels reduce friction, which makes heavy loads easier to move._

_It is a wooden frame with a crank, and three stones tied together on ropes, packed into its jaws like peas in a pod._  
**Hiccup**: Bola launcher. I made it. The ropes wrap round the wings, the dragon comes down, and I stop being… whatever they call me.  
**You**: What do they call you?  
**Hiccup**: Mostly they don’t. That’s the problem.  
**Hiccup**: It needs to go up on the cliff. It’s heavy, it’s badly balanced, and it will probably go off on the way.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): what makes it easier to ask for help  
> _Hiccup needs help carrying something heavy, but he finds it hard to ask. What helps someone ask?_  
> - ✅ Knowing a friend will say yes and not laugh → That’s it. It’s the laughing I’m afraid of, more than the asking.
> - Pretending you need nothing → I’ve tried that. You end up carrying everything alone, and it’s heavier than it looks.
> - Doing it all by yourself → Sometimes that works. Not with a launcher. It has opinions.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about what makes it easier to ask for help: a friend who says yes and does not laugh._

_Your Nubbin, awake now, looks at the launcher, then at the open door, then at you._  

**Choices**

- **Take the back end.**
    - You take the back end. It is heavier than it looks, and it creaks in a way that sounds like a warning.
    - **Hiccup**: Thank you. Nobody ever takes the back end.
- **Gobber said to stay here.**
    - **Hiccup**: Gobber also says the soup is fish. It isn’t always fish.
    - He starts pushing it out on his own. You find you have taken the back end anyway.

#### `reader:a19-01-launcher-done` · [main](main.md#a19-01-launcher-done)

_Out into the lane you go, past a burning cart and a Viking chasing his own helmet down the hill._  
_The launcher wobbles at every stone. One wheel squeaks like a mouse being stepped on._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): what a simile tells us  
> _“One wheel squeaks like a mouse being stepped on.” What does this comparison tell you about the squeak?_  
> - ✅ That it is thin, high and a little pitiful → Yes. A simile compares two things using “like” or “as”, and picks the part that matters: the small, high, pitiful sound.
> - That the wheel is very small → Not quite. The comparison is about the sound, not the size of the wheel.
> - That there is a real mouse under the wheel → Ha, no! A simile says one thing is like another, so the squeak is only like a squashed mouse.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out what a simile says about a sound._

**Hiccup**: Left! Your left! It likes to go left. It’s got opinions.  
_Your Nubbin rides on top, holding on with both paws, looking at the sky._  
_Behind you, the shouting of the raid is a long way off, and getting longer._  

#### `reader:a19-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a19-01-complete)

_You get the launcher up the slope behind the forge, to a lip of rock over the dark sea._  
**Hiccup**: There. Now we wait for the one nobody sees.  
_Hiccup kneels behind the crank. His hands are shaking a little, but he holds them still on purpose._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, notice): slow breathing helps when we are nervous  
> _Nerves are a body alarm: the heart races, the hands shake, the breath goes short._  
> _Slow breaths tell the alarm to quieten. Try in for four counts and out for six, a few times._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that nerves are a body alarm and that slow breathing, with a longer breath out, helps it calm._

**Hiccup**: If I hit it, they’ll have to look at me. That’s all I want. One look.  
_Out over the water, very high, something screams like tearing cloth._  
_Hiccup breathes in slowly, and out. Whatever comes next, he is going to meet it with steady hands._  

### Glossary

- **hatchling**: A baby animal that has just come out of its egg.
- **jetty**: A small pier of wood or stone that sticks out into the water, where boats tie up.
- **bellows**: A squeezable bag or pump that blows air onto a fire to make it hotter.
- **anvil**: A heavy block of iron that a smith hammers hot metal on.
- **bola**: A set of weighted ropes thrown to tangle up an animal.
- **crank**: A handle you turn round to make a machine work.

## A19-2. The Night Fury

- `a19-02-s1`: Watch the sky with Hiccup, and see where the shot goes. · _Stay by the launcher on the cliff and keep your eyes on the stars._
- `a19-02-s2`: Get Hiccup down to the village in one piece. · _Run for the torch posts in the square and keep behind them._
- `a19-02-s3`: Stand by Hiccup while he tells his father. · _Stay with Hiccup in the square while Stoick listens, or doesn’t._

#### `reader:a19-02-shot` · [main](main.md#a19-02-shot)

_Below, a tower of torches burns, and Vikings throw everything they have at the sky._  
_Then there is that sound again, a high rising whistle, and a tower bursts apart in blue-white fire._  
**Hiccup**: That’s it. That’s the one. It never steals, never shows itself, never misses.  
_You look up and see nothing but stars. Then not stars, where something black has crossed in front of them._  

> **Lesson** (science, 5-ESS1-2, notice): stars are far-away suns, and the Pole Star stays still in the north  
> _Stars are suns, very far away. We only see them at night because the daytime sky is too bright._  
> _Sailors long ago steered by them. In the north, the Pole Star stays almost still while the other stars wheel around it._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that stars are distant suns, and that sailors in the north steered by the Pole Star._

**Hiccup**: Come on. Give me something. Anything to aim at.  
_The whistle comes again. Hiccup sees the flash, swings the launcher round, and pulls._  
_The bola spins up into the dark. Far out there is a shriek, and a shape tumbles out of the sky behind the ridge._  
**Hiccup**: I hit it. I hit it! Did anybody see that?  
_Down in the village, nobody did. Your Nubbin did, and so did you._  

**Choices**

- **Tell him you saw it.**
    - **Hiccup**: You saw it. Somebody saw it. Right. Good. I’m not mad.
- **Point to where it fell.**
    - You point at the dark ridge. Hiccup lines up your arm with his eye and learns the spot like a map.
- **Ask if he’s sure.**
    - **Hiccup**: No. Yes. It fell. Things don’t fall like that unless something has gone wrong with them.

#### `reader:a19-02-shot-done` · [main](main.md#a19-02-shot-done)

_Behind you, a wooden stake snaps. Something large has landed on the cliff, and it is not black, and it is very awake._  
_It is long and red, with a crest of spikes down its back, and it breathes out a long, low, rumbling sigh._  
**Hiccup**: Ah. Not the one I was hoping for.  
_The rumble shakes the stones under your feet. Your Nubbin’s ears flatten._  

> **Lesson** (music, 4-PS3-2, ask): faster vibrations make a higher sound  
> _The Night Fury’s whistle was high and thin, and this dragon’s rumble is low. Which sound has the faster vibration?_  
> - ✅ The high whistle → Yes. The faster something vibrates, the higher the note: a whistle wiggles the air very fast, and a rumble slowly.
> - The low rumble → Not quite. A low sound is a slow wobble, and the high whistle is the fast one.
> - They vibrate at the same speed → If they did, they would sound the same. The whistle is higher because it vibrates faster.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that faster vibrations make a higher pitch, comparing a whistle and a rumble._

_The Nightmare breathes in, and the air round its jaws begins to glow._  

#### `reader:a19-02-chase` · [main](main.md#a19-02-chase)

_It is a Monstrous Nightmare. It heaves the rest of itself over the lip of the cliff, on fire along its whole back._  
**Hiccup**: Ah. Not that one. Run. Running would be good.  
_You run, all three of you, down the slope, with the heat of it on your neck. Your Nubbin runs through Hiccup’s legs, twice._  

> **Lesson** (health, 4-LS1-1, notice): the heart beats faster to feed the muscles when we run  
> _When you run, your heart beats faster. It pumps blood, and blood carries oxygen to your leg muscles._  
> _After you stop, it takes a few minutes for your heart and breathing to slow down again._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that the heart beats faster during exercise to carry oxygen to the muscles._

**Hiccup**: Left! No, other left! Behind the torch post!  
_You throw yourselves behind a torch post. The fire splits round it, and the wood begins to smoke._  
_Then a man the size of a door steps out and meets the Nightmare with nothing but his shoulder and a roar._  
_The Nightmare thinks about it, and goes. Above it, the last of the raid goes too, with half the village’s sheep._  

#### `reader:a19-02-chase-done` · [main](main.md#a19-02-chase-done)

**Stoick**: Hiccup.  
**Hiccup**: Hi, Dad.  
_Stoick the Vast is the chief of Berk. He is very large, very tired, and covered in soot._  
_Hiccup’s shoulders have gone up round his ears. He is trying to look like a person who has done nothing at all._  
_Neither of them says anything else, which says quite a lot._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): reading what silence shows about characters  
> _Neither of them says any more, “which says quite a lot”. What can the silence tell us?_  
> - ✅ That both of them feel awkward and worried → Yes. What characters do not say can show their feelings as clearly as what they do say.
> - That they have nothing to say → Look at Hiccup’s shoulders. A person with nothing to say would not look so tense.
> - That they are about to sing → Ha, not on Berk! The silence is full of feelings, not songs.
> _For grown-ups: Read what a silence between two characters shows about how they feel._


#### `reader:a19-02-nobody` · [main](main.md#a19-02-nobody)

**Hiccup**: I hit a Night Fury. Up on the cliff. It came down past the ridge, you could send someone to…  
**Stoick**: Stop. Every time you step outside, something gets broken. Tonight it was a torch post and the fish store.  
**Stoick**: I’ve a village to feed, and the winter coming, and the dragons have had the sheep.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Eco.1.K-2, notice): scarcity means people must choose how to use limited food and time  
> _Stoick has only so much food, so many sheep and so many hours before winter._  
> _When there is not enough of something to go round, people have to choose. That is what scarcity means._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that scarcity means there is not enough to go round, so people have to choose._

**Hiccup**: I know what I saw.  
_Behind Stoick, a few Vikings are laughing: not loudly, just enough._  
**Stoick**: Gobber, see him home. And who’s this?  
**Gobber**: The new help, Stoick. Good arms. The small one’s some sort of hatchling. Southern. Harmless.  
_Stoick looks at you, then at your Nubbin, and decides he has no room in his head for either of you tonight._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): whether being busy excuses not listening  
> _Stoick has a village to feed and little time. Is that a good reason not to listen to his son?_  
> - Being busy is a reason, but not an excuse → That’s a fair answer. Everyone is busy sometimes, but listening for a minute can cost very little.
> - A leader must put the whole village first → That’s a fair answer. A chief has many people to look after, though his son is one of them.
> - It depends how important what Hiccup says is → That’s a fair answer. It is hard to know what is important until you have listened.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about whether being busy is a good reason not to listen to someone._


**Choices**

- **Say you saw it too.**
    - **Stoick**: Then you were both looking at the dark. It’s easy to see things in the dark.
    - He isn’t unkind about it. He just has no room for it tonight.
- **Say nothing. Stand next to Hiccup.**
    - You say nothing. You stand next to Hiccup, close enough that he can tell.
    - **Hiccup**: Thanks.

#### `reader:a19-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a19-02-complete)

_Gobber walks you both back to the forge, and leaves you at the door with a look that means stay put this time._  
**Hiccup**: He thinks I’m a joke. They all do. I’ll find it tomorrow. I’ll find it, and I’ll…  
_He doesn’t finish. He goes up to his corner and draws by candlelight for a long time instead._  
_From the loft you can hear the charcoal scratching. It sounds like someone trying to remember exactly what he saw._  

> **Lesson** (art, 2-PS1-1, notice): drawing helps us notice details, and soft charcoal suits shadows  
> _Artists draw to see. When you draw something slowly, you notice shapes, shadows and tiny details you missed._  
> _Each material suits a job. Soft charcoal makes dark, smudgy shadows, and a hard pencil makes fine lines._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that careful drawing helps us notice details, and that soft charcoal suits shadows._

_Charcoal is made by burning wood with very little air, so it comes out black and light, and draws a soft, smudgy line._  

### Glossary

- **ridge**: A long, narrow strip of high ground along the top of a hill.
- **scarcity**: When there is not enough of something to go round, so people have to choose.

## A19-3. The Fleet Sails

- `a19-03-s1`: Go up to the Great Hall and hear what the chief has decided. · _The Great Hall is at the top of the village. Stand near the back._
- `a19-03-s2`: Help load the fleet before it sails. · _Carry stores from the fish sheds down to the ships on the jetty._
- `a19-03-s3`: Look at what Hiccup has been drawing. · _His notebook is open on the forge bench while he fetches charcoal._

#### `reader:a19-03-hall` · [main](main.md#a19-03-hall)

_In the morning the Great Hall smells of smoke and wet wool. Stoick stands at the head of the long table with a map._  
**Stoick**: The nest. Find it, and the raids stop. I’m taking the ships out one more time, before the ice.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, 2-ESS2-2, ask): compass directions: the way home is opposite to the way out  
> _The nest is far to the north. If the ships sail north to find it, which way must they sail to come home?_  
> - ✅ South → Yes. South is the opposite of north, so sailing home means heading back the way you came.
> - North → If they keep sailing north, they will go further from home. Home is the opposite way: south.
> - East → East is sideways to north. To come straight back, they sail the opposite direction: south.
> _For grown-ups: Used compass directions: the way back from a journey north is south._

_Nobody cheers. The last time the ships went out, fewer came back._  
**Stoick**: I want every ship. Gobber stays. Somebody has to train the young ones.  
**Gobber**: And Hiccup?  
_Stoick looks down the table at his son, who is trying to be very small behind a jug._  
**Stoick**: Hiccup trains with the rest. It’s time.  
**Hiccup**: Dad, I really don’t think that…  
**Stoick**: You’ll have an axe. You’ll have Gobber. You’ll learn. That’s the end of it.  

#### `reader:a19-03-hall-done` · [main](main.md#a19-03-hall-done)

**Gobber**: Dragon training, then. Morning, in the arena. Bring the lad.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): words with more than one meaning: train  
> _Stoick said somebody must “train the young ones”, and “train” can also mean a vehicle on rails. Which does Stoick mean?_  
> - ✅ To teach and practise → Yes, and Vikings had no railways! Here, “train” means to teach someone and let them practise.
> - A vehicle on rails → That meaning exists, but there were no trains in Viking times. Stoick means to teach and practise.
> _For grown-ups: Used the story to choose the right meaning of the multiple-meaning word “train”._

**Gobber**: Bring the hatchling, too. It can learn what not to be.  
_Gobber winks at your Nubbin, who salutes him with one stubby wing._  
_The arena is a deep stone pit behind the Great Hall, roofed over with iron chains._  
_Hiccup groans, softly, into the jug._  

#### `reader:a19-03-docks` · [main](main.md#a19-03-docks)

_All day you carry stores down to the jetty: barrels of salt fish, coils of rope, shields stacked like plates._  
_Four ships are being loaded. Each takes twelve barrels of fish and eight coils of rope._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): multiply: 4 × 12  
> _Four ships each take 12 barrels of fish. How many barrels is that altogether?_  
> - ✅ 48 → Yes! 4 × 12 is ten barrels each (40) and two each (8 more), so 40 + 8 = 48.
> - 16 → That is 4 + 12. We need four groups of twelve: 12 + 12 + 12 + 12.
> - 44 → Close. Try 4 × 10 = 40 and 4 × 2 = 8, then add: 40 + 8.
> _For grown-ups: Multiplied 4 groups of 12 to find 48 barrels._

_Your Nubbin carries one oar, end first, with tremendous seriousness. It takes most of the afternoon._  
_Hiccup comes down to the jetty at the last minute. His father is checking a rope that does not need checking._  
**Stoick**: Gobber will teach you. Listen to him.  
**Hiccup**: I will.  
**Stoick**: Good.  
_That seems to be everything. Then Stoick puts one great hand on Hiccup’s head, quickly, as if it might be seen, and goes aboard._  
_The ships go out on the grey tide. Hiccup watches until he cannot tell which sail is which._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): a hollow ship floats because it pushes aside enough water  
> _A big wooden ship floats because it is hollow, so it is full of air and light for its size._  
> _Water pushes up on it. A ship floats as long as it pushes aside water that weighs as much as the ship does._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned why a large, hollow ship floats._


**Choices**

- **Wave the ships off.**
    - You wave. Your Nubbin waves its oar, which nearly takes Hiccup’s ear off.
- **Stand with Hiccup until the sails are gone.**
    - You stand with him until there is nothing left to watch but grey water.

#### `reader:a19-03-docks-done` · [main](main.md#a19-03-docks-done)

**Hiccup**: He used to do that when I was little. The hand. I’d forgotten.  
_Hiccup is quiet for a moment, still looking at the water._  
_Stoick is not a man for speeches. He shows he cares with things he does, not things he says._  
**Hiccup**: He never said “be careful”. He just checked the rope.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): seeing care in what people do, not only in what they say  
> _Stoick checked a rope that did not need checking, and gave a quick pat. What might that really be saying?_  
> - ✅ That he cares, even though he finds it hard to say → Yes. Some people show love through small actions, because the words feel too big.
> - That he thinks Hiccup is a nuisance → Look at the pat. People who find us a nuisance do not touch our heads gently.
> - That the rope was loose → The story says it did not need checking. The rope was an excuse to stay a little longer.
> _For grown-ups: Read a character’s small actions to see how much he cares._
