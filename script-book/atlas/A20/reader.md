# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A20: The Weeping

## A20-1. The Bright One

- `a20-01-s1`: Step into the grey-and-gold page and find Frigg’s hall. · _Touch the page, then go in at the door where the spindle is turning._
- `a20-01-s2`: Carry Frigg’s cloak up to Breidablik, Baldr’s hall. · _Follow the path up the rise to the pale hall; you will see it long before you reach it._
- `a20-01-s3`: Sit with Baldr while your Nubbin sleeps. · _Take the bench by the long table; there is no hurry in this hall._

#### `reader:a20-01-page` · [main](main.md#a20-01-page)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page washed in grey and gold: a bright hall, a hall of mists, and a long white plain._  
_Across the top, in plain letters: a story from the Prose Edda, written down in Iceland about eight hundred years ago._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): where Iceland is: an island in the North Atlantic  
> _Iceland is an island in the North Atlantic, just south of the Arctic Circle. Imagine sailing there from Scotland._  
> _Which direction do you sail?_  
> - ✅ North-west → Yes. Iceland lies north-west of Scotland, across open sea.
> - South-east → That would take you towards France. Iceland is the other way: north-west.
> - Due south → South would take you towards Spain. Iceland lies north-west of Scotland.
> _For grown-ups: Used compass directions to place Iceland: north-west of Scotland._

_Snorri Sturluson wrote it down. He was a Christian, setting out the old stories of his people’s gods._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, notice): the word vellum, and books made by hand  
> _Books in Snorri’s day were written by hand, on vellum. Vellum is animal skin, usually calfskin, scraped thin and smooth._  
> _A big book could need the skins of many calves. That is one reason books were so precious._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the word vellum, and how books were made by hand about 800 years ago._

_His book is one telling, not scripture. These gods are still honoured today, by people whose faith is called Heathenry._  
_The page goes cool and soft under your hand, like linen off the line. Then it is swept boards, and you are standing on them._  
_A long hall, full of the small sound of a spindle. A woman sits by the fire with her work in her lap, and looks up._  
**Frigg**: A guest. Come in out of the draught. This is Fensalir, and I am Frigg.  
**Frigg**: There are more errands in this house than there are hands. Will you run some of them, while you stay?  
**Fulla**: And the small one? Does it carry things?  
_Your Nubbin holds up both arms, to show it will carry anything at all._  

**Choices**

- **Bow, as you would to someone old and kind.**
    - Frigg inclines her head to you, exactly as low as you bowed to her.
    - **Frigg**: Guests and hosts bow the same distance in this house. It saves a great deal of arguing.
- **Say your name, plainly.**
    - **Frigg**: A good name. I’ll remember it.
    - She says it back to you once, and you have the odd feeling it has been written down somewhere.
- **Ask what needs carrying first.**
    - **Fulla**: Oh, I like this one.

#### `reader:a20-01-page-done` · [main](main.md#a20-01-page-done)

**Frigg**: Then the first errand is the easiest. Take this cloak up the hill to my son. He gives his away to anyone who looks cold.  
**Fulla**: Breidablik. The hall on the rise. You can’t miss it. Nobody ever has.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): division: 12 shared between 4  
> _Baldr gives his cloak to anyone who looks cold. Suppose Frigg sews 12 cloaks for him to share equally between 4 cold travellers._  
> _How many cloaks does each traveller get?_  
> - ✅ 3 → Yes. 12 ÷ 4 = 3, so each traveller gets three cloaks.
> - 4 → 4 is the number of travellers. Sharing 12 equally between 4 is 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
> - 8 → 8 is 12 − 4. Sharing equally is division: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a sharing (division) problem: 12 ÷ 4 = 3._


#### `reader:a20-01-hall` · [main](main.md#a20-01-hall)

_Breidablik stands on a rise above everything. Its roof is so pale it is hard to say where it stops and the sky begins._  
_Fulla said nothing unclean can be in this hall. You wipe your feet twice, and your Nubbin wipes both of its._  
_Inside there is a long table, and a man at it, mending a strap. He looks up._  
_He is so kind and so glad that he seems to shine, and the whole room is lighter for him._  
**Baldr**: You’ve come from my mother. I know her cloak. She thinks I’m always cold.  
**Baldr**: I’m never cold. Put it on the bench, and sit. Everyone who climbs this far ought to sit.  
_The light off him is like sun off snow in the morning: everywhere at once, and easy on the eyes._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, ask): snow reflects most of the light that falls on it  
> _The light off Baldr is described as sun off snow in the morning. Snow looks dazzling in sunshine._  
> _What makes it so bright?_  
> - ✅ It reflects most of the light that falls on it → Yes. Fresh snow bounces back most of the sunlight that lands on it. That is why people wear dark glasses on snowy mountains.
> - It makes its own light → Snow cannot make light. It only bounces back what the Sun sends it.
> - It is hot → Snow is cold. Brightness is about light bouncing back, not about heat.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that snow reflects most of the sunlight that hits it._

_Your Nubbin sits down in it, and blinks, and slowly tips over sideways._  
**Baldr**: Ah. It does that to the small ones. Leave it be. It will wake when it’s ready.  

#### `reader:a20-01-hall-done` · [main](main.md#a20-01-hall-done)

_Your Nubbin is asleep on the warm boards, in the brightest part of the floor, with one arm over its face._  
**Baldr**: Stay till it wakes. I’d like the company.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): being loved and still lonely (a think question)  
> _Baldr is loved by everyone, yet he says he would like the company._  
> _Can someone be well loved and still feel lonely?_  
> - Yes: being near people is not the same as feeling understood → A thoughtful answer. Company and closeness are not always the same thing.
> - No: if everyone loves you, you cannot be lonely → A fair view. But think of Baldr, who is loved by all and still asks you to stay.
> - Maybe: it depends on what that person needs → A careful answer. Different people need different kinds of company.
> _For grown-ups: Reflected on whether being loved always stops loneliness (no single right answer)._


#### `reader:a20-01-sit` · [main](main.md#a20-01-sit)

_You sit on the bench. Baldr goes back to his strap, and for a while there is no sound but the needle going through leather._  
**Baldr**: My mother says you’re running her errands. That’s kind. She has more of them than usual this year.  
**Baldr**: Everyone climbs up here in the end with a question. Mostly it’s which of two people is right.  
**Baldr**: I tell them as kindly as I can. It’s the part of the day I like best.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): a character’s traits from what he does  
> _Baldr settles quarrels by telling people the answer “as kindly as I can,” and says it is the part of his day he likes best._  
> _What does that tell us about him?_  
> - ✅ He cares more about people than about being proved right → Yes. Settling a quarrel kindly shows he wants people to feel looked after, not beaten.
> - He likes bossing people about → Nothing he does is bossy. He listens, and he tells people as kindly as he can.
> - He is bored by everything → He says it is the part of the day he likes best. He is not bored.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a character trait (kindness) from what the character does._

_He looks over at your Nubbin, which is snoring very slightly in the light._  
**Baldr**: Ask me something, then. You’ve climbed as far as anyone.  

**Choices**

- **Ask him why he is so bright.**
    - **Baldr**: I don’t know. I’ve never been anything else. I’d like to see the dark properly one day, just to know.
- **Ask him if he has brothers.**
    - **Baldr**: Hödr. He’s blind, and stronger than any of us, and he laughs at my jokes before I finish them.
    - **Baldr**: You’ll meet him. He’s always just outside where the noise is, listening.
- **Don’t ask anything. Just sit.**
    - So you sit. After a while Baldr puts the strap down and sits too, doing nothing, as if he never gets the chance.
    - **Baldr**: That was a good question.

#### `reader:a20-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a20-01-complete)

_Your Nubbin wakes when the light moves across the floor. It gets up and moves after it._  
**Baldr**: It’ll follow me about now. The small ones do. I don’t know what they think they’ll find.  
_He folds his mother’s cloak and lays it on the end of the bench, where he will see it every time he passes._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.NF.A.1, ask): unit fractions by folding  
> _Baldr folds his mother’s cloak in half, then in half again._  
> _When it is folded like that, each folded part is what fraction of the whole cloak?_  
> - ✅ One quarter → Yes. Half of a half is a quarter: 1/4. Four equal parts make the whole cloak.
> - One half → One fold makes halves. He folded again, so each part is half of a half: one quarter.
> - One third → Thirds need three equal parts. Folding in half twice makes four equal parts, so one quarter.
> _For grown-ups: Found a unit fraction by folding: half of a half is one quarter._

**Baldr**: Tell her I’ll wear it. She’ll know I mean it kindly.  
_Going down the hill, your Nubbin stops every few steps to look back at the pale hall._  

#### `reader:a20-01-ember` · [main](main.md#a20-01-ember)

_Variant of `a20-01-complete`._

_Your Nubbin is carrying its little lantern, as it does everywhere now. Inside it, the red pebble from the high pass glows._  
**Fulla**: A lamp with no flame. It’s warm, though. My hands are warm just being near it.  
**Frigg**: Somebody gave that away who had very little left to give. Keep it close, little one.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): why someone might give away almost the last thing they have (a think question)  
> _Frigg says someone gave that away who had very little left to give._  
> _Why might a person give away almost the last thing they have?_  
> - Because they cared more about someone else than about keeping it → A generous answer. Giving can say “you matter more to me than this.”
> - Because they wanted to be rid of it → Possible. But Frigg says they had little left to give, which sounds more like love than clearing out.
> - Because they hoped it would help someone who needed it → A good reason. A small gift from someone with little can mean more than a big gift from someone with plenty.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why someone might give away a precious thing (no single right answer)._

## A20-2. Bad Dreams

- `a20-02-s1`: Take lamp oil up to Breidablik, late at night. · _Fulla has the oil jar at Fensalir’s door; carry it up the rise._
- `a20-02-s2`: Walk down with Baldr while he tells the gods his dreams. · _Go with Baldr to the great hall where the gods meet, and stay by the door._
- `a20-02-s3`: Keep the hall going through the long council. · _Keep the fire fed and the cups filled until the gods have decided._

#### `reader:a20-02-oil` · [main](main.md#a20-02-oil)

_It is late. Fensalir’s fire is down to red, and Fulla is still up, holding a jar of lamp oil._  
**Fulla**: His lamp’s been burning three nights running. He doesn’t sleep. Take this up to him? Mind the step.  
_Breidablik is never dark, but tonight the lamp is lit as well, as if he wanted more light than his own._  
_Baldr sits at the long table with his hands round a cup that has gone cold._  
**Baldr**: Oh. It’s you. Come in. I’m glad it’s you, and not somebody who’d ask how I am.  
**Baldr**: I’ve been dreaming. The same road every night, going down. And at the bottom of it, somebody saying my name.  
**Baldr**: Not unkindly. Quietly. As if they were setting a place for me.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): what makes a worry smaller  
> _Baldr is glad it is you, and not somebody who would ask how he is._  
> _Which of these is most likely to make a worry smaller?_  
> - ✅ Telling someone I trust, even a little at a time → Yes. A worry shared is usually lighter. In the morning Baldr will tell the gods, and he will feel it ease.
> - Keeping it to myself → Worries kept alone tend to grow in the dark. Baldr has had three nights of that.
> - Pretending it is not there → That is tempting. But the worry waits. Baldr tried it, and the dream came back.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about telling someone you trust as a way to shrink a worry._

_Your Nubbin climbs onto the bench beside him and sits as close to his light as it can get._  

**Choices**

- **Tell him dreams are only dreams.**
    - **Baldr**: That’s what I keep telling myself. I’m not as good at believing me as I used to be.
- **Say he should tell the others.**
    - **Baldr**: Yes. You’re right. I hate to worry them. But it’s worse, keeping it up here alone.
- **Sit, and wait for him to say more.**
    - You sit, and he doesn’t say more. After a while he puts his hand on your Nubbin’s head, and leaves it there.

#### `reader:a20-02-oil-done` · [main](main.md#a20-02-oil-done)

**Baldr**: In the morning I’ll tell them. All of them. Will you walk down with me?  
_He sleeps a little after that, with the lamp still lit. Your Nubbin sleeps in his light, and you keep watch._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-4, ask): a lamp changes stored energy into light and heat  
> _Baldr’s lamp has burned three nights running, on oil._  
> _A burning lamp changes the energy stored in its oil into two things we can sense. Which two?_  
> - ✅ Light and heat → Yes. That is why you can warm your hands near a lamp flame.
> - Sound and cold → A lamp flame gives a little warmth, not cold, and almost no sound.
> - Wind and water → Burning makes light and heat. Burning oil does not make wind or water.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a lamp changes stored energy in oil into light and heat._


#### `reader:a20-02-telling` · [main](main.md#a20-02-telling)

_In the morning the gods are gathered in their great hall. They stop talking when Baldr comes in, because he never looks like this._  
_He tells them plainly: the road, the going down, his name said quietly at the bottom of it._  
_Odin listens with his one eye on his son and says nothing at all. Thor’s hands close and open on his knees._  
**Thor**: A dream. Dreams are smoke. Show me the thing that means him harm, and I’ll deal with it.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): metaphor: dreams are smoke  
> _Thor says, “Dreams are smoke.” That is a metaphor: it says one thing is another, to make a point._  
> _What does Thor mean?_  
> - ✅ Dreams are thin and drift away, so there is nothing solid to fight → Yes. Smoke has no shape to grab. Thor wants something he can hit, so a dream seems like nothing to him.
> - Dreams cause fires → Thor is not saying dreams burn things. He means they are as thin and shapeless as smoke.
> - Dreams smell bad → It is not about smell. A metaphor lets one thing stand for the quality of another: thin, drifting, hard to hold.
> _For grown-ups: Explained a metaphor (dreams are smoke)._

**Odin**: Not every harm stands where you can reach it.  
**Frigg**: Then we will go where it stands. Whatever it is.  
_They begin to take counsel together, slowly, the way old friends do when a matter is serious._  
_Fulla finds you a stool by the door. Your Nubbin keeps edging along the floor towards Baldr’s light._  

#### `reader:a20-02-telling-done` · [main](main.md#a20-02-telling-done)

_The talk goes on through the morning and into the dark. You fetch, and carry, and listen._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.A.1, ask): time intervals  
> _Suppose the gods began their council at 8 in the morning, and the fire was burning low by 8 in the evening._  
> _For how many hours did they talk?_  
> - ✅ 12 hours → Yes. From 8 in the morning to 8 in the evening is 12 hours: 4 to noon, then 8 more.
> - 8 hours → 8 is the clock number, not the gap. Count on: 4 hours to noon, then 8 more, which makes 12.
> - 20 hours → Careful: we want the gap between the times, not the sum. Count on from 8 to 8: 12 hours.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a time interval across the day: 8 am to 8 pm is 12 hours._


#### `reader:a20-02-council` · [main](main.md#a20-02-council)

_The hall gets dark and the fire gets low. Nobody has noticed but you._  
**Thor**: We could ward him. Stand round him day and night.  
**Odin**: And he would live in a ring of spears. That is no life for him.  
**Baldr**: I’d rather not, if it’s all the same. I’d miss people.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): safe is not always the same as well (a think question)  
> _Odin says a life inside a ring of spears is no life. Baldr would rather be among people._  
> _Is being safe the same as being well?_  
> - No: a safe life can still be lonely or small → A thoughtful answer. Safety matters, but so does being able to live. Frigg’s plan tries to keep both.
> - Yes: safety is what matters most → A fair view. Safety matters a great deal. Baldr would ask what it costs him to be that safe.
> - It depends on what the safety costs → A careful answer. A little safety costs little. A ring of spears costs a whole way of life.
> _For grown-ups: Reflected on whether being safe is the same as being well (no single right answer)._

_Frigg has said nothing for a long time. She is turning something over, the way she turns her spindle._  
**Frigg**: Then I will ask. Everything in the world, one by one. I will ask each of them to promise not to harm him.  
**Thor**: Everything? There’s a great deal of everything.  
**Frigg**: Then I had better start early.  

**Choices**

- **Build the fire up again.**
    - You build the fire up. Odin watches you do it, and nods, once, as if that were a kind of counsel too.
- **Fill the cups.**
    - You fill the cups, and Thor takes his with a nod. His hands are steadier for having something to hold.
- **Sit by the door and listen.**
    - You sit by the door and listen. It is the kind of night somebody should remember all of, start to finish.

#### `reader:a20-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a20-02-complete)

_By dawn it is settled. The gods go out of the hall into a cold, clear morning._  
**Frigg**: Tomorrow we walk. You, and I, and Fulla. We will ask the whole world.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.MD.A.2, ask): distance, speed and time  
> _Frigg says tomorrow they will walk. Say she walks 4 kilometres each hour (about 2½ miles), for 6 hours._  
> _How far does she go?_  
> - ✅ 24 km (about 15 miles) → Yes: 4 km × 6 hours = 24 km. That is a long walk for one day.
> - 10 km → 10 is 4 + 6. Walking 4 km an hour for 6 hours means 6 groups of 4: 24 km.
> - 2 km → Not quite. Each hour adds 4 km, so six hours add 6 × 4 = 24 km.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a distance problem: 4 km an hour for 6 hours is 24 km._

**Baldr**: Mother. You don’t have to.  
**Frigg**: I know.  
_Your Nubbin falls asleep on the way home, and you carry it. It is facing back up the hill._

## A20-3. The Oaths

- `a20-03-s1`: Walk with Frigg while she asks the fire, the water, the iron and the stones. · _Keep a step behind Frigg; she stops at each thing she asks._
- `a20-03-s2`: Carry Frigg’s asking to the beasts and the birds. · _Go out over the meadows and along the wood edge; anything that runs, flies or creeps should be asked._
- `a20-03-s3`: Let your Nubbin make its own promise. · _Bring your Nubbin to Frigg on Fensalir’s step._

#### `reader:a20-03-asking` · [main](main.md#a20-03-asking)

_Frigg walks out at first light with no escort, only Fulla and you, and your Nubbin trotting to keep up._  
_She stops at the hearth first, and kneels, and asks the fire. The flames lie down low along the logs, all at once._  
**Frigg**: That is yes. Fire never says much.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): the word oath  
> _Frigg is asking everything in the world for an oath: a solemn promise._  
> _Which sentence uses “oath” correctly?_  
> - ✅ The knight swore an oath to protect the village. → Yes. An oath is a very serious promise, often spoken aloud.
> - She ate an oath for breakfast. → An oath is not food. It is a serious promise.
> - The oath rained all morning. → Weather is not an oath. An oath is a solemn promise.
> _For grown-ups: Learned the word oath (a solemn promise)._

_At the spring she asks the water, and it goes still as glass. At the smithy she asks the iron, and every blade on the wall rings._  
_She asks every metal by name, and the earth, and the stones. The stones say nothing, which for a stone is a promise._  
_She asks the trees, every kind, a whole wood of them. They bend their tops to her as if a wind went over._  
**Fulla**: She asks the sicknesses herself, at night. They take the longest. They always want to argue.  
**Frigg**: Everything can be asked. It only takes time, and remembering not to leave anything out.  
_West of Valhall you pass an old oak. On one of its branches is a sprig so small and new it has only two leaves._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, notice): mistletoe grows on another tree  
> _That little sprig is mistletoe, and it is a real plant. It grows on the branch of another tree, such as an apple or a poplar._  
> _Its green leaves make food from sunlight, but it sends root-like threads into the host tree to take water. So it is called a partial parasite._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that mistletoe is a green plant that grows on another tree’s branches._

_Your Nubbin stops and looks at it. Frigg is already walking on._  

#### `reader:a20-03-asking-done` · [main](main.md#a20-03-asking-done)

**Frigg**: The beasts and the birds now. My feet are older than my asking. Will you carry it for me?  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): being a trustworthy messenger  
> _Frigg trusts you to carry her asking exactly as she gave it._  
> _What makes someone a good messenger?_  
> - ✅ Passing the words on exactly, without changing them → Yes. A messenger is trusted because the words arrive just as they were sent.
> - Making the message funnier → A joke might be fun, but it changes the message. A good messenger keeps it as it is.
> - Keeping the best parts for yourself → Then the one who is waiting would not get the whole message. A good messenger passes on all of it.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about what makes a messenger trustworthy._


#### `reader:a20-03-beasts` · [main](main.md#a20-03-beasts)

_You go out over the meadows with Frigg’s asking. It sits in your mouth like a smooth stone, and you say it the way she said it._  
_The hare listens with both ears and thumps the ground, once. The bear lies down flat, which takes some time._  
_A reindeer lowers her antlers until they touch the grass. A fox looks away, then back, then sits._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-1, ask): how body parts help animals survive: the reindeer  
> _A reindeer has wide hooves and a thick coat. Reindeer live in snowy lands._  
> _How do those two features help a reindeer survive there?_  
> - ✅ Wide hooves spread its weight so it does not sink in snow, and the thick coat keeps in body heat → Yes. Wide hooves work a little like snowshoes, and a thick coat keeps the cold out.
> - Wide hooves help it fly, and the coat helps it swim → Reindeer cannot fly, though they are strong swimmers. Their wide hooves and thick coat are for snow and cold.
> - They do not help at all → Every feature of a survivor usually has a job. These two have a big one in the snow.
> _For grown-ups: Linked an animal’s body parts (hooves, coat) to surviving in snow._

_The birds you ask all together, from the top of a hill. They go up in one great turning cloud and come down again._  
_The ravens come last. They hear you out, and nod, and fly off towards Valhall to tell somebody._  
_Snakes and poisons Frigg asks herself. Some askings, she says, are not for guests._  
**Fulla**: That’s everything with legs and wings. Everything with neither, too. Is that all?  
_Your Nubbin is tugging at your sleeve. Nobody has asked it._  

#### `reader:a20-03-promise` · [main](main.md#a20-03-promise)

_Frigg is sitting on Fensalir’s step, tired, with her hands open in her lap. Your Nubbin walks straight up to her._  
_It stands in front of her with its feet together and its arms at its sides, as straight as it can stand._  
**Fulla**: Oh. It wants to be asked.  
**Frigg**: Of course it does. Everything should be. Come here, little one.  
**Frigg**: Will you promise not to harm my son? Not ever, not by anything you do?  
_Your Nubbin thinks about this for a long time, very seriously._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): a promise you make yourself (a think question)  
> _Frigg wants your Nubbin to make its own promise, not to have someone promise for it._  
> _Why might a promise mean more when you make it yourself?_  
> - Yes: only the one who promises can choose to keep it → A clear reason. A promise is a choice, and only the promiser can make it.
> - Not quite: a promise passed on can still be honest → A fair point. A messenger can be faithful. But the one who made the promise still has to keep it.
> - It depends on whether the person really means it → A careful answer. A promise is only as strong as the meaning behind it.
> _For grown-ups: Reflected on what makes a promise mean something (no single right answer)._


**Choices**

- **Let it put its paw in Frigg’s hand.**
    - It puts its paw in her hand, flat, and holds it there until she closes her fingers round it.
    - **Frigg**: That will hold.
- **Let it bow, the way the hare did.**
    - It bows so low it nearly topples. Then, to be sure, it thumps the step with one foot, like the hare.
    - **Frigg**: Twice promised. I’ll count it twice.
- **Promise for it.**
    - **Frigg**: No. Let it promise for itself. A promise carried by somebody else is only an errand.
    - Your Nubbin nods at her, hard, three times. Frigg nods back three times.

#### `reader:a20-03-complete` · [main](main.md#a20-03-complete)

_By evening it is done: fire and water, iron and every metal, stones, earth and trees, sickness, beasts, birds, poison and snakes._  
**Frigg**: Everything has promised. Everything I could think of.  
_She has been spinning as she walked, a twist of thread for every promise. Now the spindle is full, and she cuts the thread._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): drop spindles: spinning thread by hand  
> _Frigg’s spindle is a drop spindle, one of the oldest tools for making thread. People used them for thousands of years before spinning wheels or machines._  
> _You twist wool or flax into thread by letting the spindle hang and turn. Hand-spinners still use them today._

_Before you go, Baldr puts something in your hand: a sprig with two leaves, from a tree at the edge of the old ground._  
**Baldr**: It was too young to be asked, once. It’s older now. So am I. Keep it for me.  
_Your Nubbin wakes in his light and looks at him for a long moment. Then it gets up and comes back to you, on its own._  
_Líf and Lífthrasir walk out together into the morning, across the fields that nobody sowed._  
_You are back in the Grove, the atlas on your knee. Something clinks in your pocket: a golden game piece, and your Nubbin has brought home a sprig to match._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, ask): theme of the whole page  
> _The page opened with promises, and ends with a sprig kept for Baldr._  
> _Which theme fits the page best?_  
> - ✅ It is never too late to make room for someone → Yes. Baldr says there was always room, and Hödr comes and sits.
> - Gods always win in the end → The gods lose Baldr, and the page does not say they win everything.
> - Nothing can ever be mended → The page ends green and hopeful, so that does not fit.
> _For grown-ups: Chose the theme of the whole page._

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): pity or hatred (a think question)  
> _Hazel’s note says: don’t hate her for it, feel sorry for her._  
> _Why might pity be more useful than hatred, even towards someone who refused to help?_  
> - Pity lets us see why she is hurting → A kind answer. When we see the hurt, we are better placed to understand.
> - Hatred only adds more hurt to the world → A clear answer. Hatred gives the hurt somewhere to grow.
> - It is hard: both can be true at once → An honest answer. Pity does not mean forgetting what she did.
