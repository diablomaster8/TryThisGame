# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A22: The Candle

## A22-1. The Candle’s Story

- `a22-01-s1`: Step into the painted page and hear how the candle began. · _Touch the candle in the window on the atlas page, then sit at the edge of the painted bed._
- `a22-01-s2`: Walk into the valley on the morning of Antonio’s ceremony and meet the family. · _Let the page turn, then climb the hill from the market square to the house with green shutters._

#### `reader:a22-01-story` · [main](main.md#a22-01-story)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a valley ringed by green mountains: white walls, red roofs, tall palms and a candle in one window._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, notice): finding Colombia: its coasts, its mountains and its coffee country  
> _This page is set in a real country: Colombia, in the north-west corner of South America._  
> _It has a coast on the Caribbean Sea to the north and on the Pacific Ocean to the west._  
> _The Andes mountains run up through it in three long ranges, and coffee grows on their slopes._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Colombia is in north-west South America, with two coasts, three Andes ranges and coffee country._

_The page does not come alive at once. It stays painted, and you step into the paint._  
_It is night, years ago. In a bedroom made of brushstrokes, an old woman sits beside a little girl in round glasses._  
**Abuela Alma**: Come in, then. You may as well hear it too. Mirabel has heard it a hundred times, and she still wants it.  
**Mirabel**: Tell the river part!  
**Abuela Alma**: First the beginning. When I was young there was trouble in the land where we lived, and we had to leave our home.  
_The painting changes: a young couple on a road at night, with three babies wrapped against the cold._  
**Abuela Alma**: Pedro carried two of the babies, and I carried one. We walked for days with other families who had to leave too.  
_Far behind them, on a ridge, there is a thin dark line of riders. The painting does not bring them any closer._  
**Abuela Alma**: At a river, the trouble caught up with us. Pedro told me to cross with the babies. He stayed on the bank, so that we could.  
**Abuela Alma**: He did not come. Pedro died there, and I have missed him every day.  
_In the painting, the candle in her hand blazes, and the mountains rise round the river like hands closing round a flame._  
**Abuela Alma**: The candle gave us a home where nobody could take us from each other. A house that loves us. A miracle.  

**Choices**

- **Keep listening.**
    - You keep very still. The little girl has her fist in the blanket, holding on to the part about the river.
- **Ask about Pedro.**
    - **Abuela Alma**: He was kind, and he was brave, and he was not very good at dancing. That is enough for one night.
    - She says it lightly. Her hand on the blanket does not move at all.

#### `reader:a22-01-story-done` · [main](main.md#a22-01-story-done)

**Abuela Alma**: Every child in this family is given a gift when they are old enough. Tomorrow is yours, Mirabel.  
**Mirabel**: What will it be?  
**Abuela Alma**: Sleep, and see.  
_Mirabel lies awake anyway, with Abuela’s story still in the room._  
_Her grandmother’s family left home with almost nothing but each other. In many places and times, families still have to do that._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): imagining what matters most when a family must leave home  
> _Abuela’s family left home in a hurry, carrying three babies and very little else. If you had to leave quickly, what would matter most to take?_  
> - The people I love → That is the answer many families give. People matter most, and they are the hardest thing to leave behind.
> - Something that reminds me of home → A fair answer. A small thing from home can carry a lot of memory, and many families take one.
> - Something to keep me comfortable → A fair answer too. Comfort matters when everything else is changing, and a blanket can feel like home.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about what a family would take if they had to leave home quickly, and how that feels._


#### `reader:a22-01-casita` · [main](main.md#a22-01-casita)

_The page turns by itself. The paint goes thin and bright and real: morning, ten years later, and a market full of noise._  
_Up the hill is a house with white walls, green shutters and a red roof. Its shutters open and close by themselves._  
_A young woman in round glasses and an embroidered skirt comes down the steps two at a time, with a list._  
_You know the glasses. The little girl from the painting has grown up, and the house is the one the candle made._  
**Mirabel**: Hi! Hello! You’re the traveller. Abuela said there’d be a traveller. Sorry, are you a traveller?  
_The floor tiles clack in a ripple across the doorway, like a dog thumping its tail._  
**Mirabel**: That’s Casita. Casita, be nice. Guest.  
**Mirabel**: Come and meet everyone: my abuela, my tías and tíos, my primos. You’ll lose track.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.2.4, ask): Spanish family words: abuela, tía, primo  
> _In Spanish, abuela means grandmother and tía means aunt. From the way Mirabel lists her family, what do you think primo means?_  
> - ✅ Cousin → Yes. A boy cousin is a primo and a girl cousin is a prima. Antonio and Camilo are Mirabel’s primos, and Dolores is her prima.
> - Uncle → Close, but an uncle is a tío. A cousin is a primo, or a prima for a girl.
> - Baby → Not quite. A baby is a bebé. A cousin is a primo, or a prima for a girl.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out the Spanish family word primo (cousin) from context, alongside abuela and tía._

_A shutter swings out and taps you gently on the shoulder._  
**Mirabel**: I’m Mirabel. I don’t have a gift, before anyone tells you. It’s fine. It’s totally fine.  

**Choices**

- **Say you are the traveller.**
    - **Mirabel**: Great. Perfect. You’re in the blue room. The house likes guests. Mostly.
- **Point out that your Nubbin is travelling too.**
    - **Mirabel**: Oh, hello! Look at you. Antonio is going to lose his mind. In a good way.
- **Ask what the list is for.**
    - **Mirabel**: Tonight. Antonio’s gift ceremony. My little cousin. Everything has to be perfect, so it’s a long list.

#### `reader:a22-01-lamp` · [main](main.md#a22-01-lamp)

_Abuela Alma meets you in the courtyard. She is older than in the painting, straight as a broom, with a black shawl._  
_She looks at the little lamp at your belt, the one from your Long-Grass post. Its glass is dark._  
**Abuela Alma**: Nobody in this house walks about in the dark. Come.  
_She lights it from the candle in the window. The flame is gold and steady, and it isn’t yours, and it is very kind of her._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, ask): a flame can light another wick without being used up  
> _Abuela lights your lamp from her candle. Does her candle’s flame get any smaller?_  
> - ✅ No: one flame can light many wicks → Right. The heat from the first flame warms the new wick until it catches. Each flame then burns its own wax.
> - Yes: it gives away half its flame → It is a nice idea, but flames do not work like that. The first flame passes on heat and stays the same size.
> - It goes out → Not at all. Lighting a second wick takes only a little heat, and the first flame keeps burning.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that heat from one flame can light another wick, and the first flame is not used up._

> _Abuela tips her candle to your wick, and a second flame catches. What does her flame pass to your wick?_  
> - ✅ Heat → Yes. Heat from her flame warms your wick until it catches. Energy moves as heat, and her flame stays just as big.
> - Half of its flame → Flames do not split in half. Her flame passes on heat, and stays the same size.
> - Water → Water would put a flame out. Her flame passes on heat.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that heat from one flame can light another wick._

> _For grown-ups: Learned that a flame points up because hot gases rise, and that a draught bends it._

**Abuela Alma**: That one burns as if somebody is glad of it.

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, notice): heat moves from a hot flame through glass to your hand

> _For grown-ups: Learned that heat moves from a flame through glass to cooler things, and why we must not touch it._


#### `reader:a22-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a22-01-complete)

_Your room is small and blue, with a window over the valley. The bed turns its own covers down when you look at it._  
_Your Nubbin climbs onto the windowsill and looks across the courtyard at the candle in the window opposite._  
_Downstairs somebody is laughing, somebody is cooking, and three people are arguing about chairs._  
_Each of the three is carrying four chairs, and each wants to put them somewhere different._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): equal groups: 3 × 4  
> _Three people are each carrying four chairs. How many chairs is that altogether?_  
> - ✅ Twelve → Yes. Three groups of four is 4 + 4 + 4 = 12, which we write as 3 × 4 = 12.
> - Seven → That is 3 + 4. We need three groups of four: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12, or 3 × 4 = 12.
> - Nine → Try it as three groups of four: 4, 8, 12. So 3 × 4 = 12.
> _For grown-ups: Used equal groups to find 3 × 4 = 12._

_Somewhere in the walls there is a small scurry, and then nothing._

## A22-2. The Family

- `a22-02-s1`: Go down to the market with Mirabel. · _Follow Mirabel down the hill to the square._
- `a22-02-s2`: Meet the rest of the Madrigals at the house. · _Go back up the hill with Mirabel and talk to the family in the courtyard._
- `a22-02-s3`: Eat in the kitchen with Julieta and Agustín. · _Follow the smell of arepas to the kitchen._

#### `reader:a22-02-market` · [main](main.md#a22-02-market)

_Market morning. The square is full of stalls: oranges, coffee sacks, bolts of bright cloth and somebody’s goat._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, notice): coffee country: how coffee grows in the Andes  
> _The coffee sacks are no accident. This valley is in Colombia’s coffee country, high in the Andes, where the cool mountain air suits coffee bushes._  
> _Coffee beans are really seeds. They grow inside bright red fruits called coffee cherries._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Colombian coffee grows on mountain slopes, and that beans are seeds inside red coffee cherries._

**Mirabel**: Okay. Quick tour. Everyone in my family has a gift, and everyone in town knows exactly what it is.  
_A tall young woman walks past with a donkey over each shoulder._  
**Mirabel**: That’s Luisa, my sister. Super strong. Hi, Luisa!  
**Luisa**: Hi. Can’t stop. The church is crooked again.  
_A woman at a stall has a small grey cloud over her head. It is raining on her onions._  
**Mirabel**: Tía Pepa. Her weather is her mood. Tía, the cloud.  
**Tía Pepa**: What cloud? There is no cloud. I am perfectly calm.  
_The cloud thunders, very quietly. A man in a yellow shirt hurries over and fans it away with his hat._  
**Mirabel**: Tío Félix. He says his gift is Tía Pepa.  
**Tío Félix**: It is a very big gift. I am a lucky man!  

**Choices**

- **Tell Tía Pepa the cloud is fine.**
    - **Tía Pepa**: It is fine, isn’t it?
    - The cloud turns into a small, pleased rainbow.
- **Ask Félix how he copes.**
    - **Tío Félix**: Umbrellas. Many umbrellas. And I tell her she’s beautiful, which is true, so it’s easy.
- **Check on your Nubbin.**
    - Your Nubbin has found the goat, and the goat has found your Nubbin. They are deciding whether to be friends.

#### `reader:a22-02-market-done` · [main](main.md#a22-02-market-done)

_Mirabel ticks something off her list and nearly walks into a post._  
**Mirabel**: Party shopping done: three bags of oranges at four Shells a bag, and one loaf for five Shells. I paid with a twenty-Shell coin.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.D.8, ask): two-step money problem: multiply, add, then subtract from 20  
> _How many Shells of change should Mirabel get back?_  
> - ✅ Three → Yes. Oranges cost 3 × 4 = 12 Shells. With the loaf that is 12 + 5 = 17 Shells. 20 − 17 = 3 Shells of change.
> - Eight → That is 20 − 12, which forgets the loaf. With the loaf, 12 + 5 = 17 and 20 − 17 = 3.
> - Seventeen → Seventeen is what she spent. The change is what is left of 20: 20 − 17 = 3.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a two-step money problem: 3 × 4 = 12, 12 + 5 = 17, 20 − 17 = 3._

**Mirabel**: Next, my cousins. Brace yourself.  

#### `reader:a22-02-cousins` · [main](main.md#a22-02-cousins)

_In the courtyard a young woman steps out of a cloud of petals: perfect hair, perfect smile, roses at her feet._  
**Mirabel**: My sister Isabela. Flowers. Everyone loves her. I love her!  
**Isabela**: Mirabel. And a guest. How nice.  
_She smiles, and an orchid grows out of the wall beside your head. It is a lovely smile, and it never changes._  
_A girl in a pale dress drifts past, whispering very fast to nobody._  
**Mirabel**: Cousin Dolores. She hears everything. If you sneeze in the market, she knows.  
**Dolores**: You did sneeze in the market. Bless you.  
_A boy turns round, and he is Mirabel, then Félix, then himself again, a lanky teenager, grinning._  
**Camilo**: Camilo. Faces. I can do yours if you stand still.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): what makes a person themselves, apart from how they look  
> _Camilo can look exactly like Félix, but he is still Camilo. What really makes a person themselves?_  
> - ✅ What they care about and how they treat people → Yes. A face can be copied, but your kindness, your humour and what you care about are yours alone.
> - Their hair and clothes → Those are part of how we look, and they can change. What stays is what we care about and how we treat people.
> - Their gift → Mirabel has no gift, and she is entirely herself. Who we are is what we care about and how we treat others.
> _For grown-ups: Reflected on what makes a person themselves: values and kindness, not looks or gifts._


**Choices**

- **Stand still.**
    - Camilo looks at you hard, and then you are looking at yourself, a little taller, with better hair.
    - **Camilo**: I improved you. You’re welcome.
- **Don’t stand still.**
    - **Camilo**: Fair. People hate it. Mostly my sisters. Mostly when I do them.

#### `reader:a22-02-kitchen` · [main](main.md#a22-02-kitchen)

_In the kitchen a woman with a kind, tired face is cooking arepas. A boy with a burnt finger eats one, and the burn is gone._  
**Julieta**: You must be hungry. Everyone who comes up that hill is hungry. Sit.  
_The arepa is hot and buttery, with cheese in the middle. The ache in your legs from the long page just stops._  

> **Lesson** (health, K-LS1-1, notice): what corn and cheese give a growing body  
> _Arepas are eaten all over Colombia. They are made from ground corn, grilled or fried, often with cheese._  
> _Corn gives you energy to run about. Cheese gives protein and calcium, which help muscles and bones grow._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that corn gives energy, and cheese gives protein and calcium for growing muscles and bones._

**Agustín**: Welcome! Welcome. Don’t mind the bee. I’m used to the bee.  
_A gentle man in glasses waves from the doorway. A bee is sitting on his nose._  
**Mirabel**: Mamá and Papá. So that’s everyone. Everyone with a gift, I mean, and me.  
_Across the courtyard there is a tower with a dark door. Everybody manages not to look at it, all at once._  
_Félix starts to say a name. Pepa’s cloud cracks with thunder, and Félix turns it into a cough._  
_Then everybody talks at once, about chairs._  

**Choices**

- **Ask about the tower.**
    - **Mirabel**: Oh! That’s nobody. Nobody’s up there. It’s just a room.
    - She says it in a rush. Julieta puts another arepa on your plate, which is also an answer.
- **Let it go.**
    - You let it go. Mirabel gives you a small grateful look, and the house rattles a single tile, like a sigh.
- **Watch your Nubbin.**
    - Your Nubbin is at the courtyard’s edge, looking up at the tower. A small grey face looks back from a crack, and vanishes.

#### `reader:a22-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a22-02-complete)

_By afternoon you know the gifts by heart: healing, weather, strength, flowers, hearing, faces._  
_Mirabel hangs paper lanterns, carries chairs and ties ribbons all day, and nobody once asks her to._  
**Mirabel**: It’s fine. Somebody has to do the stuff that isn’t magic.  
_She says it brightly. Your Nubbin leans against her ankle anyway._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): reading feelings from actions as well as words  
> _Mirabel says “It’s fine” in a bright voice, and your Nubbin leans against her anyway._  
> _How can you tell how someone really feels when their words say one thing?_  
> - Watch their face and what they do → A good clue. Actions often tell us more than words, and a writer shows feelings that way too.
> - Listen to how they say it → Another good clue. A bright voice that is a little too bright can hide a sore feeling.
> - Ask them kindly → The kindest clue of all. Asking gently, and waiting, gives people room to say what is true.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how to read someone’s real feelings from their words, voice and actions._


### Glossary

- **arepa**: A round cake of ground corn, grilled or fried and often filled with cheese, eaten in Colombia.

## A22-3. Antonio’s Door

- `a22-03-s1`: Find Antonio before his ceremony. · _Look in the nursery Antonio shares with Mirabel._
- `a22-03-s2`: Walk Antonio up to his door with Mirabel. · _Climb the stairs two steps behind Mirabel and Antonio._

#### `reader:a22-03-hiding` · [main](main.md#a22-03-hiding)

_Evening: the courtyard is full of the whole town in their best clothes, every one holding a candle. Antonio is not._  
**Tía Pepa**: Where is he? Where is my baby?  
**Mirabel**: I’ll find him. Come on. He likes hiding places.  
_You find him under the bed in the nursery._  
_He is five, he is lying on his tummy with his chin on his fists, and he is not coming out._  
**Antonio**: What if I don’t get one?  
_Mirabel lies down flat on the floor, so their faces are level. You do too, and so does your Nubbin._  
**Mirabel**: Then you get to be you, which is already the best thing in this house.  
**Antonio**: You didn’t get one.  
**Mirabel**: No. I didn’t.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): what makes a person special, with or without a gift  
> _Mirabel has no gift, yet she tells Antonio that being himself is the best thing in the house. Does she believe it?_  
> _What makes a person special?_  
> - Yes, she means it → A fair answer. Mirabel does seem to mean it for Antonio, even if it is harder to believe for herself.
> - Partly, and she is still hurting → A thoughtful answer. People can believe something for someone else and still struggle to believe it about themselves.
> - It’s how we treat other people → A strong answer. Mirabel is special because of what she does for Antonio right now: she lies on the floor beside him.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about what makes a person special beyond any gift or talent._


**Choices**

- **Hold out your hand.**
    - Antonio looks at your hand for a long time, then takes Mirabel’s instead. That is the right answer, and you both know it.
- **Let your Nubbin go under first.**
    - Your Nubbin wriggles under the bed and lies down beside him, nose to nose. Antonio giggles before he can stop himself.
- **Wait.**
    - Nobody hurries him. After a while a small hand comes out from under the bed and finds Mirabel’s sleeve.

#### `reader:a22-03-hiding-done` · [main](main.md#a22-03-hiding-done)

**Antonio**: Will you walk me? Both of you?  
**Mirabel**: All the way to the door.  
_Antonio’s hands are cold and his heart is going fast. Fear does that to a body: it gets ready to run._  

> **Lesson** (health, CASEL: Self-management, do): slow breathing calms a frightened body  
> _A slow breath tells the body that it is safe. Breathe in for four counts, hold for one, and out for six._  
> _(Do it five times with Antonio, until his hands feel warmer.)_  
> Check: `edu:breathe:slowly:5`
> _For grown-ups: Practised slow breathing and learned that it tells a frightened body it is safe._


#### `reader:a22-03-door` · [main](main.md#a22-03-door)

_The stairs are lined with family. On the landing a new door glows faintly, with no picture on it yet._  
_Mirabel walks Antonio up, holding his hand. You walk two steps behind, between rows of candlelit faces._  
_Halfway up, Mirabel slows down. For a moment you see another night instead: little Mirabel at a glowing door, and Abuela stooping to her._  
**Abuela Alma**: Whatever happens tomorrow, little one, you are loved in this house. That does not depend on any door.  
_In the memory, little Mirabel reaches for the handle. The door’s light goes out, and the door fades back into wall._  
_Then it is now again. Antonio touches his door._  
_It blazes with light, and a picture of him grows on it, with animals all round._  
_A toucan swoops down and lands on his head. A jaguar pads out of the jungle in his new room, then capybaras, then coatis._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-LS4-1, ask): animals of Colombia’s forests and wetlands: the capybara is the largest rodent  
> _These animals live in Colombia’s forests and wetlands. Which of them is the biggest rodent in the world?_  
> - ✅ The capybara → Yes. Rodents have front teeth that never stop growing, and the capybara is the largest rodent alive.
> - The jaguar → The jaguar is a big cat, not a rodent. The capybara is the largest rodent.
> - The toucan → The toucan is a bird with a huge beak. The capybara is the largest rodent.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that the capybara is the world’s largest rodent, among animals of Colombia’s forests and wetlands._

**Antonio**: They’re talking! I can hear them!  
_The courtyard roars. Mirabel cheers louder than anybody, and goes on cheering a beat too long._  

**Choices**

- **Cheer with Mirabel.**
    - You cheer with her, until she stops, and then you stop too.
- **Look at Mirabel.**
    - She catches you looking and smiles hard. Her glasses have steamed up, and she says it’s the candles.

#### `reader:a22-03-door-done` · [main](main.md#a22-03-door-done)

_Antonio’s jaguar sniffs your Nubbin from head to foot. Your Nubbin holds very still, then sniffs it back._  
_Your Nubbin trills at the jaguar. Antonio listens, delighted._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, ask): sound words that copy the sound they name  
> _Some words sound like the thing they name, such as “sniff”. Which of these words does that?_  
> - ✅ Sniff → Yes. “Sniff” copies the sound of a nose, and “trill” does too. Words like this are called onomatopoeia.
> - Garden → “Garden” does not sound like a garden. “Sniff” sounds like what it names.
> - Jaguar → “Jaguar” does not sound like a jaguar. “Sniff” sounds like what it names.
> _For grown-ups: Identified sound words (onomatopoeia) such as sniff and trill._

**Antonio**: She says it smells like a garden. And your Nubbin says you snore.

## A22-4. The Cracks

- `a22-04-s1`: Follow Mirabel through the party. · _Stay close to Mirabel at the edge of the courtyard._
- `a22-04-s2`: Help Mirabel show the family the cracks. · _Stand on the landing by the candle with Mirabel._

#### `reader:a22-04-party` · [main](main.md#a22-04-party)

_The party fills the house. Music, dancing, food on every surface, and Luisa holding up the piano so people can dance under it._  
_A man in the corner plays a tiple, a small Colombian guitar, and the whole courtyard keeps its beat._
