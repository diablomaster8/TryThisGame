# Atlas, page A24: The Law of the Jungle

## A24-1. The Cave

*The Jungle Book (Rudyard Kipling, 1894): “Mowgli’s Brothers”* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Law of the Jungle page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time once Nova’s atlas is in the Grove; the first quest on the page.

**Cast:** Father Wolf (Father Wolf (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Mother Wolf (Raksha, Mother Wolf (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Tabaqui (Tabaqui the jackal, the Dish-licker (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Shere Khan (Shere Khan, the lame tiger (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Mowgli (Mowgli, the man’s cub (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film)

### Step 1: Step into the Seeonee hills and find the wolves’ cave.

Hint: Touch the page where the cave mouth is, then climb the slope towards it. · check: `explore:a24-seeonee:cave-mouth`

#### <a id="a24-01-page"></a>Scene `a24-01-page`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at hills the colour of a warm brick, going blue at the edges with dusk._  
_Tucked into the page is one of Nova’s cards, written small. “Kipling was born in Bombay, and he loved India.”_  
_“He also believed Britain had the right to rule it, and he wrote that too. He was wrong about that.”_  
_“Read him knowing both. N.”_  
_You put the card back. The dusk comes up off the paper like warm breath, and there is dry grass under your boots._  
_A slope of rocks, and a cave mouth. Above it, against the first stars, is a long flat rock with nobody on it yet._  
_In the cave a big grey wolf is waking up and scratching. A she-wolf lies with her nose across four tumbling cubs._  
**Father Wolf** _(think)_: Hrrm. Dusk already, and hunting to do. Who’s that at the door? It smells like a guest. A very small one, and a strange one.  
**Mother Wolf**: Let them in, if they’ve come politely. Nothing that walks in politely gets eaten in this house.  

**Choices**

- **Bow at the cave mouth and wait to be asked.**
    - **Mother Wolf**: Good manners. Come in, then, and mind the cubs’ tails. They leave them everywhere.
- **Let your Nubbin go in first.**
    - Your Nubbin trots in, sniffs the nearest cub, and is sniffed back, thoroughly, from both ends.
- **Sit down outside and let them look at you.**
    - **Father Wolf**: Hm. It sits like it means no harm. Come in out of the dew.

#### <a id="a24-01-page-done"></a>Done `a24-01-page-done`

_The cave smells of warm fur and old bones. Mother Wolf watches you with eyes like two green lamps._  
**Mother Wolf**: Guests of the Free People sit by the wall. That’s you. And your little one sits wherever it likes, apparently.  

### Step 2: Keep still in the cave while Tabaqui and the tiger’s roar go by.

Hint: Stay by the wall. Watch the cave mouth, not the jackal. · check: `watch:a24-cub:cave`

#### <a id="a24-01-cub"></a>Scene `a24-01-cub`

_A thin shadow slides into the cave mouth on its belly: a jackal, smiling with all its teeth._  
**Tabaqui**: Oh, chief of the wolves, what a cave! What a fine family! Such teeth on them already!  
**Tabaqui** _(happy)_: And a guest. How grand. Is there a bone, for poor Tabaqui? Even a dry one. I am not proud.  
**Father Wolf**: There’s a bone by the wall. Take it and go.  
**Tabaqui**: I only mention, in passing, that Shere Khan has come to hunt in these hills. Lame Shere Khan. Thought you’d like to know.  
_He crunches the bone, says something unkind about the cubs’ ears, and is gone._  
_Below in the valley a tiger’s whine rises, long and angry. Father Wolf’s fur stands up._  
**Father Wolf** _(angry)_: The fool. He’s hunting Man. A woodcutter’s fire; he’s missed, and burnt his feet on it, by the sound.  
_Something rustles in the bushes. Father Wolf crouches to spring, and stops himself in mid-air._  
**Father Wolf** _(shock)_: Man! A man’s cub. Look!  
_In the cave mouth, holding on to a low branch, stands a brown baby who has only just learned to walk._  
_He isn’t frightened in the least. He toddles in, pushes in among the cubs, and settles down to get warm._  

**Choices**

- **Nudge your Nubbin to make room for him.**
    - Your Nubbin shuffles over. The baby leans on it as if it were a cushion, which, to be fair, it nearly is.
- **Keep perfectly still.**
    - You don’t move. Mother Wolf notices that, and something in her eyes softens towards you.

#### <a id="a24-01-cub-done"></a>Done `a24-01-cub-done`

**Mother Wolf** _(soft)_: A man’s cub. So that’s what they look like. No fur at all, and not a bit afraid of us.  
_The baby has found her warm side and gone to sleep against it, as if he had always lived here._  

### Step 3: Stand behind Mother Wolf while Shere Khan comes to the door.

Hint: Stay behind her. The cave mouth is too narrow for a tiger, and she knows it. · check: `sit:a24-mother-wolf:cave`

#### <a id="a24-01-door"></a>Scene `a24-01-door`

_The moonlight in the cave mouth goes dark. A tiger’s huge striped head is pushed into the gap, and his shoulders after it._  
_He can’t get further in. The cave is too narrow, and he knows it, and it makes him angrier._  
**Shere Khan** _(angry)_: A man’s cub went this way. Its parents have run off. Give it to me.  
**Father Wolf**: We are the Free People. We answer to our own leader, not to a tiger who hunts cows and woodcutters.  
**Shere Khan** _(angry)_: Free? Must Shere Khan stand in a wolf’s doorway and ask twice? Give me what is mine!  
_Mother Wolf comes forward over her cubs. In the dark her eyes and the tiger’s meet, and hers don’t move._  
**Mother Wolf** _(angry)_: You’ll have my answer, then. The cub is mine. He will not be killed. He will live to run with the pack.  
**Mother Wolf**: Go back to your woodcutters with your burnt feet, lame one. Go!  
_Shere Khan backs out of the cave mouth, growling. From outside he shouts that this isn’t the end of it._  
**Mother Wolf** _(soft)_: O thou Mowgli—for Mowgli the Frog I will call thee—  
**Mother Wolf** _(soft)_: One day you will hunt the one who hunted you. Lie still, little frog.  

**Choices**

- **Tell her that was the bravest thing you’ve seen.**
    - **Mother Wolf**: Brave? I was being a mother. It looks the same from outside.
- **Say nothing. Breathe out.**
    - You breathe out. So does Father Wolf, rather louder, and he pretends he didn’t.

#### <a id="a24-01-door-done"></a>Done `a24-01-door-done`

**Father Wolf** _(think)_: He’ll go to the pack, you know. The Law says the cubs must be shown at the Council.  
**Mother Wolf**: Then we’ll show him. At the full moon. Let them look.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a24-01-complete"></a>Complete `a24-01-complete`

_Night settles. Mother Wolf licks the man’s cub clean, then the wolf cubs, then, after some thought, your Nubbin._  
_Your Nubbin is so surprised it falls over. Then it curls up among the cubs, between Mowgli and the smallest wolf, and sleeps._  
**Mother Wolf** _(happy)_: It can stay there. A thing that sleeps in a wolf’s den is a wolf, more or less. Mostly less.  
_You lie by the wall and listen to six things breathing in the dark. One of them is yours._

## A24-2. The Council Rock

*The Jungle Book (Rudyard Kipling, 1894): “Mowgli’s Brothers”* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Law of the Jungle page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A24-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A24-1

**Cast:** Akela (Akela, the great grey Lone Wolf (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Shere Khan (Shere Khan, the lame tiger (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Baloo (Baloo, the brown bear who teaches the Law (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Bagheera (Bagheera, the black panther (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Mother Wolf (Raksha, Mother Wolf (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Father Wolf (Father Wolf (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Young Wolf (The young wolves of the Seeonee pack (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film)

### Step 1: Go up to the Council Rock with the wolves at the full moon.

Hint: Walk behind Mother Wolf. Stop at the edge of the circle; guests don’t go in. · check: `walk:a24-council-rock:full-moon`

#### <a id="a24-02-climb"></a>Scene `a24-02-climb`

_At the full moon the wolves climb to the Council Rock. Its hilltop is so heaped with boulders that a whole pack can sit hidden there._  
_Mowgli toddles beside Mother Wolf with a fist in her fur, and the cubs tumble round him._  
**Father Wolf**: The Law says every cub is shown to the pack, so that any wolf will know it again. Then it may run where it likes.  
**Father Wolf**: And no grown wolf may lift a tooth against it until it has made its first kill. That’s the Law.  
_On the flat rock lies a great grey wolf, old and strong, stretched out at his full length in the moonlight._  
**Father Wolf**: Akela. The Lone Wolf. He leads because he is strong, and cunning, and fair.  
_Your Nubbin looks at the moon, takes a deep breath, and lets out a howl. It comes out like a kettle._  
**Young Wolf** _(shock)_: What was that?  
**Mother Wolf** _(happy)_: A guest. Don’t stare. It’s rude.  

**Choices**

- **Hush your Nubbin gently.**
    - It hushes. Then, very quietly, it practises the howl again under its breath.
- **Tell it that was a fine howl.**
    - It sits up very straight. For the rest of the night it looks at the moon as if they had an understanding.

#### <a id="a24-02-climb-done"></a>Done `a24-02-climb-done`

_You stop at the edge of the circle, among the rocks, where the guests and the old ones sit._  
_Mother Wolf pushes Mowgli into the moonlight. He sits there laughing and playing with some pebbles._  

### Step 2: Watch the Council decide whether the man’s cub may stay.

Hint: Stay at the edge and keep quiet. Only the Council speaks. · check: `watch:a24-council:rock`

#### <a id="a24-02-council"></a>Scene `a24-02-council`

**Akela**: You know the Law. Look, wolves. Look well at each cub.  
_A muffled roar comes up from behind the rocks. Shere Khan has followed._  
**Shere Khan** _(angry)_: The cub is mine. Give him to me. What have the Free People to do with a man’s cub?  
**Akela**: Who speaks for this cub? The Law says two who are not his father and mother must speak for him.  
_Nobody answers. Mother Wolf gets ready for what she knows would be her last fight, if it comes to fighting._  
_Then a big brown bear heaves himself up on his hind legs and grunts. He has sat so still you took him for a rock._  
**Baloo** _(think)_: I’ll speak for him. A man’s cub never hurt anyone. I’m a poor hand at speeches, but that’s true. Let him run with you.  
**Baloo**: I’ll teach him myself.  
**Akela**: We need another. Who speaks besides Baloo?  
_A black shadow drops down into the circle: Bagheera the panther, the colour of a moonless night, with a voice like warm milk._  
**Bagheera**: Now to Baloo’s word I will add one bull, and a fat one, newly killed, not half a mile from here.  
**Bagheera**: Accept the man’s cub under the Law, and the bull is the pack’s. A fair trade, I think.  
_The young wolves’ ears go up, because they are always hungry. A whole bull, for one small cub who can’t do anyone harm?_  

**Choices**

- **Stay where you are and keep very still.**
    - You keep still. Beside you, your Nubbin keeps so still it forgets to blink.
- **Put your hand on your Nubbin’s back.**
    - Your Nubbin leans into your hand. Across the circle, Mother Wolf sees, and nods once.

#### <a id="a24-02-council-done"></a>Done `a24-02-council-done`

_The wolves come one by one to look at Mowgli, and then they go down the hill after the bull._  
**Akela**: He is the pack’s now. Take him home, and bring him up properly.

## A24-3. Baloo’s Lessons

*The Jungle Book (Rudyard Kipling, 1894): “Kaa’s Hunting”* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Law of the Jungle page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A24-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A24-2

**Cast:** Baloo (Baloo, the brown bear who teaches the Law (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Mowgli (Mowgli, the man’s cub (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Bagheera (Bagheera, the black panther (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film)

### Step 1: Join Mowgli at Baloo’s lessons in the glade.

Hint: The glade is below the Council Rock, by the fallen tree. Bring your Nubbin. · check: `visit:a24-baloo:glade`

#### <a id="a24-03-glade"></a>Scene `a24-03-glade`

_Seasons go by, as they do on this page. Mowgli is a boy of seven now, brown and quick and full of opinions._  
_Baloo sits on a fallen tree in a glade with his eyes half shut, like a schoolmaster who has seen everything twice._  
**Baloo**: Sit. Both of you. All three of you. The Law of the Jungle is old, and long, and it doesn’t care if you’re bored.  
**Mowgli** _(angry)_: I’m always bored. You’ve been teaching me for ever.  
**Baloo**: Wolves only need a little of the Law. A man’s cub must learn all of it. So must a guest.  
**Baloo**: Every branch that will hold you, and every rotten one that won’t. How to speak politely to the wild bees.  
**Baloo**: How to beg pardon of a bat you’ve woken. How to call out to the water-snakes before you jump into their pool.  
_Your Nubbin is trying to look at Baloo, the bees and the bat all at once. It has gone slightly cross-eyed._  

**Choices**

- **Listen hard and say each one back.**
    - **Baloo**: Good. A guest who listens. You can teach the cub manners while I teach him the Law.
    - **Mowgli**: Hmph.
- **Whisper to Mowgli that you’re bored too.**
    - **Mowgli**: Really? I thought guests liked everything.
    - **Baloo**: I heard that. I hear everything. Bears do.

#### <a id="a24-03-glade-done"></a>Done `a24-03-glade-done`

**Baloo**: First, the Strangers’ Hunting Call. You give it when you hunt outside your own ground, until you’re answered.  
**Baloo**: It asks leave, because you’re hungry. The answer gives it, as long as you eat what you catch and never kill for sport.  

### Step 2: Learn the Master Words of the jungle from Baloo.

Hint: Say the Words back exactly, with the right voice for each people. · check: `talk:a24-baloo:master-words`

#### <a id="a24-03-words"></a>Scene `a24-03-words`

**Baloo**: Now the Master Words. They matter more than anything else I teach. Say them right, and the jungle will let you be.  
**Baloo**: There is a word for the Hunting People, and a word for the Birds, and a word for the Snakes.  
**Mowgli** _(happy)_: Yes, yes, I know them. I know them better than you do. Listen.  
_Mowgli gabbles the Words the way a child says a poem for a grown-up, to get it over with._  
**Baloo**: Again. Slowly. For the Hunting People.  
**Baloo**: We be of one blood, ye and I.  
**Baloo**: Use the hunters’ voice for that, deep in the chest. For the Birds, you whistle it, like Chil the kite.  
**Baloo**: For the Snakes you say it with a hiss that can’t be written down. You’ll learn it from them.  
_Mowgli yawns in the middle of the hiss. Baloo cuffs him, not hard, but hard enough to sit him down._  
**Mowgli** _(angry)_: Ow! You always hit me! I hate you!  
**Bagheera** _(soft)_: He cuffs you because he loves you, little brother. Better a bear’s cuff now than a snake’s bite later.  

**Choices**

- **Say the Words for the Hunting People, slowly.**
    - You say them slowly, in the deepest voice you have. Baloo’s little eyes close all the way, which is praise.
- **Ask Baloo why the Words work.**
    - **Baloo**: Because everyone in the jungle has said them, for longer than the hills. A thing everyone keeps is a law.
- **Sit down next to Mowgli while he sulks.**
    - You sit beside him and say nothing. After a while he stops sulking, mostly so he can show you the bird whistle.

#### <a id="a24-03-words-done"></a>Done `a24-03-words-done`

_Your Nubbin, at the back, has been listening. It puffs itself up and gives a small, determined hiss._  
**Baloo** _(shock)_: That’s… not wrong, actually. Huh. The snakes might take that.  

### Step 3: Practise the calls in the glade until Baloo is satisfied.

Hint: Match each call to its people: the hunters, the birds, the snakes. Baloo answers when you get it right. · check: `puzzle:a24-calls:glade`

#### <a id="a24-03-calls"></a>Scene `a24-03-calls`

**Baloo**: Now we practise. I’ll be the jungle. You come into my ground and you say the right thing to the right one.  
_Baloo goes behind a bush and becomes, in turn, a very bad bird, a very bad snake and a passable bear._  
**Baloo** _(happy)_: Tweet. I am a kite. Very fierce. Speak.  
**Mowgli** _(happy)_: He’s the worst bird in the world.  
**Baloo**: I heard that too.  
_Mowgli whistles the Birds’ word without thinking. He is good at this, when he stops pretending he isn’t._  
_Your Nubbin hisses at the bear and growls at the bird. Baloo patiently explains it the right way round, twice._  

#### <a id="a24-03-calls-done"></a>Done `a24-03-calls-done`

**Baloo** _(think)_: Enough. The cub knows it and won’t say it, and the guest says it and is still learning it.  
**Baloo**: And the small one has its own law, which is a mystery to bears.  

### Complete

#### <a id="a24-03-complete"></a>Complete `a24-03-complete`

_Baloo lies back on his tree with a huge, satisfied grunt, as if he had done all the work himself._  
**Baloo**: The Law is like the creeper on a tree trunk. It holds everything up, and you only see it when it’s gone.  
**Mowgli**: Can I go now?  
**Baloo** _(think)_: Go. And don’t go near the Monkey-People. I mean it.  
_Mowgli looks away very quickly, the way a boy looks away who has been somewhere he shouldn’t._

## A24-4. The Monkey-People

*The Jungle Book (Rudyard Kipling, 1894): “Kaa’s Hunting”* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Law of the Jungle page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A24-3, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A24-3

**Cast:** Mowgli (Mowgli, the man’s cub (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Bandar-log (The Bandar-log, the Monkey-People (The Jungle Books); monkeys only, no human-coded design), Chil (Chil the kite (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Baloo (Baloo, the brown bear who teaches the Law (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Bagheera (Bagheera, the black panther (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Kaa (Kaa, the rock python (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film)

### Step 1: Look for Mowgli, who has gone off sulking under the trees.

Hint: Follow the chattering. The Monkey-People are never quiet for long. · check: `watch:a24-bandar-log:treetops`

#### <a id="a24-04-trees"></a>Scene `a24-04-trees`

_Mowgli lies on Bagheera’s back in the noon heat, telling a secret to anyone who’ll listen._  
**Mowgli** _(happy)_: The Bandar-log are nice to me. They give me nuts. They say I’ll be their leader one day. Baloo never says that.  
**Baloo** _(angry)_: The Monkey-People have no law. They have no leader. They have no memory.  
**Baloo**: They boast and chatter and pretend to be great, and then a nut falls and they forget it all and laugh.  
_Mowgli goes red and stalks off to sulk under a tree. Above him, the leaves start to whisper with a hundred grey hands._  
**Bandar-log** _(happy)_: Hoo! Look at us! Look how clever we are! Nobody in the jungle is as clever as us!  
**Bandar-log**: Everybody agrees! We just agreed! What were we agreeing about? Ooh, a nut!  
_A hundred monkeys drop through the branches like falling fruit. When they swing back up, Mowgli is with them._  
**Mowgli** _(shock)_: Baloo! Bagheera!  

**Choices**

- **Shout for Baloo and Bagheera.**
    - They are already running. Baloo runs like a landslide, and Bagheera like a shadow that is late for something.
- **Run underneath and keep the monkeys in sight.**
    - You run. Your Nubbin keeps its eyes on the treetops so well that it walks into a tree.

#### <a id="a24-04-trees-done"></a>Done `a24-04-trees-done`

_The monkeys travel the treetops along their own roads, high and fast. Below them, nobody can keep up for long._  
_Mowgli, swung from hand to hand, sees a kite circling high above. He takes a breath._  

### Step 2: Watch for Chil the kite, and see where he goes.

Hint: Look up. Chil circles over the monkeys, and then he turns back. · check: `watch:a24-chil:sky`

#### <a id="a24-04-chil"></a>Scene `a24-04-chil`

_Mowgli whistles the Master Word of the Birds, the way he did in the glade when he forgot to pretend he couldn’t._  
_High above, Chil the kite tips one wing, and drops lower to see who has called him by the proper word._  
**Chil**: You’re no bird, little one. And yet you have the Word.  
**Mowgli** _(shock)_: Watch where they take me! Tell Baloo, and Bagheera! The bear and the panther of the Seeonee wolves!  
**Chil**: Whose trail? Who calls?  
**Mowgli**: Mowgli! The Frog, the wolves’ man-child! Please! Watch where I go!  
_Chil swings away, higher and higher, until he is a dot against the blue, keeping the treetops in sight._  
_You and Baloo and Bagheera are still far behind on the ground when a shadow crosses the path._  
**Chil**: They’ve taken him to the Cold Lairs. The Monkey-People stay there a night, a month. Who knows? They don’t.  

**Choices**

- **Thank Chil properly, with the Birds’ word.**
    - **Chil**: Good hunting, guest. You whistle like a duck. But you whistle. That’s more than most.
- **Turn to Baloo. What now?**
    - **Baloo**: Now I think. Bears don’t think fast. But they think all the way down.

#### <a id="a24-04-chil-done"></a>Done `a24-04-chil-done`

**Bagheera** _(angry)_: The Cold Lairs. They’re not afraid of us there. They know we can’t climb the way they do.  
**Baloo**: They’re afraid of one thing. Kaa. He can climb as well as they can, and they know it in their bones.

**Cast:** Mowgli (Mowgli, the man’s cub (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Baloo (Baloo, the brown bear who teaches the Law (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Kaa (Kaa, the rock python (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Bagheera (Bagheera, the black panther (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Grey Brother (Grey Brother, Mother Wolf’s eldest cub (The Jungle Books); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Messua (Messua, the village woman who takes Mowgli in (The Jungle Books); design with Indian advisers)

### Step 1: Go back with Mowgli to the Council Rock.

Hint: Baloo and Kaa are waiting. They know already. · check: `visit:a24-council-rock:dawn`

#### <a id="a24-16-rock"></a>Scene `a24-16-rock`

_You come back to the Council Rock in the grey before dawn. Baloo is there, very old now and almost blind._  
_Kaa is coiled round the rock, three times, the way he lies when he means to stay a while._  
**Baloo** _(soft)_: Cub. You’ve come to tell us something. Don’t bother. We know. We’ve known since spring.  
**Mowgli** _(sad)_: I don’t want to go. But I want to go. Both. I don’t understand it.  
**Kaa** _(think)_: Changing your skin. I do it every year, and it never gets easier. It itches, and then it fits.  
**Kaa**: Man goes to Man at the last, though the Jungle does not cast him out.  
**Baloo** _(soft)_: Make your own trail, cub. Make your own lair. Find your own people. We’ll still be here. In a way.  

**Choices**

- **Go and sit by Baloo.**
    - **Baloo**: Is that the guest? Your voice has changed. You used to say the Words like a frightened bird.
- **Thank Kaa for the Cold Lairs.**
    - **Kaa**: It was nothing. A dance. A wall. I enjoyed the wall.

#### <a id="a24-16-rock-done"></a>Done `a24-16-rock-done`

**Mowgli** _(sad)_: Where’s Bagheera? I can’t go without Bagheera.  
_Far off down the hill, something heavy is crashing through the bushes. Then it stops._  

### Step 2: Wait at the Council Rock for Bagheera.

Hint: He’ll come. Panthers are always last, and always on purpose. · check: `wait:a24-bagheera:council-rock`

#### <a id="a24-16-bagheera"></a>Scene `a24-16-bagheera`

_Bagheera comes up the hill at a trot, breathing hard, with the morning sun on his black coat._  
**Bagheera** _(think)_: I’ve killed a bull. Down in the valley. Young and fat. I paid for you with a bull, cub, at the beginning.  
**Bagheera**: Now I’ve paid it again. The debt’s all square. So you go free. Owing nobody. That’s how it should be.  
**Mowgli** _(sad)_: Bagheera. I’ll come back. I’ll come and see you.  
**Bagheera** _(soft)_: Don’t. Don’t come back for us. Come back for you, if you ever need to. We’ll know.  
_He licks Mowgli’s feet, once, the way the panthers do it. Then he turns away down the hill._  
_At the bottom he looks back._  
**Bagheera** _(soft)_: Good hunting on a new trail, Master of the Jungle! Remember, Bagheera loved thee.  
_And he’s gone into the trees. Mowgli stands with his hands open, the way you do when you’ve dropped something._  
**Mowgli** _(sad)_: Guest. You came at the start. You were in the cave, the first night. Say the Words with me. One more time.  
_So you say them, the two of you, standing on the Council Rock in the early sun._  
_You give the Master Words back to Mowgli, the way Baloo taught them, and your Nubbin bows with you._  

#### <a id="a24-16-bagheera-done"></a>Done `a24-16-bagheera-done`

_Mowgli looks at you for a long time. Then he puts his hand on your Nubbin’s head._  
**Mowgli** _(soft)_: Will you walk the first mile with me? Just the first. Then you go home, and I go on.

#### <a id="a24-16-trail"></a>Scene `a24-16-trail`

**Grey Brother** _(happy)_: The jungle isn’t shut. We’ll be his four brothers, wherever he lives. There’s no law that says we can’t.

**Mowgli** _(soft)_: Here. This is the mile. The stone with the moss on.

**Choices**

## A24-17. The White Seal

*The Jungle Book (Rudyard Kipling, 1894): “The White Seal”* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Law of the Jungle page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A24-16, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A24-16

**Cast:** Sea Catch (Sea Catch, Kotick’s father (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Matkah (Matkah, Kotick’s mother (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Kotick (Kotick, the white seal (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Old Seal (The holluschickie and old bulls of Novastoshnah (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film)

### Step 1: Turn the page, and step onto the seals’ beach in the north.

Hint: Keep to the rocks at the top of the beach. The bulls are busy, and very large. · check: `explore:a24-novastoshnah:beach`

#### <a id="a24-17-beach"></a>Scene `a24-17-beach`

_The jungle page has a last leaf folded into it. You open it, and the air goes cold and smells of salt and fish._  
_These are the Pribilof Islands in the Bering Sea. Unangax̂ families were brought here long ago to work the seal harvest, and made the islands home._  
_Below you is a beach called Novastoshnah, roaring from end to end with seals: thousands and thousands of them._  
_The big bulls are shoving each other for places on the rocks, chest to chest, bellowing. Nobody gets hurt; everybody gets loud._  
**Sea Catch** _(angry)_: Out of my place! Out! I was here last year, and the year before! This is my rock, and I’m keeping it!  
_A big grey bull with a scarred neck wins his rock, and flops down on it, very pleased with himself. That’s Sea Catch._  
**Matkah** _(think)_: You’re late. You’re always late. And you’ve been fighting again. Look at your neck.  
_That’s Matkah, his wife: smaller, soft-eyed, sensible. Beside her, in the shelter of the rock, is something new._  
**Matkah** _(shock)_: Sea Catch, our baby’s going to be white!  
**Sea Catch** _(think)_: Nonsense. There’s never been such a thing as a white seal.  
**Matkah**: I can’t help that. There’s going to be one now.  

**Choices**

- **Creep closer to look at the pup.**
    - He is pale as sea-foam, all over, with soft blue eyes. Everyone else on the beach is dark grey.
- **Keep your Nubbin back from the shoving bulls.**
    - A bull the size of a wardrobe flops past. Your Nubbin hides behind your legs and peeks round them.

#### <a id="a24-17-beach-done"></a>Done `a24-17-beach-done`

_They name the pup Kotick. He crawls up to your Nubbin, nose to nose, and they look at each other for a long while._  
_Then your Nubbin, who believes it is a wolf, decides on the spot that it is a seal as well._  

### Step 2: Paddle in the surf while Kotick learns to swim.

Hint: Stay in the shallows. The big waves are for seals, not for guests. · check: `swim:a24-surf:novastoshnah`

#### <a id="a24-17-surf"></a>Scene `a24-17-surf`

_Seal pups can’t swim when they’re born. They have to learn, and it’s a struggle, and Kotick is no different._  
**Matkah** _(happy)_: In you go. The waves will lift you. Don’t fight them. Ride them. That’s all swimming is.  
_Kotick goes in, and a wave picks him up and puts him back on the beach upside down. He tries again._  
_You wade in up to your knees with your Nubbin in your arms. The water is so cold it makes your teeth ache._  
_Your Nubbin wriggles out of your arms and paddles beside Kotick, doggedly, the way it does everything._  
**Kotick** _(happy)_: I did it! I’m swimming! Look, Mother! Look at the little round one, it’s swimming too!  
_By autumn Kotick swims like a seal born to it, which he is. Then the whole herd turns south, out to the open sea._  
_When they come back the next summer, Kotick is a year older, and still as white as foam._  

**Choices**

- **Cheer for Kotick from the shallows.**
    - Kotick does a roll in the water, showing off, and swallows a mouthful of sea. He comes up spluttering and pleased.
- **Wrap your Nubbin in your coat after.**
    - It shivers inside your coat for a while, and then falls asleep, smelling strongly of seaweed.

#### <a id="a24-17-surf-done"></a>Done `a24-17-surf-done`

**Kotick** _(think)_: There are the young ones going inland. The bachelors. Where do they go? I’m going to see.  
_You follow him up the beach. You wish, already, that you hadn’t._  

### Step 3: Follow Kotick inland, and turn back with him.

Hint: Stay with Kotick. When he turns away, turn with him. · check: `watch:a24-drove:inland`

#### <a id="a24-17-drove"></a>Scene `a24-17-drove`

_Men from the village are walking a herd of young seals slowly inland, away from the sea, with long sticks._  
_The men work quietly, at a job they were made to do for a company far away that owns it._  
_Kotick follows at the back, curious. Then he understands what happens to the young seals at the end of the walk._  
_He turns away. You turn with him, and put your hand over your Nubbin’s eyes, and the page turns too._  
**Kotick** _(shock)_: They don’t come back. None of them come back. Every summer. Mother, every summer?  
**Matkah** _(sad)_: Every summer. It’s been so since before your grandfather’s grandfather. There’s nothing to be done.  
**Kotick** _(angry)_: Then I’ll find somewhere else. A beach where men can’t come. There must be one, somewhere in the sea.  
**Old Seal** _(happy)_: Find a beach where men can’t come? Listen to the white one. Go and catch the moon while you’re at it.  

**Choices**

- **Tell Kotick you believe him.**
    - **Kotick**: Do you? You’re the first. Well, second. Your little round one looked like it did.
- **Sit down next to him on the cold beach, and wait.**
    - You sit beside him. After a while he stops shaking, and looks out at the sea instead of inland.

#### <a id="a24-17-drove-done"></a>Done `a24-17-drove-done`

**Kotick** _(angry)_: I’m going to look. Every summer, every winter, until I find it. And then I’ll bring them all.  
_Your Nubbin sits up beside him on the beach and looks out to sea too, as if it means to help._

## A24-18. Where No Man Comes

*The Jungle Book (Rudyard Kipling, 1894): “The White Seal”* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Law of the Jungle page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A24-17, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A24-17

**Cast:** Kotick (Kotick, the white seal (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Sea Lion (The old sea lion (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Sea Vitch (Sea Vitch, the walrus (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Gull (The gulls of the northern islands (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Sea Catch (Sea Catch, Kotick’s father (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Matkah (Matkah, Kotick’s mother (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film), Old Seal (The holluschickie and old bulls of Novastoshnah (“The White Seal”, The Jungle Book); Kipling’s text; original design, nothing from any Disney film)

### Step 1: Search the northern sea with Kotick for a safe beach.

Hint: Ask everyone. The old sea lion first. Mind the walrus; he’s grumpy. · check: `swim:a24-search:islands`

#### <a id="a24-18-search"></a>Scene `a24-18-search`

_Kotick searches for five seasons. The page carries you along with him, from island to island, through fog and ice._  
**Kotick** _(think)_: Old sea lion! You know everything. Is there a beach in the sea where men never come?  
**Sea Lion**: Never heard of one. Men come everywhere, sooner or later. Ask Sea Vitch. He’s older than me, and ruder.  
_Sea Vitch the walrus is asleep on a rock, enormous, whiskery, with tusks like two old yellow candles._  
**Sea Vitch** _(angry)_: What? Who? Go away. I was asleep. I’m always asleep. What kind of a seal is white, anyway? Shoo.  
**Kotick** _(angry)_: You fat old clam-cracker! I only asked you a question!  
_The gulls on the rocks above hear that, and love it. They have never liked Sea Vitch._  
**Gull** _(happy)_: Clam-cracker! Clam-cracker! Sea Vitch is a clam-cracker! Clam-crack-crack!  
**Sea Vitch**: Hrrmph. Ask the Sea Cows, then, and leave me in peace. If they’re still alive. Nobody’s seen them for years.  

**Choices**

- **Apologise to Sea Vitch for the noise.**
    - **Sea Vitch**: Hm. Manners. From a land-thing. The world is full of surprises. Still go away.
- **Shoo the gulls away.**
    - The gulls rise up shrieking, and settle again two feet away, and start again. Gulls are like that.

#### <a id="a24-18-search-done"></a>Done `a24-18-search-done`

**Kotick** _(think)_: The Sea Cows. I’ve never even seen one. Nobody has. Everyone says they’re a story.  
_Five summers and five winters go by. Kotick grows big and strong, and stays as white as the first day._  

### Step 2: Follow the Sea Cows through the dark water with Kotick.

Hint: Stay close behind Kotick. The page lets you breathe; it’s that kind of page. · check: `swim:a24-sea-cows:tunnel`

#### <a id="a24-18-cows"></a>Scene `a24-18-cows`

_At the end of the fifth winter, far to the west, Kotick finds them: huge, slow, dark shapes grazing on the kelp._  
_Sea Cows. Bigger than walruses, quieter than fish, with small heads and soft lips, moving very slowly._  
**Kotick** _(think)_: Hello? Excuse me? I’m looking for a beach. A safe one. Where men don’t come. Please.  
_The Sea Cows say nothing; they can’t, or they won’t. They only chew the kelp, and look at him._  
_Then, very slowly, they begin to move north, along the coast. Kotick follows, and you follow Kotick._  
_At last they dive, and swim into a black opening in the cliff, deep under the water. A tunnel._  
_You hold your breath and your Nubbin and go in after them. The atlas is a kind page here; your breath lasts._  
_Then light ahead, green and gold. You come up into a quiet bay, with long smooth beaches all round it._  

**Choices**

- **Look round the bay.**
    - There are beaches sheltered from the wind, with cliffs on every side and no paths down. Nobody has ever walked here.
- **Turn and thank the Sea Cows.**
    - They are already going, slow and silent, back into the dark of the tunnel. One of them looks back, once.

#### <a id="a24-18-cows-done"></a>Done `a24-18-cows-done`

**Kotick** _(happy)_: This is it. This is it. Men can’t come here. Nobody can, unless they’re a seal, and swim under the cliffs.  
_Kotick hauls out on the beach and lies there, breathing, in the sun. Your Nubbin lies down beside him._  

### Step 3: Go home with Kotick, and watch him bring the seals.

Hint: Stay at the top of the beach. This is shoving, not fighting, and nobody gets hurt. · check: `watch:a24-fight:novastoshnah`

#### <a id="a24-18-home"></a>Scene `a24-18-home`

_Kotick swims all the way home to Novastoshnah and tells the seals about the hidden beaches._  
_They laugh at him, of course: a white seal, with his head full of stories._  
**Old Seal** _(happy)_: Hidden beaches! Go and lie in the sun, white one, and stop bothering your elders.  
_So Kotick, who has grown big and strong in five years of searching, puts his chest against the biggest bull’s chest._  
_And he shoves, and roars, and shoves again, all along the beach, until every bull who laughed has been pushed into the sea._  
**Sea Catch** _(happy)_: That’s my son! That’s my son! Come on, then, who’s next? Who else says there are no beaches?  
_Sea Catch joins in, shoving with his scarred old neck and shouting for joy. Nobody bleeds, but everybody is very wet._  
**Kotick** _(angry)_: I’m going to the new beaches! Who’s coming with me?  

**Choices**

- **Cheer from the top of the beach.**
    - Your Nubbin cheers too, like a kettle: a wolf kettle, or a seal kettle. It doesn’t mind which.
- **Look for Matkah.**
    - **Matkah**: He was always going to do it. I told his father so. His father didn’t believe me either.

#### <a id="a24-18-home-done"></a>Done `a24-18-home-done`

_The seals stop laughing. They look at the white seal, and they look at each other._  
_And then, a few at a time, then more, then nearly ten thousand, they follow him out to sea._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a24-18-complete"></a>Complete `a24-18-complete`

_You stand on the empty beach at Novastoshnah and watch the herd go, a long dark line with one white seal at the front._  
_Every summer after, more of them go. The men who come to the beach find fewer seals, and then none._  
_Your Nubbin watches until the white speck is gone. Then it turns round and looks at you, as if it has been somewhere._  
_The page goes soft, like sea-fog. You are back at your own kitchen table, with the atlas open at the end of the book._  
_There is a small white seal sitting by the pond outside, flopping, looking pleased with itself. It seems to know the way._  
_Kotick took them to the hidden beaches, and the seals live there still, safe, in that sea where no man comes._
