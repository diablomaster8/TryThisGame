# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A24: The Law of the Jungle

## A24-1. The Cave

- `a24-01-s1`: Step into the Seoni hills and find the wolves’ cave. · _Touch the page where the cave mouth is, then climb the slope towards it._
- `a24-01-s2`: Keep still in the cave while Tabaqui and the tiger’s roar go by. · _Stay by the wall. Watch the cave mouth, not the jackal._
- `a24-01-s3`: Stand behind Mother Wolf while Shere Khan comes to the door. · _Stay behind her. The cave mouth is too narrow for a tiger, and she knows it._

#### `reader:a24-01-page` · [main](main.md#a24-01-page)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at hills the colour of warm brick, going blue at the edges with dusk._  
_A card is tucked into the page in Nova’s small writing. “Rudyard Kipling was born in Bombay in 1865, and he loved India.”_  
_“He also believed Britain had the right to rule India. He was wrong: India was never Britain’s to rule.”_  
_“Read his animals with both facts in your pocket. N.”_  
_You put the card back. Warm dusk breathes off the paper, and there is dry grass under your boots._  
_This is Seoni, in the very middle of India. The hills are low and rocky, and at night the jungle wakes up._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.3-5, ask): finding a place on a map: Seoni is in the middle of India  
> _Nova’s atlas shows the whole of India, a country shaped a bit like a kite. Where is Seoni on it?_  
> - ✅ Right in the middle. → Yes. Seoni is in central India, in a state called Madhya Pradesh. Real tigers still live in forests near there, in a park called Pench.
> - On the far northern edge. → Not quite. The far north is where the great Himalayan mountains are. Seoni is in the middle of the country, with hills and forests around it.
> - On an island out at sea. → Good guess about islands. India has some. But Seoni is inland, in the middle of the country, a long way from any sea.
> _For grown-ups: Located Seoni in the middle of India and learned that real tigers live in nearby forests._

_Ahead is a slope of rocks with a cave in it, and above it a long flat rock with nobody on it yet._  
_In the cave a big grey wolf is stretching. A she-wolf lies with her nose across four tumbling cubs._  
**Father Wolf**: Hrrm. Who is that at the door?  
**Father Wolf**: A guest, by the smell. A very small one, and a strange one.  
**Mother Wolf**: Let them in, if they have come politely. Nobody who knocks politely gets eaten in this house.  

**Choices**

- **Bow at the cave mouth and wait to be asked.**
    - **Mother Wolf**: Good manners. Come in, then, and mind the cubs’ tails.
    - **Mother Wolf**: They leave them everywhere.
- **Let your Nubbin go in first.**
    - Your Nubbin trots in, sniffs the nearest cub, and is sniffed back, thoroughly, from both ends.
- **Sit down outside and let them look at you.**
    - **Father Wolf**: Hm. It sits like it means no harm.
    - **Father Wolf**: Come in out of the dew.

> _For grown-ups: Learned that many night animals have a mirror layer in their eyes that helps them see in dim light._

**Mother Wolf**: Guests of the Free People sit by the wall. That is you.  
**Mother Wolf**: And your little one sits wherever it likes, apparently.  

#### `reader:a24-01-cub` · [main](main.md#a24-01-cub)

_A thin shadow slides into the cave mouth on its belly: a jackal, smiling with every one of his teeth._  
**Tabaqui**: Oh, chief of the wolves, what a cave! What a family!  
**Tabaqui**: Such fine strong teeth on those cubs already!  
**Tabaqui**: And a guest! How grand.  
**Tabaqui**: Is there a bone, for poor Tabaqui? Even a dry one.  
**Tabaqui**: I am not proud.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): flattery: kind-sounding words used to get something  
> _Tabaqui says lots of lovely things about the cave and the cubs. What does he really want?_  
> - ✅ A bone, and the news he can sell. → Right. His words are sweet because they are working for him. We call that flattery: praise that is meant to get something, not to be kind.
> - To be the wolves’ best friend. → He acts like it. But then he crunches the bone and runs off to gossip. A real friend’s kind words don’t come with a hand held out.
> - To teach the cubs to hunt. → Ha! No. Tabaqui is the sort who follows hunters and picks up the scraps. His sweet talk is how he gets them.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out that Tabaqui’s flattery was a way of getting a bone, and that kind words can have a hidden aim._

**Father Wolf**: There is a bone by the wall. Take it and go.  
**Tabaqui**: I only mention, in passing, that Shere Khan is hunting in these hills. Lame Shere Khan.  
**Tabaqui**: Thought you would like to know.  
_He crunches the bone, flatters the cubs’ teeth once more, says something unkind about their ears, and is gone._  
_Down in the valley a tiger’s whine rises, long and angry. Father Wolf’s fur stands up._  
**Father Wolf**: The fool. He is hunting Man, and a woodcutter’s fire has burnt his feet.  
_Something rustles in the bushes. Father Wolf crouches to spring, and stops himself in mid-air._  
**Father Wolf**: A man’s cub! Look!  
_In the cave mouth, holding on to a low branch, stands a brown baby who has only just learned to walk._  
_He is not frightened in the least. He toddles in, pushes among the cubs, and settles down to get warm._  

**Choices**

- **Nudge your Nubbin to make room for him.**
    - Your Nubbin shuffles over. The baby leans on it as if it were a cushion, which, to be fair, it nearly is.
- **Keep perfectly still.**
    - You do not move. Mother Wolf notices that, and something in her eyes softens towards you.

#### `reader:a24-01-cub-done` · [main](main.md#a24-01-cub-done)

**Mother Wolf**: A man’s cub. So that is what they look like.  
**Mother Wolf**: No fur at all, and not a bit afraid of us.  
_The baby finds her warm side and goes to sleep against it, as if he had always lived here._  
_Four wolf cubs sleep in a heap beside him, a tangle of tails and ears. Each one has four small paws._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): multiplication as equal groups (4 groups of 4)  
> _Four cubs are asleep in a heap. Each cub has four paws._  
> _How many wolf paws are in the heap?_  
> - ✅ 16. → Yes. Four groups of four is 16: 4 × 4 = 16. Four paws, four paws, four paws, four paws.
> - 8. → That is four plus four. But there are four cubs, and each has four paws. Count them in fours: 4, 8, 12, 16. Sixteen paws.
> - 4. → That is how many paws one cub has. With four cubs we count four paws four times: 4, 8, 12, 16. Sixteen paws.
> _For grown-ups: Used equal groups to work out 4 × 4 = 16 paws._


#### `reader:a24-01-door` · [main](main.md#a24-01-door)

_The moonlight in the cave mouth goes dark. A huge striped head is pushed into the gap, and shoulders after it._  
_He cannot get further in. The cave is too narrow, and he knows it, and it makes him angrier._  
**Shere Khan**: A man’s cub went this way. Its parents have run off.  
**Shere Khan**: Give it to me.  
**Father Wolf**: We are the Free People. We answer to our own leader, not to a tiger who hunts cows and woodcutters.  
**Shere Khan**: Free? Must Shere Khan stand in a wolf’s doorway and ask twice?  
**Shere Khan**: Give me what is mine!  
_Mother Wolf comes forward over her cubs. In the dark her eyes and the tiger’s meet, and hers do not move._  
**Mother Wolf**: You have my answer. The cub is mine.  
**Mother Wolf**: He will not be harmed, and he will grow up to run with the pack.  
**Mother Wolf**: Go back to your woodcutters with your burnt feet, lame one. Go!  
_Shere Khan backs out of the cave mouth, growling. From the dark he shouts that this is not the end of it._  
**Mother Wolf**: You are mine now, little one. I will call you Mowgli, the Frog, because you have no fur at all.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): what bravery is: being scared and doing the right thing anyway  
> _Mother Wolf was brave. Her heart must have been pounding._  
> _What do you think makes someone brave?_  
> - Not feeling scared at all. → That is a fair answer. Lots of people think it. But then a brave deed would be easy. Many brave people say they were frightened and did it anyway.
> - Feeling scared, and doing the right thing anyway. → Fair answer too. Courage is not the lack of fear. It is a decision you make with your legs still wobbling.
> - Caring about someone enough to stand up for them. → A fair answer. Mother Wolf said she was only being a mother. Sometimes love gives you a steadier voice than you thought you had.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about what courage is, and whether being brave means being unafraid._

**Mother Wolf**: Lie still, little Frog. You are safe in here.  

**Choices**

- **Tell her that was the bravest thing you have seen.**
    - **Mother Wolf**: Brave? I was being a mother.
    - **Mother Wolf**: It looks the same from outside.
- **Say nothing. Breathe out.**
    - You breathe out. So does Father Wolf, rather louder, and he pretends he did not.

#### `reader:a24-01-door-done` · [main](main.md#a24-01-door-done)

**Father Wolf**: He will go to the pack, you know. The Law says every cub must be shown at the Council.  
**Mother Wolf**: Then we will show him. At the full moon.  
**Mother Wolf**: Let them look.  
_Above the cave mouth, a pale moon is hanging, almost round, but not quite._  

> **Lesson** (science, 5-ESS1-2, ask): the Moon goes from full to full in about a month  
> _The wolves meet at the full moon. How long is it from one full moon to the next?_  
> - ✅ About a month. → Yes. The Moon takes about 29 and a half days to go from full to full. It shrinks away to nothing, then grows back to a bright round circle again.
> - About a day. → The Moon does change a little each night, but not that fast. From one full moon to the next takes about a month.
> - About a year. → A year is the Sun’s big circle with the seasons. The Moon’s shapes come round much faster. A full moon returns about every month.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that the Moon takes about a month to go from full moon to full moon._

## A24-2. The Council Rock

- `a24-02-s1`: Go up to the Council Rock with the wolves at the full moon. · _Walk behind Mother Wolf. Stop at the edge of the circle; guests do not go in._
- `a24-02-s2`: Watch the Council decide whether the man’s cub may stay. · _Stay at the edge and keep quiet. Only the Council speaks._

#### `reader:a24-02-climb` · [main](main.md#a24-02-climb)

_At the full moon the wolves climb to the Council Rock. The hilltop is so heaped with boulders that a whole pack can sit hidden there._  
_Mowgli toddles beside Mother Wolf with a fist in her fur, and the cubs tumble round him._  
**Father Wolf**: The Law says every cub is shown to the pack, so that any wolf will know it again. Then it may run where it likes.  
_On the flat rock lies a great grey wolf, old and strong, stretched out in the moonlight._  
**Father Wolf**: Akela, the Lone Wolf. He leads because he is strong, and cunning, and fair.  
_Your Nubbin looks at the moon, takes a deep breath, and lets out a howl. It comes out like a kettle._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, ask): sound is made by vibrations; animals use sound to talk across distance  
> **Father Wolf**: Wolves howl to talk across the hills. What is a howl, really, to the air around it?  
> - ✅ Air wobbling very fast, called vibrating. → Yes. A throat wobbles the air, the air wobbles your ear, and you hear it. Open country carries a wolf’s howl for kilometres.
> - A sort of light you can hear. → Light and sound are different things. Light can cross empty space, and sound cannot. Sound is air wobbling, called vibrating.
> - Nothing. It is only inside our heads. → Good thinking about hearing. But sound is real, out in the air. Put a hand on your throat and hum: that buzz is the vibration.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a howl is a vibration of the air, and that wolves use it to talk over long distances._

**Young Wolf**: What was that?  
**Mother Wolf**: A guest. Don’t stare.  
**Mother Wolf**: It is rude.  

**Choices**

- **Hush your Nubbin gently.**
    - It hushes. Then, very quietly, it practises the howl again under its breath.
- **Tell it that was a fine howl.**
    - It sits up very straight. For the rest of the night it looks at the moon as if they had an understanding.

#### `reader:a24-02-climb-done` · [main](main.md#a24-02-climb-done)

_You stop at the edge of the circle, among the rocks, where the guests and the old ones sit._  
_Mother Wolf pushes Mowgli into the moonlight. He sits there laughing, with a heap of pebbles from the hilltop._  
**Mowgli**: Eight white ones and five grey ones. Look.  
**Mowgli**: I sorted them.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.D.8, ask): adding and comparing: 8 + 5 = 13, and 3 more white than grey  
> **Mowgli**: How many pebbles do I have altogether? And how many more white ones than grey ones?  
> - ✅ 13 altogether, and 3 more white ones. → Yes. 8 + 5 = 13, and 8 − 5 = 3. Adding finds the total. Subtracting finds the difference.
> - 13 altogether, and 5 more white ones. → The total is right: 8 + 5 = 13. Well done. To find how many more, take the smaller from the bigger: 8 − 5 = 3.
> - 12 altogether, and 3 more white ones. → The difference is right: 8 − 5 = 3. The total is one more than that. Count on from 8: 9, 10, 11, 12, 13.
> _For grown-ups: Practised adding two numbers within 20 and finding the difference by subtracting._


#### `reader:a24-02-council` · [main](main.md#a24-02-council)

**Akela**: You know the Law. Look, wolves.  
**Akela**: Look closely at each cub.  
_A muffled roar comes up from behind the rocks. Shere Khan has followed._  
**Shere Khan**: The cub is mine. Give him to me.  
**Shere Khan**: What have the Free People to do with a man’s cub?  
**Akela**: Who speaks for this cub? The Law says two, who are not his father and mother, must speak for him.  
_Nobody answers. Mother Wolf gets ready for what she knows would be her last fight, if it comes to fighting._  
_Then a big brown bear heaves himself up on his hind legs. He has sat so still you took him for a rock._  
**Baloo**: I will speak for him. A man’s cub never hurt anyone.  
**Baloo**: I am a poor hand at speeches, but that is true. Let him run with you.  
**Baloo**: I will teach him myself.  
**Akela**: We need another. Who speaks besides Baloo?  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.3-5, ask): why a fair rule asks for more than one voice (fairness and rules)  
> _The Law says two wolves, who are not his parents, must speak for a cub. Why might the Law ask for two?_  
> _There is no right answer._  
> - One voice could be wrong, so two is safer. → Fair answer. Two people can check each other, and a mistake is harder to make. Judges often sit in groups for the same reason.
> - Parents should decide, not anyone else. → Fair answer. Parents know the cub best, and they speak first. The Law adds others in case the parents are missing, scared, or the cub has nobody.
> - It depends whether the two speakers are honest. → Sharp answer. Any rule is only as good as the people who follow it. Baloo and Bagheera each promise something real.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why a fair rule might need more than one person to speak up for someone._

_A black shadow drops into the circle: Bagheera the panther, the colour of a moonless night, with a voice like warm milk._  
**Bagheera**: I will add my own gift to Baloo’s word: a whole bull, not far from here, for the pack to share.  
**Bagheera**: Accept the man’s cub under the Law, and the bull is the pack’s. A fair trade, I think.  
_The young wolves’ ears go up, because they are always hungry. A whole bull, for one small cub?_  

**Choices**

- **Stay where you are and keep very still.**
    - You keep still. Beside you, your Nubbin keeps so still it forgets to blink.
- **Put your hand on your Nubbin’s back.**
    - Your Nubbin leans into your hand. Across the circle, Mother Wolf sees, and nods once.

## A24-3. Baloo’s Lessons

- `a24-03-s1`: Join Mowgli at Baloo’s lessons in the glade. · _The glade is below the Council Rock, by the fallen tree. Bring your Nubbin._
- `a24-03-s2`: Learn the Master Words of the jungle from Baloo. · _Say the Words back slowly, with the right voice for each people._
- `a24-03-s3`: Practise the calls in the glade until Baloo is satisfied. · _Match each call to its people: the hunters, the birds, the snakes._

#### `reader:a24-03-glade` · [main](main.md#a24-03-glade)

_Seasons go by, as they do on this page. Mowgli is a boy of seven now, brown and quick and full of opinions._  
_Baloo sits on a fallen tree in a glade with his eyes half shut, like a schoolmaster who has seen everything twice._  
**Baloo**: Sit. Both of you.  
**Baloo**: All three of you. The Law of the Jungle is old, and long, and it does not care if you are bored.  
**Mowgli**: I am always bored. You have been teaching me for ever.  
**Baloo**: Wolves need only a little of the Law. A man’s cub must learn all of it.  
**Baloo**: So must a guest.  
**Baloo**: Which branches will hold you, and which are rotten. How to speak politely to the wild bees.  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): bees carry pollen from flower to flower, so fruit and seeds can grow  
> **Baloo**: Why speak politely to bees? Because they are busy.  
> **Baloo**: They carry pollen from flower to flower. What grows because of it?  
> - ✅ Fruit and seeds. → Right. Pollen from one flower reaches another, and then that flower can make fruit and seeds. Without bees, many trees would have far less fruit.
> - Rocks. → Ha. Rocks do their growing very slowly, and bees have nothing to do with it. Pollen helps flowers turn into fruit and seeds. That is what bees are for.
> - Clouds. → Clouds are made of water, far above the flowers. Bees carry pollen, and the flowers use it to make fruit and seeds.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that bees spread pollen between flowers so that plants can make fruit and seeds._

**Baloo**: How to beg pardon of a bat you have woken. How to call to the water-snakes before you jump into their pool.  
_Your Nubbin tries to look at Baloo, the bees and the bat all at once. It has gone slightly cross-eyed._  

**Choices**

- **Listen hard and say each one back.**
    - **Baloo**: Good. A guest who listens.
    - **Baloo**: You can teach the cub manners while I teach him the Law.
    - **Mowgli**: Hmph.
- **Whisper to Mowgli that you are bored too.**
    - **Mowgli**: Really? I thought guests liked everything.
    - **Baloo**: I heard that. I hear everything.
    - **Baloo**: Bears do.

#### `reader:a24-03-glade-done` · [main](main.md#a24-03-glade-done)

**Baloo**: First, the Strangers’ Hunting Call. You give it when you hunt outside your own territory, until you are answered.  
**Baloo**: It asks leave, because you are hungry. The answer gives it, as long as you eat what you catch and never kill for sport.  
**Mowgli**: Why ask? If I am hungry, I am hungry.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.3-5, ask): respecting other people’s land and rules by asking leave  
> _Why does the Strangers’ Call ask leave before hunting on another wolf’s ground?_  
> - ✅ Asking shows respect and stops fights. → Yes. A territory is a place that someone looks after. Asking says: I know it is yours. Real animals mark theirs with scent and calls.
> - So the wolves can hunt everything. → The opposite. The Call comes with a promise: eat what you catch, kill for no sport. Asking leave is for respect, and it keeps the peace.
> - It is just a funny noise. → It sounds funny to a bear. But the noise has a meaning: please, may I? It is how strangers say they come in peace.
> _For grown-ups: Understood that asking leave before using someone else’s ground is a way of showing respect and avoiding fights._

_Your Nubbin looks at the ground it is sitting on, and then at the next patch of ground, and tiptoes politely between them._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-1, notice): sloth bears eat termites, ants, fruit and honey; mothers carry cubs on their backs  
> **Baloo**: In the real jungle, a bear like me is called a sloth bear.  
> **Baloo**: We have long claws for digging out termites, and we love ants, fruit and honey.  
> **Baloo**: Sloth bear mothers carry their cubs on their backs. Sloth bears are protected by law in India today.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Baloo is a sloth bear: it digs for termites and ants, eats fruit and honey, carries its cubs on its back, and is protected by law in India._


#### `reader:a24-03-words` · [main](main.md#a24-03-words)

**Baloo**: Now the Master Words. They matter more than anything else I teach.  
**Baloo**: Say them right, and the jungle will let you be.  
**Baloo**: There is a word for the Hunting People, a word for the Birds, and a word for the Snakes.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): a word with more than one meaning: master  
> **Baloo**: I call them the Master Words. “Master” is a word with a few meanings.  
> **Baloo**: Which one do I mean?  
> - ✅ The most important, the chief ones. → Yes. Master here means chief, the one that matters most. Master can also mean someone really good at a skill, as in master of a craft.
> - The owner of a pet. → That is another meaning of master. But the Words do not belong to anyone. Here it means the chief ones, the ones that matter most.

### Glossary

- **tusks**: Very long teeth that stick out of an animal’s mouth, such as a walrus’s or an elephant’s.
- **kelp**: A large brown seaweed that grows in cold, shallow seas, like a forest under the water.
