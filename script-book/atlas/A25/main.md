# Atlas, page A25: The Nothing

## A25-1. The Bookshop

*Die unendliche Geschichte (The Neverending Story), Michael Ende, 1979: ch. I, the antiquarian bookshop* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Nothing page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time once Nova’s atlas is in the Grove; the first quest on the page.

**Cast:** Bastian (Bastian Balthazar Bux, the boy who reads the book (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design), Mr Coreander (Carl Conrad Coreander, the bookseller (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design)

### Step 1: Step through the page, behind the shop bell.

Hint: Touch the little bell on the page. Stay behind it; nobody in the shop can see you there. · check: `explore:a25-shop:bell`

#### <a id="a25-01-shop"></a>Scene `a25-01-shop`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page that is mostly a glass door. There is a little bell above it, and painted letters on the glass._  
_It’s raining on the page. Real rain: it runs down the glass and off the edge on to your table._  
_You touch the bell. It rings, and you are standing behind it, inside a dim shop that smells of dust and pipe smoke._  
_Books go up to the ceiling. Between the towers, in a deep armchair, an old man in a black suit is reading._  
_Nobody looks round at you. Your Nubbin waves at the old man, experimentally, and he turns a page._  
_The door bangs open. A boy tumbles in, out of breath, soaked through, with a school bag on his back._  
_He is about ten, round, and knock-kneed, with wet hair in his eyes. He shuts the door and leans on it._  
**Mr Coreander** _(angry)_: Out. I don’t sell to children. Children sneeze on books and ask if they’ve got pictures.  
**Bastian**: I’m not buying. There are boys outside. Just till they go past. Please.  
**Mr Coreander**: Hm. What’s your name?  
**Bastian** _(sad)_: Bastian Balthazar Bux.  
**Mr Coreander**: That’s a name with too many b’s in it. Sit down, then. Don’t touch anything.  

**Choices**

- **Keep very still behind the bell.**
    - You keep still. The bell, which is right by your ear, decides to keep still too.
- **Take out your notebook and write down the shop’s name.**
    - You write it down, letters from the glass backwards and then the right way round. Your pencil sounds very loud.
- **Let your Nubbin sniff the nearest pile of books.**
    - Your Nubbin sniffs the nearest pile. It sneezes on a book, which proves the old man right about something.

#### <a id="a25-01-shop-done"></a>Done `a25-01-shop-done`

_A telephone rings in the back room. The old man gets up, grumbling, and leaves his book on the armchair._  
_It is bound in copper-coloured silk. On the cover, two snakes, one light and one dark, are biting each other’s tails._  

### Step 2: Follow the boy through the rain to his school.

Hint: Keep him in sight. He’s slow on his feet, but he knows the short cuts. · check: `walk:a25-street:school`

#### <a id="a25-01-run"></a>Scene `a25-01-run`

_Bastian looks at the book. He looks at the back room, where the old man is shouting into the telephone about an invoice._  
_Then he picks the book up, puts it under his coat, and runs. He leaves the door open and the bell still ringing._  
_You go after him. Your Nubbin goes after you, very fast and quite flat, the way it runs in thunderstorms._  
**Bastian** _(sad)_: I’m a thief now. I’m a thief. I’ve never stolen anything in my life, and now I have.  
_He can’t go home, he tells a lamp-post. His father won’t shout; his father hardly says anything at all, any more._  
_He is late for school, and he has a maths test, and he stops at the school gate with his hand on it._  
**Bastian**: Can’t go in there either.  
_He goes in by the back door instead, and up the back stairs, past the caretaker’s cupboard, all the way to the top._  

#### <a id="a25-01-run-done"></a>Done `a25-01-run-done`

_At the top of the stairs there is a door nobody uses. It isn’t locked, because it has never needed to be._  

### Step 3: Sit in the attic with Bastian while he opens the book.

Hint: Find a corner on the old gym mats. Don’t make a sound; he thinks he’s alone. · check: `sit:a25-attic:mats`

#### <a id="a25-01-attic"></a>Scene `a25-01-attic`

_The school attic is long and low, full of broken desks, rolled-up maps and a pile of old gym mats._  
_Rain drums on the roof slates. It is grey up here, and it smells of dust and chalk._  
_Bastian finds a heap of grey army blankets, wraps himself in two, and sits on the mats like a parcel._  
_He takes out his sandwich and his apple, looks at them, and puts them back. He might be up here a long time._  
**Bastian**: Just the first page. Then I’ll take it back. Probably.  
_He opens the book. You look over his shoulder, unseen, and see that it is printed in two colours of ink._  
_Your Nubbin climbs into the blanket heap and settles against his side, because it’s warm there._  
_Bastian doesn’t notice. He has already started reading._  

**Choices**

- **Sit beside him on the mats.**
    - You sit down beside him. He shifts over without looking up, as people do for someone they can’t see.
- **Sit by the skylight and read your own copy.**
    - Your atlas page, when you look at it, is showing exactly the same words. You read along.

_A forest grows in from the corners, and the rain on the roof turns into wind in leaves._  
_You are not in the attic any more. You are on a forest path at night, beside a campfire, and someone is singing badly._  
_Very faintly, if you listen, you can still hear a boy turning a page._

## A25-2. The Messengers

*Die unendliche Geschichte (The Neverending Story), Michael Ende, 1979: ch. I, the Howling Forest and the four messengers* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Nothing page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A25-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A25-1

**Cast:** Pyornkrachzark (Pyornkrachzark, the rockchewer messenger (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design), Blubb (Blubb, the will-o’-the-wisp messenger (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design), Ückück (Ückück, the night-hob messenger on a bat (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design), Gluckuk (Gluckuk, the tiny messenger on a racing snail (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design), Bastian (Bastian Balthazar Bux, the boy who reads the book (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design)

### Step 1: Join the messengers at the campfire in the forest.

Hint: Follow the bad singing. It’s coming from the clearing with the fire. · check: `visit:a25-forest:campfire`

#### <a id="a25-02-fire"></a>Scene `a25-02-fire`

_In the clearing sits a will-o’-the-wisp, a little ball of light, singing to keep its courage up. It isn’t working._  
_A rockchewer comes crunching out of the trees. He is the size of a hill and is eating a small piece of it._  
_Then a night-hob riding a bat, and a tiny no bigger than your thumb, on a racing snail that has run all the way._  
**Blubb** _(shock)_: Strangers! Friendly ones? Say friendly. I’m Blubb. I’m very nervous.  
**Pyornkrachzark** _(happy)_: Pyornkrachzark. Sit. Pebble?  
**Ückück**: Ückück. We are all going the same way, it seems. To the Ivory Tower, with bad news.  
**Gluckuk**: Gluckuk. My snail is the fastest in the whole of my country. Please don’t step on him.  
_Your Nubbin sits down next to the snail, very carefully, and they look at each other for a long time._  

**Choices**

- **Accept the pebble, politely.**
    - You hold it as if you might eat it later, and Pyornkrachzark nods, satisfied. Good manners matter, whatever size you are.
- **Ask what the bad news is.**
    - **Ückück**: The same as everyone’s, I expect. That’s what frightens me.

#### <a id="a25-02-fire-done"></a>Done `a25-02-fire-done`

_They have each come from a different corner of Fantastica, to the same fire, carrying the same news._  

### Step 2: Hear what the messengers have seen, and go with them to look.

Hint: Walk with Ückück to the edge of the wood. You don’t have to go close. · check: `walk:a25-forest:edge`

#### <a id="a25-02-gap"></a>Scene `a25-02-gap`

**Blubb**: In my marsh, a piece of the bank went. Not burnt. Not flooded. Just not there any more.  
**Pyornkrachzark** _(sad)_: Mountain too. Bit missing. Didn’t eat it. Honest.  
**Gluckuk**: A lake at home. One day it had a hole in the middle where nothing was. Not water. Not dry. Nothing.  
**Ückück**: Come. It’s here too, now. I saw it from the air.  
_At the edge of the wood the trees just stop. Not a cliff, and not a clearing: the picture simply isn’t there._  
_When you look at the gap, it feels like your eyes have gone blind on that side. The singing birds go quiet as you come near._  
_Your Nubbin will not go a step closer. It sits down hard in the leaves and takes hold of your boot._  
**Ückück**: It’s growing. Every land is reporting it. The Empress must know what it is.  

**Choices**

- **Step back with your Nubbin.**
    - You step back. The birds start again, a little way off, as if nothing happened.
- **Look away from the gap, deliberately.**
    - You make yourself look at a real leaf instead, with a hole a caterpillar made. It helps.

#### <a id="a25-02-gap-done"></a>Done `a25-02-gap-done`

_In the end Blubb gives it a name, in a very small voice: “the Nothing”. Everyone wishes he hadn’t._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a25-02-complete"></a>Complete `a25-02-complete`

_The four messengers set off together at dawn, at the speed of the snail, which turns out to be very fast indeed._  
_For a moment the green fades, and you see the attic in red. Bastian has pulled the blanket round his ears._  
**Bastian** _(think)_: I’d have gone with them. I think I would. If I could.  
_He opens his satchel, breaks off a corner of his sandwich, and goes on reading._

**Notes:** CARE (the Nothing): the gap is drawn as missing pieces of the picture, a soft-edged blank in the trees, never a mouth, eyes or a creature. As the player walks toward it the music drops out instrument by instrument, and comes back when they step away. No jump scare, no sting of sound. The step never forces the player close: the check fires at the wood’s edge. The four messengers are drawn as described in our own designs; Gluckuk’s snail is a real pet-sized companion sprite for this quest.

## A25-3. The Ivory Tower

*Die unendliche Geschichte (The Neverending Story), Michael Ende, 1979: ch. II, the Ivory Tower and Cairon the physician* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Nothing page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A25-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A25-2

**Cast:** Cairon (Cairon, the black centaur, the Empress’s physician (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design), Ückück (Ückück, the night-hob messenger on a bat (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design)

### Step 1: Go with the messengers into the Ivory Tower.

Hint: Climb the white spiral road through the gardens to the hall of physicians. · check: `climb:a25-tower:hall`

#### <a id="a25-03-tower"></a>Scene `a25-03-tower`

_The Ivory Tower stands in a labyrinth of flowers, white and very tall, like a spiral shell standing on its end._  
_The halls are full of messengers from everywhere. Every one of them is carrying the same news, and every one thinks theirs is new._  
**Ückück** _(sad)_: So many. I thought I was important.  
_In a high room the Empress’s physicians are arguing. In the middle stands a black centaur with a white beard._  
**Cairon** _(angry)_: Enough. Enough! You can all stop shouting. None of you knows what’s wrong with her, and neither do I.  
**Cairon**: She is ill. She is ill in the same way the land is ill. It’s one illness. When it reaches her, there is no more Fantastica.  
_The room goes silent. Your Nubbin hides behind a pillar, which is the most sensible thing anyone has done all day._  

#### <a id="a25-03-tower-done"></a>Done `a25-03-tower-done`

_Cairon stamps a hoof, and then looks very old, and sits down on the floor like a tired horse._  

### Step 2: Help Cairon find the road to the Grassy Sea.

Hint: Unroll the maps in the physicians’ library and find the long road east. · check: `puzzle:a25-maps:grassy-sea`

#### <a id="a25-03-maps"></a>Scene `a25-03-maps`

**Cairon**: She has chosen a hero to find the cure. She has given me the Glory to carry to him.  
_Round his neck hangs an amulet: two snakes, one light, one dark, each biting the other’s tail._  
**Cairon**: AURYN. Whoever wears it acts for her. The hero is in the Grassy Sea. Atreyu, she said. A great hunter, no doubt.  
**Cairon**: I don’t know the way. I am a physician. I know the way to the kitchen, and to people’s insides.  
_The maps are huge and out of date. Half of them have gaps now where the Nothing has been._  
**Cairon**: You, traveller, with the sharp eyes. Help an old horse.  

**Choices**

- **Follow the river on the map.**
    - The river goes east for a thousand miles and ends in grass. Cairon puts his finger on it and grunts, pleased.
- **Ask Ückück, who has seen it from the air.**
    - **Ückück**: East. Keep going east until there’s nothing but grass, and then go on. You can’t miss it. Really.

#### <a id="a25-03-maps-done"></a>Done `a25-03-maps-done`

**Cairon**: Good. Then I gallop. You’re coming? I could use the company. I talk to myself on long roads, and I’m dull.  
_Your Nubbin climbs onto his back without asking. Cairon pretends not to notice, which is kind of him._

## A25-4. Atreyu

*Die unendliche Geschichte (The Neverending Story), Michael Ende, 1979: ch. II, the Grassy Sea and Atreyu* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Nothing page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A25-3, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A25-3

**Cast:** Cairon (Cairon, the black centaur, the Empress’s physician (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design), Atreyu (Atreyu of the Greenskins, the boy hunter (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design, made with an adviser), Greenskin Hunter (a hunter of the Grassy Sea, one of Atreyu’s people (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); designed with an adviser), Artax (Artax, Atreyu’s horse (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design)

### Step 1: Bring Cairon to the hunters’ camp in the Grassy Sea.

Hint: Walk ahead of him through the tall grass. The tents are by the stream. · check: `escort:a25-cairon:camp`

#### <a id="a25-04-camp"></a>Scene `a25-04-camp`

_The Grassy Sea is grass as far as you can see, moving in long waves like water. It is purple in places, where the buffalo graze._  
_The hunters’ camp is a ring of hide tents by a stream. The people are green-skinned, quiet, and very good at seeing you coming._  
**Greenskin Hunter**: A centaur, a stranger and a… whatever that is. You’re a long way from anywhere.  
**Cairon**: I bring a message for Atreyu. From the Empress. Take me to him.  
**Greenskin Hunter** _(think)_: Atreyu? Him? He’s out with the herd. He’s supposed to be learning to be a hunter tomorrow.  
_Cairon’s eyebrows go up. He doesn’t say anything, but his tail swishes once, impatiently._  

#### <a id="a25-04-camp-done"></a>Done `a25-04-camp-done`

_Someone runs to fetch him. Cairon combs his beard with his fingers, to look his best for a hero._  

### Step 2: Watch Atreyu take the amulet.

Hint: Stand at the edge of the ring and let Cairon speak. It’s not your message. · check: `watch:a25-atreyu:amulet`

#### <a id="a25-04-amulet"></a>Scene `a25-04-amulet`

_A boy comes through the grass. He is about ten years old, with green skin and dark hair, in a purple-red cloak._  
**Atreyu**: You asked for me?  
**Cairon**: No. I asked for Atreyu. The hunter.  
**Atreyu**: That’s me. I’m not a hunter yet. I was going to be, tomorrow.  
_Cairon looks at him for a long time. Then he laughs, a short tired laugh, and takes the amulet off his own neck._  
**Cairon** _(soft)_: She chose you. She never chooses wrongly, so I must have been wrong about heroes. It wouldn’t be the first time.  
**Cairon**: Go without weapons. Find out what’s wrong with her, and find the cure. Don’t ask me how. Nobody knows.  
_Atreyu puts on AURYN. He lays his bow down in the grass, very carefully, and doesn’t look at it again._  
**Atreyu**: My name means Son of All. My mother and father died when I was small, so everybody brought me up. Everyone here is mine.  
**Atreyu**: So I’m going for everyone.  

**Choices**

- **Pick up the bow and give it to a hunter to keep.**
    - **Greenskin Hunter**: I’ll keep it for him. He’ll want it when he’s back.
    - She says when, not if. Everyone hears it.
- **Say nothing. It’s his moment.**
    - You say nothing. The grass says it for you, a long sigh across the whole of the plain.

### Step 3: Get your Nubbin down off Artax’s saddle. Or don’t.

Hint: Talk to Artax. He has opinions. · check: `talk:a25-artax:saddle`

#### <a id="a25-04-saddle"></a>Scene `a25-04-saddle`

_Atreyu whistles, and a small horse comes trotting out of the grass: brown, shaggy, sure-footed._  
_By the time he arrives, your Nubbin is sitting on his saddle. Nobody saw it climb up._  
**Artax**: There is a thing on my back. It is soft and it has made itself comfortable.  
**Atreyu**: Artax, this is… I don’t know what it is.  
**Artax** _(think)_: Neither do I. It won’t get off. I have asked it twice.  
**Atreyu**: Will you come too, then? It looks like you’re coming anyway.  

**Choices**

- **“Yes. If you’ll have us.”**
    - **Atreyu**: I’ll have anyone. I don’t know where I’m going.
- **Apologise to Artax for your Nubbin.**
    - **Artax**: It’s light. And it’s warm. There are worse things to carry across the world.

## A25-5. The Swamps of Sadness

*Die unendliche Geschichte (The Neverending Story), Michael Ende, 1979: ch. III, the Swamps of Sadness and Artax* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Nothing page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A25-4, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A25-4

**Cast:** Atreyu (Atreyu of the Greenskins, the boy hunter (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design, made with an adviser), Artax (Artax, Atreyu’s horse (Die unendliche Geschichte (Michael Ende, 1979)); original design)

### Step 1: Cross the Swamps of Sadness with Atreyu and Artax.

Hint: Keep walking. When you feel like stopping, walk anyway. · check: `walk:a25-swamp:crossing`

#### <a id="a25-05-swamp"></a>Scene `a25-05-swamp`

_The swamp is grey and still. Dead trees stand in it, and the air is cold and smells of nothing much._  
_Sadness comes up out of the mud like damp. It gets into your boots first, and then into everything._  
**Atreyu**: Don’t listen to it. It’s only the swamp. It isn’t you.  
_AURYN glints on his chest. Atreyu feels the sadness too, you can see it, but it can’t quite get hold of him._  
_Artax is slowing down. Each step is a bit shorter than the last one._  
**Artax** _(sad)_: Master. I can’t go on.  
**Atreyu**: You can. You have to. Look, there’s higher ground just there.  
**Artax** _(sad)_: I know. It doesn’t matter. Nothing matters. I’m sorry. Take the little one off my back.  

**Choices**

- **Walk by Artax’s head and talk to him.**
    - You talk to him about nothing: grass, and stables, and apples. His ears move once, to listen.
- **Hold your Nubbin tight on the saddle.**
    - You keep a hand on your Nubbin. It is very cold, and it doesn’t purr.

#### <a id="a25-05-swamp-done"></a>Done `a25-05-swamp-done`

_You lift your Nubbin down. It is heavy and limp in your hands, as if the sadness has got into it as well._  

### Step 2: Stay with Atreyu while Artax stops.

Hint: Don’t look away. You don’t have to do anything. · check: `wait:a25-swamp:artax`

#### <a id="a25-05-artax"></a>Scene `a25-05-artax`

_Artax stands still. The mud comes up over his hooves, very slowly, and he doesn’t pull against it._  
_Atreyu puts both arms round his neck and holds his head, and talks to him quietly. You can’t hear what he says._  
**Artax** _(soft)_: Go on without me. Don’t stay. If you stay, it’ll get you too.  
**Atreyu** _(sad)_: I won’t forget you. Not one bit of you. Not ever.  
_The swamp closes, gently, like water closing over a dropped stone. And then there is only Atreyu, standing alone._  
_He stands for a long time. When he walks on, he doesn’t look back, because Artax asked him not to._  

#### <a id="a25-05-artax-done"></a>Done `a25-05-artax-done`

_In the red attic, Bastian is crying, and he doesn’t mind about it. Nobody can see him, and it was Artax._  

### Step 3: Sit beside your Nubbin in the reeds until it gets up.

Hint: Sit down next to it and wait. There’s no hurry, and nothing to press. · check: `sit:a25-reeds:nubbin`

#### <a id="a25-05-reeds"></a>Scene `a25-05-reeds`

_A little way on, your Nubbin lies down in the reeds and won’t get up. Its eyes are open, but it isn’t looking at anything._  
**Atreyu**: The swamp. It got into it. It’s so small.  
_You don’t pick it up and you don’t pull. You sit down in the reeds next to it, close enough that it knows._  
_Atreyu sits down too, on its other side, though he has every reason not to._  
_The reeds rustle. A long time goes by, or a short one; in the swamp it’s hard to tell._  
_Then your Nubbin sits up, and leans against your arm, and then gets up on its own._

### Step 3: Watch from behind the bell as Bastian comes back to the shop.

#### <a id="a25-20-shop"></a>Scene `a25-20-shop`

_The water goes on singing for a while after you are out of it. Then it is rain, ordinary rain, on a shop window._

_The next day the bell rings, and Bastian comes in, round and knock-kneed and wet, and very, very brave._

**Bastian** _(happy)_: My father and I talked last night. All night. He cried, and then he laughed. We haven’t done either for ages.  
**Mr Coreander**: Hm. What book?  
**Bastian**: Copper silk. Two snakes on the cover. It was on your chair.  
_Behind the bell, your Nubbin sneezes. Nobody can see it._  
**Mr Coreander**: Bless you.  
**Bastian**: I didn’t sneeze.  
**Mr Coreander**: No. I know.  
_Coreander looks at him over his spectacles for a long time. Then he smiles, slowly, as if he had remembered something._  
**Mr Coreander**: Ah. That. You went in, then. So did I, once, a long time ago. I came back with a story or two.  
_He leans back in his armchair and fills his pipe. Then he says something in German, the way the book always did._  
**Mr Coreander**: « Aber das ist eine andere Geschichte und soll ein andermal erzählt werden. »  
**Mr Coreander**: But that is another story, and shall be told another time.  
**Mr Coreander** _(think)_: I never owned such a book. Somebody must have brought it in. Or it brought itself.  
**Mr Coreander**: Keep going there, Bastian. And keep coming back. That’s the trick of it, and nobody does both.
