# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Atlas, page A26: The Wall of Uruk

## A26-1. The Wall

- `a26-01-s1`: Step into the book. Walk along the great wall. · _Touch the sunny wall on the page. Follow the old brickmaker._
- `a26-01-s2`: Find the copper box at the bottom of the wall. · _It is beside the stairs. The brickmaker will show you._

#### `sprout:a26-01-page` · [main](main.md#a26-01-page)

_Nova’s book falls open. A great brick wall shines in the sun._  
_There are palm trees and a wide river. You step in with your Nubbin._  
_An old woman sits on a pile of bricks, eating a date._  
**Old Brickmaker**: Hello, new friend! Come and walk the wall with me.  
**Old Brickmaker**: This city is called Uruk. Long ago it stood where Iraq is today.  
**Old Brickmaker**: A river runs beside it. People made their homes near the water.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): a city can sit beside a river, in a place on the map  
> _(Look at the river on the page.) Where is Uruk?_  
> - ✅ Beside a river → Yes! Uruk is beside a river. Water is good for drinking and for growing food.
> - Up on the moon → Not quite! Uruk is on the ground, beside a river. Look at the water.
> - In the middle of the sea → Not quite! It is beside a river, with land all around.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Uruk stood beside a river, in the land that is now Iraq._


**Choices**

- **Ask how you tell a good brick.**
    - **Old Brickmaker**: You knock it. A good brick rings!
- **Ask who built the wall.**
    - **Old Brickmaker**: The king says he did. But many hands helped.
    - **Old Brickmaker**: Come and see.

#### `sprout:a26-01-tablet` · [main](main.md#a26-01-tablet)

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Literacy, notice): cuneiform: writing pressed into wet clay with a reed  
> **Old Brickmaker**: Press, lift, press, lift. Each little mark is a wedge.  
> **Old Brickmaker**: The marks are called cuneiform. People wrote like this over four thousand years ago.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that long ago people wrote by pressing a reed into wet clay._

## A26-2. The King Who Never Rests

- `a26-02-s1`: Watch the king’s games in the big square. · _Follow the drum. Find a place by the well._
- `a26-02-s2`: Go up to the temple with the people. · _At dusk the crowd climbs the steps. Go with the woman from the square._

#### `sprout:a26-02-games` · [main](main.md#a26-02-games)

_In the big square, young men run races and carry stones._  
_Nobody looks happy. They look very, very tired._  
**Gilgamesh**: Again! Faster!  
**Gilgamesh**: Nobody is tired!  
_This is Gilgamesh, king of Uruk._  
_He is big and strong, and he never stops._  
**Woman of Uruk**: He has not let us go home for a month.  
_A boy sits down in the dust. He is too tired to get up._  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, ask): bodies need water and rest  
> _What does the tired boy need?_  
> - ✅ A drink and a rest → Yes! Bodies need water and rest to feel better.
> - Another race → Oh no! He needs a rest first. Bodies get tired when they run and run.
> - Nothing at all → He needs something! A drink and a rest help a tired body.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about water and rest as things a tired body needs._


**Choices**

- **Take the boy some water from the well.**
    - You carry him a cup. He drinks it all and smiles.
- **Sit down in the dust beside him.**
    - You sit beside him. Your Nubbin sits too.
    - So does his mum.

#### `sprout:a26-02-games-done` · [main](main.md#a26-02-games-done)

_The king does not see. He starts another race._  
**Woman of Uruk**: Thank you. That was kind.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): noticing how kindness makes someone feel  
> _The boy’s mum smiles at you. How does she feel?_  
> - ✅ Thankful → Yes! She feels thankful. A kind act made her feel warm inside.
> - Cross → Look at her smile. She feels thankful, because you were kind.
> - Sleepy → She might be sleepy! But her smile says she feels thankful.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that being kind helps others feel thankful and cared for._


#### `sprout:a26-02-clay` · [main](main.md#a26-02-clay)

_At dusk, tired people climb the temple steps with lamps._  
**Woman of Uruk**: Please, gods. Give the king a friend his own size.  
_High in the sky, a goddess named Aruru washes her hands._  
_She picks up a little wet clay and rolls it in her palms._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-1, ask): clay is soft when wet and hard when it dries  
> _Wet clay is soft and squishy. What happens when clay dries in the sun?_  
> - ✅ It gets hard → Yes! Clay gets hard when it dries. That is why people made bricks from it.
> - It turns into water → Not quite! Dry clay does not turn into water. It gets hard.
> - It grows legs → Ha! No legs. Dry clay just gets hard.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that clay is soft when wet and hardens as it dries._

_Then she drops it gently, far away in the wild grass._  
_Your Nubbin watches it fall, with its mouth open._  

**Choices**

- **Tell the woman what you saw.**
    - **Woman of Uruk**: Clay, in the wild? Well.
    - **Woman of Uruk**: I asked for help.
- **Keep it to yourself for now.**
    - The woman pats your arm kindly.

#### `sprout:a26-02-clay-done` · [main](main.md#a26-02-clay-done)

_Far away, in the long grass, someone new opens his eyes._  
_His name will be Enkidu._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): words that start with the same first sound  
> _Enkidu starts with the sound “eh”._  
> _Which word starts the same: egg, dog or sun?_  
> - ✅ Egg → Yes! Egg, Enkidu. Eh, eh, eh!
> - Dog → Dog starts with “d”. Egg starts with “eh”, like Enkidu.
> - Sun → Sun starts with “s”. Egg starts with “eh”, like Enkidu.
> _For grown-ups: Matched a first sound: egg and Enkidu._


#### `sprout:a26-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a26-02-complete)

_The drum goes boom in the square. The king still does not stop._  
_Your Nubbin faces the wild. Its ears stand up._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): fair rules: everyone needs a rest  
> _Is it fair to make everyone work and never rest?_  
> - ✅ No. Everyone needs rest → Yes! A fair rule gives everyone time to rest.
> - Yes, it is fair → Hmm. Everyone gets tired, so a fair rule lets everybody rest.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about why a fair rule gives everyone time to rest._


## A26-3. The Wild Man

- `a26-03-s1`: Go out to the water hole in the dry grass. · _Leave Uruk by the river gate. Follow the gazelle tracks._

#### `sprout:a26-03-herd` · [main](main.md#a26-03-herd)

_Three days from the wall, the grass is dry and the sky is huge._  
_At the water hole, four gazelles drink. A man drinks with them!_  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count four gazelles  
> _(Point and count the gazelles at the water. One, two, three, four!)_  
> Check: `edu:count:gazelles:4`

_He is hairy all over. He has never worn clothes._  
_He eats grass with the gazelles. He is happy with them._  
_Your Nubbin runs down and drinks beside the smallest gazelle._  
**Enkidu**: Hhh?  
_He looks at your Nubbin. Then he goes back to drinking._  

**Choices**

- **Let your Nubbin stay with the herd.**
    - Your Nubbin lies down in the cool mud, happy.
- **Call your Nubbin back.**
    - Your Nubbin flicks an ear. It stays right where it is.

#### `sprout:a26-03-herd-done` · [main](main.md#a26-03-herd-done)

_You sit under a thorn tree and watch._  
_Your Nubbin is in the herd, nose to nose with a fawn._  
_Enkidu strokes a gazelle’s ear, ever so gently._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-ESS3-1, ask): animals live where their needs are met, such as a water hole  
> _Why do the gazelles come to this water hole?_  
> - ✅ They need water to live → Yes! Animals live and gather where they can find water and food.
> - To play hide and seek → Maybe a little! But mostly they come because they need water.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that animals gather where they can find what they need, such as water._

#### `sprout:a26-03-pits` · [main](main.md#a26-03-pits)

_Every pit has been filled in. Every net has been torn up._  
_A man with a net comes over the hill. He stops and goes pale._  
**Trapper**: Every pit! Every net!  
**Trapper**: I worked three days.  
**Trapper**: A wild man did this. I cannot feed my family with empty nets.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): seeing how someone else feels, and being kind  
> _The man is upset about his family’s supper. How can we be kind?_  
> - ✅ Say we are sorry he is upset → Thank you. That helps a little.
> - Laugh at him → Oh dear. Laughing would hurt his feelings. Saying sorry is kinder.
> - Walk away → He would feel all alone. Saying sorry is kinder.
> _For grown-ups: Practised noticing someone’s worry and answering kindly._


**Choices**

    - **Trapper**: Thank you. I am sorry I am scared of him.

    - **Trapper**: Happy! Well, the gazelles are happy.

## A26-4. Shamhat

- `a26-04-s1`: Walk with the woman from the city to the water hole. · _Meet the trapper at the river gate. Carry her bundle._
- `a26-04-s2`: Sit by the water until Enkidu knows her words. · _Sit by the water. Do not chase. It takes days._
- `a26-04-s3`: Bring your Nubbin back from the herd. · _Go slowly. Let your Nubbin come to you._

#### `sprout:a26-04-road` · [main](main.md#a26-04-road)

_The trapper comes back with a woman from the city._  
**Shamhat**: Hello! I am Shamhat.  
**Shamhat**: I teach people words and songs.  
_You carry her bundle. It smells of bread and sweet oil._  
_Inside there are two flat loaves and three dates._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.OA.A.2, ask): add two groups to make five  
> _Two loaves and three dates. How many foods in all?_  
> - ✅ Five → Yes! Two and three make five. Count them: one, two, three, four, five.
> - Four → Let us count together: two loaves, then three dates. One, two, three, four, five. Five!
> - Six → Let us count together: one, two, three, four, five. Five!
> _For grown-ups: Added two and three to make five._

**Shamhat**: I do not want to boss him. I want to be his friend.  

**Choices**

- **Ask how she will do it.**
    - **Shamhat**: I will sit where he can see me, and wait.
- **Tell her your Nubbin ran off with the herd.**
    - **Shamhat**: Then we have the same job, you and I.

#### `sprout:a26-04-sit` · [main](main.md#a26-04-sit)

_Shamhat sits on a flat rock by the water._  
_You sit a little way off._  
_On the first day, Enkidu peeks from behind a gazelle._  
_On the second day, he comes closer and sniffs the bread._  
**Shamhat**: This is bread. This is my hand.  
**Shamhat**: That is yours.  
_By the fourth day, he sits across from her._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): waiting patiently helps a friend feel safe  
> _Shamhat does not chase Enkidu. She sits and waits._  
> _Why does waiting help?_  
> - ✅ He feels safe and comes when he is ready → Yes! Waiting kindly helps a friend feel safe.
> - Waiting does nothing → Look what happened! Waiting helped him feel safe.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about why patient waiting helps someone feel safe._

**Enkidu**: Hand.  
**Shamhat**: Yes! Hand!  
**Shamhat**: And what is your name?  
**Enkidu**: Enkidu.  

#### `sprout:a26-04-sit-done` · [main](main.md#a26-04-sit-done)

_He says it again, louder. Then he laughs a big laugh._  
**Shamhat**: There you are! I knew you were in there.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Language and Communication, ask): words that start with the same sound: hand and hat  
> _Enkidu’s first word was “hand”. Which word starts the same: hat, sun or cup?_  
> - ✅ Hat → Yes! Hand, hat. Huh, huh, huh!
> - Sun → Sun starts with “s”. Hand starts with “h”, like hat.
> - Cup → Cup starts with “k”. Hand starts with “h”, like hat.
> _For grown-ups: Matched the first sound of “hand” with “hat”._


#### `sprout:a26-04-coax` · [main](main.md#a26-04-coax)

_Enkidu walks to the gazelles, the way he always has._  
_They lift their heads. They sniff._  
_Then they run away._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-2, ask): animals use their noses and eyes to tell who is who  
> _How do gazelles know who is who?_  
> - ✅ They use their noses and eyes → Yes! Animals sniff and look. Enkidu smells like a person now.
> - They read name tags → Ha! No name tags. They use their noses and eyes.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that animals use senses like smell and sight to know each other._

**Enkidu**: Wait! It is me!  
_Your Nubbin has been left behind too. It sits alone in the grass._  
**Shamhat**: Go to yours. I will go to mine.  

**Choices**

- **Call its name.**
    - Your Nubbin thinks about it. Then it runs to you.
- **Sit down in the grass and wait.**
    - You wait. Then there is a warm weight against your side.
- **Hold out a date from the bundle.**
    - Your Nubbin comes for the date and stays for you.

#### `sprout:a26-04-coax-done` · [main](main.md#a26-04-coax-done)

_Enkidu sits with his head in his hands. Shamhat sits beside him._  
_She says nothing. After a while, he leans on her shoulder._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): what helps when we feel sad  
> _Enkidu is sad. What helps when we are sad?_  
> - ✅ A friend sitting close → Yes! Sitting close, with no words, can help a lot.
> - Being all alone → Sometimes we want a little quiet. But a friend nearby helps most.
> _For grown-ups: Named sadness and what helps: a friend sitting close._


#### `sprout:a26-04-complete` · [main](main.md#a26-04-complete)

**Enkidu**: Will it run away from me too?

**Shamhat**: Some of it. Not the best of it.

## A26-5. Bread and Barley

- `a26-05-s2`: Keep watch over the sheep with Enkidu at night. · _Take a lamp to the sheepfold and stay up with him._

#### `sprout:a26-05-bread` · [main](main.md#a26-05-bread)

_The shepherds’ camp is a ring of tents and a warm fire._  
**Shepherd**: Sit down, friend! You must be hungry.  
_You set down a round loaf and a jug of barley drink._  
**Enkidu**: What is this?  
**Shamhat**: That is bread. People eat it.  
**Shamhat**: Go on!  
_He takes a tiny bite. His eyebrows jump up._  
_He eats until he is full. Then he sings a song with no words._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): bread comes from a plant that grows grain  
> _Bread is made from flour. Flour is made from grain._  
> _Where does grain start?_  
> - ✅ A plant growing in a field → Yes! Grain grows on plants, in fields, with sun and water.
> - A cloud → Not quite! Grain grows on plants in a field.
> - A rock → Not quite! Grain grows on plants. Plants need sun and water.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that bread starts as a plant growing grain._

> _For grown-ups: Talked about washing to stay clean and healthy._

## A26-6. Ninsun’s Dreams

#### `sprout:a26-06-cups` · [main](main.md#a26-06-cups)

_The palace steward gives you a tray of silver cups._  
_There are five cups on the tray. They shine._  
_Ninsun’s hall is cool and quiet, with a fountain._  
**Gilgamesh**: Mother, I had a dream. A star fell from the sky.
