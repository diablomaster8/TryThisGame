## A27-1. A Son for Sam

- `a27-01-s1`: Step into the mountain page and find the champion’s house in Sistan. · _Touch the green city at the bottom of the page._
- `a27-01-s2`: Go to the family’s rooms and see Sam’s son. · _Follow the nurse down the passage with the lamps._
- `a27-01-s3`: Go with the nurse when she tells Sam. · _Walk behind her to the hall. Stay by the door._

#### `reader:a27-01-page` · [main](main.md#a27-01-page)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a mountain above the clouds, a vast nest, and far below, a green city._  
_A card says this is a story from the Shahnameh, the Book of Kings, finished by Ferdowsi around the year 1010._  
_People in Iran, Afghanistan and Tajikistan still recite it, and they name their children from it._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): Persian is spoken in Iran, Afghanistan and Tajikistan  
> _The Shahnameh is loved in Iran, Afghanistan and Tajikistan. One language links all three countries._  
> _Which one?_  
> - ✅ Persian. → Yes. Persian, in a few forms: it is called Farsi in Iran, Dari in Afghanistan and Tajik in Tajikistan.
> - Latin. → Not Latin. The language is Persian, in a few forms: Farsi, Dari and Tajik.
> - Greek. → Not Greek. The language is Persian, in a few forms: Farsi, Dari and Tajik.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Persian, in its different forms, links Iran, Afghanistan and Tajikistan, where the Shahnameh is loved._

_Ferdowsi spent about thirty years on it. This is one of its stories, retold in our own words._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.OA.A.1, ask): add to find a date: 980 + 30  
> _Suppose Ferdowsi began his book in the year 980 and worked for thirty years. In what year did he finish?_  
> - ✅ 1010. → Yes. 980 plus 30 is 1010, which matches “around the year 1010”.
> - 1000. → Close. Count on: 980 plus 20 makes 1000, then 10 more makes 1010.
> - 1110. → Check the hundreds. 980 plus 30 is 1010. It is about a thousand years ago.
> _For grown-ups: Added 30 years to a start date to find when the Shahnameh was finished, around 1010._

_The page goes warm under your hand, like a wall that has had the sun on it all day._  
_Then it is dust, and a gate, and you are standing in it. Inside, a big house is lit from end to end._  
**The Nurse**: A traveller? Come in.  
**The Nurse**: Nobody is turned away from this door tonight.  
_Your Nubbin sniffs the air. Somewhere in the house there is bread, and it has made a note of where._  

**Choices**

- **Thank her, and wipe your feet.**
    - **The Nurse**: Good manners. You will do well here.
    - **The Nurse**: Leave your shoes with the others.
- **Ask about the baby.**
    - **The Nurse**: A boy. Perfect.
    - **The Nurse**: We are all very…
    - She stops, smiles too quickly, and takes your coat.
- **Let your Nubbin follow its nose.**
    - It finds the kitchen in four seconds. The cook gives it a crust without being asked.

#### `reader:a27-01-page-done` · [main](main.md#a27-01-page-done)

**The Nurse**: Stay as long as you like. The master will want to thank every guest.  
**The Nurse**: When he knows.  
_She says the last three words very quietly, to herself._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): Persian word parts: shah (king) and nameh (book)  
> _Shahnameh joins two Persian words: shah, which means king, and nameh, which means book. What does Shahnameh mean?_  
> - ✅ Book of Kings. → Yes. Shah plus nameh: Book of Kings.
> - King of Books. → Nearly, but the words come in the other order. Shah plus nameh means Book of Kings.
> - Letter to a Queen. → Nameh can mean letter as well as book, but shah means king. So it is the Book of Kings.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out that Shahnameh means Book of Kings from its two Persian parts._


#### `reader:a27-01-birth` · [main](main.md#a27-01-birth)

_The family’s rooms are at the back of the house, warm and full of women talking softly._  
_In a cradle by the brazier lies a baby, asleep with both fists up by his ears._  
_His hair is white. Not fair: white, like frost on a bough, and soft as the down on a chick._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS3-1, ask): albinism: some people are born with very little melanin; it is inherited  
> _Some people are born with very little melanin, the colour in hair, skin and eyes. This is called albinism._  
> _Which is true about it?_  
> - ✅ It is inherited from parents, and is just how a person is made. → Yes. It is passed down in families, like eye colour. You cannot catch it, and it is not a curse.
> - You can catch it from someone. → No. You cannot catch it. It is inherited, passed down in families, like eye colour.
> - It is a curse. → No. It is only how a body is made. People with albinism may need sun hats and may see less sharply, and they are just like everyone else.
> _For grown-ups: Learned plainly that albinism is an inherited trait, not a curse, and that people with it are just like everyone else._

**The Nurse**: Eight days old, and his father has not seen him. Nobody has dared to tell him about the hair.  
**The Nurse**: They think he will be angry. I think he will be a fool if he is.  
**The Nurse**: But I am only the nurse.  
_Your Nubbin climbs up the cradle leg and looks in. The baby opens his eyes and looks back._  

**Choices**

- **“He’s beautiful.”**
    - **The Nurse**: He is. Every bit of him.
- **“It’s only hair.”**
    - **The Nurse**: That is what I keep saying. To the wall, mostly.
- **“Someone should tell his father.”**
    - **The Nurse**: Someone should. And someone will.
    - **The Nurse**: It looks as if it will be me.

#### `reader:a27-01-birth-done` · [main](main.md#a27-01-birth-done)

_Your Nubbin reaches in and touches the white hair, very carefully, with one paw._  
_The baby grabs the paw and holds on. Neither of them seems to mind._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.OA.A.1, ask): days and weeks: 8 days is 1 week and 1 day  
> _The baby is eight days old. A week has seven days._  
> _How many days is that more than a week?_  
> - ✅ One day. → Yes. Eight take away seven is one. He is one week and one day old.
> - Two days. → Count again: seven days make a week, and eight minus seven is one.
> _For grown-ups: Subtracted 7 from 8 to say the baby is one week and one day old._


#### `reader:a27-01-hall` · [main](main.md#a27-01-hall)

_Sam sits in his hall: a big man, grey at the temples, with a sword across his knees that he is not using._  
_The nurse goes in. You stay by the door, with your Nubbin behind your ankles._  
**The Nurse**: My lord, God has given you what you wanted: a son, strong and handsome, with nothing wrong in him.  
**The Nurse**: Only his hair is white, like an old man’s. Remember he is a gift.  
**The Nurse**: Don’t be ungrateful for a gift.  
**Sam**: White?  
_He stands up. The sword slides off his knees onto the floor, and nobody picks it up._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): pausing before reacting to a surprise  
> _Sam is shocked, and acts before he thinks. What could help someone pause when something surprises them?_  
> _There is no one right answer._  
> - Take a slow breath. → That is a fair answer. A slow breath gives the thinking part of us time to catch up.
> - Count to ten. → That is a fair answer. Counting gives us a few seconds, and that is often enough.
> - Ask a question first. → That is a fair answer. A question, such as “what do you mean?”, turns a shock into a conversation.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about ways to pause before reacting to a surprise._

**Sam**: Show me.  

#### `reader:a27-01-hall-done` · [main](main.md#a27-01-hall-done)

_He goes past you down the passage, very fast._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): a word with two meanings: gift  
> _The nurse said a baby is “a gift”. A gift can mean a present, or a natural talent, like “a gift for music”._  
> _Which meaning did she use?_  
> - ✅ A present, given to be treasured. → Yes. She meant the baby was given to Sam, and should be treasured.
> - A natural talent. → A gift can mean that, as in “a gift for music”. But here she meant a precious present.
> _For grown-ups: Told two meanings of the word gift apart by reading the sentence._

_The nurse looks at you, and doesn’t say anything at all._  

#### `reader:a27-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a27-01-complete)

_Sam stands over the cradle for a long time. The baby looks up at him with dark, steady eyes._  
_Sam doesn’t pick him up. That is the first wrong thing._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): fairness: everyone deserves care whatever they look like  
> _Sam hesitates to hold his son because of the baby’s hair. Which idea about fairness does that go against?_  
> - ✅ Everyone deserves love and care, whatever they look like. → Yes. How someone looks is never a fair reason to love them less.
> - People should be treated by how they look. → No. That is just what is unfair here. A person’s looks are not a reason to treat them worse.
> - It depends on the hair. → It does not. Hair colour tells us nothing about who a person is or how much they deserve care.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about fairness: nobody should be treated worse because of how they look._

**Sam**: Leave me. All of you.  
**Sam**: I need to think.  
_The women go out in a hurry. In the passage, the nurse takes your Nubbin’s paw, as if somebody’s paw ought to be held._

## A27-2. Shame

- `a27-02-s1`: Be in the hall when Sam gives his order. · _The household gathers in the hall at first light._
- `a27-02-s2`: Follow the servants up the mountain path. · _Keep close behind the old servant. The storm is coming from the north._
- `a27-02-s3`: Stay with the baby at the foot of the high rocks. · _Sit with him in the lee of the big stone._

#### `reader:a27-02-order` · [main](main.md#a27-02-order)

_In the morning the whole household is called to the hall. Sam hasn’t slept, and it shows._  
**Sam**: My enemies will laugh. They will say Sam’s son was born old, and that it means something bad for the land.  
_It doesn’t mean anything. It is a baby with white hair, and he is hungry, and crying in the next room._  
**Sam**: Take the child away. Up to the Alborz, where nobody goes.  
**Sam**: Leave him there.  
_Nobody moves. Then an old servant at the back bows slowly, as if his back hurts._  
**The Nurse**: My lord, he is a gift.  
**Sam**: Go.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): when and how to speak up to someone powerful  
> _The nurse speaks up, but Sam does not listen. When is it right to speak up to someone powerful, even if it might not work?_  
> _There is no one right answer._  
> - When someone is going to be hurt. → That is a fair answer. Protecting someone who cannot protect themselves is a very good reason.
> - When I can do it kindly and clearly. → That is a fair answer. A calm voice is more likely to be heard than a shout.
> - Only if I have a friend beside me. → That is a fair answer. Speaking up is easier together, and it can be brave to ask someone to stand with you.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about when speaking up is right, even when it is hard._


**Choices**

- **Speak up for the baby.**
    - **You**: Please. He is your son.
    - **You**: He hasn’t done anything.
    - **Sam**: You are a guest in this house, and a guest does not tell me what my son is.
    - Your hands are shaking, but you said it, and the nurse heard you say it.
- **Look Sam in the eye, and say nothing.**
    - He looks back at you for a moment. Then he looks at the floor, and keeps looking at it.
- **Go and stand with the nurse.**
    - She doesn’t look at you, but she leans on your shoulder, just a little.

#### `reader:a27-02-order-done` · [main](main.md#a27-02-order-done)

_Sam goes out. The order stays in the room after him, like smoke._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): simile: an order that lingers like smoke  
> _The narrator says Sam’s order stayed in the room “like smoke”. Why is smoke a good comparison?_  
> - ✅ Smoke lingers and is hard to clear away. → Yes. The order hangs in the air, and everyone can feel it.
> - Smoke is cold. → Smoke is usually warm. The point is that it lingers and is hard to clear.
> - Smoke is loud. → Smoke is quiet. The point is that it lingers, as the order does.
> _For grown-ups: Explained why a simile, an order lingering like smoke, works._

**The Nurse**: Go with them. Please.  
**The Nurse**: Somebody kind should be there.  

#### `reader:a27-02-path` · [main](main.md#a27-02-path)

_Two servants carry the baby out of the city in a basket. You and your Nubbin follow._  
_The road becomes a path, and the path becomes stones. Ahead, the Alborz goes up and up into cloud._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-ESS2-1, ask): why clouds and rain gather over mountains  
> _Mountains often bring storms. Wind is pushed up the slope, cools, and its water vapour turns into tiny drops._  
> _What forms?_  
> - ✅ Clouds, and then rain. → Yes. Rising air cools, vapour turns to tiny drops, and clouds form. When the drops grow heavy, they fall as rain.
> - Sand. → No. The vapour in the air turns to tiny drops of water, which make clouds.
> - Snowmen. → Ha. Snow can form up high where it is cold, but the first thing to form is cloud.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that air pushed up a mountain cools, and its water vapour forms clouds and rain._

**Old Servant**: You don’t have to come. It isn’t your shame.  
**You**: It isn’t yours either.  
**Old Servant**: I am the one carrying him. That makes it mine, I think.  
_The wind gets up. The first rain comes sideways, hard and cold, out of the north._  

#### `reader:a27-02-path-done` · [main](main.md#a27-02-path-done)

_By the time the path runs out, the storm is all round you, and you can’t see the city any more._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.OA.A.1, ask): add distances: 4 + 3 + 2  
> _The servants walked 4 km on the road, 3 km on the path and 2 km on the stones. How far did they walk in all?_  
> - ✅ 9 km. → Yes. 4 plus 3 is 7, and 7 plus 2 is 9.
> - 7 km. → That is the road and the path. Add the 2 km on the stones as well: 9 km.
> - 12 km. → Check the sum: 4 plus 3 plus 2 is 9, not 12.
> _For grown-ups: Added three distances, 4 km, 3 km and 2 km, to make 9 km._


#### `reader:a27-02-rocks` · [main](main.md#a27-02-rocks)

_At the foot of the high rocks the servants set the basket down, in the lee of a big stone._  
**Old Servant**: This is as far as anyone has ever been. Nobody climbs above here.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, 4-ESS2-2, ask): the Alborz mountains lie south of the Caspian Sea  
> _The Alborz are real mountains, in northern Iran. They run along the southern shore of which sea?_  
> - ✅ The Caspian Sea. → Yes. The Alborz curve along the south of the Caspian Sea. Their highest peak, Damavand, is about 5,600 metres.
> - The Red Sea. → No. The Red Sea is far to the south-west. The Alborz lie south of the Caspian.
> - The Black Sea. → No. The Black Sea is further west, north of Turkey. The Alborz lie south of the Caspian.
> _For grown-ups: Located the Alborz mountains south of the Caspian Sea in northern Iran._

_The other servant is already walking away. The old one stays a moment longer, holding the edge of the basket._  
**Old Servant**: I have carried him up a mountain. I will carry that the rest of my life.  
_He goes, and you don’t. The rain gets in everywhere, and the baby is crying, thin and furious._  
_Your Nubbin climbs into the basket and curls round his feet, the warmest thing it knows how to do._  

**Choices**

- **Wrap him in your coat.**
    - He stops crying for long enough to get angry about the coat instead.
- **Hum to him.**
    - You hum the only tune you can think of. You can’t hear yourself over the wind, but he can, because he goes quiet.
- **Hold him, and keep your back to the wind.**
    - You pick him up and turn your back to the storm. He fits against your chest like he has always been there.

#### `reader:a27-02-rocks-done` · [main](main.md#a27-02-rocks-done)

_Above the storm, something very large goes over._  
_For a moment the rain stops falling on you, as if a roof had been put up._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, ask): words that show what we sense: sound words  
> _Writers use sound words so you can hear a storm. Which of these is a sound word: howl, damp or grey?_  
> - ✅ Howl. → Yes. “The wind howls” lets you hear it. Damp and grey are words for touch and sight.
> - Damp. → Damp is a touch word. Howl is the sound word: you can hear a wind howl.
> - Grey. → Grey is a sight word. Howl is the sound word.
> _For grown-ups: Picked out a sound word from words for sound, touch and sight._


#### `reader:a27-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a27-02-complete)

_The shadow goes over again, lower. The wind it makes flattens the grass._  
_You hold on to the baby, and your Nubbin holds on to you. Nobody else in the world knows where the three of you are._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): fear is normal; naming it and staying close helps  
> _You are a long way from home, and you are scared. Which of these helps most?_  
> - ✅ Know that fear is normal, and stay close to someone. → Yes. Fear is a sign we care about being safe. Naming it and staying close helps it shrink.
> - Pretend I’m not scared. → Pretending is understandable, but a feeling we hide often gets bigger. Naming it helps.
> - Run off alone. → Running alone could make things worse. Staying close to someone is safer and calmer.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about how fear is normal, and how naming it and staying close helps._

## A27-3. The Simurgh

- `a27-03-s1`: Hold on to the baby when the great bird comes down. · _Don’t let go of the basket._
- `a27-03-s2`: Arrive at the nest on the peak. · _There is nothing to do but be carried._

#### `reader:a27-03-descent` · [main](main.md#a27-03-descent)

_She comes down out of the cloud like a ship out of fog: a bird bigger than a house._  
_Her feathers are every colour a sunset has ever been, and her tail goes on behind her for a long time._  
_She lands on the big stone above you, and the stone creaks. She looks down at the basket with one gold eye._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-2, ask): birds of prey have very sharp eyesight  
> _The Simurgh found a small basket in a storm, from high above. Eagles and hawks hunt mainly by sight._  
> _Which sense do they rely on?_  
> - ✅ Very sharp eyesight. → Yes. Many birds of prey see far more sharply than we do, so they can spot small things from high up.
> - A strong sense of smell. → Most birds of prey do not smell well. They rely on very sharp eyesight.
> - Hearing the baby cry. → Hearing helps a little, but the storm was loud. Birds of prey rely mainly on very sharp eyesight.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that birds of prey rely on very sharp eyesight to spot things from high up._

_Then she reaches down, very fast, and takes the baby up in her talons, blanket, coat and all._  
_She looks at you, and at your Nubbin, still hanging on to the blanket with all four limbs. Then she takes you too._  

**Choices**

- **Shout.**
    - You shout. The wind takes the shout away, and the ground takes itself away after it.
- **Hold on, and keep your eyes open.**
    - Below, the whole mountain falls away, and the storm is a grey floor under your feet.

#### `reader:a27-03-descent-done` · [main](main.md#a27-03-descent-done)

_Up through the cloud, and out into sun. Above the storm, the sky is enormous, and very blue, and very quiet._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.MD.A.2, ask): add distances in metres and convert to kilometres  
> _She climbs 800 metres to the top of the cloud, then 1,200 metres more. How far is that in kilometres?_  
> - ✅ 2 km. → Yes. 800 plus 1,200 is 2,000 metres, and 1,000 metres make a kilometre.
> - 20 km. → Check the units: 2,000 metres is 2 kilometres, not 20.
> - 1 km. → That is only part of it. 800 plus 1,200 is 2,000 metres, which is 2 km.
> _For grown-ups: Added 800 m and 1,200 m, then converted 2,000 m to 2 km._


#### `reader:a27-03-nest` · [main](main.md#a27-03-nest)

_The nest is on the very top of the Alborz, where the peak touches the stars. No one has ever stood up here._  
_It is built of ebony and sandalwood twined with aloe, and it is the size of a cottage._

**The Simurgh**: No. Not him.  
**The Simurgh**: He is your brother.  

> **Lesson** (art, RL.4.3, ask): imagining and describing a creature the way painters of the Shahnameh did  
> _Painters of the Shahnameh had to imagine a bird nobody had seen, with bright colours and a long, swirling tail. How would you paint her?_  
> _There is no one right answer._  
> - Huge and gold, with a tail like a river of flame. → That is a fair answer. Gold and fire colours fit a bird who comes out of the sunset.
> - Blue and green, with a tail like a swirl of clouds. → That is a fair answer. Painters often placed her among cloud swirls, as if she belonged to the sky.
> - Every colour at once, with eyes like gold coins. → That is a fair answer. “Every colour” is just how the story describes her.
> _For grown-ups: Imagined and described how to paint the Simurgh, as painters of old Persian books did._


#### `reader:a27-03-nest-done` · [main](main.md#a27-03-nest-done)

_The chicks pull their beaks back. One of them, the smallest, sits down hard and looks confused._  
**The Simurgh**: And these two are your nestmates. We have a large family now.  
**The Simurgh**: Sit up and make room.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): what helps a group welcome a newcomer  
> _The chicks wanted to peck at the stranger. What helps a group welcome a newcomer?_  
> - ✅ Someone speaking up for them, and making room. → Yes. The Simurgh spoke up, and she made room. That is how a group learns to welcome someone.
> - Waiting to see if they are useful. → A newcomer should not have to earn a place. Speaking up and making room is how groups welcome people.
> - Ignoring them. → Ignoring someone makes them feel left out. Speaking up and making room helps.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about what helps a group welcome a newcomer._

## A27-4. The Nest

- `a27-04-s1`: Help the Simurgh feed everyone in the nest. · _Share out what she brings: the chicks first, then the baby, then yourself._
- `a27-04-s2`: Keep the baby warm through the cold night. · _Sit with him under the Simurgh’s wing until morning._
- `a27-04-s3`: Stop the chicks sitting on the baby. · _Lift him out of the way whenever a chick backs up to sit down._

#### `reader:a27-04-feed` · [main](main.md#a27-04-feed)

_The Simurgh hunts at dawn and comes back with breakfast for a nest the size of a cottage._  
**The Simurgh**: You have hands. I have only a beak.  
**The Simurgh**: Share it out: the big ones first, or they will take it anyway.  
_She has brought twenty-five figs for the chicks. For the baby she brings honey in the comb and warm goat’s milk, which she will not explain._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): division with remainder: 25 shared among 4  
> _Twenty-five figs are shared equally among four chicks. How many does each chick get, and how many are left over?_  
> - ✅ 6 each, with 1 left over. → Yes. 4 times 6 is 24, so there is 1 fig left: the small one.
> - 5 each, with 5 left over. → If you gave 5 each, 20 figs are used and 5 are left, but the chicks could still have one more each. 6 each, with 1 left.
> - 7 each. → Check: 4 times 7 is 28, which is more than 25. They get 6 each, with 1 left.
> _For grown-ups: Divided 25 figs among 4 chicks: 6 each with 1 left over._

_The chicks eat like a landslide. Your Nubbin watches with great respect, and then tries to keep up._  
**The Simurgh**: Feed them before you feed yourself. That is the only rule in this nest.  
_When the four chicks have had equal shares, there is one fig left. It is a very small fig._  

**Choices**

- **Eat the fig.**
    - You eat it. It is the best fig you have ever had, and the Simurgh brings you two more without a word.
- **Split it with your Nubbin.**
    - Half each. Your Nubbin eats its half in one go and looks at yours with an expression of pure hope.
- **Give it to the smallest chick.**
