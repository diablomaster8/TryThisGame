## A28-1. We Don’t Really Mean It

*Anansesem, the Akan spider stories (Ghana), with Rattray, Akan-Ashanti Folk-Tales (1930): the storyteller’s opening (Rattray, preface)* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Box of Stories page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time once Nova’s atlas is in the Grove; the first quest on the page. Not before adviser sign-off.

**Cast:** Nana Ama (an Asante storyteller on the stool (the Anansesem tradition); placeholder name, advisers to confirm), The Children (the listeners who answer the storyteller (Anansesem tradition)), Kwaku Ananse (Kwaku Ananse the spider (Akan tales); original design)

### Step 1: Step into the courtyard on the atlas page and find a place in the ring.

Hint: Touch the carved stool on the page, then walk towards the fire. · check: `visit:a28-courtyard:evening`

#### <a id="a28-01-courtyard"></a>Scene `a28-01-courtyard`

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page the colour of a warm night: a courtyard, a carved stool, and a ring of faces round a fire._  
_You touch the stool. The paper is warm, and then the air is, and you are standing at the edge of the ring._  
_Crickets and woodsmoke. Somewhere a pot is being scraped, and somebody is laughing at whoever is scraping it._  
_Children sit on mats round the fire, elbowing for the front. Grown-ups lean in the doorways with their arms folded._  
**The Children** _(happy)_: Nana’s coming! Nana!  
_An old woman crosses the courtyard, slowly, because she can. She has a cloth over one shoulder and all the time in the world._  
_Your Nubbin looks at the one empty mat, right at the front, and then at you._  

**Choices**

- **Sit on the front mat.**
    - A small girl shuffles over to make room, without once taking her eyes off the stool.
- **Sit at the back, by a doorway.**
    - A man in the doorway nods to you, as if latecomers are expected and nearly forgiven.
    - Your Nubbin goes to the front anyway, and sits on the end of the girl’s mat.

#### <a id="a28-01-courtyard-done"></a>Done `a28-01-courtyard-done`

_The old woman lowers herself on to the stool. The fire pops._  
_Everybody goes quiet, even the grown-ups, even the man still scraping the pot._  

### Step 2: Listen to how the storyteller begins, and answer her with the children.

Hint: Stay on your mat by the fire until she has spoken. · check: `sit:a28-mat:story`

#### <a id="a28-01-stool"></a>Scene `a28-01-stool`

**Nana Ama** _(think)_: Before I begin, you must all understand something.  
**Nana Ama**: We don’t really mean to say so; we don’t really mean to say so.  
_She says it twice, the way you knock twice on a door you fully intend to open._  
_At once the children shout back at her, all together, loud enough to lift the moths off the wall._  
**The Children** _(happy)_: Tell it! Tell it anyway!  
_The girl beside you nudges you: you are meant to shout too. Everybody is._  

**Choices**

- **Shout it with them.**
    - **You**: Tell it anyway!
    - Your Nubbin shouts too. It comes out as a squeak, and the old woman points at it, delighted.
- **Mouth it, a bit shyly.**
    - The girl shouts it again, for both of you. Your Nubbin bounces in time, which seems to count.

#### <a id="a28-01-stool-done"></a>Done `a28-01-stool-done`

**Nana Ama** _(happy)_: Good. Now that we all agree none of it happened, I can tell you exactly how it happened.  
_The children laugh. You laugh too, a beat behind them._  

### Step 3: Fall into the story, and meet the one it is about.

Hint: Keep listening. When the courtyard fades, look for whoever is sitting beside you. · check: `explore:a28-tale:spider`

#### <a id="a28-01-fall"></a>Scene `a28-01-fall`

**Nana Ama**: This is a story about Kwaku Ananse, the spider. Who else would it be about?  
**The Children** _(happy)_: Ananse!  
**Nana Ama**: Once there were no stories on the earth at all. Not one. Imagine that.  
_You try. It is like trying to imagine a room with no corners._  
**Nana Ama**: Every story there was belonged to the Sky-god, Nyame, and he kept them by him.  
_Her voice goes low, and the courtyard goes with it. The walls thin out and the stars come down close._  
_Your Nubbin grips your sleeve. Then the mat under you is red earth, there are trees, and it is morning._  
_Something is sitting on a fallen log beside you, swinging a great many legs._  

#### <a id="a28-01-fall-done"></a>Done `a28-01-fall-done`

_It is a spider the size of a cooking pot, in a hat, and it is looking at you the way a trader looks at a cart._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(think)_: You have two arms. And a bag. Excellent.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: A spider has eight legs and nowhere at all to put a python.  
_Your Nubbin reaches for a silver thread on the log, and sticks to it._  
_The spider unpicks it without looking, like somebody who has done this many times before._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a28-01-complete"></a>Complete `a28-01-complete`

**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: I am Kwaku Ananse. You will carry things for me, and in return you will see the cleverest thing ever done.  
_Behind you, very faint, you can still hear the fire, and the old woman’s voice going on._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: That’s her. She’s telling us. Don’t worry about it; she gets me right. Mostly right.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Come. We are going to see the Sky-god. Walk tall. Not too tall. He is taller.  
_Your Nubbin falls in behind him, stepping where he steps, which is hard work with eight legs to copy._

## A28-2. The Sky-god’s Stories

*Anansesem, the Akan spider stories (Ghana), with Rattray, Akan-Ashanti Folk-Tales (1930): how the Sky-god’s stories came to be called spider stories (Rattray no. 17), the price; retold* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Box of Stories page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A28-1, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A28-1

**Cast:** Kwaku Ananse (Kwaku Ananse the spider (Akan tales); original design), Nyame (Nyame, the Sky-god (Akan); shown only as a voice and a light), Wisp (the Grove’s grey thief (S2); in this page only, Ananse’s trading partner)

### Step 1: Climb Ananse’s thread up to the Sky-god’s court.

Hint: Follow Ananse up the silver line from the clearing. · check: `climb:a28-web:sky`

#### <a id="a28-02-climb"></a>Scene `a28-02-climb`

_Ananse spins a thread straight up into the morning and climbs it, and you climb after him. Nobody explains how._  
_Your Nubbin rides on your shoulders, sticks to your hair, and stays stuck all the way up._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: Don’t look down. Down is where all the people who didn’t think of this are.  
_At the top is a court of white cloud. Elders sit on carved stools, and at the far end is a light too bright to look at._  
_Rich men are leaving as you arrive. Their servants carry their gifts out after them, all refused._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(think)_: Kings. Big towns. They came to buy the Sky-god’s stories. Couldn’t pay.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: I, on the other hand, have no money at all. It’s a great advantage. Nobody watches a poor spider.  

**Choices**

- **Ask what he means to pay with, then.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: With whatever he asks for. That’s the trick. Agree first. Work out how afterwards.
- **Unstick your Nubbin from your hair first.**
    - It takes a while. An elder on a stool watches the whole business with deep interest, and nods when it is done.

#### <a id="a28-02-climb-done"></a>Done `a28-02-climb-done`

_Ananse straightens his hat, takes three steps forward, and bows so low that the brim touches the cloud._  

### Step 2: Stand with Ananse while he asks the Sky-god his price.

Hint: Stay a respectful step behind Ananse in the court. · check: `talk:a28-nyame:court`

#### <a id="a28-02-price"></a>Scene `a28-02-price`

**Nyame**: Kwaku Ananse. You have come a long way up, for a spider.  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: Great one. I have come to buy your stories.  
_A murmur goes round the elders. One of them stops chewing._  
**Nyame**: Great towns have come to buy them. Kings have come. They have all gone home again with their gold.  
**Nyame**: What makes you think you can pay what they could not?  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Name the price, great one. Whatever it is, I will pay it.  
**Nyame**: Then hear it. Bring me Onini, the python. Bring me Mmoboro, the hornets that sting like fire.  
**Nyame**: Bring me Osebo, the leopard, whose teeth are like spears. And bring me Mmoatia, the fairy whom nobody sees.  
_The elders look at the little spider with his one hat and his two helpers. Somebody coughs, kindly._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: I will bring them all. And to show I mean it, I will add my own old mother, Ya Nsia. Her too.  
_Your Nubbin turns and stares at him. So do you._  
**Nyame**: So be it. Go, Kwaku Ananse.  

**Choices**

- **Whisper, “Your mother?”**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: She’ll love it up here. Very good stools.
- **Say nothing. Some things don’t need saying.**
    - Ananse glances at you sideways, as if your silence has told him something he’d rather not hear.

#### <a id="a28-02-price-done"></a>Done `a28-02-price-done`

_On the way down the thread Ananse is humming. You are counting on your fingers: a python, a swarm, a leopard, a fairy._  
_Your Nubbin is counting too, on its toes, and has run out._  

### Step 3: Walk into the forest with Ananse to plan the first capture.

Hint: Sit with Ananse on the root at the foot of the thread. · check: `walk:a28-forest:path`

#### <a id="a28-02-road"></a>Scene `a28-02-road`

_At the foot of the thread Ananse sits down on a root and looks you over properly, for the first time._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(think)_: Eight legs, I have. Two arms, you have. And one of those, you have.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: What does it do?  
_Your Nubbin, as if it has been asked, sits down in a puddle of sap and sticks._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Ah. A specialist.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Kwaku, they call me, because I was born on a Wednesday. Everyone has a day. What’s yours?  

**Choices**

- **Tell him the day you were born on.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: A fine day. Not as fine as Wednesday. But fine.
- **Say you don’t know.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: Then you can choose one. That’s better still. I’d choose Wednesday.

#### <a id="a28-02-road-wisp"></a>Variant `a28-02-road-wisp`

_Variant of `a28-02-road`._

_At the foot of the thread your bag shifts. A grey head comes out of it, torn ear first, and looks at the forest with deep suspicion._  
_It is Wisp, who would not be left at home. Nor would he leave the egg stone, and it is in his paws now._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(think)_: Hello. What’s that?  
**Wisp** _(soft)_: …mine.  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: Of course it’s yours. Lovely thing. Smooth. I’d give you a story for it.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Not now, obviously. Once I own them. Which will be very soon. A story, for the stone. Fair?  
_Wisp hisses, long and low. Then he puts out one paw to shake, and keeps the stone tight in the other._  
_Ananse shakes it with one leg, and keeps three crossed behind his back._  
**Wisp** _(soft)_: deal. …mine still.  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: Of course. Of course. Everything in its time.  
_Your Nubbin, meanwhile, has sat down in a puddle of sap and stuck. Nobody is surprised._  

**Choices**

- **Tell Wisp he doesn’t have to.**
    - **Wisp**: Hsss. Deal.
    - He has decided. You can see it in his ears.
- **Let them shake on it.**
    - You let it be. A thief and a spider deserve each other, and they both look delighted.

## A28-3. Onini the Python

*Anansesem, the Akan spider stories (Ghana), with Rattray, Akan-Ashanti Folk-Tales (1930): the python and the palm branch (Rattray no. 17, retold)* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Box of Stories page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A28-2, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A28-2

**Cast:** Kwaku Ananse (Kwaku Ananse the spider (Akan tales); original design), Aso (Aso, Ananse’s wife (Akan tales); original design), Onini (Onini the python (Rattray no. 17, retold); original design)

### Step 1: Find a long palm branch and some creeper for Ananse.

Hint: Look along the forest floor for a fallen branch; the creeper is where your Nubbin walks into it. · check: `collect:a28-branch:palm`

#### <a id="a28-03-branch"></a>Scene `a28-03-branch`

**Kwaku Ananse**: We need a palm branch. A long one. And creeper, for tying. Plenty of creeper.  
_You find a fallen palm branch longer than a canoe. Your Nubbin finds the creeper by walking into it._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Now. My wife Aso is coming. When she gets here we will have a terrible argument, and you will look shocked.  
_A spider in a headcloth comes along the path with a basket, already looking tired of him._  
**Aso** _(think)_: What are you doing with that branch, Kwaku?  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Not now, Aso. Argue with me. Loudly. By the python’s tree.  
**Aso**: I always argue with you loudly. I don’t need a reason.  

#### <a id="a28-03-branch-done"></a>Done `a28-03-branch-done`

_The three of you drag the branch to a great tree with roots like knuckles._  
_Something long is asleep up in it, and you can hear it breathing._  

### Step 2: Watch Ananse and Aso’s argument from close by, with a straight face.

Hint: Stand by the branch under the python’s tree. · check: `watch:a28-onini:argument`

#### <a id="a28-03-argue"></a>Scene `a28-03-argue`

**Kwaku Ananse** _(angry)_: He IS longer than this branch!  
**Aso** _(angry)_: He is NOT! He’s shorter! Anybody can see it!  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Longer!  
**Aso**: Shorter! Ask anybody you like!  
_They are both very good at this. You start to suspect they’ve had a great deal of practice._  
_A head comes down out of the tree, very slowly, followed by an enormous amount of python._  
**Onini**: What is all this noise about my length?  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Onini! Thank goodness. My wife says you’re shorter than this branch. I say you’re longer. Settle it for us?  
**Onini** _(angry)_: Shorter? Lay the branch down. I will lie beside it.  
_He pours himself along the branch and stretches, and stretches harder. There is still a hand’s width of branch to spare._  

**Choices**

- **Tell Onini he very nearly has it.**
    - **Onini**: Nearly is not a length a python accepts.
- **Keep a very straight face.**
    - You manage. Your Nubbin does not, and has to turn round and look hard at a tree.

#### <a id="a28-03-argue-done"></a>Done `a28-03-argue-done`

**Kwaku Ananse**: Your tail keeps slipping, that’s the trouble. My helper will hold it, so we can measure you properly.  

### Step 3: Hold the python’s tail while Ananse “measures” him.

Hint: Take the tail end of the python and don’t let go. · check: `carry:a28-onini:tail`

#### <a id="a28-03-tie"></a>Scene `a28-03-tie`

_You hold the tail end. It is heavy, dry and much stronger than you are._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Just to measure you properly. Head end now. And a bit in the middle. For accuracy.  
_Round and round goes the creeper, quick and neat, nose to tail. Onini doesn’t notice until he can’t move to notice._  
**Onini** _(shock)_: This is not measuring.  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: No. You are very long, though. I’ll tell everyone.  
_Your Nubbin, helping with a knot, ties its own foot into it._  

**Choices**

- **Tell Onini you’re sorry.**
    - **Onini**: Sorry, from the one holding my tail.
    - That is fair. You keep holding it anyway, and feel bad.
- **Pat the python’s nose, carefully.**
    - He looks at you with one cold eye. It isn’t friendship, but it isn’t biting either.

#### <a id="a28-03-tie-done"></a>Done `a28-03-tie-done`

_Ananse unties your Nubbin’s foot, one strand at a time, and leaves the python exactly as tied as he was._  

### Complete

#### <a id="a28-03-complete"></a>Complete `a28-03-complete`

**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: One! One, for a spider with no money! Aso, did you see?  
**Aso** _(think)_: I saw. I argued. You’re welcome.  
_Onini goes up the thread to the Sky-god, wrapped like a parcel and loudly offended the whole way._  
_He will be well looked after up there. It is a court, not a kitchen._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Hornets next. Find me a gourd.

## A28-4. Mmoboro the Hornets

*Anansesem, the Akan spider stories (Ghana), with Rattray, Akan-Ashanti Folk-Tales (1930): the hornets and the rain (Rattray no. 17, retold)* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Box of Stories page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A28-3, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A28-3

**Cast:** Kwaku Ananse (Kwaku Ananse the spider (Akan tales); original design)

### Step 1: Fill a gourd with water for Ananse’s “rain”.

Hint: Take the full gourd to the stream and fill it to the brim. · check: `collect:a28-gourd:water`

#### <a id="a28-04-gourd"></a>Scene `a28-04-gourd`

_Ananse cuts the top off a dry gourd and hands it to you. Then he hands you a second gourd, for water._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: The dry one is the house. The wet one is the rain. Don’t mix them up.  
_He picks a plantain leaf as wide as a door and holds it over his head, like a chief under an umbrella._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Fill it to the brim. Hornets can tell a stingy rain.  
_You fill the gourd at a stream. Your Nubbin, helping, gets about half of it._  
_Ananse practises looking dry under his leaf. He is very good at it already._  

**Choices**

- **Ask why the hornets would believe it’s raining.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: Because I’ll tell them. Nobody argues with weather.
- **Offer to hold the leaf.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: No. The leaf is the costume. I am the actor. You are the weather.

#### <a id="a28-04-gourd-done"></a>Done `a28-04-gourd-done`

_The nest hangs from a branch like a grey paper lantern. It hums, and it hums louder the closer you get._  

### Step 2: Pour the rain when Ananse gives the word.

Hint: Stand under the nest with the water gourd, and wait for Ananse. · check: `watch:a28-mmoboro:nest`

#### <a id="a28-04-rain"></a>Scene `a28-04-rain`

**Kwaku Ananse**: Now. Pour.  
_You tip water over the nest. Ananse tips the rest over his own leaf, so that it drums._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(shock)_: Rain! Rain, Mmoboro! The rain’s come, and you’ll all be soaked!  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Look at me, I have a leaf. You have nothing! Come into my gourd. It’s lovely and dry in here.  
_The humming changes note. One hornet comes out, then ten, then all of them, wet and cross and looking for a roof._  
_They pour into the dry gourd like smoke going back down a chimney._  

### Step 3: Stop up the gourd before the hornets work it out.

Hint: Take the plug of grass from Ananse and close the gourd, quickly. · check: `place:a28-stopper:gourd`

#### <a id="a28-04-stopper"></a>Scene `a28-04-stopper`

_Ananse holds out a plug of twisted grass. All eight of his legs are jiggling._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(shock)_: Now! Now, now, now!  
_A hornet’s head pokes out of the gourd’s mouth, looks about, and goes back in to tell the others._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(shock)_: Quickly, before one of them has a thought!  
_Inside the gourd the hornets are beginning to notice that it isn’t raining in there._  
_Your Nubbin stands on tiptoe beside you, ready to help with whatever this is._  

**Choices**

- **Jam the stopper in.**
    - It goes in with a squeak. The gourd buzzes in your hands like a pot coming to the boil.
- **Let your Nubbin close it.**
    - Your Nubbin sits on the hole, which is about the right size. You slide the grass in under it, carefully.

#### <a id="a28-04-stopper-done"></a>Done `a28-04-stopper-done`

_One last hornet, late for everything, lands on your Nubbin’s nose, thinks about it, and stings._  
_Your Nubbin looks at you with enormous eyes. Then it looks at the gourd, and its eyes go narrow._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(happy)_: Two!  

### Complete

#### <a id="a28-04-complete"></a>Complete `a28-04-complete`

_Ananse does a small dance on all eight legs, with the gourd held high over his hat._  
_The hornets go up the thread to the Sky-god. They buzz the whole way, but they go, and none the worse._  
_Your Nubbin’s nose has a small red lump on it. It holds a grudge against the gourd, the hornets and, a little, the sky._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Leopard next. For a leopard, we dig.  

**Rewards**

## A28-5. Osebo the Leopard

*Anansesem, the Akan spider stories (Ghana), with Rattray, Akan-Ashanti Folk-Tales (1930): the leopard and the pit (Rattray no. 17, retold)* · audience: all · where: Nova’s atlas: the Box of Stories page (Atlas campaign) · when: Any time after A28-4, from Nova’s atlas. · requires: A28-4

**Cast:** Kwaku Ananse (Kwaku Ananse the spider (Akan tales); original design), Osebo (Osebo the leopard (Rattray no. 17, retold); original design)

### Step 1: Help Ananse dig a pit across the leopard’s path.

Hint: Dig where Ananse has scratched a line, then cover the hole with sticks and leaves. · check: `build:a28-pit:path`

#### <a id="a28-05-pit"></a>Scene `a28-05-pit`

_Ananse finds the leopard’s path by its smell, which is strong, and its prints, which are larger than you would like._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Here. Dig. Deep enough for a leopard to sit in and think about his life.  
_You dig. Ananse supervises, which mostly means sitting on the spoil heap and saying “deeper”._  
_Your Nubbin digs too, with great energy and no direction, and ends up in a smaller hole of its own._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Now sticks across the top. Then leaves. It must look like nothing at all.  
_When you have finished, it looks like a path. You have to remember very hard where it isn’t._  

#### <a id="a28-05-pit-done"></a>Done `a28-05-pit-done`

**Kwaku Ananse**: Now we go home and sleep. Leopards walk at night. That is when the hole earns its keep.  

### Step 2: Come back to the pit at dawn and see what is in it.

Hint: Rest at Ananse’s, then return to the leopard’s path at first light. · check: `wait:a28-osebo:dawn`

#### <a id="a28-05-fall"></a>Scene `a28-05-fall`

_In the night there is a crash, a thump, and a long, low, astonished growl._  
_At first light you creep back along the path. The sticks have gone, and the pit has a leopard in it._  
**Osebo** _(angry)_: Who dug this? Who digs a hole in a leopard’s own road?  
_Osebo is magnificent, spotted all over, and very, very embarrassed._  
_Ananse puts on a face of enormous concern, which he must keep somewhere handy._  
**Kwaku Ananse** _(sad)_: Osebo! My poor friend! What a terrible hole! Who would do such a thing?
