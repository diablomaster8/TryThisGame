# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Atlas, page A28: The Box of Stories

## A28-1. We Don’t Really Mean It

- `a28-01-s1`: Step into the courtyard and find a mat by the fire. · _Touch the carved stool on the page. Then walk to the fire._
- `a28-01-s2`: Listen to how the storyteller begins. Shout back with the children! · _Stay on your mat by the fire until she has spoken._
- `a28-01-s3`: Fall into the story. Meet the spider. · _Keep listening. When the courtyard fades, look beside you._

#### `sprout:a28-01-courtyard` · [main](main.md#a28-01-courtyard)

_Nova’s big book opens. A warm night!_  
_A fire! Faces all in a ring._  
_You touch the carved stool. Now you are in a courtyard._  
_This is a village in Ghana. Ghana is a country in Africa._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): Ghana is a country in Africa  
> _(Point to the picture of the village.)_  
> _Is Ghana a country in Africa, or a pond?_  
> - ✅ A country in Africa. → Yes! Ghana is a country in Africa, with a village in it.
> - A pond. → Ghana is much bigger than a pond! It is a whole country.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Ghana is a country in Africa, and that this story begins in a Ghanaian village._

_Children sit on mats by the fire. Crickets sing._  
**The Children**: Nana is coming! Nana!  
_An old woman walks slowly across the yard._  
_She has all the time in the world._  
_One mat is empty, right at the front._  

**Choices**

- **Sit on the front mat.**
    - A small girl shuffles over to make room.
    - She never takes her eyes off the stool.
- **Sit at the back, by a doorway.**
    - A man in the doorway nods hello.
    - Your Nubbin goes to the front anyway.
    - It sits on the end of the girl’s mat.

#### `sprout:a28-01-courtyard-done` · [main](main.md#a28-01-courtyard-done)

_The old woman sits on the stool. The fire pops._  
_Everybody goes quiet. Even the grown-ups._  
_Quiet ears help us hear a story._  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, do): listening with quiet ears  
> _(Sit very still. Listen for five slow breaths.)_  
> _Good listening! Now you can hear the fire crackle._  
> Check: `edu:listen:quiet:5`
> _For grown-ups: Practised being still and quiet to listen, the way the village listens to a storyteller._

> Check: `edu:say:refrain:tell-it-anyway`

**Choices**

    - **You**: Tell it anyway!
    - Your Nubbin shouts too. It comes out as a squeak.
    - The old woman points at it, delighted.
- **Say it a bit shyly.**
    - The girl shouts it again, for both of you.
    - Your Nubbin bounces in time. That counts!

## A28-2. The Sky-god’s Stories

- `a28-02-s1`: Climb Ananse’s thread up to the Sky-god’s court. · _Follow Ananse up the silver line._
- `a28-02-s2`: Stand with Ananse while he asks the Sky-god his price. · _Stay one step behind Ananse._
- `a28-02-s3`: Walk into the forest with Ananse. · _Sit with Ananse on the root and listen._

#### `sprout:a28-02-climb` · [main](main.md#a28-02-climb)

_Ananse spins a thread straight up into the morning._  
_He climbs it. You climb after him._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: A spider’s silk is made inside its body.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: It comes out of tiny spouts at the back.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, notice): spiders make silk inside their bodies  
> _Spiders make silk in their bodies, and spin it out of tiny spouts._  
> _That is how a spider makes a thread._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that spiders make silk inside their bodies and spin it out of tiny spouts._

**Kwaku Ananse**: Don’t look down!  
_At the top is a court of white cloud. Elders sit on carved stools._  
_At the far end is a light too bright to look at._  
_Rich men are leaving. The Sky-god said no to all their gifts._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Kings could not pay. But I have no money.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Nobody watches a poor spider.  

**Choices**

- **Ask what he means to pay with.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: With whatever he asks for. Agree first.
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: Work out how afterwards.
- **Unstick your Nubbin from your hair.**
    - It takes a while. An elder watches and nods when it is done.

#### `sprout:a28-02-climb-done` · [main](main.md#a28-02-climb-done)

_Ananse straightens his hat. He takes three steps._  
_He bows so low that his hat touches the cloud._  
_You bow too. Bowing says: I respect you._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): showing respect to an elder  
> _Ananse bows to the elders. How can we show respect to an elder?_  
> - ✅ Say hello kindly and listen. → Yes! Kind words and good listening show respect.
> - Shout over them. → Shouting is not respectful. Kind words and listening are.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about how kind words and good listening show respect for elders._


#### `sprout:a28-02-price` · [main](main.md#a28-02-price)

**Nyame**: Kwaku Ananse. You have come a long way for a spider.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Great one, I have come to buy your stories.  
**Nyame**: Kings tried. Towns tried.  
**Nyame**: They could not pay.  
**Nyame**: Bring me four things. Onini, the python.  
**Nyame**: Mmoboro, the hornets.  
**Nyame**: Bring me Osebo, the leopard. And Mmoatia, the fairy.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: I will bring them all!  
**Kwaku Ananse**: And my old mother will come too, to sit on the best stool.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.OA.A.2, ask): add 1 more: 4 and 1 make 5  
> _(Count the four things on your fingers: python, hornets, leopard, fairy.)_  
> _Then Ananse adds his mother. How many is that?_  
> - ✅ Five. → Yes! Four things and one more makes five.
> - Four. → Four things, and Ananse’s mother makes one more. Four and one is five.
> _For grown-ups: Counted four things and added one more to make five._

_Your Nubbin stares at him. So do you._  
**Nyame**: So be it. Go, Kwaku Ananse.  

**Choices**

- **Whisper, “Your mother?”**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: She will love it up there. Very good stools.
- **Say nothing.**
    - Ananse glances at you. He knows what you are thinking.

#### `sprout:a28-02-price-done` · [main](main.md#a28-02-price-done)

#### `sprout:a28-02-road` · [main](main.md#a28-02-road)

_At the foot of the thread, Ananse sits on a root._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Eight legs, I have. Two arms, you have.  
_Your Nubbin sits in a puddle of sap. It sticks!_  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Ah. A specialist.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: They call me Kwaku. I was born on a Wednesday!  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): Akan day names  
> **Kwaku Ananse**: In Ghana, many families name a baby for the day it was born.  
> **Kwaku Ananse**: Kwaku is a boy born on a Wednesday.  
> **Kwaku Ananse**: Ama is a girl born on a Saturday.  
> _(Do you know your birth day? Tell a grown-up.)_  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that many Ghanaian families give babies a day name, such as Kwaku for a Wednesday boy._

**Kwaku Ananse**: Every child has a day name. What is yours?  

**Choices**

- **Tell him the day you were born.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: A fine day. Not as fine as Wednesday.
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: But fine.
- **Say you don’t know.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: Then you can choose one. I would choose Wednesday.

#### `sprout:a28-02-road-wisp` · [main](main.md#a28-02-road-wisp)

**Kwaku Ananse**: Later.

> - Saturday. → Saturday is later. After Tuesday comes Wednesday.
> _For grown-ups: Put days of the week in order, using a Wednesday day name (Kwaku)._

## A28-3. Onini the Python

- `a28-03-s1`: Find a long palm branch and some creeper. · _Look on the forest floor. Your Nubbin walks into the creeper._
- `a28-03-s2`: Watch Ananse and Aso argue by the python’s tree. · _Stand by the branch. Keep a straight face._
- `a28-03-s3`: Hold the python’s tail. · _Take the tail end and hold on tight._

#### `sprout:a28-03-branch` · [main](main.md#a28-03-branch)

**Kwaku Ananse**: We need a palm branch. A long one.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: And creeper for tying.  
_You find a branch. It is longer than a canoe!_  
_Your Nubbin finds the creeper by walking into it._  
_Creeper is a vine. It is bendy and strong._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-1, ask): a bendy, strong material is good for tying  
> _Which is better for tying a bundle: a stiff stick, or a bendy creeper?_  
> - ✅ A bendy creeper. → Yes! A creeper bends round things, and it is strong.
> - A stiff stick. → A stiff stick will not wrap round. A bendy creeper will!
> _For grown-ups: Compared materials: a bendy, strong creeper ties better than a stiff stick._

**Kwaku Ananse**: Now my wife Aso is coming. We will have a loud argument.  
**Aso**: I always argue with you loudly. I do not need a reason.  

#### `sprout:a28-03-branch-done` · [main](main.md#a28-03-branch-done)

_You drag the branch to a great tree._  
_Its roots look like knuckles on a big brown hand._  
_Something long sleeps up in the branches. Hhh, hhh._  
_It is breathing._  
_Tree. Three._  
_Those words rhyme!_  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): words that rhyme  
> _(Say it: tree, three. They sound the same at the end.)_  
> _Can you think of another word that rhymes with tree? Try “bee” or “me”._  
> Check: `edu:say:rhyme:tree`
> _For grown-ups: Heard and said rhyming words: tree and three._


#### `sprout:a28-03-argue` · [main](main.md#a28-03-argue)

**Kwaku Ananse**: He IS longer than this branch!  
**Aso**: He is NOT! Anybody can see!  
_They shout and shout. They have had lots of practice._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): settling a disagreement without shouting  
> _Ananse and Aso disagree. What helps most?_  
> - ✅ Check the facts together. → Yes! Looking at the facts together settles it.
> - Shout louder. → Shouting does not settle it. Checking the facts does.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about settling a disagreement by checking the facts, not by shouting._

_A head comes down out of the tree, followed by lots of python._  
**Onini**: What is all this noise about my length?  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Settle it for us, Onini. Lie down by the branch.  
**Onini**: Lay the branch down. I will lie beside it.  
_He stretches and stretches. There is still a hand’s width of branch left._  

**Choices**

- **Tell Onini he very nearly has it.**
    - **Onini**: Nearly is not a length a python accepts.
- **Keep a very straight face.**
    - You manage. Your Nubbin does not.
    - It turns to look hard at a tree.

#### `sprout:a28-03-argue-done` · [main](main.md#a28-03-argue-done)

**Kwaku Ananse**: Your tail keeps slipping, Onini.  
**Kwaku Ananse**: My helper will hold it, so we can measure you properly.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): compare lengths: shorter and longer  
> _Onini lay beside the branch. (Look at the picture.)_  
> _Is Onini shorter or longer than the branch?_  
> - ✅ Shorter. → Yes! Onini is shorter. There is branch left over.
> - Longer. → Look again. There is branch left over at the end. Onini is shorter.
> _For grown-ups: Compared two lengths and saw that the python was shorter than the branch._


#### `sprout:a28-03-tie` · [main](main.md#a28-03-tie)

_You hold the tail end. It is heavy and strong._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Just to measure you properly. Now a bit in the middle.  
_Round and round goes the creeper, nose to tail._  
**Onini**: This is not measuring!  
**Kwaku Ananse**: No. You are very long, though!  
_Your Nubbin ties its own foot into the knot._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): a snake has no legs; it moves with its body  
> _A snake has no legs. How does it move?_  
> - ✅ It slithers. → Yes! A snake slithers, pushing along with its strong body.
> - It hops. → Snakes do not hop. They slither along the ground.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a snake has no legs and slithers using its strong body._


**Choices**

- **Tell Onini you’re sorry.**
    - **Onini**: Sorry, from the one holding my tail.
    - You keep holding it anyway. You feel bad.
- **Pat the python’s nose, carefully.**
    - He looks at you with one cold eye.
    - It is not friendship. But it is not biting either.

#### `sprout:a28-03-tie-done` · [main](main.md#a28-03-tie-done)

_Ananse unties your Nubbin’s foot, one strand at a time._  
_Onini is still tied up. He looks cross and a bit sad._  
_Onini feels tricked. Being tricked feels bad._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): naming how someone feels  
> _Onini has been tricked. How does Onini feel?_  
> - ✅ Cross and sad. → Yes! Onini feels cross and sad. Being tricked hurts.
> - Very happy. → Look at his face. Onini is cross and sad, not happy.
> _For grown-ups: Named the feelings of a character who had been tricked: cross and sad._


#### `sprout:a28-03-complete` · [main](main.md#a28-03-complete)

**Kwaku Ananse**: One! One thing paid, by a spider with no money!  
**Aso**: I saw. I argued.  
**Aso**: You are welcome.

_He will be looked after. It is a court, not a kitchen._

## A28-4. Mmoboro the Hornets

- `a28-04-s1`: Fill a gourd with water for Ananse’s “rain”. · _Take the water gourd to the stream and fill it up._
- `a28-04-s2`: Pour the rain when Ananse says. · _Stand under the nest with the water. Wait for Ananse._
- `a28-04-s3`: Stop up the gourd before the hornets work it out. · _Take the grass plug from Ananse. Close the gourd fast._

#### `sprout:a28-04-gourd` · [main](main.md#a28-04-gourd)

_Ananse cuts the top off a dry gourd._  
_He gives you a second gourd for water._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: The dry one is the house. The wet one is the rain.  
_He holds a big plantain leaf over his head, like an umbrella._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Fill it to the brim!  
_You fill the gourd at the stream. Your Nubbin fills one too._  
_Half the water makes it back. Half is on its feet._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): compare amounts: more and less  
> _Your gourd is full. Nubbin’s gourd is half full._  
> _Which gourd has more water?_  
> - ✅ The full gourd. → Yes! The full gourd has more. Half full has less.
> - The half-full gourd. → Look again. The full gourd has more. Half full has less.
> _For grown-ups: Compared a full gourd with a half-full one and said which held more._


**Choices**

- **Ask why the hornets would believe it’s raining.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: Because I will tell them. Nobody argues with weather.
- **Offer to hold the leaf.**
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: No. The leaf is the costume.
    - **Kwaku Ananse**: I am the actor. You are the weather.

#### `sprout:a28-04-gourd-done` · [main](main.md#a28-04-gourd-done)

_The nest hangs from a branch like a grey paper lantern._  
_It hums. It hums louder the closer you get._  
_Hornets chew wood into paper. They build the nest from it._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, notice): hornets build paper nests from chewed wood  
> _Hornets chew wood and mix it with spit. It dries into paper._  
> _That is how they build their nest. Clever!_  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that hornets make a paper nest by chewing wood._


#### `sprout:a28-04-rain` · [main](main.md#a28-04-rain)

**Kwaku Ananse**: Now. Pour.  
_You tip the water. Splash!_  
_Ananse taps his leaf. Drum, drum, drum!_  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Rain! Rain, Mmoboro!  
**Kwaku Ananse**: You will be soaked!  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Come into my dry gourd. It is lovely in here.  
_The humming changes. Buzz, buzz, BUZZ!_  
_The hornets pour into the gourd, like smoke going down a chimney._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.2.4, do): sound words (drum, splash, buzz)  
> _(Say the sounds: splash! drum, drum, drum! buzz, buzz!)_  
> _Sound words make a story noisy and fun._  
> Check: `edu:say:sound-words:drum-buzz`
> _For grown-ups: Said sound words from the story: splash, drum and buzz._


#### `sprout:a28-04-stopper` · [main](main.md#a28-04-stopper)

_Ananse holds out a plug of twisted grass. His legs jiggle._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Now! Now, now, now!  
_A hornet’s head pokes out. It goes back in._  
_Your heart goes thump, thump. It feels fast._  
_A slow breath helps you stay calm._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): a slow breath to stay calm  
> _(Breathe in slowly, then out slowly, three times.)_  
> _Calm hands can close a gourd. Now you are ready._  
> Check: `edu:breathe:slow:3`
> _For grown-ups: Took three slow breaths to calm down when things felt hurried._


**Choices**

- **Jam the stopper in.**
    - It goes in with a squeak.
    - The gourd buzzes in your hands, like a pot coming to the boil.
- **Let your Nubbin close it.**
    - Your Nubbin sits on the hole. It is about the right size.
    - You slide the grass in under it, carefully.

#### `sprout:a28-04-stopper-done` · [main](main.md#a28-04-stopper-done)

_One last hornet lands on your Nubbin’s nose. It stings!_  
_Ananse pops the late hornet into a tiny gourd._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: A spare!  
_Your Nubbin’s eyes go wide. Then they go narrow._  
**Kwaku Ananse**: Two!  
_Two things are paid: the python and the hornets._
