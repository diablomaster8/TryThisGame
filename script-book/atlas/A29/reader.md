# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A29: The Honmoon

## A29-1. The First Hunters

- `a29-01-s1`: Step into the old ink scroll, and find out where this page is. · _Touch the scroll at the bottom of the page, not the stage._
- `a29-01-s2`: Walk along the scroll, one generation at a time. · _Follow the road along the scroll. Each painting is a new set of hunters._

#### `reader:a29-01-scroll` · [main](main.md#a29-01-scroll)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page so bright it hums: a stage, a city at night, and a tiger in a hat grinning from a corner._  
_Under the paint, older, there is a long ink scroll. Mountains, small villages, and a road between them._  
_A card from Nova is tucked into the page. It says to find Korea on the big map before you step in._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): finding a place on a map: Korea is a peninsula, with sea on three sides  
> _On Nova’s big map, Korea hangs down from the north like a thumb into the sea. How many of its sides have water?_  
> - ✅ Three sides. → Yes. Land with water on three sides is called a peninsula, and Korea is one. Seoul, the big city in this story, is the capital of South Korea.
> - Only one side. → Not quite. Look at the map again: sea on the west, the south and the east. Water on three sides makes Korea a peninsula.
> - All four sides. → Good thinking. Land with water on all four sides is an island. Korea is joined to the rest of Asia in the north, so it has water on three sides.
> _For grown-ups: Found Korea on a map and learned that it is a peninsula, with water on three sides._

_You touch the scroll. It is cool, like paper left by a window._  
_Then it is mist, and you are standing in the painting._  
_Everything is ink: the hills, the pines, the roofs, even your sleeve. Your Nubbin is a round grey brushstroke at your feet._  
_Over the far mountains come demons, drawn fast and badly, all teeth. Where they pass, the villagers go pale, as if someone wiped the colour off._  
_A voice in the painting says the demons steal people’s cheer and carry it down to their king._  
_He is a red smudge, the only colour in the scroll. His name is Gwi-Ma._  

**Choices**

- **Look at the people in the villages.**
    - A woman in the nearest village holds a bowl of rice and has forgotten why. You want very much to give her back her colour.
- **Pick up your Nubbin.**
    - You lift your Nubbin, and in your arms it goes back to its own colour, and so does your hand. Good.
    - Keep hold.
- **Look at the red smudge.**
    - You look at it a moment too long, and it seems to look back. You look away first.

#### `reader:a29-01-scroll-done` · [main](main.md#a29-01-scroll-done)

_On a hill above the villages, three small figures climb to the top. They wear long skirts and short jackets, in the painter’s soft colours._  
_They turn to face the mountains, and take one big breath together._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, ask): a voice is made by vibrations in the throat  
> _Try it: put your fingers lightly on your throat and hum. What do you feel under your fingers?_  
> - ✅ A buzzing, a tiny shaking. → Yes. Your voice is made by tiny vibrations in your throat, and the shaking makes the air shake too. That moving air is what carries a sound to someone’s ear.
> - Nothing at all. → Try again, a little lower, and press just a bit more firmly. There is a buzzing, a tiny shaking. That is the vibration that makes your voice.
> - Something hot. → It can feel warm, because your hand is warm. But the thing to feel is a buzz. A voice is made of vibrations.
> _For grown-ups: Felt the vibration in the throat when humming, and learned that vibrations make a voice._


#### `reader:a29-01-hunters` · [main](main.md#a29-01-hunters)

_The three women open their mouths and sing. You hear no words, only something like a held breath let go._  
_Where the sound goes, the ink brightens into a shining line. It runs along the mountain ridge and closes like a stitch._  
_The demons hit the line and stop. They pace along it and cannot cross, and the villagers get their colour back._  
_A label in neat brushwork says the line is called the Honmoon. A voice holds it up, and only voices can keep it up._  
_You walk on, and the scroll walks with you. Three new hunters on the hill, then three more, generation after generation._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): working out the meaning of a new word from its context: generation  
> _The scroll says “generation after generation”. Three hunters, then the next three, and the next._  
> _What is a generation?_  
> - ✅ A group of people born around the same time, followed by the next group. → Yes. Your grandparents, your parents and you are three different generations. The hunters pass the Honmoon down the generations, like a family recipe.
> - One single very old person. → Not quite. A generation is a whole group of people of about the same age. The painting shows many groups, one after another, each taking a turn.
> - Everybody who is alive today. → Close, but a generation is smaller: the people born around the same time. The hunters on the scroll are different generations, because they lived in different times.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out that a generation is a group born around the same time, using the scroll as a clue._

_Each three hope to sing so well that the line is sealed shut for good. Nobody ever has._  
_The last painting shows three girls on a stage, under lights that are not painted at all._  

**Choices**

- **Let your Nubbin hum along.**
    - Your Nubbin hums, flat and happy. The shining line wobbles very slightly, as if it has been tickled.
- **Listen without a sound.**
    - You listen. It is the kind of music that makes you stand up straighter without deciding to.

#### `reader:a29-01-hunters-done` · [main](main.md#a29-01-hunters-done)

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): Korean ink painting: brush, ink and hanji paper made from mulberry bark  
> _The label goes on: hanji is made from the bark of the paper mulberry tree, by hand, in Korea, for hundreds of years._  
> _Painters used it for scrolls like this one. Good hanji can last a very long time._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that Korean ink paintings are made on hanji, a paper from mulberry bark, and that people have used it for centuries._

#### `reader:a29-01-sable-hid` · [main](main.md#a29-01-sable-hid)

_Variant of `a29-01-complete`._

_Something brushes your ankle in the ink: Sable has come with you. She is a grey smudge in the grey mist, keeping very low._  
_She doesn’t come to your hand. She finds the deepest shadow on the scroll and goes into it, and watches you from there._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): a frightened friend needs space and time; staying near without crowding  
> _Sable is frightened, so she hides in the shadow. What helps a frightened friend?_  
> - ✅ Staying nearby, going slowly, and not crowding her. → Yes. Giving space, staying close and being patient is how you earn a scared friend’s trust. Sable chose the shadow. Leaving her there is a kind of respect.
> - Pulling her out into the light. → That would probably frighten her more. Staying nearby and going slowly works better.
> - Ignoring her completely. → She would think you had forgotten her. Staying near and going slowly is best.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a frightened friend needs space, patience and a calm presence nearby._

#### `reader:a29-02-show-done` · [main](main.md#a29-02-show-done)

> _The line in the sky “hums like a struck glass”. What does that picture help you hear?_  
> - ✅ A clear, ringing note that fades slowly. → Yes. A struck glass rings softly and then fades. A comparison with “like” or “as” is called a simile. It lets you hear with your imagination.
> - A loud crash. → A crash would be a broken glass. Here the glass is only tapped. The simile gives you a clear, soft ringing sound.
> - A sneeze. → Ha! Not this time. Think of tapping a glass with a spoon. That clear ringing is the sound the sky line makes.

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): equal sharing with division: 6 demons shared between 3 hunters  
> _There are six demons and three hunters. If they share the demons out evenly, how many does each hunter face?_  
> - ✅ 2 each. → Yes. 6 shared between 3 is 2 each, because 2 + 2 + 2 = 6. Mira would call that a very tidy evening.
> - 3 each. → Check by counting: 3 + 3 + 3 is 9, which is too many for six demons. Try 2 each: 2 + 2 + 2 is 6.
> - 6 each. → If every hunter faced 6, that would be 18 demons. With 6 demons shared between 3 hunters, each gets 2.
> _For grown-ups: Divided 6 by 3 to share demons equally between three hunters._
