## A30-1. The Jewish Town

- `a30-01-s1`: Step into the brown-ink page and find the caretaker’s house. · _Touch the page, then follow the lane to the door with the ring of keys._
- `a30-01-s2`: Carry water from the well with Rivka. · _Rivka knows the way. The well is where three lanes meet._
- `a30-01-s3`: Fetch the bread from Hannah’s bakery. · _The bakery is the door with the warm step. Your Nubbin has found it already._

#### `sprout:a30-01-page` · [main](main.md#a30-01-page)

_Nova’s atlas opens at a page of brown ink._  
_There is a steep roof, little houses, and a wavy line of water._  
**Ori**: This is Prague, a city. A wavy line runs right through it.  
**Ori**: (Point to the wavy line.)  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): finding a river on a map  
> **Ori**: A map shows land and water. What is the wavy line of water?  
> - ✅ A river → Yes! The river is called the Vltava. It runs through the middle of Prague.
> - A road → A road is for walking. Water is blue and wavy. It is a river!
> - A roof → The roof is the steep triangle on top. The wavy line is a river.
> _For grown-ups: Read a simple map: found the river, the Vltava, and Prague as a city beside it._

**Ori**: Long ago, people told a story about this place.  
**Ori**: It is a story, not a fact.  
_Rabbi Loew was a real teacher here._  
_The clay man is only in the story._  
_You step into the page._  
_A kind man with a ring of keys waves at you._  
**Avram**: I am Avram. I look after the keys.  
**Avram**: You can be our lodger.  

**Choices**

- **Shake his hand.**
    - **Avram**: A good grip! You will carry water.
- **Ask which key is which.**
    - **Avram**: This one is the big door. This one is the cupboard.
    - **Avram**: This one? I do not know!

    - He lets you take it. He looks pleased that you helped without being asked.

#### `sprout:a30-01-page-done` · [main](main.md#a30-01-page-done)

> - Sleepy → Not sleepy. Sleepy people yawn. Rivka is hopping. She is excited!
> - Cross → Not cross. She is grinning. She is excited!
> _For grown-ups: Named a feeling (excited) from how someone looks and moves._

**Rivka**: That is Shmuel, my little brother. He is five.

#### `sprout:a30-01-water-done` · [main](main.md#a30-01-water-done)

**Dvora**: Good. Now you are guests.  
**Dvora**: Rivka, take them to the bakery before it shuts.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): the first sound of a word  
> **Dvora**: Bakery. Bread.  
> **Dvora**: Both start with the same sound. Which word starts the same: bread or well?  
> - ✅ Bread → Yes! B, b, bakery. B, b, bread. The same sound at the start.
> - Well → Well starts with w. Bakery and bread both start with b!
> _For grown-ups: Matched the first sound b in bakery and bread._

_Rivka grins. She loves the bakery._  

#### `sprout:a30-01-bakery` · [main](main.md#a30-01-bakery)

**Hannah**: Those are for the Sabbath. This is a crust I burnt.  
**Hannah**: It is for you.  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-4, ask): baking cannot be undone  
> **Hannah**: The soft dough went into the hot oven and came out as bread.  
> **Hannah**: Can it turn back into dough?  
> - ✅ No, heat changed it for good → Right! Baking changes dough for good.
> - Yes, it can → Not this time. Once bread is baked, it cannot go back to dough.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that baking changes dough into bread and it cannot be changed back._

_Your Nubbin holds the crust in both paws, as if it were a medal._

## A30-2. The Rabbi

- `a30-02-s1`: Go to the study house with Avram and sit at the back. · _The bench by the door. Sit still, and listen._
- `a30-02-s2`: Answer the rabbi’s question, if you can. · _He is looking at the back bench. There is no wrong answer._

#### `sprout:a30-02-study` · [main](main.md#a30-02-study)

_The study house is one long room full of books and noise._  
_Students sit in pairs, and talk and talk about one page._  
**Avram**: It is meant to be loud. Thinking is noisy!  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, ask): how people learned long ago  
> _In the study house, students learn in pairs and talk it out._  
> _How do they learn?_  
> - ✅ Together, with a partner → Yes! Two heads think better than one.
> - All alone and silent → Not here! In this study house everyone talks.
> _For grown-ups: Compared how students learned in the past (talking in pairs) with today._

_At the front sits an old man with a grey beard._  
**Rabbi Loew**: Here is a very old story.  
**Rabbi Loew**: A man made of clay was spoken to, and he did not answer.  
**Rabbi Loew**: So the wise man knew he was only clay.  

#### `sprout:a30-02-study-done` · [main](main.md#a30-02-study-done)

_The rabbi lets the story sit in the room._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.K.3, ask): knowing who a story is about  
> _In the rabbi’s story, who was made of clay and could not answer?_  
> - ✅ A clay man → Yes! A man made of clay.
> - A baker → The baker is Hannah. The story was about a man made of clay.
> _For grown-ups: Named the character in a short story._

_It is like bread that must rest before you cut it._  

#### `sprout:a30-02-question` · [main](main.md#a30-02-question)

**Rabbi Loew**: Now. What makes a person a person?  
**Rabbi Loew**: Someone tell me.  
_A student says, “Someone who answers.” Another says, “Someone you can talk to.”_  
**Rabbi Loew**: An old prayer says: before I was made, you saw me.  
**Rabbi Loew**: So we were all clay once, and someone saw us. What makes the difference?  
_He looks at the back bench. At you._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): showing feelings without words  
> _Your Nubbin cannot speak. How can it show you it is happy?_  
> - ✅ It bounces and smiles → Yes! Faces and bodies show feelings, even without words.
> - It cannot show anything → It can! Smiles, bounces and hugs tell us how someone feels.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that people show feelings with faces and bodies as well as words._


**Choices**

- **Someone who can answer.**
    - **Rabbi Loew**: Braver, from the back of the room. Good.
- **Someone who can ask.**
    - **Rabbi Loew**: Then this is a room of people, and I worry for nothing.
- **Look at your Nubbin.**
    - **Rabbi Loew**: It does not speak. But it looked up when I said clay.
    - **Rabbi Loew**: Answering is more than words.

#### `sprout:a30-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a30-02-complete)

_At the door the rabbi looks down at your Nubbin._  
**Rabbi Loew**: Does it do as it is told?  
**Avram**: Never, Rabbi! It is a great comfort to the house.  
**Rabbi Loew**: Good! I would worry otherwise.  
_He goes out, and leaves his coat behind on the peg._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): rhyming words  
> _The rabbi forgot his coat. Coat rhymes with…which word: goat or cup?_  
> - ✅ Goat → Yes! Coat, goat. They sound the same at the end.
> - Cup → Cup does not sound like coat. Goat does: coat, goat!
> _For grown-ups: Found a rhyming word (coat and goat)._

## A30-4. The Answer

- `a30-04-s1`: Take the rabbi his supper in the study house. · _Dvora has wrapped it in a cloth. He will forget to eat it._
- `a30-04-s2`: Listen to what the rabbi asks of you. · _He is at the study house door with two men._
- `a30-04-s3`: Light the lantern in Avram’s window. · _The lantern hangs by the door._

#### `sprout:a30-04-ask` · [main](main.md#a30-04-ask)

_Dvora sends you across the lane with bread and soup._  
_The rabbi sits very still with his hand over his eyes._  
**Rabbi Loew**: I asked how to keep my town safe. That was all.  
**Rabbi Loew**: The answer is: be patient, be careful, be kind, and wait.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): calm waiting  
> _The rabbi has to wait. What can help us wait?_  
> - ✅ Slow breaths → Yes. In through the nose. Out through the mouth. Slowly.
> - Shouting → Shouting makes waiting feel harder. Slow breaths help.
> _For grown-ups: Practised the idea that slow breathing helps while we wait._

_Your Nubbin pushes the soup an inch closer. It is a good suggestion._  

#### `sprout:a30-04-ask-done` · [main](main.md#a30-04-ask-done)

_In the morning the bowl is empty._  
_The rabbi is asleep at the table, with his cheek on a book._  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, ask): food and sleep help bodies  
> _The rabbi ate his soup and had a sleep._  
> _What do bodies need to stay strong?_  
> - ✅ Food and sleep → Yes! Food, water and sleep keep us strong.
> - Only sweets → Sweets are a treat. Bodies need good food and sleep.
> _For grown-ups: Named food and sleep as things bodies need._

## A30-5. Clay from the River

- `a30-05-s1`: Follow the three of them to the river, at a distance. · _Keep well back. Stop at the willows above the bank._
- `a30-05-s2`: Let your Nubbin help, if they will have it. · _Your Nubbin has made up its own mind. Stay on the bank._

#### `sprout:a30-05-follow` · [main](main.md#a30-05-follow)

_You follow at a distance, through the sleeping lanes, out to the river._  
_The river is wide and dark, and it talks to itself._  
_You stop in the willows. Your Nubbin holds on to your trouser leg._  
_Below you, three people kneel on the mud with their sleeves rolled up._  
_They dig clay out of the river bank with their hands._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): places give people what they need  
> _The three people take clay from the river bank._  
> _Where does the clay come from?_  
> - ✅ From the river bank → Yes! Rivers leave soft clay on their banks.
> - From the sky → Not from the sky. The clay is in the mud by the river.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that people use what a place gives them, like clay from a river bank._


**Choices**

- **Go a little closer.**
    - You go one tree closer, and stop.
    - It feels like walking into a quiet room.
- **Stay where you are.**
    - You stay in the willows. Your Nubbin looks up at you, thinking.

#### `sprout:a30-05-clay` · [main](main.md#a30-05-clay)

_The clay on the ground makes a shape: a head, shoulders, two long arms._  
_It is as long as a door._  
_The three people smooth it, like making bread._  
_The wind dries the clay._  
_Isaac carries water up from the river in cupped hands, but it is slow._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-1, ask): clay is soft when wet and hard when dry  
> _Wet clay is soft. What happens when the wind dries it?_  
> - ✅ It gets hard → Yes! Dry clay is hard. Wet clay is soft.
> - It gets bigger → It does not grow. Dry clay gets hard, so it needs water to stay soft.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that clay is soft when wet and hard when dry._

_Your Nubbin runs down the bank, fills its cheeks, and trots back._  
_Splish! It wets the clay, very carefully._  
_Then it goes again, and again._  

#### `sprout:a30-05-clay-done` · [main](main.md#a30-05-clay-done)

_Near dawn Isaac carries your Nubbin up the bank himself._  
_It is muddy up to its ears._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): feeling proud after helping  
> _Your Nubbin helped all night. How might it feel now?_  
> - ✅ Proud → Yes! Helping can make us feel proud.
> - Grumpy → It might be a bit tired, but helping usually feels proud.
> _For grown-ups: Named the feeling of pride after helping._

**Isaac**: Go home. He knows you are here.

## A30-6. The Word

- `a30-06-s1`: Go back down to the river when the rabbi sends for you. · _Jacob is at the door. Go with him._
- `a30-06-s2`: Watch the rabbi write three letters. · _Stand where he puts you. Your Nubbin will want to be nearer._

#### `sprout:a30-06-tellings` · [main](main.md#a30-06-tellings)

_Before sunrise, Jacob knocks at the door. The rabbi wants you at the river._  
**Rabbi Loew**: You followed. I thought you would.  
**Rabbi Loew**: Come and see, and be quiet a little while.  
_The clay man lies on the bank under the grey sky._  
_He is as long as a door._  
_Some people tell this story one way, and some tell it another._  
_Stories grow when they are told many times._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, ask): how stories travelled long ago  
> _Long ago there were few books. How did most stories travel?_  
> - ✅ By people telling them aloud → Yes! People told stories to each other, again and again.
> - By computer → There were no computers then. People told stories aloud.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that stories travelled by being told aloud, so they changed a little each time._

**Rabbi Loew**: Here, I will write one plain word on his brow: truth.  
**Rabbi Loew**: A word anyone can read. Three letters.  

**Choices**

- **Why truth?**
    - **Rabbi Loew**: A guard should stand on something true.
    - **Rabbi Loew**: And anyone in the lane can read it.
- **Say nothing, as he asked.**
    - **Rabbi Loew**: Good. You were listening.
    - **Rabbi Loew**: That is most of it.

#### `sprout:a30-06-letters` · [main](main.md#a30-06-letters)

**Rabbi Loew**: Hebrew is read from the right. So the first letter goes on the right.  
**Rabbi Loew**: Alef, א. It is a quiet letter.  
**Rabbi Loew**: It makes no sound of its own.  
**Ori**: Alef. The quiet one goes first.  
**Rabbi Loew**: Mem, מ, from near the middle. And tav, ת, the very last letter.  
_Three letters in a row, from right to left: אמת._  
**Rabbi Loew**: Emet. That means truth.  
**Rabbi Loew**: Truth is when something is real and honest.  
**Ori**: (Put your finger on the right, and slide it left: alef, mem, tav.)  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Literacy, do): three Hebrew letters, read from right to left  
> **Ori**: Point to alef, then mem, then tav, moving your finger from right to left.  
> Check: `edu:point:letters:alef-mem-tav`
> _For grown-ups: Pointed to alef, mem and tav from right to left, and heard that emet means truth._

_The clay man opens his eyes._  
_He sits up slowly, and waits to be told what to do._  
**Rabbi Loew**: Get up, then, my friend. We will walk home.  

#### `sprout:a30-06-letters-done` · [main](main.md#a30-06-letters-done)

_He stands up, a head taller than Isaac, with his hands open._  
_There were three letters on his brow: alef, mem, tav._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, ask): count to 3  
> _(Count the letters on his brow: alef, mem, tav.) How many letters are there?_  
> - ✅ 3 → Yes! Three letters: one, two, three.
> - 2 → Count with your finger: alef is one, mem is two, tav is three!
> - 4 → Count again: one, two, three. Three letters.
> _For grown-ups: Counted three letters to say how many._
