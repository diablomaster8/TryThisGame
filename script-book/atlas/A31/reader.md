# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Atlas, page A31: The Hero Twins

## A31-1. The Hearth

- `a31-01-s1`: Step into the kitchen on the atlas page. · _Put your hand on the warm page, then go in through the doorway._
- `a31-01-s2`: Sit by the hearth and listen to the old story. · _Sit on the low stool by the fire and watch the smoke go up._

#### `reader:a31-01-page` · [main](main.md#a31-01-page)

_Nova’s atlas falls open at a page that smells of woodsmoke. There is a hearth, a stack of tortillas and a green mountain with cloud on its shoulder._  
_You put your hand flat on the page, and it is warm. Then the warmth is a fire, and you are standing in a doorway in the highlands of Guatemala._  
_A grandmother sits by the fire, patting dough into rounds and laying them on a clay griddle. Pat, pat, turn._  
_A girl of about nine is doing her homework at the table, badly, because she keeps watching the door._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.3-5, notice): Guatemala is in Central America, south of Mexico; K’iche’ Maya people live there and speak K’iche’ today  
> **Grandmother**: This kitchen is in Guatemala. Look south of Mexico on a map, and there we are, in Central America.  
> **Grandmother**: Many people here are K’iche’ Maya, and K’iche’ is one of more than twenty Mayan languages spoken in Guatemala.  
> **Grandmother**: We speak it today, at this table. It is not a language from a book. It is the one I scold you in.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned where Guatemala is on a map, and that K’iche’ Maya people live there and speak K’iche’ today._

**Granddaughter**: Grandmother. Someone’s at the door. With a… what is that?  
**Grandmother**: A guest, and a small guest. Come in, both of you. The cold gets in.  
_Your Nubbin goes straight to the fire and sits down, as if it has been coming here for years._  

**Choices**

- **Sit on the low stool she points to.**
    - **Grandmother**: Good. Now you’re the right height for a story.
- **Offer to help with the tortillas.**
    - You try. Yours comes out the shape of a country nobody would want to live in.
    - **Grandmother**: You eat that one yourself. That’s the rule.
- **Let your Nubbin warm its paws.**
    - It holds its paws to the fire, then turns round and warms its back, very seriously.

#### `reader:a31-01-page-done` · [main](main.md#a31-01-page-done)

**Granddaughter**: Is it a dog?  
**Grandmother**: It’s a guest. You don’t ask a guest what it is. You give it a tortilla.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): thinking about how to welcome a stranger  
> _The grandmother welcomed a stranger with food before asking a single question. What do you think that says about how to welcome someone?_  
> - Be kind first. Questions can wait. → That’s a fair answer. The grandmother would agree: first make someone comfortable, then get curious.
> - Ask questions first, so you know who they are. → That’s a fair answer too: knowing who is at your door matters. The grandmother just feeds people while she works it out.
> - It depends on the guest. → That’s a thoughtful answer. A good host notices who is in front of them, and what they need most.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how to welcome a stranger, with no single right answer._

_Your Nubbin is given a tortilla, warm from the griddle. It holds it in both paws like a letter from home._  

#### `reader:a31-01-story` · [main](main.md#a31-01-story)

**Granddaughter**: Grandmother. Tell the old one. The twins one. With the ball.  
**Grandmother**: You’ve heard it a hundred times.  
**Granddaughter**: So it’s a hundred and one. Please.  
**Grandmother**: Our people wrote it down long ago, in our own language, so it wouldn’t be lost.  
**Grandmother**: People still read it. And grandmothers still tell it, which is better, because you can interrupt a grandmother.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, notice): a story can be told aloud and written down; oral stories change as they are told  
> **Grandmother**: The book was written down by K’iche’ authors in the 1550s, in our own language. People still read it.  
> **Grandmother**: But a told story is different. I can leave things out, add things, answer you back.  
> **Grandmother**: A story that is told by many voices keeps being told. That is what a living story is.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a story can be both written and told aloud, and that a told story keeps changing and living._

**Granddaughter**: You can’t interrupt a book.  
**Grandmother**: You can. It just doesn’t notice.  
_She lays another tortilla on the griddle. The smoke goes up past the beam, slow and grey, and you watch it go._  
**Grandmother**: Before the sun was the sun, there were two brothers who played ball…  
_The smoke thickens, and the kitchen goes soft at the edges. You can still hear her voice, but now there is grass under your feet._  

**Choices**

- **Pick your Nubbin up first.**
    - You scoop it off the hearth. It is warm right through, like a loaf.
- **Keep your eyes on the grandmother.**
    - **Grandmother**: I’m still here. I’ll be here the whole time. Go on.

#### `reader:a31-01-complete` · [main](main.md#a31-01-complete)

_You are standing on a hillside in the early morning, in a world that is mostly still being made._  
_Somewhere close, something heavy and rubbery goes thock against a wall. Then again: thock._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, ask): sound is made by vibration  
> **Grandmother**: Thock. The ball hits the wall, and you hear it from here. What is happening to make that sound?  
> - ✅ The ball and the wall vibrate, and the air carries the shake to our ears. → Yes. A sound is a vibration, a very fast shake, passed through the air to your ears.
> - The ball is only loud because it is heavy. → Weight helps, because a heavy ball hits harder and shakes more. But the sound itself is the shake that travels through the air.
> - The sound travels without anything shaking. → Every sound starts with something shaking. Put your fingers on your throat and hum, and you can feel it.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that sound is a vibration that travels through the air to our ears._

**Grandmother**: Listen. That’s them.  
_Your Nubbin’s ears go up. Whatever that noise is, it wants it._  

#### `reader:a31-01-ribbons` · [main](main.md#a31-01-ribbons)

_Variant of `a31-01-complete`._

_The girl’s eyes go to your Nubbin’s ribbons: gold and silver, stitched together down the middle in green._  
**Granddaughter**: Why’s it got two colours?  
_She counts the colours anyway, thread and all, under her breath: gold, silver, green, and round again._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.OA.C.5, ask): a repeating colour pattern: find the 7th  
> _The girl’s colours go gold, silver, green, gold, silver, green, and on. What colour is the 7th?_  
> - ✅ Gold → Yes. The pattern repeats every three: 1 gold, 2 silver, 3 green, 4 gold, 5 silver, 6 green, 7 gold.
> - Silver → Let’s count: 1 gold, 2 silver, 3 green, 4 gold, 5 silver, 6 green, 7 gold. The 7th is gold.
> - Green → Green comes 3rd and 6th. Count on: the 7th starts the pattern again with gold.
> _For grown-ups: Found the 7th item in a repeating three-colour pattern._

_Before you can answer, her grandmother hands her a tortilla, and the question goes in with it._  

### Glossary

- **highlands**: High, hilly land, often with mountains.

## A31-2. The Ballplayers

- `a31-02-s1`: Watch the two brothers play ball. · _Go down the hill to the ballcourt and watch from the wall._
- `a31-02-s2`: Help your Nubbin bring back the ball that went over the wall. · _Follow your Nubbin down the slope and carry the ball back up together._

#### `reader:a31-02-court` · [main](main.md#a31-02-court)

_Below the hill is a ballcourt: two long sloping walls, with a strip of beaten earth between them._  
_Two men are playing on it, and they are loud about it. Every point gets a shout, and every miss gets a louder one._  
**Hun Hunahpu**: Mine! That was mine, and you know it.  
**Vucub Hunahpu**: It hit your knee. Knees don’t count. Knees have never counted.  
_They never touch the ball with their hands. They take it on the hip, the thigh, the shoulder, and it flies off heavy and black._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.OA.A.1, ask): add and subtract within 100 in a word problem  
> _Hun Hunahpu has scored 7 points and Vucub has scored 5. How many have they scored together?_  
> - ✅ 12 → Yes. 7 + 5 = 12. And Hun is ahead by 2, which he will mention.
> - 11 → Close: count on five from 7, and you get 8, 9, 10, 11, 12. They have 12 together, and Hun leads by 2.
> - 13 → One too many: count on five from 7, and you get 8, 9, 10, 11, 12. The answer is 12.
> _For grown-ups: Added 7 and 5 in a word problem and found the difference._

**Grandmother**: Hun Hunahpu and his brother, Vucub Hunahpu. Good players. Terrible neighbours.  
_Down under the court, a long way under, somebody is trying to sleep._  
_The ball bounces off the end wall, spins once, and rolls straight towards your Nubbin._  

**Choices**

- **Let your Nubbin have a go.**
    - It hip-bumps the ball, and the ball does not move. Your Nubbin does, backwards, and sits down.
    - **Vucub Hunahpu**: Good form. Wrong direction.
- **Roll it back to them.**
    - **Hun Hunahpu**: Hands! Hands on the ball!
    - **Vucub Hunahpu**: Leave it. Guests get one hand-ball. It’s an old rule. I made it up just now.

#### `reader:a31-02-fetch` · [main](main.md#a31-02-fetch)

_The next shot goes high over the wall and away down the hill. Both brothers look at each other, then at your Nubbin._  
**Hun Hunahpu**: Little one. Would you?  
_Your Nubbin is already gone, bouncing down the slope after the ball, bouncing almost as well as the ball does._  
_You find it sitting proudly on the ball, which is nearly as big as it is._  
_The ball is heavier than you expect. It’s made from tree sap, worked until it bounces, and it smells faintly of smoke._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): rubber is made from the milky latex of a tree; materials have properties  
> **Grandmother**: The sap is called latex. It comes out of the rubber tree thick and white, like milk that has been left out.  
> **Grandmother**: Left alone, it dries into something stretchy that bounces. People in this part of the world were using it more than three thousand years ago.  
> **Grandmother**: A tree gave them a material that was stretchy, bouncy and waterproof. All they needed was the idea.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that rubber is made from the latex of a tree, and that it is stretchy and bouncy._

**Grandmother**: People here made balls like that long before anyone else in the world had one. Remember that, when you play.  
_You carry it back up between you. Your Nubbin carries the important end._  

**Choices**

- **Let your Nubbin hand it over.**
    - It pushes the ball up to Hun Hunahpu with its whole body. He bows to it, very low, and it bows back.
- **Throw it back over the wall.**
    - It sails over and lands with a thock. A cheer from the court: apparently that counts as a point for somebody.

#### `reader:a31-02-complete` · [main](main.md#a31-02-complete)

_Then the owls come. Four of them, out of a clear sky, and they land on the end wall in a row._  
**Owls**: From the lords of Xibalba, One Death and Seven Death. You are loud. You are very loud.  
**Owls**: They summon you. Come down and play against them. Bring your gear.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): the meaning of an unknown word from context  
> _The owls say the lords “summon” the brothers. What does summon mean?_  
> - ✅ To order someone to come → Yes. You can tell from the story: the brothers are told to come down and play.
> - To invite someone to dinner → A summons is not an invitation you can turn down. The owls expect the brothers to go.
> - To send someone away → It is the opposite. To summon is to call someone to you.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out the meaning of “summon” from the story._

**Hun Hunahpu**: They want a game. Good. We’re the best there is.  
_That evening the brothers tie their ball and gear up in the roof of their mother Xmucane’s house, where she won’t have to look at it._  
_They tell her not to worry. People always tell people that._  

### Glossary

- **summon**: To order someone to come to you.
- **latex**: The milky, sticky sap of some trees, used to make rubber.

## A31-3. The Road Down

- `a31-03-s1`: Listen to the grandmother tell what happened on the road down. · _Stay on the empty road and let her tell it._
- `a31-03-s2`: Look at the tree by the road. · _The tree full of gourds stands just off the road. Go and look; you needn’t touch._

#### `reader:a31-03-road` · [main](main.md#a31-03-road)

_The page goes quiet. There is a road going down between rocks, and nobody on it._  
**Grandmother**: This part I tell. You don’t need to see it.  
**Grandmother**: They went down and down, across rivers, to a place where four roads meet.  
**Grandmother**: At the bottom they met the lords of Xibalba, and a test nobody could win.  
**Grandmother**: And they didn’t come back up. They stayed there, in Xibalba.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): handling the feeling of missing someone  
> _Xmucane is left at home, waiting. Which is a healthy way to carry the feeling of missing someone?_  
> - ✅ Let yourself feel it, and talk about them. → Yes: a big feeling is easier to carry when it is shared. Saying their name and telling their stories keeps them close.
> - Keep busy so you never think about it. → Being busy can help for a while. But the feeling waits for us, and it eases when we say it out loud to someone.
> - Pretend it does not hurt. → Many people try that, but it is heavy to carry alone. Telling someone you miss them makes it lighter.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about healthy ways to carry the feeling of missing someone._

_The road stays empty. A little wind moves the dust on it._  

**Choices**

- **Sit down at the side of the road.**
    - You sit. Your Nubbin sits too, close enough that you can feel it breathing.
- **Ask what happened to them.**
    - **Grandmother**: They stayed down there. That’s enough for now. The story isn’t finished with them.

#### `reader:a31-03-tree` · [main](main.md#a31-03-tree)

_By the side of the road there is a tree you didn’t notice before. It is heavy with round green gourds._  
_In the atlas’s margin there is a little brown drawing of it, every gourd the same._  
**Granddaughter**: Grandmother. Why can’t anyone go near it?  
**Grandmother**: The lords said nobody was to touch that tree. So of course somebody did.  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): a dried gourd shell is hard and waterproof, so it makes a bowl or cup  
> **Grandmother**: A gourd is a fruit with a thick skin. When it dries out, the skin turns hard and light, and the inside can be scooped away.  
> **Grandmother**: People in Guatemala still make bowls and cups from dried gourds. They are strong, light, and they hold water.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a dried gourd has a hard, waterproof shell, so it makes a bowl or cup._

**Grandmother**: Eat your tortilla.  
_Your Nubbin looks up into the branches for a long time. Then it pats the trunk once, the way it pats you when you’re tired._  

**Choices**

- **Look for the gourd that’s different.**
    - You look, and every gourd is the same. That’s the point, you think.
- **Leave the tree be.**
    - You step back onto the road. Some things you don’t need to find.

#### `reader:a31-03-complete` · [main](main.md#a31-03-complete)

_The page turns a little, in a wind from nowhere. The road goes on being empty._  
**Grandmother**: At home, Xmucane made supper for the two grandsons she still had. Three tortillas each, because they were growing.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): multiplication as equal groups  
> _Xmucane and her two grandsons eat three tortillas each. How many tortillas is that altogether?_  
> - ✅ 9 → Yes. Three people, three tortillas each: 3 x 3 = 9.
> - 6 → That counts only two people. Xmucane eats too: 3 people x 3 tortillas = 9.
> - 3 → That is one person’s share. Three people x 3 tortillas = 9.
> _For grown-ups: Solved an equal-groups word problem: 3 people with 3 tortillas each._

**Grandmother**: Nobody came up the road. They waited.  
**Granddaughter**: And then?  
**Grandmother**: And then a girl went to look at a tree she wasn’t allowed to look at. That’s how most good stories start.  

## A31-4. The Calabash Tree

- `a31-04-s1`: Watch the girl who comes to the forbidden tree. · _Hide behind the rock near the tree and watch._
- `a31-04-s2`: Go with the owls and Xquic into the forest. · _Walk behind the owls through the red-barked trees._
- `a31-04-s3`: Help press the red sap into a heart shape. · _Hold the bowl under the cut bark while the sap runs, then help shape it._

#### `reader:a31-04-xquic` · [main](main.md#a31-04-xquic)

_Down in Xibalba it is never quite night and never quite day. A girl comes along the road on her own, looking over her shoulder._  
**Grandmother**: Xquic. Daughter of one of the lords. She’d heard about the tree, and she was not good at being told no.  
**Xquic**: Everybody says don’t go near it. Nobody says why. If they said why, I might not go.  
_She stops under the branches. You and your Nubbin are behind a rock, which is more or less hiding._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): a character’s motive  
> _Xquic goes to the forbidden tree even though she has been told not to. Why does she go?_  
> - ✅ She is curious, and nobody gave her a reason. → Yes: she says herself that if they said why, she might not go. Her motive is curiosity, and being left in the dark.
> - She is hungry for gourds. → She never says that. Look at what she says about “why”: she is curious, not hungry.
> - She wants to be caught. → Nothing in the story says so. Her words show a curious girl who wants a reason.
> _For grown-ups: Used a character’s own words to work out her motive._

**The Gourd**: They’re only round things in a tree. You don’t want them. Hold out your hand.  
_The atlas’s margin has a brown line about it: she held out her hand, and something strange happened in her palm._  
**Xquic**: It’s gone. There’s nothing in my hand.  
**The Gourd**: Nothing you can see. Go home. Nothing will hurt you. You’ll understand later.  

**Choices**

- **Stay hidden.**
    - You stay behind the rock. Your Nubbin, who thinks hiding is a game, keeps popping up to see if it’s won.
- **Step out and walk her to the road.**
    - **Xquic**: Who are you? No, don’t tell me. I’ve had enough surprises for one day.

#### `reader:a31-04-owls` · [main](main.md#a31-04-owls)

_Some months go by in the margin. When the page settles, Xquic stands in her father’s hall, and everybody can see she is expecting a baby._  
**Grandmother**: Her father was furious. He told his four owls to take her out to the forest and bring back her heart in a bowl.  
**Grandmother**: The owls didn’t like it at all.  
_You walk behind them, through trees with red bark. The owls keep stopping and looking at each other._  
**Owls**: We don’t want to do this. We’ve never wanted to do anything we were told.  
**Xquic**: Then don’t. My heart is mine. It doesn’t belong to them.  
**Owls**: But he’ll want to see something in the bowl.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): refusing to do wrong and finding another way  
> _The owls were ordered to do something wrong. What is the wisest thing they can do?_  
> - ✅ Refuse the wrong thing, and find another way to help. → Yes: refusing is brave, and finding another way is wise. The owls do both.
> - Do it anyway, because they were ordered to. → An order does not make a wrong thing right. The owls say so in their own way.
> - Run away and leave Xquic. → That would keep the owls safe, but it would leave Xquic alone. They choose to help her.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about refusing to do wrong and finding another way to help._

**Xquic**: Then let’s give him something. Look at that tree.  

**Choices**

- **Go and look at the tree.**
    - The bark is cut in one place. Where it’s cut, a thick red sap is running, slow as honey.
- **Tell the owls they can be brave.**
    - **Owls**: Can we? Nobody’s ever asked us to.
    - All four owls stand up a little taller.

#### `reader:a31-04-sap` · [main](main.md#a31-04-sap)

**Xquic**: Help me. Catch it in the bowl. Then we shape it.  
_You hold the bowl. Your Nubbin, who has never been asked to help with anything this important, holds the edge of it as well._  
_Xquic presses the sap into a round, lumpy shape with her thumbs, and the owls tell her where it needs more lump._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-1, notice): sap carries water and food through a tree; bark protects it  
> _Inside a tree, tubes carry water up from the roots and sugary sap down from the leaves. The bark is the tree’s skin._  
> _When bark is cut, sap seeps out and slowly thickens over the wound, like a plaster. Sap can be white like rubber, clear like maple, or red._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that sap carries water and food through a tree, and that bark and sap protect it._

**Owls**: More on that side. Hearts are not neat.  
_When it’s done, it shines dark red in the bowl. It’s only sap, from a tree, and it even smells a little of woodsmoke._  
**Xquic**: Now go. Tell him you did it. Tell him I’m gone.  
**Owls**: Where will you go?  
**Xquic**: Up. To the world. There’s a grandmother up there who doesn’t know she has me yet.  

**Choices**

- **Press the last of it smooth.**
    - You smooth the top with your thumb. The owls say it is too neat now, and rough it up again.
- **Let your Nubbin leave a pawprint in it.**
    - A tiny paw-shaped dent, right on top, and the owls decide that’s fine. Hearts have marks on them.

#### `reader:a31-04-complete` · [main](main.md#a31-04-complete)

_The owls carry the bowl back to Xibalba. The lords put the sap heart on the fire, and the smell fills the hall, and they all lean in._  
**Grandmother**: And while they were sniffing, the owls flew off and showed Xquic the way up. The lords were fooled by a smell.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): multiplication as equal groups  
> _Four owls fly away, and each owl has two wings. How many wings is that altogether?_  
> - ✅ 8 → Yes. Four groups of two: 4 x 2 = 8 wings.
> - 6 → Count in twos: 2, 4, 6, 8. There are four owls, so that is eight wings.
> - 4 → That is the number of owls. Each owl has two wings: 4 x 2 = 8.
> _For grown-ups: Used equal groups to multiply 4 x 2._

**Granddaughter**: By owls!  
**Grandmother**: By owls. Never trust a lord who can’t tell a heart from a tree.  
_Your Nubbin sniffs its paw for a long time afterwards. It still smells a bit of the tree._  

### Glossary

- **forbidden**: Not allowed.

## A31-5. Xmucane’s Test

- `a31-05-s1`: Walk Xquic up to Xmucane’s house. · _Go up the hill to the house with the garden and the fire._
- `a31-05-s2`: Go down to the garden with one plant in it. · _Sit down by the corn plant with Xquic and wait._
- `a31-05-s3`: Carry the net of corn up to Xmucane. · _Take one end of the net; Xquic takes the other. Go slowly on the hill._

#### `reader:a31-05-door` · [main](main.md#a31-05-door)

_The page turns to a little thatch house on a hillside, with a garden and a fire. It looks a lot like the kitchen you came in by._  
_An old woman stands in the doorway with two older boys behind her: Xmucane, and Hun Hunahpu’s first sons._  
**Xquic**: Grandmother. I’m your daughter-in-law. I’m carrying your son’s children.  
**Xmucane**: My son is gone, down in Xibalba. Go away. You’re a liar, or a trick, or both.  
**Xmucane**: Then here is a test. My grandsons are hungry. Bring me a whole net of corn from the garden down the hill, and I’ll believe you.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): seeing a situation from two points of view  
> _Xmucane sets Xquic an impossible test: is that fair? Why might Xmucane do it, and how might Xquic feel?_  
> - It is unfair. Xquic is a stranger who needs help. → That’s a fair answer: Xquic is alone and frightened, and a test makes it harder. Xmucane would say she is only protecting her family.
> - It is understandable. Xmucane has lost her son and is afraid. → That’s a fair answer: grief makes people guard what is left. Xquic would say that being doubted still hurts.
> - Both are true at once. → That’s a thoughtful answer. Xmucane is afraid, and Xquic is hurt, and neither feeling cancels the other.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about two sides of a hard moment: a grandmother’s fear and a stranger’s hurt._

_You look down the hill. In the garden there is one corn plant: one._  

**Choices**

- **Offer to carry the net.**
    - **Xquic**: Thank you. I think the net is the easy part.
- **Tell Xmucane she might be telling the truth.**
    - **Xmucane**: Might. Everybody might. Go and get the corn.

#### `reader:a31-05-garden` · [main](main.md#a31-05-garden)

_One plant, with one ear on it. Xquic stands in front of it with the empty net in her hands._  
**Xquic**: If I pick this, there’s nothing. If I don’t, there’s nothing.  
_She sits down on the earth. Your Nubbin sits next to her, and so, after a moment, do you._  
_Then she says the names of the ones who look after food, softly, as if she is asking friends for a favour._  
_She draws the silk from the one ear, gently, and lays it in the net. And the net is full._  
**Grandmother**: Full, from one ear. That’s the part you hold on to.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS1-1, notice): the life cycle of corn: seed, plant, ear, seeds  
> **Grandmother**: A corn plant starts as a kernel. It sprouts, grows tall, makes silk and pollen, and the silk catches the pollen.  
> **Grandmother**: Every strand of silk leads to one kernel. A single ear grows several hundred kernels, and each one is a seed that could start the whole cycle again.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the corn life cycle: kernel, seedling, plant, silk and pollen, ear, and new kernels._

_Your Nubbin climbs into the net to check. It is corn all the way down._  

**Choices**

- **Touch the ear left on the plant.**
    - It’s still there. Whatever happened, the plant wasn’t robbed.
- **Say thank you to nobody in particular.**
    - **Xquic**: Yes. That. Thank you.

#### `reader:a31-05-carry` · [main](main.md#a31-05-carry)

_The net is heavier than the ball, heavier than the bowl, heavier than anything you’ve ever carried in a book._  
_You take one end and Xquic takes the other. Your Nubbin takes the middle, which mostly means it rides in it._  
_Halfway up, the older brothers come out to watch. They don’t help._  
**Xquic**: Artists, those two. They play the flute, and paint, and sing. They never carry anything.  
_You put the net down in front of Xmucane. She looks at it, and goes on looking for a long time._
