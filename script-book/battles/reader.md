# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Battles: the Grove Circuit, Play-fight Day and the story battles

## Variant scenes

Versions of the variants with no parent scene in this storyline.

#### `reader:bc-playday-open` · [main](main.md#bc-playday-open)

_Fern has painted a sign on the green: PLAY-FIGHT DAY. BRING A NUBBIN. BRING A SNACK._  
**Fern**: Nobody gets hurt. Nobody gets cross. A tired Nubbin has a nap. That’s the whole rulebook.  
**Clover**: I wrote it. It’s one page. Byte wanted a second page. We said no.  
_Everybody borrows a practice Nubbin today. Yours is a Swim type. Clover’s is a Fly type._  
_Hearts float over each one: seven over yours, five over Clover’s. When the hearts run out, it’s nap time._  
_Along the top runs the turn strip, a row of tiny faces showing who goes next._  
**Clover**: My flyer is quick, so it gets two turns for every one of yours. Watch the strip.  
_Fly, Fly, Swim. Fly, Fly, Swim. Fly, Fly…_  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.OA.C.5, ask): continue a repeating pattern and find a later term by its rule  
> **Clover**: The strip repeats every three turns: Fly, Fly, Swim.  
> **Clover**: Whose is the ninth turn?  
> - ✅ Yours: the Swim one → Right. Every third turn is Swim: the 3rd, the 6th, the 9th. Nine is a multiple of three.
> - Clover’s flyer → Fair guess: it does get most of them. But count in threes: turns 3, 6 and 9 are Swim. So the ninth turn is yours. The rule tells you without counting every one.
> _For grown-ups: Used the rule of a repeating turn pattern (Fly, Fly, Swim) to work out who has the ninth turn._

**Fern**: Hers is small but clever. Like Clover. Also like Clover, it never stops moving.  

#### `reader:bc-playday-bout` · [main](main.md#bc-playday-bout)

**Clover**: Turns go round. Yours, then mine, in the order on the strip. Nobody pushes in.  
_Clover’s Fly type hovers over yours, looking extremely pleased with itself._  
**Fern**: Swim soaks Fly! Wet wings are slow wings. Go on, try it.  
_Splash. Clover’s flyer lands with a small, damp bump and looks at its feet._  
**Clover**: That’s cause and effect. The Swim move was the cause. A soggy flyer is the effect.  
**Clover**: The wheel goes round: Fly goes over Run, Run is too quick for Power, Power makes a bigger wave than Swim.  
_Clover’s flyer yawns and naps under a leaf. Her next Nubbin trots in. It’s a Run type._  
_Fern’s spare Nubbins wait on the bench: a Fly, a Power and a Swim. The Power one is eating the bench._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-5-ETS1-2, ask): cause and effect: use a rule to choose the solution that works best  
> **Clover**: My Run type is next. Which of Fern’s spares would work best against it?  
> - ✅ A Fly type → Yes. Fly goes over Run, so the Run type can’t catch it. Cause, then effect.
> - A Power type → Power is strong, so it’s a sensible guess. But Run is too quick for Power. The Run type would dodge every swing. A Fly type goes over Run instead.
> - A Swim type → Swim and Run are even: neither one beats the other, so it’s a fair fight. Fly would do better. Fly goes over Run.
> _For grown-ups: Used the type wheel’s rule (Fly goes over Run) to predict which choice would work best._


#### `reader:bc-playday-hug` · [main](main.md#bc-playday-hug)

_The bout is over. Both Nubbins toddle to the middle and hug, the way Clover said they would._  
**Clover**: That’s how every bout ends here. A hug. Then a snack.  
**Fern**: Then a nap. Then, if I’m honest, another snack.  
_Your Nubbin breaks its plum in half and gives the bigger half to the one it beat. Nobody told it to._  
**Clover**: Winning is nice. Sharing is nicer. Ask anybody who’s been given half a plum.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): think about why a winner might share with the one they beat  
> **Clover**: Why do you think your Nubbin shared with the one it beat?  
> - So they can still be friends → I think so too. A play-fight is a game. Friends are for after the game, and before the next one.
> - Because the other one looked sad → That’s kind noticing. Losing can feel a bit sore, and a share says “you still matter”.
> - It just likes plums less than hugs → Ha. Could be! Some Nubbins would swap any plum for a good hug. Sharing was the hug in plum form.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why sharing after a game helps friendships, with no single right answer._

_The two of them sit side by side, sticky and pleased, watching the next bout._
