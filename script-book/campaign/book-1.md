# Hazel's Grove: The campaign, Book 1: The Letter

## Chapter 1: The Letter

Goal: Hatch your first egg and pet the Nubbin that comes out. (`firstHatch`) · Hint: The eggs are in the long grass. Hold one and shake it to hurry it along. Tap a Nubbin and choose Pet.

#### <a id="c1-letter"></a>Opening `c1-letter`

_Late spring. The road runs out a little before the Grove does._  
_The last of it is grass, and then a gate._  
_The gate is stuck. It always was._  
_You lift and push, the way she taught you._  
_It gives with the same small groan as when you needed both hands. It sounds as if it has missed the argument._  
_The Grove is overgrown and quiet. The pond has gone to reeds at one end._  
_Nobody has cut the grass since she left._  
_On the signpost, under a stone, a letter in your grandmother’s hand._  
_Your name is on the front. The writing leans more than you remember, as if every word were walking uphill._  
_You don’t open it. Not yet._  
_You go to the cottage first._  
_The door is on the latch. The kettle is on the stove, cold._  
_Beside it, one cup, washed and turned upside down._  
_She always left one out like that. For whoever came next._  
_The kitchen smells of apples and cold ashes and, under that, of her: wet wool and peppermint._  
_Her coat is not on its hook. Her boots are not by the door._  
_On the windowsill, a row of eggshells, rinsed and dry, each one dated in pencil in the same leaning hand._  
_You sit down on the step, where she used to sit, and open the letter._  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: “If you’re reading this, I’ve gone up the hill and I’m not coming down for a while. Don’t be dramatic about it.”  
_Up the hill is where the flowers are. Everyone in the valley knows what that means._  
_…_  
_“Don’t be dramatic about it.” It is so exactly her that, for a while, you can’t read the next bit._  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: “There are two eggs in the long grass. They were waiting for someone with warm hands. That’s you.”  
_You look at your hands. They are just hands._  
**Gran Hazel**: “Rules. One: feed them before you feed yourself. Two: never, ever throw one. Three: when you don’t know what to do, sit down next to them and wait.”  
_She used to make you say them back to her, at the gate, before you were allowed in._  
**Gran Hazel**: “Moss will be watching from the hill. He’s older than the trees and twice as stubborn. He’ll come down when he decides you’re worth it.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: “Love, Gran. P.S. The gate sticks. Lift and push.”  
_You laugh. It comes out the wrong shape._  
_Something small and grey watches from the top of the western hill, very still, and does not come down._  
**Fern** _(happy)_: Hello? Oh! You’re Hazel’s. You’ve got her chin. I’m Fern, from next door. I’ve been keeping the trees alive, badly.  
_She sees the letter in your hand, and her face changes._  
**You**: Is she…?  
**Fern** _(soft)_: No. Not up there. Not the way people mean.  
**Fern** _(soft)_: I go and look at the flowers. Every week. There isn’t a new one. I’d know. I know all of them.  
**Fern**: She went off at the end of the winter. Big coat, bag of fruit, the walking stick she pretends she doesn’t need. Said she had some feeding to do.  
**Fern**: She’s done it before. Went off a whole winter once. Came back with a limp and a Nubbin nobody else could catch.  
**Fern**: She told me you’d come. Didn’t say when. I put the stone on the letter so it wouldn’t blow away. That was my job.  
**Fern** _(happy)_: I’m very proud of the stone, actually.  
_From the long grass, by the farthest of the Grove’s four lantern posts, something hums. Low, like a kettle thinking about it._  
**Fern**: Hear that? They’ve been at it for a week. I’ll be around if you need anything. Mostly I need things from you, honestly.  

**Choices**

- **It’s a very good stone.**
    - **Fern**: Thank you. Nobody ever mentions the stone.
    - **Fern**: It’s from the stream. I tried four. This one had the right sort of face.
    - You go to find the eggs. Behind you, Fern pats the stone once, the way you’d pat a dog that has done well.
- **What do you need from me?**
    - **Fern**: Oh, loads. A ladder. Someone to hold the ladder. Someone to tell me the pear tree isn’t my fault.
    - **Fern**: The pear tree is a bit my fault.
    - You go to find the eggs. Fern goes to look at the pear tree, guiltily, from a distance.
- **Say nothing. Go to the eggs.**
    - **Fern**: Straight to work, without a word. Oh, you’re definitely hers.
    - The long grass closes round your knees, warm and loud with crickets, and the humming gets nearer.

#### <a id="c1-close"></a>Closing `c1-close`

_It is warm. That is the first thing._  
_Small and damp and warmer than anything has a right to be._  
_It smells of rain and warm bread, the way new ones do. Bits of shell are stuck to its head like a hat._  
_It looks at your hand for a long time before it leans into it. Then it leans into it entirely._  
_You do not move for a while. Neither does it._  
**Sparks** _(shock)_: HEY. New Keeper. Don’t move. There’s a lantern post behind you and I’ve nearly fixed it.  
_There is a crackle, a small blue flash, and the smell of burnt hair._  
**Sparks** _(happy)_: Fixed it. Mostly. The hair grows back, before you ask. I’m Sparks. I run the fast ones. Yours is cute. Is it fast? It looks fast.  
**Fern**: It is three minutes old, Sparks.  
**Sparks**: Potential, Fern. I’m talking about potential.  
_Your Nubbin sneezes. A tiny blue spark jumps off the end of its nose and fizzles out in the grass._  
**Sparks** _(happy)_: Did you SEE that? It likes me. It’s caught it off me.  
**Fern**: That isn’t how anything works.  
_Your Nubbin looks up at you, then at Sparks, then at you again, and leans a little closer to your leg._  
**Sparks**: Right. Dusk. Watch this. Hazel had me do all four posts last winter, before she went… wherever she went.  
_One by one, the lanterns come up: the pond, the stream, the old wall._  
_Gold on the water. Gold in the grass._  
_The fourth, out by the long grass, catches, gutters, and settles into a thin, uncertain flicker._  
**Sparks** _(angry)_: Ugh. That one. New wick, new glass, new everything. Three times.  
**Fern**: Has it ever worked?  
**Sparks** _(think)_: Never has, not properly. Not since before my time.  
_The air by the fourth post is cooler than it ought to be on a spring night. The long grass, probably._  
**Sparks**: Hazel said to leave it be. She’d stand out there some evenings, with it going like that. Just stand.  
**Fern** _(soft)_: Like she was waiting for someone to knock.  
_Nobody says anything for a moment. The fourth lantern flickers._  
_On the hill, the grey shape has moved one step down._

## Chapter 2: Shells and Promises

Goal: Collect fruit from the trees and feed a Nubbin by hand, twice. (`feedTwice`) · Hint: Tap a tree to shake it. Tap fruit to pick it up. Tap a Nubbin and choose Feed.

#### <a id="c2-open"></a>Opening `c2-open`

_Morning. Mist on the pond._  
_Your Nubbin is asleep on your foot, which is a problem, because you need the foot._  
_A woman comes up the lane smelling of salt and weed, with a bucket that slops at every step and rolled sleeves gone stiff with brine._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Morning. Marina. I keep the tide pools past the ridge. Hazel used to trade me fruit for swimming lessons. For the Nubbins, not for her.  
**Marina**: She could swim fine. She said water was for fish and fools, and she was neither.  
**Marina** _(think)_: You’ve not eaten. Don’t argue, you’ve got the look. What’s rule one?  
**You**: Feed them before you feed yourself.  
**Marina**: That’s the one. Then, for pity’s sake, feed yourself. She never did that bit either.  
**Marina**: The trees drop fruit on their own, but a shake hurries them. A Nubbin fed by your hand remembers the hand. That matters more than people think.  
**Sparks** _(happy)_: Are we doing hand-feeding? I’m GREAT at hand-feeding. Show them, Marina.  
**Marina**: You show them. You’ve got more fingers left than anyone would expect.  
**Sparks**: Flat palm. Fruit in the middle. Fingers flat. FLAT. They don’t know fingers aren’t fruit, and honestly, from their side, fair.  
_Your Nubbin looks at the fruit, then your hand, then your face, as if checking all three are on the same side._  
**Marina**: Don’t push it at them. Hold still. Let them come the last bit. The last bit is theirs.  
**Sparks**: That’s the bit I’m bad at. I go “eat it, eat it, EAT IT”.  
**Marina**: We know.  
_Your Nubbin comes the last bit. It takes the plum, very politely._  
_Then it takes Sparks’s thumb, very politely, and holds on._  
**Sparks** _(shock)_: FLAT. I said FLAT. I didn’t do it FLAT.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Rule four. Don’t be Sparks.  
_The plum is warm from the tree and splits in your fingers. Your Nubbin eats your half too, and looks at you as if you offered._  
_Marina looks at the hill a moment, and then at the cottage, with its one cup turned upside down._  
**Marina** _(soft)_: She’ll be back. She owes me a bag of plums. Hazel doesn’t leave debts.  
**Marina** _(think)_: Oh, and if a man in a grey coat comes by talking about “investment”, don’t sell him anything. Not fruit. Not eggs. Nothing.  
**Sparks**: Corvin. Runs the Circuit, up the valley. His lot win everything. They’re so fast. They’re so quiet.  
**Sparks** _(think)_: I don’t like quiet ones. I like the ones that yell.  
_Marina looks up the valley the way you would look at weather coming._

## Chapter 3: The One Who Hides

Goal: Win a little of Sable’s trust (20). (`sableTrust20`) · Hint: Sable runs when you come close. Leave fruit nearby, be patient, pet her when she lets you. Never throw her.

#### <a id="c3-open"></a>Opening `c3-open`

## Chapter 4: Moss Remembers

Goal: Pet Moss on the hill and light all four lanterns. (`lanternsAndMoss`) · Hint: Tap a lantern post to light it. Moss sits on the western hill. The fourth post only flickers, but it counts.
