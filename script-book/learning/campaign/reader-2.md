> - She’s joking. She’ll be fine. → She makes jokes, because she is still Gran. But this part is true, and she wants you to know it. Clear words mean nobody gets a shock later, and everyone can say goodbye.
> _For grown-ups: Your child thought about why honest, plain words about a serious illness can be kind, and that sad or scared feelings are allowed._

#### `reader:c11-moss-here` · [main](../../campaign/book-3.md#c11-moss-here)

_Moss has come down from the hill. He sits by Gran Hazel’s boot and looks up at her._  
**Gran Hazel**: Hello, old thing.

**Gran Hazel**: I’m always late. You’re thinner.

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): multiple-meaning words: even  
> _“We’re even,” says Moss. Some words have more than one meaning. What does even mean here?_  
> - ✅ Equal. Neither of them is ahead. → Yes. She’s late, he’s thinner: one each. Nobody owes anybody. That is the joke.
> - A number that splits into two equal groups. → That is a real meaning of even, like 2, 4 or 40! But Moss means equal here. She’s late, he’s thinner: one each. Neither of them is ahead.
> - Flat and smooth. → That is a meaning too, like an even floor. But Moss means equal here. She’s late, he’s thinner: one each. Neither of them is ahead.
> _For grown-ups: Your child worked out which meaning of a multiple-meaning word (even) fits the sentence._

_She laughs, and has to stop, and holds her side, and laughs anyway._  
_Then she puts her hand on his head, the way she did every evening for forty years. He shuts his eyes._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.OA.C.3, ask): odd and even numbers  
> _Every evening for forty years. Is 40 an odd number or an even number?_  
> - ✅ Even → Yes. 40 splits into two equal groups of 20, with none left over. Any number ending in 0 is even.
> - Odd → Let’s check. Split 40 into two equal groups: 20 and 20, with none left over. So 40 is even. Any number ending in 0 is even.
> _For grown-ups: Your child decided whether a number is odd or even by splitting it into two equal groups._

### Glossary

- **poorly**: Ill. A British word for being unwell.

- **fuss**: A lot of attention or worry about something.
- **habit**: Something you do so often that you do it without thinking.
- **body language**: What the way you sit, stand, move or turn your face says about how you feel, without any words.
- **figurative language**: Words used in a special way, not exactly true, to help you picture or feel something.
- **grub**: A young insect, like a baby beetle, that looks like a small fat worm.
- **dramatic**: Making a big, showy fuss, like in a play.
- **latch**: A small bar or catch that keeps a gate or door shut.
- **condensation**: Tiny drops of water that form when water in the air cools down.
- **collapsed**: Fallen down or caved in.
- **evidence**: Something you can show that proves a thing is true.
- **dreadful**: Very bad. Here it means the Keepers were very upset and fussy.
- **unhurried**: Slow and calm, not in a rush.
- **simile**: A comparison that uses “as” or “like”, such as “as small as buttons”.
- **perspective**: The way things look smaller the further away they are, and how artists draw that.
- **stagger**: To walk unsteadily, as if you might fall.
- **dusk**: The time just after the Sun sets, when the sky is getting dark.

- **apprentice**: Someone who learns a job by working beside an expert.
- **brisk**: Quick, lively and no-nonsense.
- **life cycle**: The stages a living thing goes through, from start to grown-up and on to new young.
- **obviously**: In a way that is so clear anyone can see it.
- **method**: A way of doing something, step by step.
- **coincidence**: When two things happen together just by chance, with no reason linking them.
- **statistically**: Going by the numbers and how likely things are.
- **by heart**: Knowing something so well you can say it without looking.
- **newcomer**: Someone who has only just arrived.
- **flickers**: Shines unsteadily, getting brighter and dimmer.

- **oral history**: History kept by telling it out loud, from one person to the next.
- **habitat**: The place where an animal or plant lives and finds what it needs.
- **stubborn**: Not giving up or changing your mind easily.
- **lighthouse**: A tower with a bright light that helps ships find their way at night.
- **hopeful**: Feeling that something good might still happen.
- **collector**: Someone who gathers things and keeps them.
- **friction**: A force made when two surfaces rub together. It slows or stops movement.

- **amends**: Something good you do to help put right a wrong.

- **tilted**: Leaning to one side, not straight up.
- **routine**: Something you do the same way, at the same time, again and again.

- **tradition**: Something a family or community does again and again, often every year.
