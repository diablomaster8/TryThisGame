# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Fun quests: Avatar: The Last Airbender (Q46–Q65)

## Q46. Not the Cabbages!

- `q46-s1`: Find all 8 cabbages that rolled across the meadow, and help put the cart’s wheel back on. · _Round things roll downhill. Look along the stream bank and under the bushes._
- `q46-s2`: Shake the big fruit tree until nothing else falls, then mend the cart with planks and rope. · _Tap the tree until it’s bare. The planks and rope are in the shed by the gate._
- `q46-s3`: Build the merchant a cart that can’t tip over. · _You need 4 planks, 2 wheels and a sack of stones. Keep it wide, and keep the heavy stones low. Tuk’s diagram is in your journal._

#### `reader:q46-crash` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q46-crash)

_A wooden cart creaks up the main path, piled high with cabbages. A man in a straw hat pushes it very, very carefully._  
**The cabbage merchant**: Afternoon! Fine cabbages, crisp cabbages, cabbages for every occasion. Weddings. Birthdays. Snacks.  
**The cabbage merchant**: I’ve pushed this cart all over the world, and I have a very good feeling about this Grove.  
_Behind you, a Nubbin in full racing stride comes round the bend. It is not looking where it’s going. It never is._  
_Thud. Wobble. A long, slow tip. Cabbages roll in every direction._  
**The cabbage merchant**: Ohhh, the cabbages! Somebody catch the cabbages!  
_Your Nubbin sits up in the wreck, wearing a cabbage leaf like a hat. It looks very pleased with itself._  
**The cabbage merchant**: The wheel’s come off. I had eight cabbages, and three have rolled into the stream!  
**The cabbage merchant**: The rest are scattered all over the meadow.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 1.OA.A.1, ask): subtract within 20 to solve a word problem  
> **The cabbage merchant**: Eight cabbages, and three are in the stream. So how many are out in the meadow?  
> - ✅ 5 → Five! Eight take away three is five. You’re quicker than my cart.
> - 11 → Eleven would mean adding. But three cabbages went away into the stream, so we take them away. 8 − 3 = 5. Five cabbages in the meadow.
> - 3 → Three is how many are in the stream. Take those three away from eight. 8 − 3 = 5. Five cabbages are in the meadow.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a take-away word problem: 8 cabbages, 3 in the stream, so 5 in the meadow._


**Choices**

- **Apologise for your Nubbin**
    - **You**: Sorry. My Nubbin is still learning about corners.
    - **The cabbage merchant**: Corners. Yes. Lots of people are still learning about corners.
- **Offer to buy the dented ones**
    - **The cabbage merchant**: Dented cabbages are still cabbages! That is my whole philosophy.
- **Point out that this is the race track**
    - **You**: To be fair, this is the race track.
    - **The cabbage merchant**: To be fair, I didn’t see a sign.
    - There is a sign. It is very large. He is standing on it.

#### `reader:q46-crash-done` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q46-crash-done)

_You collect the cabbages one by one. The last one has rolled all the way down to the stream bank._  
**The cabbage merchant**: Why do they always roll downhill? Not once has a cabbage rolled uphill to find me.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): gravity, and balanced and unbalanced forces  
> _Every cabbage is pulled towards the ground by a force called gravity._  
> _On flat ground, the ground pushes back up just as hard. The forces are balanced, so the cabbage stays put._  
> _On a slope, the pushes don’t balance any more. Gravity wins, and a round cabbage rolls downhill._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that gravity pulls things down, and that unbalanced forces make a cabbage roll down a slope._

_You slot the wheel back onto the axle. The cart stands up straight, and so does the merchant._  
**The cabbage merchant**: All eight! Only a little bit muddy.  
_Your Nubbin offers him the cabbage-leaf hat. He accepts it with great dignity._  
**The cabbage merchant**: You have a gift, friend. A cabbage gift. The rarest kind.  

#### `reader:q46-fruit` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q46-fruit)

_The merchant parks his cart under the big fruit tree, a long way from the race track._  
**The cabbage merchant**: Shade, peace and quiet. Nothing can go wrong in the shade.  
_A breeze stirs the branches. High above the cart, something round and heavy wobbles on its stalk._  
**The cabbage merchant**: Is that tree looking at me?  
_The fruit drops. It is enormous. It lands right on the cart, which folds neatly in half._  
**The cabbage merchant**: …I’m not going to wail. I’m saving my best wail for something worse.  
_He picks the fruit out of the wreck, sniffs it, and gives it to your Nubbin, who eats it at once._  
**The cabbage merchant**: Right. Planks, rope, and someone to shake that tree bare before I park under it again.  

> **Lesson** (science, K-2-ETS1-1, ask): define the real problem before solving it  
> _Good builders start by asking: what exactly went wrong?_  
> _Why is shaking the tree bare a clever first step?_  
> - ✅ So any loose fruit falls now, and not on the mended cart later. → Exactly. The problem isn’t the shade. It’s heavy fruit hanging right over my head.
> - Because the tree is too noisy. → The tree is quite quiet, really. The real problem is the heavy fruit hanging above the cart. Shake it all down now, and nothing can fall on the cart later.
> - So your Nubbin gets a snack. → A lovely bonus! But the main reason is that heavy fruit was hanging right over the cart. Shake it down now, and nothing else can fall on it later.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out the real problem (fruit hanging above the cart) before choosing a fix._


#### `reader:q46-fruit-done` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q46-fruit-done)

**The cabbage merchant**: Good as new. Better than new. New had a squeak.  
_He ties the last knot in the rope and gives the cart a proud pat._  
**The cabbage merchant**: From now on I park in the middle of the meadow. Nothing falls on you in the middle of a meadow.  
_Somewhere very high above the meadow, a bird hears this, and thinks about it._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ask): spot foreshadowing and predict what might happen next  
> _Writers sometimes drop a hint about what might happen later. That hint is called foreshadowing._  
> _Why do you think the writer tells us about the bird?_  
> - ✅ It’s a hint that something might fall from the sky after all. → That’s my guess too. The merchant says nothing can fall on him… and the writer shows us a bird.
> - No reason. It’s just a bird. → Could be! But writers don’t often mention a bird for no reason, just after someone says nothing can fall on them. It’s a hint: the middle of the meadow may not be as safe as he thinks.
> - The bird wants to buy a cabbage. → Ha! Maybe. But it’s more likely a hint that the middle of the meadow isn’t as safe as he thinks.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed foreshadowing: a small detail the writer uses to hint at what might happen next._

_Your Nubbins share the fallen fruit and watch him polish a cabbage on his sleeve._  
**The cabbage merchant**: Mark my words. My luck has turned.  

#### `reader:q46-glider` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q46-glider)

_Overhead, a shadow swoops in a wide, happy circle. A boy in orange and yellow is riding the wind on a wooden glider._  
**Pemba**: Hi! Hello! Nice Grove! Is that a vegetable stall? I love vegetables!  
_He leans to wave. The glider dips. You can see exactly where this is going, and so can the merchant._  
**The cabbage merchant**: No, no, no, no—  
_Pemba lands, rolls, and bounces off the cart. The cart does not bounce. It bursts into cabbages._  
**The cabbage merchant**: The cabbages! Again! Three times in one afternoon!  
**Pemba**: Oh no. That was me, wasn’t it? I’m Pemba. I’m really sorry. Can I help? I’m good at helping.  
_A boy with a boomerang on his back jogs up, out of breath, and looks at the mess._  
**Tuk**: Let me guess. You saw a cart and thought, “That looks soft.”  
**The cabbage merchant**: What I need is a cart that cannot tip. Not for Nubbins, not for fruit, not for flying boys.  
**Tuk**: Now that’s a problem I can plan for. I’ve got diagrams. I’ve always got diagrams.  
**The cabbage merchant**: Thirty-one carts I’ve lost. Every one taught me something. The first taught me wheels. The ninth taught me rope.  
**Tuk**: Here’s the secret. Things tip over when their weight leans out past the edge of whatever they stand on.  
**Tuk**: We want a stable cart. A steady one. One that hardly leans at all, whatever hits it.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-5-ETS1-2, ask): compare designs: a wide, low base is more stable than a tall, narrow one  
> **Tuk**: So, which cart is harder to push over?  
> - A tall, narrow cart with the heavy stones on top → Ha! That one falls over if a butterfly sneezes. The weight is high up and the edges are close together, so it leans past them in no time. Wide and low is the steady one.
> - ✅ A low, wide cart with the heavy stones at the bottom → Exactly. Wide means the edges are far apart. Heavy stones down low mean the weight has a long, long way to lean before it gets past an edge.
> - They’re both just as easy to tip → Try it with two piles of books: one tall and thin, one low and wide. Give them each a little push. The tall one topples. The low, wide one just sits there. Wide and low is steadier.
> _For grown-ups: Compared two designs and explained why a wide, low base with weight at the bottom is harder to tip._


**Choices**

- **Make it wide**
    - **Tuk**: Wide. Good. Like a duck sitting down. Ducks never tip over.
- **Make it low**
    - **Tuk**: Low wheels, heavy bottom. You can’t knock over something that’s already nearly lying down.
- **Give it a warning bell**
    - **The cabbage merchant**: A bell! So they hear me coming!
    - **Tuk**: Fine. A bell. But it’s also going to be wide and low, because I have standards.

#### `reader:q46-glider-done` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q46-glider-done)

_Tuk unrolls his diagram on a flat rock. It has arrows, measurements, and one small drawing of a duck._  
**Tuk**: The cart bed is 2 metres long and 1 metre wide. I want a rope all the way round the edge, so no cabbage falls off.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.D.8, ask): find the perimeter of a rectangle  
> **Tuk**: So how much rope do I need to go all the way round? Long side, short side, long side, short side.  
> - ✅ 6 metres → Yes! 2 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 6. The distance all the way round is called the perimeter.
> - 3 metres → That only gets you along one long side and one short side. The rope has to go all the way round. 2 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 6 metres. That’s the perimeter.
> - 2 metres → That’s only one long side! Add all four sides: 2 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 6 metres. The distance all the way round a shape is its perimeter.
> _For grown-ups: Found the perimeter of a 2 m by 1 m rectangle by adding all four sides (6 m)._

_You and your Nubbin drag the sack of stones into place. The cart sinks a little and settles, like a hen on a nest._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, do): keep the heavy part low to make something stable  
> **Tuk**: Now the stones. Put them at the very bottom, under the cabbages. Not on top!  
> _Drag the sack to the lowest spot on the cart. Then give the cart a nudge, and watch it hardly move._  
> Check: `edu:place:stones:low`
> _For grown-ups: Placed the heaviest load low down and saw that it made the cart harder to tip._

_The new cart sits low and wide, like a sensible duck._  
**Pemba**: Can I test it? I want to test it. Please let me test it.  
**The cabbage merchant**: Test it? With what?  
**Pemba**: With me!  

#### `reader:q46-complete` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q46-complete)

_Pemba takes a run-up and throws himself at the cart with all his might._  
_He bounces off. The cart doesn’t move. Not one cabbage stirs._  
**The cabbage merchant**: It holds! It holds! Oh, it’s beautiful. I could weep. I might weep.  
**Pemba**: Doesn’t it make you want to give up? Losing everything, over and over?  
**The cabbage merchant**: Every time I lose the lot, somebody stops to help me pick it up. I’ve met half the world that way.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): think about how people bounce back after things go wrong  
> _The merchant has lost thirty-one carts. And he’s still smiling._  
> _Can something good come out of a bad day? What do you think?_  
> - Yes. He made friends, and learned to build better carts. → That’s how I see it. Thirty-one carts, thirty-one lessons, and more friends than I can count.
> - No. Losing your cabbages is just bad luck. → That’s fair. It IS bad luck, and it’s fine to feel cross about it. I just find the cross part passes quicker when someone helps you pick things up.
> - Sometimes, but not always. → A wise answer. Some bad days are just bad. Others turn into stories you tell for years.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about setbacks: how help from others and learning from mistakes can make a bad day better._

**Tuk**: Everybody laughs at diagrams until the diagrams work.  
**The cabbage merchant**: Keep the old cart, friend. Put cabbages in it. It deserves a quiet retirement after the day it’s had.  
_The old cart is yours now, a planter on wheels. Somehow, you’ve also picked up his famous wail of despair._  
**Pemba**: Do the wail! Do it at something small! Like a dropped spoon!  

### Glossary

- **scattered**: Spread about all over the place.
- **philosophy**: A big idea about how to live, or how to think about things.
- **axle**: The rod through the middle of a wheel that lets it turn.
- **gravity**: The pull that draws things down towards the ground.
- **foreshadowing**: A hint in a story about something that might happen later.
- **diagram**: A drawing that shows how something is built or how it works.
- **stable**: Steady, and hard to knock over.
- **perimeter**: The distance all the way round the edge of a shape.
- **retirement**: The time when someone stops working, after a long time at a job.
- **despair**: The feeling that everything has gone wrong and there’s no hope left.

**Notes:** Lessons: 8 − 3, gravity and unbalanced forces, defining the problem, foreshadowing, comparing a tall and a wide cart, perimeter of the cart bed, putting the weight low (do), and a think question on bouncing back. The merchant’s famous wail is replaced with original lines; the emote is the Learning Grove “Not the cabbages!” version.

## Q47. The Crystal Tunnel

- `q47-s1`: Follow the singing nomads into the tunnel under the ridge. · _The tunnel mouth is behind the waterfall at the foot of the ridge. Bring at least one Nubbin._
- `q47-s2`: Find the way out of the crystal maze. Keep a Nubbin right beside you so the crystals stay lit. · _If your Nubbin wanders more than a step away, the light goes out. Stop, and call it back. There’s no hurry._

#### `reader:q47-nomads` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q47-nomads)

_A painted wagon rolls up to the foot of the ridge, jingling with bells. Three nomads lounge on top. One is tuning a lute._  
**Wandering Juno**: Greetings, travellers! I’m Juno. We go where the wind goes. Today the wind is going under that hill.  
**Tuk**: There’s a perfectly good path over the hill.  
**Wandering Juno**: Over is for people in a hurry. Under is for people with stories. Also, the path has a goat on it. A mean one.  
**Nalu**: Is the tunnel safe?  
**Wandering Juno**: Safe as a song, friend. Here, I’ll sing you the one about it.  
**Wandering Juno**: ♪ Under the hill, under the hill,  
**Wandering Juno**: ♪ The rocks are cold and the bats keep still.  
**Wandering Juno**: ♪ Bring a snack and bring a mate,  
**Wandering Juno**: ♪ Don’t let go or you’ll be late—  
**Wandering Juno**: ♪ Doo-be-doo, the moss is blue, under the hill with you!  
**Tuk**: That song has no useful information in it.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.2.4, ask): hear rhyming words at the ends of lines  
> _Songs often rhyme at the ends of lines. That’s part of what makes them so catchy._  
> _Which word in Juno’s song rhymes with “hill”?_  
> - ✅ still → Hill and still! Mate and late! Doo and blue and you! I’m a poet.
> - cold → Say them out loud: hill, cold. The endings sound different. Hill… still! Those two end with the same sound, so they rhyme.
> - bats → Say them out loud: hill, bats. Different endings. Hill… still! Those end with the same “-ill” sound, so they rhyme.
> _For grown-ups: Found rhyming word pairs in a song (hill/still, mate/late)._

_Pemba is already humming it. So is your Nubbin. You suspect you’ll be humming it for a week._  

> **Lesson** (music, do): clap a steady beat  
> **Wandering Juno**: Clap along with me! Clap on the strong beats: UN-der the HILL, UN-der the HILL.  
> _Clap, clap. Clap, clap. Your Nubbin claps too, a little bit late, every time._  
> Check: `edu:clap:beat:under-the-hill`
> _For grown-ups: Clapped the steady beat of a song._


**Choices**

- **Join in on the doo-be-doo**
    - **Wandering Juno**: A natural! The moss approves.
- **Ask how long the tunnel is**
    - **Wandering Juno**: Long enough to have a good time. Short enough to come out the other side. Probably.
    - **Tuk**: “Probably” is my least favourite word.
- **Ask about the goat**
    - **Wandering Juno**: He bit my lute once. We don’t talk about it.

#### `reader:q47-nomads-done` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q47-nomads-done)

_The tunnel swallows the last of the daylight. Tuk’s torch flickers, sputters, and goes out._  
**Tuk**: Of course. Of course it does.  
_It is completely dark. You hold your hand up in front of your face. You can’t see it at all._  
**Pemba**: My eyes are open! I checked! Twice!  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-2, notice): we can only see things when light reaches our eyes  
> **Nalu**: We only see something when light bounces off it and into our eyes.  
> **Nalu**: Down here there’s no light at all. Nothing can bounce into our eyes, so we see nothing, even with our eyes wide open.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that we see objects only when light from them reaches our eyes, so in total darkness we see nothing._

**Wandering Juno**: Ah. This is the part of the song we haven’t written yet.  
**Nalu**: Nobody move. Let’s think.  

#### `reader:q47-crystals` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q47-crystals)

_It is very dark. Somewhere ahead, water drips, and something with lots of wings flaps away._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-2, notice): how bats find their way in the dark  
> **Wandering Juno**: Those wings were bats. They don’t need light to fly about in here.  
> _Many bats make tiny, very high squeaks and listen for the echoes bouncing back off the walls._  
> _The echoes tell them where things are. This is called echolocation._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that many bats find their way in the dark by echolocation: squeaking and listening for echoes._

**Nalu**: Everyone stay close. Pemba, stop humming.  
**Pemba**: I’m not humming. That’s Tuk.  
**Tuk**: It’s a stress hum. It’s different.  
_Your Nubbin presses against your leg. Where it touches you, the wall beside you starts to glow soft green._  
_You step apart. The glow fades. You step together. It comes back, brighter._  
**Nalu**: The crystals! They only light up when two friends are close together.  
**Wandering Juno**: So that’s why we always come down here in pairs. I thought it was for the snacks.  
**Tuk**: So the plan is “stay together and walk”. That’s… a good plan. I’m annoyed I didn’t think of it.  
**Tuk**: Also. For the record. I hum when I’m scared. Of the dark. There. Now everybody knows.  
**Wandering Juno**: Brave thing, saying that in the dark. Nobody can even see you blush.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): think about whether saying “I’m scared” is brave  
> _Tuk told everyone he’s scared of the dark. Juno called that brave._  
> _Is it brave to say “I’m scared”? What do you think?_  
> - Yes. It’s hard to tell people how you really feel. → I think so too. And now his friends know to stay close. That helps.
> - No. Being brave means not being scared. → Lots of people think that. Here’s another way to see it: brave is doing something even though you’re scared. Tuk is scared, and he’s still walking. I’d say that counts.
> - Everyone’s scared of something. It’s just normal. → True! Nearly everyone is. Maybe that’s why saying it out loud helps: someone usually says “me too”.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about fear and courage: whether naming a fear out loud is a brave thing to do._


**Choices**

- **Pick your Nubbin up and carry it**
    - Your Nubbin settles into your arms. The whole wall lights up like a lantern.
- **Hold its paw and walk side by side**
    - Your Nubbin grips your finger tightly. The crystals glow a steady green.
- **Make Tuk hold Pemba’s hand**
    - **Tuk**: Fine. Fine! But nobody tells anyone about this.
    - **Pemba**: I’m going to tell everyone.

#### `reader:q47-crystals-done` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q47-crystals-done)

_Step by step, side by side, you follow the green glow through the maze._  
_At last, a crack of daylight. You squeeze through into warm air, blinking. Your Nubbin doesn’t let go._  
**Wandering Juno**: See? Out the other side. I said probably.  
**Tuk**: How long were we down there? It felt like a week.  
**Nalu**: We went in at six o’clock, when the sun was setting.  
**Pemba**: And the bell down in the valley just rang half past seven!  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.A.1, ask): work out a time interval  
> **Nalu**: In at 6:00. Out at 7:30. How long were we underground?  
> - ✅ 1 hour and 30 minutes → Yes. Six to seven is one hour, and seven to half past seven is thirty minutes more.
> - 7 hours and 30 minutes → 7:30 is the time now, not how long we were in there. From 6:00 to 7:00 is one hour, then 30 minutes more to 7:30. An hour and a half.
> - 30 minutes → It felt longer than that, and it was! From 6:00 to 7:00 is a whole hour. Then 30 more minutes to 7:30. One hour and 30 minutes.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a time interval: from 6:00 to 7:30 is 1 hour 30 minutes._

**Tuk**: An hour and a half. In the dark. Humming. I need a nap.  

#### `reader:q47-complete` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q47-complete)

_The nomads wave goodbye from the far side of the ridge, singing their song again, slightly wrong._  
**Nalu**: Take some of these. They broke off on the way out.  
_She hands you a pouch of crystal shards. They glow faintly whenever two friends sit near them._  
**Tuk**: And now there’s a tunnel between two of your sections. You can thank me for not getting us lost.  
**Pemba**: You were humming the whole time.  
**Tuk**: Strategically.  
**Wandering Juno**: Remember, friends: the dark is only scary when you’re alone in it. Doo-be-doo!  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, ask): find the theme of a story  
> _A story’s theme is its big idea: the thing it teaches all the way through._  
> _What do you think the theme of this adventure is?_  
> - ✅ Hard things are easier with a friend beside you. → Yes. The crystals only glowed for friends, Tuk was braver with company, and nobody got lost alone.
> - Always bring a spare torch. → Very good advice! But a theme is bigger: it shows up all through the story. The crystals, Tuk’s fear, the hand-holding: they all say hard things are easier with a friend.
> - Goats are mean. → One goat is mean. Most goats are lovely. The big idea all through the story is that hard things are easier with a friend beside you.
> _For grown-ups: Identified the theme of a story: hard things are easier together._


### Glossary

- **nomad**: Someone who moves from place to place and has no one fixed home.
- **lute**: An old stringed instrument, a bit like a guitar with a rounded back.
- **sputters**: Makes small spitting, popping sounds, like a flame about to go out.
- **echolocation**: Finding your way by making sounds and listening for the echoes.
- **strategically**: Done on purpose, as part of a clever plan.
- **theme**: The big idea a story teaches all the way through.

**Notes:** Lessons: rhyme pairs in the nomads’ (original) song, clapping the beat (do), light and seeing, bats and echolocation, a think question on naming a fear, 6:00 to 7:30, and the theme of the story. The glowing crystals are game magic; no lesson treats them as real science.

## Q48. The Great Divide

- `q48-s1`: Lead both clans down the canyon path without a fight breaking out. · _Walk between the two groups. When tempers rise, stop for a rest and a drink of water._
- `q48-s2`: Keep a straight face while Pemba finishes his story about the clans’ ancestors. · _Tap only when the laugh meter is in the green. Looking at your Nubbin makes it worse._

#### `reader:q48-canyon` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q48-canyon)

_Two groups of travellers stand at the top of the canyon, glaring at each other across a pile of luggage._  
**Elder Maud**: The Hollins will go first. We are clean, orderly, and we fold our socks.  
**Chief Brack**: The Drays will go first. We are honest, hardy, and we don’t own socks.  
**Elder Maud**: That is not the boast you think it is.  
**Tuk**: The canyon guide quit this morning. Something about a sprained everything. So, who wants to guide?  
**Pemba**: Me! How hard can it be? It’s just walking downhill with people who don’t like each other.  
**Nalu**: Pemba, please don’t say that part out loud.  
**Chief Brack**: Fine. But no Hollin food in the canyon. They cook with too much… tidiness.  
**Elder Maud**: And no Dray food. It smells of grudges.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): choose a fair way to settle a disagreement  
> **Nalu**: They both want to go first. What would be a fair way to decide?  
> - ✅ Toss a coin. Each clan has the same chance. → Yes. A coin doesn’t take sides. That’s what makes it fair.
> - Let the loudest clan go first. → Then everyone just shouts louder! Being loud doesn’t make you right. A coin toss is fairer: both clans get exactly the same chance.
> - Let the clan with socks go first. → Ha! The Drays would never agree to that. It’s a rule that only helps one side. A fair way gives both sides the same chance, like tossing a coin.
> _For grown-ups: Chose a fair way to decide between two groups (a coin toss gives each the same chance)._


**Choices**

- **Let the Hollins go first**
    - **Chief Brack**: Typical.
    - The Drays follow, loudly, two steps behind.
- **Let the Drays go first**
    - **Elder Maud**: Of course.
    - The Hollins follow, sniffing, two steps behind.
- **Make them walk side by side**
    - **Elder Maud**: Absolutely not.
    - **Chief Brack**: Absolutely not.
    - They walk side by side anyway, because the path is narrow. Neither speaks.

#### `reader:q48-canyon-done` · [main](../../../fun/avatar.md#q48-canyon-done)
