# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Fun quests: Monty Python (Q1–Q20)

## Q1. Two Shrubberies

- `q01-s1`: Make a shrubbery and give it to the three hoods. · _The Workshop makes one from reeds and wood. Then use Give._
- `q01-s2`: Make a second shrubbery, a bit higher, with a tiny path. · _Make another shrubbery and a path. Put them next to the first one._

#### `sprout:q01-intro` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q01-intro)

_Splash, splash goes the waterfall._  
_Three Nubbins in brown hoods stand on the path._  
_The middle one looks tall. It is standing on a log!_  
**The Hooded Three**: Boing-a-boing!  
_Your Nubbin jumps. A little bird flies away._  
**You**: Please may we go past?  
**The Tall Hood**: Not yet. First, we need something green. Something leafy. A… bush!  
_A shrubbery is a little bush. Green and leafy._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): first sounds: sh  
> **The Tall Hood**: Shrubbery starts with a shh sound. Shh!  
> **The Tall Hood**: Which word starts with shh too?  
> - ✅ Shoe → Yes! Shh-oe. Shoe and shrubbery start the same.
> - Bird → Bird starts with a b sound. Shh-oe starts with shh, like shrubbery!
> - Log → Log starts with l. Listen: shh-oe. Shoe starts with shh!
> _For grown-ups: Your child listened for the “sh” sound at the start of words._

_The other two nod. One nods a bit late._  

**Choices**

- **What kind of shrubbery?**
    - **The Tall Hood**: A nice one. Green, please.
    - Green, like leaves.
- **Chant back: Boing-a-boing!**
    - You shout it back, loud and silly.
    - The hoods look at each other. One writes it down.
- **One shrubbery, coming up!**
    - **The Tall Hood**: Good. We will wait here. Swaying.

#### `sprout:q01-first` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q01-first)

_You put the shrubbery down. Plop._  
_The hoods look at it. Top, sides, and underneath._  
**The Tall Hood**: It has leaves. We like the leaves.  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, notice): what plants need  
> **The Tall Hood**: A shrubbery is alive. It is a plant.  
> **The Tall Hood**: Give it water and sunshine, and it will grow.  
> _For grown-ups: Your child heard that plants need water and light to grow._

_They chant softly. It means thank you._  

#### `sprout:q01-second` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q01-second)

_You step towards the path. The tall hood holds up a paw._  
**The Tall Hood**: Wait. There is a second thing.  
**You**: You said one shrubbery!  
**The Tall Hood**: Now we would like two. Put the new one up higher.  
**The Tall Hood**: And a tiny path between them. Just right for a beetle.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.G.A.1, ask): positions: higher and lower  
> **The Tall Hood**: The new shrubbery goes higher. Is higher up or down?  
> - ✅ Up! → Yes! Higher means up. Point up with me!
> - Down → Down is lower. Higher means up. Point up, up, up!
> _For grown-ups: Your child practised the position words higher and lower._

_One hood holds its paws a beetle apart._  
**The Tall Hood**: Also, we have a new name. It goes like this…  
_Clatter, bang, ting-a-ling! Like spoons falling down the stairs._  
**You**: I can’t say that!  
**The Tall Hood**: Nobody can. That is why it is so scary.  

**Choices**

- **OK. Higher, with a path.**
    - **The Tall Hood**: Good. Use little stones. We love little stones.
- **Can the path be pretend?**
    - **The Tall Hood**: No. We tried that. Someone fell off it.

#### `sprout:q01-terraced` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q01-terraced)

_Two shrubberies. One low, one a little higher._  
_A tiny stone path wiggles between them._  
_A beetle walks along it. It looks very pleased._  

> **Lesson** (science, do): insects have six legs  
> _A beetle is an insect. Insects have six legs._  
> _(Count its legs together: one, two, three, four, five, six!)_  
> Check: `edu:count:beetle-legs:6`
> _For grown-ups: Your child counted a beetle’s legs and learned insects have six._

**The Tall Hood**: Oh. Oh, that is lovely.  

#### `sprout:q01-complete` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q01-complete)

_The hoods step aside. Then they step back, to look again._  
**The Tall Hood**: You may pass. Every day, if you like.  
**You**: Will you stay here?  
**The Tall Hood**: We have a shrubbery and a path. Of course we stay.  
**The Tall Hood**: We guarded this path for years.  
**The Tall Hood**: A shrubbery is a much nicer reason to stand here.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): naming feelings  
> _The hoods are smiling at their shrubbery._  
> _How do they feel now?_  
> - ✅ Happy → Yes! Happy and proud. They love their garden.
> - Cross → Look at their smiles. They feel happy, not cross.
> _For grown-ups: Your child named the hoods’ feeling from their words and faces._

_One hood gets a tiny rake. Scritch, scratch._  
_At dusk they practise their new name. Clatter, ting… oops!_  

**Notes:** The chant “Boing-a-boing!” replaces the main game’s chant in every sprout scene.

## Q2. The Cardboard Knight

- `q02-s1`: Win a karate game against Sir Dentwhistle on the plank bridge. · _Choose Karate on the bridge. Any of your Nubbins can play._
- `q02-s2`: Win a second karate game. · _If your Nubbin is tired, swap in a fresh one._
- `q02-s3`: Win the last karate game. · _If your Nubbin keeps losing, let it rest first._

#### `sprout:q02-intro` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q02-intro)

_A plank bridge goes over the stream._  
_In the middle stands a Nubbin in cardboard armour._  
_A cardboard helmet. Two arm guards. Two knee pads._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 5  
> _(Count the cardboard pieces together.)_  
> _One helmet. Two arm guards. Two knee pads._  
> _One, two, three, four, five. Five pieces!_  
> Check: `edu:count:armour:5`
> _For grown-ups: Your child counted five pieces of cardboard armour._

_A snail is slowly climbing up its leg._  
**You**: Hello! May we cross, please?  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: Nobody crosses my plank. Not one toe!  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: I am Sir Dentwhistle. I have guarded it for three whole days!  
_It holds up its little fists. Time for a karate game!_  

**Choices**

- **Bow politely, then play.**
    - **Sir Dentwhistle**: Polite! I shall beat you very politely.
- **Can’t we go round?**
    - There are stepping stones just down the stream.
    - **Sir Dentwhistle**: Nobody uses those. They are too low. Down there.

#### `sprout:q02-round1` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q02-round1)

_Hi-yah! Your Nubbin does its best move._  
_Plonk! The cardboard helmet falls into the grass._  
_Sir Dentwhistle has very flat hair._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): snails and their shells  
> _Look, the snail is still climbing. Slowly, slowly._  
> _A snail carries its shell on its back._  
> _When it is scared, it hides inside. Safe!_  
> _For grown-ups: Your child learned that a snail carries its shell, which keeps it safe._

**Sir Dentwhistle**: Pfft. I didn’t even feel it.  
**You**: It was your helmet!  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: A helmet is only a hat. Again!  

#### `sprout:q02-scarf` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q02-scarf)

_Sir Dentwhistle wraps on a long woolly scarf._  
_Round and round and round. Three times!_  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: My mum knitted this. Nothing gets past the scarf.  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: She meant the cold. But still.  

> **Lesson** (health, ask): dressing for the weather  
> _What is a woolly scarf for?_  
> - ✅ Keeping warm → Yes! Wool keeps your neck cosy when it is cold.
> - Eating → Ha! Not for eating. A scarf keeps you warm in cold weather.
> _For grown-ups: Your child talked about how a scarf keeps us warm in cold weather._

**You**: Has anyone tried?  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: That is not the point.  
_The snail reaches its shoulder. What a view!_  

#### `sprout:q02-round2` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q02-round2)

_Hi-yah! Your Nubbin spins._  
_The scarf unwinds and floats off down the stream._  
_Clatter, clatter! Off fall both cardboard arm guards._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.OA.A.1, ask): take away 2  
> _Sir Dentwhistle had four pieces left. Two just fell off._  
> _(Hold up four fingers. Fold two down.)_  
> _How many pieces are left?_  
> - ✅ Two → Yes! Two knee pads left. Four take away two is two.
> - Three → Let’s count what’s left: knee pad, knee pad. One, two. Two!
> - None → Not yet! The two knee pads are still on. Two left.
> _For grown-ups: Your child took two away from four to find how many pieces were left._

**Sir Dentwhistle**: Barely bothered. I was too warm anyway.  
_Its little round glasses steam up. It is cross!_  

#### `sprout:q02-glasses` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q02-glasses)

_Sir Dentwhistle wipes its glasses on its tummy._  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: Last round. I still have my glasses and knee pads.  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: If I can’t see you, you can’t see me.  
**You**: That’s not how seeing works!  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): how we see  
> _We see with our eyes._  
> _Glasses help some people’s eyes see clearly._  
> _If you close your eyes, other people can still see you!_  
> _For grown-ups: Your child heard that we see with our eyes, and that glasses help some eyes._

**Sir Dentwhistle**: It is on my bridge.  
_Your Nubbin wants to win, then have a snack._  

**Choices**

- **Ask your Nubbin to be gentle.**
    - Your Nubbin nods. It will be kind.
- **Do your very best karate!**
    - Your Nubbin cracks its knuckles. It has no knuckles!

#### `sprout:q02-round3` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q02-round3)

_Hi-yah! Clonk, clonk! Off fall both knee pads._  
_The glasses fly off too. The snail goes round them._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.OA.A.1, ask): zero  
> _Both knee pads fell off. How many cardboard pieces are left?_  
> - ✅ None! Zero! → Yes! Zero. All gone. Just a little Nubbin in a vest.
> - Two → Look: the knee pads are on the grass. None left on. Zero!
> _For grown-ups: Your child found that when every piece is gone, there are zero left._

**Sir Dentwhistle**: I am fine! Never better! Which one of you said that?  
_It is talking to a fence post._  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: Come back! I’m not done being brave!  
_The fence post does not move. Not one bit._  

#### `sprout:q02-complete` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q02-complete)

_Sir Dentwhistle sits down on the plank. Flump._  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: You may cross. Not because you won. I just want a sit.  
_You pick up the cardboard helmet and hold it out._  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: Keep it. I have lots more. At home. In a box.  
_It does not have lots more. It keeps looking at the helmet._  
**Sir Dentwhistle**: Remember: losing is fine if you don’t mind. I never mind!  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): losing well  
> _Sir Dentwhistle lost. What can we do when we lose a game?_  
> - ✅ Take a breath and say “good game” → Yes! That helps everyone feel good.
> - Stamp and shout → It’s OK to feel cross when we lose. Then we take a big breath and say “good game”.
> _For grown-ups: Your child talked about what to do when we lose a game._

_You cross the plank in two big steps._  
_Now your Nubbins lose karate games with a smile._  

**Choices**

- **Shall we say we both did well?**
    - **Sir Dentwhistle**: Yes! I was just going to say that.
    - It shakes your hand. Then the fence post’s.
- **You lost three times, you know.**
    - Sir Dentwhistle is quiet. The snail is quiet too.
    - **Sir Dentwhistle**: …I know. I was there.
    - **Sir Dentwhistle**: Thank you for telling the truth. That helps.

**Notes:** Sprout art: Sir Dentwhistle wears cardboard armour (helmet, two arm guards, two knee pads) over its scarf and glasses. Round 1 knocks off the helmet; round 2 the scarf and both arm guards; round 3 the glasses and both knee pads. Each piece clatters off like a cardboard box, never anything more.

## Q3. The Bridge of Three Questions

- `q03-s1`: Count all the Nubbins in your Grove, then go back to the bridge. · _Tap each Nubbin once. Look in the pond too!_
- `q03-s2`: Find out how fast a Fly Nubbin can carry a coconut. · _Get a coconut from the palm tree by the sea. Time a Fly Nubbin._

#### `sprout:q03-intro` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q03-intro)

_By a rope bridge sits a very old Keeper._  
_His hood has moss on it. Maybe he does too!_  
**Old Gorrel**: Stop! Answer three questions to cross my bridge.  
**Old Gorrel**: Get one wrong, and… splash! Into the stream.  
_You look down. The water is shallow, full of happy ducks._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): animal sounds  
> _Listen to the ducks! What sound does a duck make?_  
> - ✅ Quack! → Yes! Quack, quack! (Quack together.)
> - Moo → Moo is a cow! A duck says quack, quack.
> - Baa → Baa is a sheep! A duck says quack, quack.
> _For grown-ups: Your child matched the duck to its sound._

**Old Gorrel**: Question one. How many Nubbins live in your Grove?  
**You**: Um. Let me check.  
**Old Gorrel**: Go and count. I am very good at waiting.  

**Choices**

- **Make a big guess.**
    - **Old Gorrel**: Hmm. Probably wrong. Go and count.
- **Go and count properly.**
    - **Old Gorrel**: Wise. The last guesser is still in the stream. He likes it.

#### `sprout:q03-count` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q03-count)

_You come back with the number._  
_Old Gorrel checks it on his sleeve._  
**Old Gorrel**: Right. Bother.  
**Old Gorrel**: Question two. What did your newest Nubbin eat for breakfast?  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): clapping syllables  
> _Let’s clap “breakfast”. Break-fast._  
> _(Clap twice together: break… fast!)_  
> _Two claps!_  
> Check: `edu:clap:syllables:breakfast`
> _For grown-ups: Your child clapped the two beats in “breakfast”._

**You**: Half a run fruit. The other half fell in the pond.  
**Old Gorrel**: Right. And very sad.  

#### `sprout:q03-third` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q03-third)

**Old Gorrel**: Question three.  
_He leans in. The ducks go quiet._  
**Old Gorrel**: How fast can a Fly Nubbin fly, carrying a coconut?  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.MD.A.2, ask): heavy and light  
> _Is a coconut heavy or light?_  
> - ✅ Heavy → Yes! A coconut is big and heavy. Heavy things are hard to carry.
> - Light → A feather is light. A coconut is heavy, with a hard shell.
> _For grown-ups: Your child compared heavy and light things._

**You**: I don’t know!  
**Old Gorrel**: Nor do I. Nobody has ever answered.  
**Old Gorrel**: Go and find out. I shall wait.  
_He sits back, smug as a cushion._  

#### `sprout:q03-timed` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q03-timed)

_You time two Fly Nubbins._  
_The first one zooms across the pond. Whoosh!_  
_The second flies halfway, sits on the coconut, and has a think._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ask): opposites: fast and slow  
> _One Nubbin zoomed. One sat down. Which one was fast?_  
> - ✅ The zoomy one → Yes! Fast. The sitting one was slow. Fast and slow are opposites.
> - The sitting one → The sitting one stopped! The zoomy one was fast. The sitter was slow.
> _For grown-ups: Your child used the opposite words fast and slow._

_So the answer is: it depends on the Nubbin!_  

#### `sprout:q03-complete` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q03-complete)

**Old Gorrel**: Well? How fast?  
**You**: Which Fly Nubbin? The zoomy one, or the sitting one?  
_Old Gorrel opens his mouth. Then he shuts it._  
**Old Gorrel**: I don’t know! Nobody ever asked me a question back!  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): it is OK not to know  
> **Old Gorrel**: I don’t know the answer. And that is all right!  
> _Nobody knows everything. We can say “I don’t know”._  
> _Then we ask, or we find out together._  
> _For grown-ups: Your child heard that nobody knows everything, and we can ask and find out._

_He steps back… and plops into the stream. Splash!_  
_Quack, quack, quack! say the ducks._  
**Old Gorrel**: I’m fine! It’s lovely in here! The bridge is yours!  
**Old Gorrel**: Being asked a question is rather nice.  
_He floats away, waving. His hood lands on the post._  

## Q4. Clip-Clop

- `q04-s1`: Find out where the coconut shells came from. · _Follow the path to the palm tree by the sea._
- `q04-s2`: Decide what happens to the coconut shells. · _Go back to the lookout wall and talk to the Run Nubbin._

#### `sprout:q04-intro` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q04-intro)

_Clop. Clop-clop. Clop._  
_A Run Nubbin trots behind you, banging two coconut shells._  
_You stop. It stops. You walk. It clops!_  

> **Lesson** (music, do): copying a rhythm  
> _(Clap the clops together.)_  
> _Clop. Clop-clop. Clop. Your turn!_  
> Check: `edu:clap:rhythm:clop-clopclop-clop`
> _For grown-ups: Your child clapped back a simple clip-clop rhythm._

_Warden Ollery leans over the lookout wall._  
**Warden Ollery**: Coconut shells? Up here? How odd!  
**Warden Ollery**: Coconuts grow on palm trees, by the sea.  
**Warden Ollery**: There is only one palm tree here. Far away, on the beach.  
**Warden Ollery**: So how did they get up the hill?  
_The Run Nubbin clops twice. Clop, clop!_  

**Choices**

- **Ask the Run Nubbin.**
    - It clops. Then it points its whole body at the sea.
- **Does it matter?**
    - **Warden Ollery**: Everything matters up here. There’s not much else to look at.

#### `sprout:q04-gullwick` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q04-gullwick)

_Under the palm tree lies a Fly Nubbin called Gullwick._  
_Its wings are very big, and very tired._  
**Gullwick**: Carried it. All the way. Uphill. Puff, puff.  
**Gullwick**: Dropped it on a rock. Crack! Two halves.  
**Gullwick**: Run Nubbin found them. Very happy. I am very tired.  
_Gullwick lies flat in the sand. Flump._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): saying thank you  
> _Gullwick worked very hard. What could we say?_  
> - ✅ Thank you! → Oh. Nice. Very tired. Still nice.
> - Hurry up! → Gullwick is tired. A “thank you” would help it feel better.
> _For grown-ups: Your child thought about thanking someone who worked hard._


**Choices**

- **Say a big thank you to Gullwick.**
    - Gullwick lifts one wing, just a little.
    - **Gullwick**: Nobody said that before. Nice.
- **Why carry it so far?**
    - **Gullwick**: It looked like it wanted to go up.
    - Maybe that explains everything.

#### `sprout:q04-return` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q04-return)

_Back to the wall you go. Clop, clop. Right behind you._  
**You**: A Fly Nubbin carried a coconut up from the beach!  
**Warden Ollery**: All that way? With those little wings?  
**Warden Ollery**: Maybe the shells should go back to the beach.  
_The Run Nubbin hugs the shells tight._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, notice): banging makes sound  
> _Clop! When two hard shells bang, they shake a tiny bit._  
> _That shaking makes the sound._  
> _Horses’ feet go clip-clop on a hard road, too._  
> _For grown-ups: Your child heard that hitting two hard things makes them shake, and that makes sound._

_This is the sound it was born to make!_  

**Choices**

- **They’re yours. Keep them!**
    - The Run Nubbin trots in a happy circle. Clop-clop-clop!
- **Keep them, but share turns.**
    - It agrees. As long as its turn is first. And last.

#### `sprout:q04-decided` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q04-decided)

_The Run Nubbin tucks the shells under its arm._  
_It gives you a long, happy look._  
**Warden Ollery**: That’s settled, then. Clop away, little one!  
_Clop. Clop-clop. Off it goes._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): first sounds: c  
> _Clop and coconut start with the same sound. C-c-c!_  
> _Which word starts with c too?_  
> - ✅ Cat → Yes! C-c-cat. Clop, coconut, cat!
> - Sun → Sun starts with sss. Listen: c-c-cat. Cat starts like clop!
> _For grown-ups: Your child listened for the hard “c” sound at the start of words._


#### `sprout:q04-complete` · [main](../../../fun/python.md#q04-complete)

_The Run Nubbin is called Trotwell. It clops all round the Grove._  
_Soon every Nubbin wants a go!_  
**Warden Ollery**: I like the noise. It keeps me company.  
_Trotwell teaches you to trot. Lots of knees!_  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, do): trotting on the spot  
> _(Stand up and trot on the spot!)_  
> _Knees up! Clop, clop, clop, clop, clop!_  
> Check: `edu:trot:knees-up:5`
> _For grown-ups: Your child trotted on the spot with high knees._

_Now, when you run, you can sound like a horse!_  
_Far away, Gullwick sleeps on the warm sand._  
_Beside it: a fruit and a note. “Thank you. From the wall.”_
