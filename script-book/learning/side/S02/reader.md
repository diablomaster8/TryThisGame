# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S2: Wisp

## S2. Wisp

- `s2-step1`: Sit up after dark by Sable’s fruit, and keep very still. · _Leave fruit at the edge of the long grass, then wait nearby at night without moving._
- `s2-step2`: Keep an eye on Wisp. When the weather turns, find where he sleeps. · _This picks up in the chapter 6 rain. The warm things went somewhere warm._
- `s2-step3`: Keep winning Sable’s trust. Wisp is never far from her. · _This picks up in chapter 7. Once Sable leans on you, visit Luna’s night garden after dark._

#### `reader:s2-fruit` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-fruit)

_Morning. The fruit you left for Sable is gone._  
_That should be good news. But Sable is curled in the long grass looking thinner, not fatter._  
_There is frost on the grass round her, though it is nearly midsummer._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-ESS2-3, notice): what frost is and when it forms  
> **Luna**: Frost is water from the air that freezes onto cold grass, in tiny ice crystals.  
> **Luna**: It only forms when the ground is at freezing or colder: 0°C (32°F). In midsummer, that’s very strange.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that frost is frozen water from the air, and forms at 0°C (32°F) or below._

**Luna**: You’ve seen the frost. Good. Most people see frost at midsummer and go in for a cardigan.  
**Luna**: I saw her eat one piece last night. Then something else came out of the dark and took the rest.  
**Luna**: Not shared. Took.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): sharing versus taking  
> **Luna**: “Not shared. Took.” Do you know what I mean by the difference?  
> - ✅ Sharing is when the owner chooses to give. Taking is grabbing without asking. → That’s it. Sable didn’t choose. That’s what bothers me.
> - There’s no difference. The fruit’s gone either way. → In the morning it looks the same, doesn’t it? An empty dish. But sharing is when the owner chooses to give. Taking is when nobody asks. Sable didn’t get a choice.
> _For grown-ups: Explained the difference between sharing (a choice) and taking (no choice)._

**You**: Another Nubbin?  
**Luna**: A grey one. Thinner than her. He moves like he’s always expecting a boot.  
**Luna**: Sit up tonight. Leave the fruit where you always do, and keep still. Still as a post.  
**Luna**: He wants something here. I’d like to know what, before he takes it.  

#### `reader:s2-thief` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-thief)

_Past midnight. Sable creeps out of the grass to the fruit and takes one bite._  
_Then the grass parts again. Something comes through: grey, thin as a stick, one ear torn to a fringe._  
_He shoulders her aside, not hard, and drags the fruit towards the dark. Sable lets him._  
_Then he sees your lantern. He creeps up to it and presses his cheek against the warm glass._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, notice): heat moves from warmer things to cooler things  
> _Heat always moves from a warmer thing to a cooler thing._  
> _The candle warms the glass. The glass warms his cold cheek. He has found the warmest thing in the Grove._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that heat flows from warmer objects to cooler ones._

_He is shaking. He keeps shaking, even there._  
_Then he sees you._  
**Wisp**: Hsss. Hers. Not yours. Not hands.  
_And under the hiss, much smaller, comes another voice._  
**Wisp**: …she ate. she ate today. good.  
_He is gone before you can breathe out. Sable stares at the place where he went for a long time._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): what a character’s words reveal about him  
> **Luna**: He has two voices. The hiss and the whisper. What does the little whisper tell us about him?  
> - ✅ He cares whether Sable has eaten. → Yes. The hiss says “go away”. The whisper says “she ate, good”. Both are him.
> - He only wants the fruit. → That’s what the hiss would have us think. But listen to the whisper again: “she ate today. good.” He’s glad she ate. He cares about her.
> - He’s angry at everyone. → The hiss is angry, yes. But the whisper is glad Sable ate. Underneath the hiss, he cares about her.
> _For grown-ups: Used a character’s words to work out his hidden feelings._

**Luna**: Greys don’t talk. They don’t. I’ve known a dozen.  
**Luna**: Something’s talking through the cracks in that one. Two somethings.  
**Luna**: Call him Wisp. He’ll hate it. Everyone should have a name they hate a little.  

#### `reader:s2-missing` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-missing)

_Over the next days, things go missing from the Grove. Not just fruit now. Warm things._  
**Fern**: Someone’s had my left glove. Left me the right. I can’t decide if that’s cruel or considerate.  
_Your left sock, off the line. A candle stub from the cottage window. The tea cosy, which nobody admits to missing._  
_And the round stone Hazel kept by the hearth for warming eggs. Its dish is empty._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-1, ask): insulation: materials that keep warmth in  
> **Clover**: He only takes warm things. Which of these keeps warmth in best, by trapping air?  
> - ✅ The woolly glove and the tea cosy → Yes. Wool and padding hold lots of tiny pockets of still air. Heat moves through still air very slowly. That’s insulation. And the stone? Warmed by a fire, a stone holds its heat and lets it out slowly.
> - The candle stub → A lit candle makes heat, true. But it doesn’t keep heat in. Wool and padding do. They trap tiny pockets of still air, and heat moves through still air slowly. That’s insulation.
> - A metal spoon → Metal lets heat rush straight through it. A spoon in hot tea gets hot fast. Wool and padding keep warmth in. They trap tiny pockets of still air. That’s insulation.
> _For grown-ups: Sorted materials by how well they keep heat in, and learned the word insulation._

**Clover**: He’s cold. That’s all it is. Can’t we feed him too? I can see the light through his ears.  
**Luna**: Shoo him off, Clover. I mean it.  
**Luna**: Every time Sable gets near a hand, he hisses at it. And she listens. She was taught to listen to that voice.  
**Clover**: He was taught too. Somebody taught him.  
**Luna**: …I know. It doesn’t make him any quieter.  
_In the fork of the fruit tree, Wisp licks his torn ear and watches you, like something that has been right before._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, do): share equally into two groups  
> **Clover**: Just in case. Twelve fruit. Two piles. If we feed him, it’s got to be fair.  
> _(Put the fruit into two equal piles.)_  
> **Clover**: Six and six. Twelve shared between two is six each. Nobody can say that isn’t fair.  
> Check: `edu:share:fruit:6-each`
> _For grown-ups: Divided 12 into 2 equal groups of 6._


**Choices**

- **Put down two piles of fruit: one each.**
    - Wisp waits until you are far away and eats his pile. Then he eats most of hers.
    - **Clover**: That’s not quite how I pictured it. But he did eat.
- **Clap your hands and shoo him off.**
    - Wisp bolts to the edge of the grass and no further. Sable flinches at the clap too.
    - **Luna**: Not the hands. Never the hands. Use your voice next time.
- **Sit down where you are, and wait.**
    - You sit. Wisp hisses until he runs out of hiss, and Sable eats, watching you both.
    - **Luna**: That works on her. I didn’t know it worked on him.

#### `reader:s2-rain` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-rain)

_It has rained for a day and a night. The stream is over its stones, brown and loud._  
_Behind the cottage, where the chimney bricks hold the day’s heat, the woodpile has slumped into a pool._  
_Under it is a nest, or what is left of one: Fern’s glove, the sock, the tea cosy, all sodden through._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, notice): why wet things can’t keep you warm  
> **Clover**: A soaked glove can’t keep anything warm. The water fills up all the little air pockets.  
> **Clover**: And water carries heat away much faster than air does. That’s why wet and cold is the worst cold.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that wet materials lose their insulation because water carries heat away faster than air._

_And Wisp, on the last dry log, shaking so hard the water ripples round him. Hazel’s egg stone is in his paws._  
**Wisp**: mine. warm. mine.  
**Wisp**: …was warm. was.  
_He tries to stand, and his legs fold. He is too cold to hiss._  
**Clover**: The bathhouse is warm. Hazel kept it warm for exactly this. Please.  
**Luna**: If you carry him, he’ll fight. Hold him anyway. Hold him like you expected it.  
_Across the yard, in the long grass, Sable is watching. She has seen hands pick up a Nubbin before._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): seeing a moment from someone else’s side  
> _Sable has seen hands pick up a Nubbin before, and it didn’t end well. How might she feel, watching now?_  
> - ✅ Scared, because last time hands meant something bad. → That’s what I think too. So whatever you do, do it gently. She’s learning what hands are for.
> - Happy that someone is helping. → Maybe, a little, underneath. I hope so. But last time she saw hands lift a Nubbin, it went badly. She’s probably scared. So be gentle where she can see.
> - She doesn’t care. → She’s watching very hard for someone who doesn’t care. I think she’s scared. Last time hands lifted a Nubbin, it went badly. Be gentle where she can see.
> _For grown-ups: Imagined how a character might feel based on what has happened to her before._


**Choices**

- **Pick him up. Hold on.**
    - He fights, then stops fighting. He weighs less than the stone he won’t let go of.
    - In the steam of the bathhouse his fur lifts and his eyes go sharp. He looks at your hands, and bites one, hard.
    - **Wisp**: Hsss. Hands. Hands, hands.
    - He scrambles to the warmest corner with the stone, and glares until his tail stops dripping.
    - **Clover**: He’s warm now. Look at him. Warm right through.
    - He is. And he is still shaking.
- **Fetch a blanket, and let Clover lift him.**
    - Clover wraps him up like a loaf and carries him in at a run, talking to him the whole way.
    - In the steam of the bathhouse his fur lifts and his eyes go sharp. He bites her, hard, through the blanket.
    - **Clover**: Ow. Yes. All right. That’s fair.
    - He scrambles to the warmest corner with the stone. He is warm now, right through.
    - And he is still shaking.

#### `reader:s2-number` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-number)

**Clover**: Warm. Dry. Fed. And look at him.  
**Luna**: …Then it was never the cold.  

> **Lesson** (health, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): how fear shows in the body  
> **Clover**: He’s warm and dry now, and still shaking. Why?  
> - ✅ He’s frightened. → Yes. Fear lives in the body too. It can make you shake, breathe fast and feel your heart thump. Getting warm fixes cold. It doesn’t fix scared. That takes much longer.
> - He’s still a bit cold. → That’s what we thought. But he’s warm right through now. Feel the air in here. It’s fear. Fear can make a body shake, breathe fast and thump inside. Warmth doesn’t fix that.
> - He’s excited about the stone. → He does love that stone. But look at his ears, flat back. He’s frightened. Fear can make a body shake, even somewhere warm.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that fear shows in the body (shaking, fast breathing, a racing heart)._

_Nobody says anything for a while. The rain drums on the bathhouse roof._  
_High on Wisp’s side, half washed off, is a number someone once painted on in grey._  
**Luna**: That paint. I’ve seen that paint before. Square numbers, very neat.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, notice): noticing clues  
> _Luna is collecting clues: grey paint, square numbers, very neat. Small details that hint at a bigger answer._  
> _Good readers do the same. They notice the small things the story puts in on purpose._  
> _For grown-ups: Noticed how small details in a story can be clues to something bigger._

**Luna**: I’m going to ask around. Don’t tell Sable. I know that’s silly.  
_When you come out, Sable is by the bathhouse door. She sniffs your hands very carefully, and doesn’t run._  

#### `reader:s2-night` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-night)

_Night, from Luna’s garden. The moonflowers are open. Two grey shapes sit at the edge of the Grove._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-LS2-2, notice): night-flowering plants and their pollinators  
> **Luna**: Moonflowers open at dusk, not in the day. They’re big and white, so they show up in the dark.  
> **Luna**: Moths that fly at night visit them for nectar, and carry pollen from flower to flower.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that some flowers open at night and are pollinated by night-flying moths._

**Luna**: Get down. Listen.  
_Wisp is close to Sable, closer than he lets anyone get. Tonight his voice is all whisper._  
**Wisp**: hands are for throwing. they all throw. in the end.  
**Wisp**: come back to the dark. no hands there. the dark never threw anyone.  
_Sable doesn’t answer. She looks at Wisp, then at your window. She moves towards neither._  
**Luna**: Moss says the cold leaves them half a voice. And he’s spending all of it talking her out of the Grove.  
_Clover has crept up behind you in a dressing gown, holding a bag of fruit as if it might go off._  
**Clover**: You don’t feed him because he’s earned it. You feed him because you’re you.  
**Luna**: He bit the hand that carried him, Clover.  
**Clover**: He was cold. I’d have bitten too.  
**Luna**: She leaned on a shin this week. A shin. Do you know how far that is, for her?  
**Luna**: One more month of that voice in her ear and she’ll be back under a hedge. She won’t come out twice.  
**Luna**: I’m not choosing against him. I’m choosing her. Somebody has to.  
**Clover**: And where does he go? Back to the dark. On his own. Talking to nobody.  
_Neither of them looks at you. They both very carefully don’t._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): think question: mercy  
> _Being kind to someone who hasn’t earned it is called mercy. Luna and Clover both love Sable._  
> _Should you keep feeding someone who bit you? What do you think?_  
> - Yes. Kindness shouldn’t depend on being thanked. → That’s a fair answer. It’s mine, too. He was taught to bite. Maybe he can be taught something else.
> - No. Sable has to come first. → That’s a fair answer. Somebody has to put her first. It doesn’t mean you don’t care about him.
> - Let Sable choose. It’s her life too. → That’s a fair answer, and a brave one. I’d only wonder: is that a big choice to hand someone so small? It’s still a fair answer.
> - I don’t know. They’re both right. → That’s a fair answer. Sometimes two people are both right, and you still have to choose. That’s what makes it hard.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about mercy and a hard choice between two good reasons; there was no wrong answer._


**Choices**

- **Keep feeding him. Even after the bite.**
    - In the morning you put down two piles of fruit, not one. And the next morning, and the one after.
    - Wisp eats his with his back to you. On the fourth morning, he eats it facing you.
    - **Wisp**: hands are for throwing. hands are for… not hers. maybe not hers.
    - Sable sees. Sable sees everything. She comes one step closer than she did yesterday.
    - It isn’t free. Your other sock goes missing on the fifth morning, and the hiss is still there most nights.
    - **Luna**: He’s still talking to her. I can hear him. I hope you’re right.
- **Drive him off. Sable needs the quiet.**
    - You walk at him, steady, not fast, until he backs into the long grass. Then further. Then gone.
    - **Wisp**: Hsss. See? See? Hands.
    - Sable watches him go and doesn’t follow. She doesn’t look at you for the rest of the night, either.
    - But near dawn she eats her whole pile, every scrap, and sleeps in the open where you can see her.
    - **Luna**: It was the sensible thing. I keep telling myself so.
    - **Clover**: I know it was. I’m still not speaking to you till Thursday.
- **Let Sable decide.**
    - You put down one pile of ten fruit between them, and step back.
    - Sable looks at it, then at you. Then she pushes half the pile across the grass with her nose, to Wisp.
    - **Wisp**: …hers. she gave. hers.
    - He eats it at the very edge of the Grove. When he has finished, he doesn’t leave. He just doesn’t come in.
    - **Luna**: Well. That’s a better answer than either of ours.
    - **Clover**: Or we’ve just handed the smallest one here the biggest choice, because we couldn’t make it.
    - **Luna**: …Yes. That too.

#### `reader:s2-complete` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-complete)

_Morning. Frost on the grass at the edge of the Grove, in two small circles where two grey things slept._  
_Wisp is curled in one of them, the egg stone between his paws. It has been cold for weeks. He keeps it anyway._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, ask): warm things cool to match their surroundings  
> **Moss**: That stone was warm by Hazel’s fire once. It’s been cold for weeks. Where did its warmth go?  
> - ✅ Out into the cooler air and ground around it. → Yes. Heat leaks from warm things into cooler things, until they’re all the same. He keeps it anyway.
> - Wisp ate it. → He’d try, if he could. He can’t. The heat leaked out into the cooler air and ground, a little at a time, until the stone matched them.
> - It’s still inside, hiding. → A lovely thought. Not quite. Heat always moves from warm to cool. It leaked out into the air and ground until the stone matched them.
> _For grown-ups: Explained that warm objects lose heat to cooler surroundings until they match._

**Moss**: He’ll never give that back, you know. Not if he lives to be as old as me.  
**Moss**: Feed them before you feed yourself. She never said only the good ones. I checked. I was there.  
_…_  
**Moss**: Grey things remember who fed them. That’s all I’ll say. I’m old, and I’ve seen it go both ways.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, notice): theme: the big idea a story keeps returning to  
> _A theme is the big idea a story keeps coming back to._  
> _This story keeps asking one question: who deserves kindness? Moss’s answer is in Hazel’s rule._  
> _For grown-ups: Identified the theme of a story: who deserves kindness, and mercy._


#### `reader:s2-complete-sable` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-complete-sable)

_Variant of `s2-complete`._

_Morning. Frost on the grass at the edge of the Grove, in two small circles, side by side._  
_Wisp is still at the edge, the egg stone between his paws. He hasn’t come in. He hasn’t gone, either._  
**Moss**: She chose. That’s worth more than anything we’d have chosen for her.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.NF.A.1, ask): finding half of a group  
> **Moss**: There were ten fruit in last night’s pile. She pushed half of them across to him. How many did she give?  
> - ✅ 5 → Five. Half of ten is five: two equal parts, five and five. Exactly fair. She measured it with her nose.
> - 2 → That would be a very small half. Half means two equal parts. Ten split into two equal parts is five and five. She gave five.
> - 8 → That would leave her only two. Generous, but not half. Half means two equal parts. Ten makes five and five. She gave five, and kept five.
> _For grown-ups: Found one half of 10 by splitting it into two equal parts._

**Moss**: Feed them before you feed yourself. She never said only the good ones. I checked. I was there.  
_…_  
**Moss**: Grey things remember who fed them. That’s all I’ll say. I’m old, and I’ve seen it go both ways.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, notice): respecting someone’s right to choose  
> **Moss**: When we let someone choose something about their own life, we show them respect.  
> **Moss**: Even when they’re small. Especially then. Nobody had ever let her choose anything before.  
> _For grown-ups: Talked about respecting others’ choices about their own lives._


#### `reader:s2-complete-driven` · [main](../../../side/S02.md#s2-complete-driven)

_Variant of `s2-complete`._

_Morning. Frost on the grass at the edge of the Grove, in one small circle where one grey thing slept._  
_In the hedge there is a gap the size of a thin Nubbin, and a sock caught on a thorn._  
_Sable sits by the gap for most of the morning. Then she comes back and eats her fruit, all of it._  
**Luna**: She’s eating. That’s what we wanted.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): having two feelings at once  
> _Luna is glad Sable is eating. She is also sad about Wisp. Both at once._  
> _You can feel two feelings at the same time, even opposite ones. Both are real, and both are allowed._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that people can feel mixed emotions, like glad and sad, at the same time._

**Moss**: Yes.  
**Clover**: It’s Thursday. I’m speaking to you again.  
**Clover**: I’ve hung a blanket on the hedge. In case anyone thin comes past. Nobody in particular.  
_…_  
**Moss**: Sensible things are sometimes right. It doesn’t make them warm.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, ask): understanding what a character means  
> _Moss says, “Sensible things are sometimes right. It doesn’t make them warm.” What does he mean?_  
> - ✅ A choice can be wise and still feel sad or unkind. → Yes. It was a sensible choice. Sable is eating. And somewhere out there, Wisp is on his own.
> - It’s a cold morning. → It is. But I meant the other kind of warm. A choice can be wise and still feel sad. Sable is eating. And Wisp is out there on his own.
> - You made the wrong choice. → No. I didn’t say that, and I don’t think it. I said it was sensible, and perhaps right. Only that right choices can still feel sad.
> _For grown-ups: Explained the meaning behind a character’s line about wise choices that still feel sad._


### Glossary

- **fringe**: A ragged edge of loose threads or strands, like the end of a scarf.
- **hearth**: The floor in front of a fireplace.
- **insulation**: Material that slows heat from escaping, such as wool, feathers or padding.
- **sodden**: Completely soaked with water.
- **clue**: A small detail that helps you work something out.
- **mercy**: Kindness shown to someone, even when they haven’t earned it.
- **theme**: The big idea or message a story keeps coming back to.
