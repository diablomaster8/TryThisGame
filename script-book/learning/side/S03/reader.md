# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S3: The Cycle

## S3. The Cycle

- `s3-step1`: Ask one Sun Keeper and one Moon Keeper what really happened at Midsummer. · _Fern, Marina, Sparks, Clover and Pixel are Sun. Luna, Ember, Byte, Dusty and Nova are Moon. Ask one from each side._
- `s3-step2`: Help Sparks and Byte develop the old Midsummer photo-finish plates. · _From chapter 6, go to Sparks’s workshop. Rock the tray on Byte’s steady count, not on Sparks’s “feeling”._
- `s3-step3`: Show the plates to Fern and to Ember. · _Fern is usually in her orchard. Ember is at the Circuit fence most evenings._
- `s3-step4`: Host the Lantern Moot at the pond, after the Long Night. · _In chapter 11, tap the pond and choose Call a Moot. All ten Keepers come._

#### `reader:s3-stands` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-stands)

_The Circuit stands are split down the middle: Sun Keepers on the left, Moon Keepers on the right, an empty bench between._  
**Fern**: Don’t sit there. That’s the gap. Nobody sits in the gap.  
**You**: Why not?  
**Fern**: Because it’s the gap. Hazel sat in it every race, on purpose, to annoy everyone.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): fairness: staying on nobody’s side  
> **Fern**: Go on, guess. Why do you think Hazel really sat in the gap?  
> - ✅ To show she was on nobody’s side, and they were all still one valley. → That’s what she said. Also to annoy everyone. Mostly the first thing.
> - She got there late. → Hazel was never late in her life. Early, and cross about it. No, she sat there on purpose: to show she wasn’t on either side. One valley, she used to say.
> - It was the best seat. → Byte says it’s the worst seat. He’s checked. She sat there to show she was on nobody’s side. To remind us we’re one valley.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how staying neutral can help people in a quarrel remember what they share._

_At the front stand two tea urns: same make, same tea, same shop. One has a sun painted on it, and one a moon._  
**Sparks**: Midsummer, years back, Fern’s Bramble went over the cliff rail, Sun says Ash barged her, Moon says rigged, OK bye.  
_Sparks sits down very fast and studies the race card as if it holds the meaning of life._  
**Sparks**: I didn’t say any of that. That was the wind.  
_This is a feud: a quarrel that has gone on for years. Both sides pretend not to listen. Both are bad at it._  
_Up in the owners’ box, Corvin isn’t watching the start line. He’s watching the stands._  

**Choices**

- **Sit in the gap, like Hazel did.**
    - You sit in the gap. Five heads turn from the left, and five from the right.
    - **Fern**: Oh, you’re hers all right.
    - **Byte**: That is the worst seat. You can’t see the cliff bend. I’ve checked.
- **Sit with the Sun side.**
    - **Sparks**: YES. Sun gets the new Keeper! Sun gets the new Keeper!
    - Across the gap, Luna writes something in a very small notebook.
- **Sit with the Moon side.**
    - **Byte**: Welcome. Our biscuits are better. I ran a blind taste test. It was mostly blind.
    - **Fern**: Traitor. Lovely to see you. Traitor.

#### `reader:s3-two-stories` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-two-stories)

_You get two stories. They agree about the weather and nothing else._  
_Sun says: Ember’s Ash came round the cliff bend like a cannonball, and Fern’s Bramble went over the rail._  
_Moon says: the cliff-bend flag was in the wrong place, so Ash went wide where the flag told her to._  
**Byte**: I have a chart. Both stories, colour-coded. The colours are also disputed.  
**Byte**: Two hundred and fourteen grievances in all. Three are about the race. The rest are about the urns.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.B.3, ask): reading a scaled bar graph  
> **Byte**: This year’s chart. Each square stands for 5 grievances. Urns: 9 squares. Biscuits: 4 squares.  
> **Byte**: How many more grievances about the urns than about the biscuits?  
> - ✅ 25 → Correct. Five more squares, and each square is 5. Five fives are 25. I’ve underlined it twice.
> - 5 → That’s the difference in squares. Good start. But each square stands for 5 grievances. 5 squares × 5 = 25 more grievances.
> - 45 → That’s all the urn grievances: 9 squares × 5 = 45. Biscuits have 4 × 5 = 20. So the difference is 45 − 20 = 25.
> _For grown-ups: Read a bar graph where each square stands for 5, and found a difference (25)._

**Ember**: After Midsummer, nobody would race Ash. Not Sun. Not Moon. Do you know who would?  
**Ember**: Him. He said he’d make her a champion.  
_Nobody, on either side, talks about Bramble after the rail. The story just stops there, like a path at a cliff._  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): evidence: how to settle what really happened  
> _Two stories, and they can’t both be right. What would help find out what really happened?_  
> - ✅ Evidence: something recorded at the time, like a photo. → Yes. Evidence doesn’t take sides. A photo taken on the day can’t change its mind afterwards.
> - Whoever shouts loudest. → That’s how they’ve done it for years. It hasn’t worked. Loud isn’t the same as right. You need evidence: something recorded at the time, like a photo.
> - A vote. → Votes are good for deciding what to do. They can’t decide what already happened. For that, you need evidence: something recorded at the time, like a photo.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that evidence recorded at the time is better than opinion for finding out what happened._


#### `reader:s3-plates` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-plates)

_Sparks turns up at your gate looking like she has swallowed a secret, and it’s fizzing._  
**Sparks**: OK. OK OK OK. Confession. I’ve got the photo-finish plates from Midsummer. From the camera on the cliff bend. Mine.  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): how a photo finish and old glass plates work  
> **Sparks**: A photo-finish camera takes a picture right on the line, for when a race is too close for eyes.  
> **Sparks**: Old cameras caught the picture on a glass plate. You develop it in trays of chemicals, in the dark. Then it appears.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned what a photo finish is and how old photographs were developed on glass plates._

**Sparks**: I never developed them. If it was Ash, Fern would never forgive Ember. If it wasn’t, I’d never forgive me.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): a character’s motive  
> _Sparks has had these plates for years. Why do you think she never developed them?_  
> - ✅ She was scared of what they would show, and who would get hurt. → …Yes. Not knowing was horrible. Knowing felt worse. Until now.
> - She forgot about them. → I wish. I thought about them every single race. She was scared. Whatever the plates showed, someone she loved would be hurt.
> - She was too busy. → I’m always busy. That wasn’t it. She was scared of what they’d show. Whatever it was, someone she loved would be hurt.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out why a character avoided doing something (her motive)._

**Sparks**: Trouble is, my darkroom timer’s broken. And the only person in the valley with a timer that works is…  
_She can’t say it. She points up the lane to the Moon end, at a cottage with labels on its windows._  
**Sparks**: Him.  

**Choices**

- **Fetch Byte. Tell him it’s for science.**
    - **Byte**: Science? You should have opened with that. I’ll bring three timers. One to check the other two.
- **Fetch Byte. Tell him Sparks asked nicely.**
    - **Byte**: She did not.
    - You admit she did not.
    - **Byte**: I’ll come anyway. Someone has to see it done properly.

#### `reader:s3-flag` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-flag)

_Sparks’s darkroom is red and cramped and smells of vinegar. Byte times, Sparks rocks the trays, and they argue in whispers._  
**Byte**: Red lamp only. The plates don’t react to dim red light. Anything brighter and they’re ruined.  
**Byte**: Rock on the count. One, and two, and— that was not two. That was one and a half.  
**Sparks**: I rock on FEELING, Byte.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): time as equal groups  
> **Byte**: Each plate needs 3 minutes in the tray, one plate after another. There are 3 plates. How long altogether? Quickly. Precisely.  
> - ✅ 9 minutes → Nine. Three lots of three. I’ll set the timer three times.
> - 6 minutes → That’s 3 + 3. We have three plates, not two. 3 + 3 + 3, or 3 × 3. Nine minutes.
> - 33 minutes → That’s how long it FEELS. It’s three lots of three minutes. 3 × 3 = 9. Nine minutes.
> _For grown-ups: Used multiplication as equal groups to find a total time (3 × 3 = 9 minutes)._

_The first plate swims up out of the dark: an enormous, blurry, perfectly lit thumb._  
**Byte**: Your thumb.  
**Sparks**: It’s a GOOD thumb.  
_The second plate comes up slower, on the count: the cliff bend at dawn, before the race. An empty course, and a flag._  
_And a man in a grey Circuit coat, pulling up the flag and moving it._  
**Byte**: Two posts nearer the edge. Look at the turf. Old hole there. New hole here.  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): what makes good evidence  
> _Byte says the turf proves the flag was moved. What makes the holes such good evidence?_  
> - ✅ You can still see them, and they don’t belong to either side. → Exactly. Holes don’t have opinions. Holes don’t own an urn.
> - Byte says so, and he’s clever. → I am. But that’s not evidence. That’s just me. The holes are evidence because anyone can look and check. They don’t take sides.
> - Holes are always suspicious. → Dusty would disagree. Strongly. The holes are good evidence because anyone can see them and check. They don’t take sides.
> _For grown-ups: Explained that good evidence can be checked by anyone and doesn’t depend on who is speaking._

**Sparks**: Nobody on either side moved that flag. Nobody. It was a steward.  
_The last plate is the finish, with the owners’ box behind it. Corvin isn’t watching the race. He’s watching the stands._  
**Byte**: The flag sent them wide. Ash went wide because the course told her to. Bramble went wide because Ash did.  
**Sparks**: We’ve been fighting about the wrong thing for years. Byte. We have two URNS.  
**Byte**: The urns were your idea.  
**Sparks**: They were a GOOD idea at the TIME.  

#### `reader:s3-courier` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-courier)

_Sparks wraps the plates in a tea towel with a sun on it. Byte wraps a second tea towel, with a moon on it, round that._  
**Sparks**: You show them. They both like you. It’s very annoying.  
**Byte**: A neutral courier. I approve. I’d like that noted somewhere.  
_Neutral: on nobody’s side. Courier: someone who carries things. You are, it seems, both._  
_The plates are heavier than they look. Glass, and years of not knowing._  
**Sparks**: Be careful with them. And with Fern. And with Ember. Mostly with Ember.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): sharing hard news kindly  
> **Sparks**: This news is going to hurt them both. What’s a kind way to share it?  
> - ✅ Somewhere quiet, gently, and give them time to take it in. → Yes. That. Do that. I’d have shouted it across the Circuit.
> - Shout it across the Circuit so everyone knows at once. → That’s what I’d do. That’s why I’m not doing it. Hard news is easier to hear somewhere quiet, said gently, with time to take it in.
> - Leave the plates on their doorsteps without a note. → Then they’d find out alone. Nobody should find out alone. Hard news is kindest somewhere quiet, said gently, with someone there.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how to share upsetting news gently and kindly._


**Choices**

- **Show them together, at the same time.**
    - Getting Fern and Ember into one place takes a week. You manage it by telling each that the other won’t come.
    - **Fern**: You absolute snake. Hazel did that to me once.
- **Show them one at a time.**
    - You go to Fern first, then to Ember. Each asks, before anything else, whether the other one has seen it yet.

#### `reader:s3-truce` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-truce)

_Fern looks at the plates for a long time. So does Ember. Neither looks at the other._  
**Ember**: I don’t need to be forgiven. I need to know who to be angry at.  
**Ember**: Him. It’s always him. He had Ash by the autumn.  
**Fern**: All those years. I hated you because of a flag. That’s… honestly very efficient of him.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): think question: saying sorry for a mistake you didn’t know you made  
> _Fern blamed Ember for years, and it wasn’t Ember’s fault. Fern didn’t know._  
> _Should Fern say sorry? What do you think?_  
> - Yes. She hurt Ember, even by mistake. → That’s a fair answer. Fern would probably agree. A mistake still leaves a bruise.
> - No. She was tricked. The one who moved the flag should say sorry. → That’s a fair answer. Ember says nearly the same: she wants to know who to be angry at.
> - They could both say sorry for the years they lost. → That’s a fair answer, and a generous one. Sometimes “sorry” just means “I wish it had been different.”
> _For grown-ups: Thought about apology and blame when someone was tricked; there was no wrong answer._

**Ember**: There should be a Moot. Everyone. Both sides. Out in the open.  
**Fern**: After midwinter. When there’s time to do it properly. And one urn.  
**Ember**: One urn.  
_It’s the first thing they’ve agreed on in years: a truce. They both look faintly ill about it._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, notice): meetings where people settle things together  
> _“Moot” is a very old English word for a meeting where people come together to talk things out._  
> _Long ago, English villages held moots to settle quarrels and agree on rules, out in the open._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the old word “moot” and how communities met to settle disagreements fairly._


#### `reader:s3-invitations` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-invitations)

_Pixel has been put in charge of the invitations. So there is a planning meeting about the invitations._  
**Pixel**: Gold ink or silver? Gold is Sun. Silver is Moon. Black is a funeral. I’ve tested twelve inks. Everything means something.  
**Clover**: Green? Nobody’s green.  
**Pixel**: …Clover, that’s genius. Green. Nobody’s green.  

> **Lesson** (art, ask): mixing colours to make green  
> **Pixel**: Problem. I have twelve inks and none of them is green. Which two paints do I mix?  
> - ✅ Yellow and blue → Yellow and blue. Green! I knew that. I wanted to see if you knew that.
> - Red and blue → That makes purple. Lovely, but very royal. Nobody wants to be royal. Yellow and blue make green.
> - Red and yellow → That makes orange. Very Sun. Too Sun. Yellow and blue make green.
> _For grown-ups: Practised colour mixing: yellow and blue paint make green._

_Dusty has offered to dig a hole for the one urn. Nobody asked for a hole, and nobody has the heart to say so._  
**Nova**: I’ve been to this valley four times and I still don’t know if I’m Sun or Moon. Somebody said it’s my scarf.  
**Nova**: I’ve decided I’m weather.  
**Luna**: Ember says she’s coming “to make sure it’s done right”. That’s Ember for yes.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): division word problem: how many packs are needed  
> **Pixel**: Ten Keepers, plus one for you. Eleven invitations. Green cards come in packs of 4. How many packs do I buy?  
> - ✅ 3 packs → Three. Two packs is only 8, not enough. Three packs is 12, with one spare. For mistakes. I make mistakes.
> - 2 packs → Two packs is 8 cards. Three people wouldn’t get one. Imagine the grievances. Three packs is 12. Enough for eleven, with one spare.
> - 11 packs → That’s 44 cards! I’d be writing until spring. Each pack has 4. Three packs is 12, enough for eleven, with one spare.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out how many packs of 4 are needed for 11 cards (3, with one spare)._


#### `reader:s3-moot` · [main](../../../side/S03.md#s3-moot)

_Evening at the pond. Ten Keepers, one urn, green invitations, and nobody sure where to stand._  
_Dusty has dug the hole. The one urn stands in it, so that it is exactly level with nobody. Dusty is very proud._  
**Gran Hazel**: A Moot! I haven’t been to a Moot since my hair was brown. Isn’t this awkward. I love it.  
_You set the plates on the bench by the pond, between the two groups. Sun and Moon come to look, and then everyone._  
**Sparks**: It was the flag. I had it in a box the whole time. I’m loud about everything except the one thing.  
**Fern**: Bramble went over that rail because a man moved a flag. Not because of Ash. Not because of anyone here.  
_Pixel pours the last of the sun urn and the last of the moon urn into the one urn. Byte tastes it, gravely._  
**Byte**: Same tea. It was always the same tea. I’d like that read into the record.  
**Sparks**: Byte, I swear by every lantern in this valley—  
**Byte**: Into the record.  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): planning a fair test  
> **Byte**: For the record, I tested it properly first. Which test is fairest, to prove two teas are the same?  
> - ✅ Same cups, same amount, same heat, and the taster doesn’t know which is which. → Yes. Change nothing except the thing you’re testing. And hide the labels, so nobody tastes what they expect. That’s a fair test. I did it eleven times. Same tea, every time.
> - Ask Sparks which one she likes best. → Sun. Obviously. Sun. Exactly why not. She knows which is which. A fair test hides the labels and keeps everything else the same.
> - Taste the sun tea hot and the moon tea cold. → Then you’re testing hot against cold, not sun against moon. A fair test changes only one thing. Same cups, same amount, same heat, labels hidden.
> _For grown-ups: Learned what makes a fair test: change one thing, keep the rest the same, and test blind._

**Ember**: So. Midsummer. A Cup. Everybody runs. Same course, new flags, and I check them.  
**Fern**: We check them.  
**Byte**: I’ll check the checking.  
**Gran Hazel**: And who’s keeping the Moot, love? Somebody has to bang the spoon.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, notice): ending a quarrel by talking openly  
> **Gran Hazel**: Disagreeing is fine, love. Valleys are full of it. It’s the whispering behind urns that does the damage.  
> **Gran Hazel**: Say it to their faces, kindly, with the evidence on the table. That’s how a feud ends.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard how talking openly and honestly, with facts, can end a long quarrel._


**Choices**

- **Bang the spoon on the urn.**
    - You bang the spoon on the urn, and ten Keepers stop talking at once. It is the most powerful you have ever felt.
    - **Pixel**: Ribbons! Gold and silver, stitched down the middle in green. One for every Nubbin who runs the Cup.
    - **Gran Hazel**: Moot-Keeper. Told you it’d suit.
- **Give the spoon to Fern and Ember to share.**
    - Fern and Ember hold the spoon between them and bang the urn together. Both miss, and the whole pond cheers.
    - **Pixel**: Ribbons! Gold and silver, stitched down the middle in green. One for every Nubbin who runs the Cup.
    - **Gran Hazel**: You’re still the Moot-Keeper, love. Somebody had to bring the spoon.

### Glossary

- **feud**: A long, bitter quarrel between two people or groups.
- **grievance**: A complaint about something you feel was unfair.
- **evidence**: Facts or things you can see and check that show what is true.
- **develop**: To grow or build up; with photos, to treat film or plates with chemicals so the picture appears.
- **steward**: Someone whose job is to look after a place or an event, like a race.
- **neutral**: Not on either side in a quarrel.
- **courier**: Someone who carries messages or packages from one person to another.
- **truce**: An agreement to stop fighting or quarrelling.
- **moot**: An old word for a meeting where people gather to talk things out and decide together.
- **fair test**: An experiment where you change only one thing and keep everything else the same.
