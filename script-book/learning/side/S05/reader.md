# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S5: Fossil’s Last Walk

## S5. Fossil’s Last Walk

- `s5-step1`: Carry Fossil to the cliff where Dusty found her egg. · _The cliff is east, past the stream. Walk, don’t run. She’s very old, and she likes to look at things._
- `s5-step2`: Take Fossil to Hazel’s first tree and shake it once. · _It’s the low, crooked tree across the stream. Build a gentle shake, not a huge one. One fruit only._
- `s5-step3`: Hold Fossil up in the shallows of the pond. · _Bring Fossil to the pond’s edge and choose Swim. One of your swimmers can hold her, or Marina will._
- `s5-step4`: Sit with them until dark. · _Stay still near Fossil and your youngest Nubbin until night falls. If you wander off, she’ll wait._

#### `reader:s5-list` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-list)

_Dusty is at the gate with a very old Nubbin in the crook of one arm and a much-folded bit of paper in the other hand._  
_The Nubbin is the colour of sand. When she blinks, she does it slowly, as if each blink costs something._  
**Dusty**: This is Fossil. I dug her egg out of the cliff when I was eleven. So I called her Fossil. I was eleven. It was the best name I had.  
**Dusty**: She’s very old now. She’s fading. Not her glow. Her. You’ll have seen it, if you’ve looked.  
**Dusty**: Twenty years. You’d think I’d have run out of things to say to her. I’ve got a list.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.OA.A.1, ask): addition word problem within 100  
> _Dusty was 11 when they dug out Fossil’s egg. That was 20 years ago. How old is Dusty now?_  
> - ✅ 31 → Thirty-one. Don’t say it like that. Thirty-one is young. For a cliff.
> - 9 → Ha. I wish. That’s 20 take away 11. Twenty years have passed since Dusty was 11, so we add: 11 + 20 = 31.
> - 30 → Nearly. Don’t round me down, I’ve earned that year. Start at 11 and add 20: 21, 31. Dusty is 31.
> _For grown-ups: Added 11 + 20 = 31 to work out an age from a starting age and years passed._

_Dusty unfolds the paper. It has been folded and unfolded so often it has gone soft as cloth._  
**Dusty**: Three things. My back’s gone; I can’t carry her and do the things. Would you?  
**Dusty**: One, the cliff. Two, Hazel’s first tree. Three, the water. She used to swim like she was late for something.  
_Fossil looks up at you, unhurried, and holds out her arms the way very small children do._  
_Fossil says nothing. Her slow blink lands on you, and stays._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS1-1, notice): life cycles: being born, growing, growing old, dying  
> _Every living thing has a life cycle. It is born, it grows, it grows old, and in the end it dies._  
> _Fossil is near the end of hers. That is why Dusty has a list._  
> **Dusty**: Old isn’t over. Old is when you do the things that matter. While you can.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that every living thing has a life cycle that includes growing old and dying, in plain, gentle words._


**Choices**

- **Lift her with both hands, like an egg.**
    - You lift her the way you’d lift a full cup. She settles against you, approving.
    - **Dusty**: That’s it. That’s exactly it.
- **Let her ride on your shoulder.**
    - She climbs up, slowly, and sits on your shoulder like a very old queen inspecting something ridiculous.
    - **Dusty**: Oh, she likes you. The last person who tried that got their ear nibbled.

#### `reader:s5-cliff` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-cliff)

_At the foot of the cliff the rock is striped like a cake: brown, red, grey, brown. Each stripe is a layer._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-ESS1-1, do): rock layers: the oldest is at the bottom  
> **Dusty**: Every stripe was mud or sand once, laid down flat, one on top of the last. Over ages, it pressed into rock.  
> **Dusty**: Go on. Tap them in order. Oldest first.  
> _(Tap the layers from oldest to newest.)_  
> **Dusty**: Bottom to top. The bottom one was laid down first, so it’s the oldest. Everything above came later.  
> Check: `edu:order:rock-layers:oldest-first`
> _For grown-ups: Ordered rock layers by age, learning that lower layers are usually older than the ones above._

**Dusty**: Here. I was twelve. It was raining. I used a trowel.  
_Fossil leans out and taps the rock, twice, very firmly._  
**Dusty**: …Eleven. Fine. Eleven. And it was sunny.  
_Tap._  
**Dusty**: A spoon. It was a spoon. A big spoon. You try digging out a whole egg with a spoon. It took two days.  
_Tap._  
**Dusty**: …Four days. And a teaspoon. I’d like it noted that I was extremely thorough.  
_Fossil lays her head against the rock and leaves it there a while. Dusty doesn’t say anything at all._  
**Dusty**: I tell it wrong on purpose. Every time. Twenty years, and she’s never once let me get away with it.  
_By her head a little fern grows out of a crack, curled tight. Pressed into the stone beside it is a much older one._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS4-1, notice): fossils show what lived long ago  
> **Dusty**: That’s a fossil. A real one. Not her. The fern.  
> **Dusty**: Long ago a fern was buried in mud. Very slowly the mud turned to stone, and kept the fern’s shape.  
> **Dusty**: Fossils tell us what lived here before any of us. Ferns, it turns out. Ferns were here first.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned how a fossil forms and that fossils show us what lived long ago._


#### `reader:s5-tree-ask` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-tree-ask)

**Dusty**: Next, Hazel’s first tree. The crooked one over the stream. Shake it once. She was very specific.  
**Dusty**: Well. I was. On her behalf.  
_Fossil falls asleep on the way. She wakes exactly as you reach the tree, as if she set an alarm._  
_The tree is low and crooked, and its trunk is wider than you can reach round._  
**Dusty**: Hazel planted it the year she came. Forty years old. Twice as old as Fossil.  
**Dusty**: If it ever came down, you could count the rings in the stump: one ring for every year it grew. Let’s not find out.  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): tree rings: one ring for every year of growth  
> _One ring for every year the tree grew. This one would have forty._  
> _That is how people can tell how old a tree is: they count the rings in the stump._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a tree grows one ring each year, so counting rings shows its age._


#### `reader:s5-tree` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-tree)

_Every Nubbin you own has followed you here. They sit in a row under the branches with their mouths open._  
**Dusty**: Once. Not twice. Twice is just weather.  
_You shake it, once. One fruit drops: small, wrinkled, an odd yellow-brown, like an apple that has heard bad news._  
_It misses every open mouth in the row and lands in Fossil’s lap, as if it knew the way._  
**Dusty**: That’s the one. Hazel gave her one the day she hatched. She ate it in one go and was sick on my shoe.  
_Fossil takes the fruit in both hands but doesn’t eat it yet. She holds it, and smells it, and closes her eyes._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-2, notice): smell and memory  
> _Smell is one of the senses most closely tied to memory._  
> _One sniff of something from long ago can bring the past rushing back, as if you were there again._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that the sense of smell is strongly linked to memories._

**Dusty**: Hazel stood where you are and said, “Well, she’s yours now. Good luck to you both.” I think she meant me.  
_Fossil eats a third of it, very slowly. Then she hands the rest to Dusty, who eats it, though it is horrible._  
**Dusty**: Tastes like being eleven.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.NF.A.1, ask): fractions of a whole  
> _Fossil ate one third of the fruit. What fraction did Dusty eat?_  
> - ✅ Two thirds → Yes. Three thirds make a whole. Fossil had one, so Dusty had the other two. Two horrible thirds.
> - One third → That’s Fossil’s share. A whole fruit is three thirds. Take away Fossil’s one third, and two thirds are left for Dusty.
> - One half → Halves would be two equal parts. This fruit was cut into three equal parts: thirds. Fossil ate one third, so Dusty ate the other two thirds.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out that a whole is three thirds, so taking away one third leaves two thirds._


#### `reader:s5-water-ask` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-water-ask)

**Dusty**: Last one. The water. She can’t swim on her own any more.  
**Dusty**: Marina taught her, years back, with Hazel’s lot. Says she was the only one who swam sideways on purpose.  
_Marina is already at the pond, sleeves rolled up, as though somebody sent for her._  
_Dusty is very bad at secrets._  
**Marina**: I was passing. With a towel. As you do.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): using clues to find a character’s real motive  
> _Marina says she was “just passing”. Sleeves rolled up. A towel. What’s really going on?_  
> - ✅ Dusty asked Marina to come and help, and Marina is pretending it’s nothing. → Yes. The towel and the sleeves are the clues. Nobody just passes with a towel.
> - Marina was going for a swim anyway. → Possible. But look at the clues: sleeves rolled up for lifting, a towel ready, and Dusty bad at secrets. Dusty asked Marina to come. Marina is pretending it’s nothing.
> - She’s lost. → Marina knows every rock in this valley. She’s not lost. The towel and sleeves are clues: Dusty asked Marina to help, and she’s pretending it’s nothing.
> _For grown-ups: Used clues in the text to work out a character’s real reason for being there._

**Dusty**: Course you were.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): quiet kindness  
> _Sometimes kind people pretend a big favour is nothing. That way, the other person doesn’t feel they owe them._  
> _Dusty knows. Marina knows Dusty knows. Nobody says so. That’s a kind of kindness too._  
> _For grown-ups: Noticed a quiet kind of kindness: helping without making a fuss about it._


**Choices**

- **Let one of your swimmers hold her.**
    - Your swimmer wades out and waits, very serious, as if it has been given the most important job in the valley.
    - **Marina**: Paw under the chin. Like a cup. There you go.
    - **Dusty**: It’s holding her like she’s the last egg in the valley. …Don’t tell it I said that.
- **Ask Marina to hold her.**
    - Marina wades in to her waist and holds out her hands.
    - **Marina**: Come on then, old girl. Same as always.

#### `reader:s5-water` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-water)

_You lower Fossil into the water, and it takes her weight. For a moment she is stiff and heavy. Then she is light._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): water pushes up on things in it  
> **Marina**: Water pushes up on anything that’s in it. That push holds up some of her weight.  
> **Marina**: That’s why she suddenly feels light. The water is helping to carry her.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that water pushes upwards on objects in it, which makes them feel lighter._

_Then, very slowly, she kicks._  
**Dusty**: Look at her. That’s the stroke. The little sideways one. Like she’s late for something.  
**Dusty**: To the reeds and back, Fos. Then you can be smug about it.  
_She swims to the reeds and back. It takes a long time, and nobody hurries her._  
_When you lift her out she is shivering, and her eyes are bright. She isn’t looking at Dusty. She’s looking at the Grove._  

> **Lesson** (health, ask): why bodies shiver  
> **Marina**: Towel. Quick. Why do you think she’s shivering?  
> - ✅ Her body is shaking its muscles to make heat after the cold water. → Yes. Shivering is your muscles working fast to warm you up. A towel and a cuddle help it along.
> - She’s scared. → Look at her eyes. Bright. Not scared. Shivering after cold water is the body shaking its muscles to make heat. A towel helps.
> - She’s excited. → She might be, a little! But the shiver is from the cold. Shivering is the body shaking its muscles to make heat. A towel helps it along.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that shivering is the body making heat by moving muscles quickly._

**Dusty**: …That’s not on my list. Fossil’s got a list of her own.  

#### `reader:s5-secret` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-secret)

_You set Fossil down on the bank. She walks across the Grove on her own, one slow step at a time._  
_Not to Dusty, and not to you. She goes to your youngest Nubbin, who is busy falling over a fruit, and sits beside it._  
**Dusty**: …Oh.  
_Your youngest stops falling over and looks at Fossil, and Fossil looks back. Then they just sit._  
**Dusty**: She did this for me once. I was small and I’d had a bad day. She just sat. Didn’t fix anything. Didn’t try.  
**Dusty**: Nobody ever taught her that. She just knew, years before I did.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): being there for someone  
> **Dusty**: She didn’t fix my bad day. Not one bit of it. So why do you think it helped?  
> - ✅ Because I wasn’t alone with it any more. → That’s it. You can’t always fix it. You can always sit next to it.
> - It didn’t help. Only fixing things helps. → I thought that too, when I was small. But some things can’t be fixed. Having someone beside you means you’re not alone with them. That helps more than you’d think.
> - Because she was warm. → She was. Still is, just about. But mostly it helped because I wasn’t alone with it any more. Someone was there.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about how being with someone helps, even when you can’t fix their problem._


#### `reader:s5-gift` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-gift)

_The sun goes down behind the western hill, in the west, where it sets every evening. Fossil doesn’t move, nor does your youngest, and after a while nor do you._  
_The sky turns orange, then pink, then a deep, quiet blue._  
_One by one, the first stars come out over the pond._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-ESS1-1, notice): why stars only show at night  
> _The stars were there all day. The Sun’s bright light hid them._  
> _Once the Sun has set and the sky grows dark, the stars can shine through._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that stars are in the sky all the time but are hidden by daylight._

_Behind you, the rest of your Nubbins have given up waiting and fallen asleep in a heap. One of them snores in a very small voice._  
**Dusty**: That’s your whole army, is it?  
_Dusty looks at the heap, and then at Fossil, and says no more. Dusty is smiling._  
_When it’s dark, the old Nubbin gets up and touches her nose to your youngest’s nose._  
_Then Fossil walks back to Dusty, slowly, the way she does everything._  
_Your youngest sits up straighter than you’ve ever seen it sit._  
_On its stats sheet, under Stamina, are new words: “A gift from Fossil.”_  

#### `reader:s5-home` · [main](../../../side/S05.md#s5-home)

_Dusty picks Fossil up to carry her home, bad back or not. The ball above her head is very pale now._  
_It doesn’t glow. It only gets paler, the way a sky does at the end of the day._  
**Dusty**: Thank you. I mean it. I don’t say that much. Ask anyone. They’ll tell you I never say it.  
**Dusty**: I think a Nubbin’s glow fades slower with someone walking beside it. I’d like to think so.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): think question: what helps when someone we love is very old  
> _Fossil is very old. Dusty knows she won’t be alive much longer, and it hurts._  
> _What do you think helps, when someone you love is near the end of their life?_  
> - Doing the things they love, together. → That’s a fair answer. It’s why I made the list.
> - Telling them what they mean to you. → That’s a fair answer. I should do more of that. I’m bad at it. Ask anyone.
> - Just being there, like Fossil did. → That’s a fair answer. She taught me that one herself.
> - I don’t know. It just feels sad. → That’s a fair answer, and an honest one. It is sad. Being sad about it is allowed.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about ageing and saying goodbye; there was no wrong answer. Feelings of sadness were named as normal._

_Fossil reaches out to you. In her fist is a curled seed from the fern at the foot of the cliff._  
**Dusty**: She wants you to plant it. Fossil-fern. Grows out of rock, if you’re patient. Course it does.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS1-1, notice): real ferns grow from spores, not seeds  
> **Dusty**: Mind you, real ferns don’t make seeds. They make spores, tiny as dust, in brown dots under their leaves.  
> **Dusty**: The wind carries the spores off, and new ferns grow where they land. This is a Grove fern. It ignores rules.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that real ferns reproduce with tiny spores rather than seeds._

**Dusty**: And have this. Don’t argue. I bought it the week after I dug her out. Never needed it like I needed the spoon.  
_Dusty’s trowel. The handle is worn pale where a hand has held it for twenty years._  
_At the gate, Dusty lifts it and pushes without thinking, one-handed, with Fossil in the other arm._  
_On the western hill, Moss watches the gate long after it has closed._  

### Glossary

- **crook**: The bend on the inside of your elbow.
- **life cycle**: The stages a living thing goes through: being born, growing, growing old and dying.
- **layer**: A flat sheet of something lying on top of or under another, like the stripes in a cliff.
- **fossil**: The shape of a plant or animal from long ago, preserved in rock.
- **specific**: Exact and particular, with no room for mistakes.
- **fraction**: A part of a whole, such as one half or one third.
- **smug**: Very pleased with yourself, and showing it.
- **spore**: A tiny speck, like dust, that ferns and mushrooms use to grow new ferns and mushrooms.
