# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S6: Ash’s True Name

## S6. Ash’s True Name

- `s6-s1`: Ember has asked you over. Go and see Ash. · _Ember’s house is past the dojo on the Moon side. Knock. She’ll pretend she wasn’t waiting._
- `s6-s2`: Find what Hazel wrote about the egg she gave Ember. · _In the cottage, tap the bookshelf by the stove. Hazel kept her record books on the bottom shelf._
- `s6-s3`: Take the register to Ember, and practise the three notes by the pond. · _Pick the pattern Hazel wrote: low, high, and a little one going up. There’s no timer, and a wrong try costs nothing._
- `s6-s4`: Be in Ember’s kitchen when she whistles to Ash. · _Go to Ember’s house after dark. Stand by the door and let her do it._

#### `reader:s6-kitchen` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-kitchen)

_A note under your door at dawn: COME OVER. Then PLEASE, crossed out, and written again._  
_Ember’s house is very tidy. It’s the kind of tidy you get when someone cleans because they’re angry._  
**Ember**: She’s out of Corvin’s garden. Don’t ask how. I don’t care how, and I never will.  
_A grey Nubbin sits in the corner of the kitchen, facing the wall. Beside her is a full bowl._  
**Ember**: Ash.  
**Ember**: Ash, love. It’s me. Ash.  
_The grey Nubbin doesn’t turn. A drip falls from the tap. She doesn’t turn for that either._  
**Ember**: Four hundred and twelve times since yesterday. I counted. Tell Byte I counted and I’ll deny it.  
**Ember**: She eats when I leave the room. She sleeps standing up.  
**Ember**: It isn’t her name any more. To her it’s just a noise.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): use a character’s actions to work out her real feelings  
> _Ember says she doesn’t care. But look at her clues: the PLEASE on the note, and all that counting._  
> _How does Ember really feel about Ash?_  
> - ✅ She cares about Ash more than anything. → Yes. People sometimes hide big feelings behind cross words. Ember’s clues give her away.
> - She doesn’t mind either way. → That’s what she says. But nobody counts to four hundred and twelve about something they don’t mind. Her actions show she cares a lot.
> - She’s cross with Ash. → She does sound cross. But she’s cross because she’s worried. The PLEASE and the counting show how much she cares.
> _For grown-ups: Used clues in what a character does, not just what she says, to work out her real feelings._


**Choices**

- **Sit on the floor near Ash, and wait.**
    - You sit for a long time. Ash doesn’t look at you, but she stops pressing quite so hard into the wall.
    - **Ember**: …Hazel used to do that. Sit on the floor as if it were a perfectly good chair. It drove me mad.
- **Say “Ash” yourself, gently.**
    - Ash doesn’t turn. Ember lets out a long breath. It might be relief.
    - **Ember**: See? It’s not me. It isn’t just me.
- **Ask what Corvin called her.**
    - **Ember**: Nothing. He didn’t call them anything. He just stood where they could see him.
    - **Ember**: Years of nobody saying her name. You’d forget too.

#### `reader:s6-egg` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-egg)

_Ember puts the kettle on. She doesn’t make any tea._  
_The kettle starts to whistle, thin and high. Ember doesn’t move to take it off._  
**Ember**: When she was tiny she answered to everything. Her name, my name, the kettle.  
_The whistle gets louder. At last Ember lifts the kettle off the stove, and the sound dies away._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, notice): sounds are made by vibrations  
> _Why does a kettle whistle? Steam rushes out through a tiny hole in the whistle on the spout._  
> _That makes the air there shake very fast. A fast shake like that is called a vibration, and vibrations make every sound._  
> _No vibration, no sound. When the steam slowed down, the whistle stopped._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that sounds are made by vibrations, using Ember’s whistling kettle._

**Ember**: I named her Ash a week after I got her. Before that she was just the egg from Hazel’s.  
**Ember**: Go home. Thanks for coming. Don’t make it a thing.  

#### `reader:s6-fern` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-fern)

_Fern is leaning on the fence between your gardens, eating an apple. It is clearly from your tree._  
**Fern**: You’ve been at Ember’s. You’ve got the look. The look of someone told not to make it a thing.  
**You**: Ash won’t answer to her name.  
**Fern**: Ember asked you over? Ember doesn’t like anyone. She doesn’t like me, and I’m delightful.  
**Fern**: …Poor Ash. Poor Ember. Don’t tell her I said “poor”.  
**You**: Ash came from one of Hazel’s eggs.  
**Fern**: Then she’s in the book. Hazel wrote down every egg she ever gave away, and the first sound each one made.  
**Fern**: “For the record,” Hazel said. “Whose record?” I said. “Theirs,” said Hazel.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): choose the right meaning of a multiple-meaning word  
> _“Record” is a word with more than one meaning._  
> _When Hazel says “for the record”, which kind of record does she mean?_  
> - ✅ Writing something down so it’s remembered. → That’s it. A record is a written note of what happened. Hazel kept a record of every egg.
> - The fastest time ever, like a race record. → That’s a real meaning too! Sparks loves those. But Hazel means writing things down, so they’re remembered.
> - A music disc that plays songs. → Ha. Hazel did have a few of those. But here a record means a written note, kept so nobody forgets.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out which meaning of a multiple-meaning word (“record”) fits the sentence._

**Fern**: Bottom shelf, behind the jam. Don’t eat the jam. Nobody knows what the jam was.  

#### `reader:s6-register` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-register)

_Behind the jam: a fat book, soft at the corners. On the cover, in Hazel’s writing: “EGGS.”_  
_Underneath, smaller: “Do not lend this to Byte.”_  
_Halfway through, a new section begins, under a heading she has underlined twice: “Given away.”_  
_Forty years of eggs, in pencil, with the weather. You can’t help reading the others on the way._  
**Gran Hazel**: “Egg 7. Given to Sparks, aged six. First sound: a bang. Not from the egg. From Sparks.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “Egg 23. Given to Byte, who asked for a receipt. Gave him one. First sound: a hiccup. He wrote that down too.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “Egg 41. Given to Ember, aged fifteen, very serious. Promised to feed it before herself. Meant it.”  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.OA.A.1, ask): find the difference between two numbers by counting on  
> _Byte’s egg was number 23. Ember’s egg was number 41._  
> _How many more eggs had Hazel given away by Ember’s egg than by Byte’s?_  
> - ✅ 18 → Yes! 41 − 23 = 18. Count on from 23: 7 more makes 30, then 11 more makes 41. 7 + 11 = 18.
> - 28 → Close, but let’s check. From 23, count on 7 to reach 30, then 11 more to reach 41. 7 + 11 = 18.
> - 64 → 64 is 41 + 23, so that’s adding them. We want the difference: 41 − 23 = 18.
> _For grown-ups: Found the difference between two numbers by counting on (41 − 23 = 18)._

**Gran Hazel**: “First sound: three notes. Low, high, and a little one at the end, like a question.”  
_Hazel has drawn the notes as three dots: one low, one high, and a tiny one trailing upwards._  

> **Lesson** (music, notice): written music shows high and low notes  
> _Hazel’s dots work just like written music._  
> _In written music, notes are drawn as dots. A dot higher up means a higher sound. A dot lower down means a lower one._  
> _So you can see a tune’s shape before you hear it: low, high, and a little step up._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that written music shows high and low notes by how high the dots sit._


#### `reader:s6-practise` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-practise)

_Ember reads the page twice. Then she shuts the book on her own thumb, on purpose._  
**Ember**: And if I do it and she doesn’t turn round?  
**Ember**: Right now I can still hope she remembers.  
**Ember**: If I whistle her real name and she doesn’t turn, I’ll know she’s forgotten it for good.  
**Ember**: I’d rather not know.  
_She follows you to the pond anyway, the register under her arm, scowling at the water._  
**Ember**: I can’t sing. Don’t ask me to sing. I can whistle.  
**Ember**: You first. I’m not doing it till I know it doesn’t sound stupid.  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, do): feel your own voice vibrate  
> _Before you whistle, warm up like a singer. Hum a long “mmmm” and rest your fingers on the front of your throat._  
> _Can you feel the buzz? That’s your voice box vibrating. Now hum a high note, then a low one._  
> Check: `edu:hum:throat-buzz`
> _For grown-ups: Felt their own throat vibrate while humming, to learn that sound comes from vibration._

_Your Nubbins gather on the bank to watch. It is the most interesting thing to happen all week._  
_Your Nubbins hum along. One hums so low that the water shivers. Another squeaks, and a frog leaves._  
**Ember**: Low, high, little one. Like a question. If a duck answers, we’re stopping.  

> **Lesson** (music, notice): pitch: high and low notes  
> **Ember**: Low, high. That’s pitch. How high or low a sound is.  
> _Fast vibrations make high notes. Slow vibrations make low notes. A good whistle can slide from one to the other._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the word pitch, and that faster vibrations make higher notes._

_There are no ducks on the pond. Ember checks anyway._  

**Choices**

- **Whistle: high, low, a little one.**
    - One of your Nubbins sneezes. Ember laughs once, before she can stop it.
    - **Ember**: That was a duck. The shape’s upside down. Again.
- **Whistle: low, a little one, high.**
    - The last note goes up and keeps going, like a kettle.
    - **Ember**: That’s not a question. That’s a complaint. Again.
- **Whistle: low, high, and a little one at the end.**
    - Low, high, and a small one tipped up at the end, like a question.
    - Every Nubbin on the bank turns its head at once. Your first one trots over to see who called.
    - **Ember**: …Oh.
    - **Ember**: Again. Slower. I want to hear it.

#### `reader:s6-ember-tries` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-ember-tries)

_Ember puts two fingers in her mouth and whistles the three notes, quietly, to nobody._  

> **Lesson** (music, notice): a tune is a pattern of notes  
> _Ember whistled quietly, and the notes still came out in their pattern: low, high, and a small one at the end._  
> _A tune is a pattern of high and low notes. Play it slow, fast or quiet, and the pattern stays the same._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a tune is a pattern of notes that stays recognisable at different speeds._

_Your Nubbins don’t come to her. They don’t know her yet._  
_From the far side of the pond, a duck nobody has ever seen before answers, loudly._  
**Ember**: No.  
_Ember glares at the duck until it leaves. It takes a while. It is a very confident duck._  
_It doesn’t matter. She heard herself do it._  
**Ember**: Tonight. Before I can think about it. You’re coming, and you’re not saying anything.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): what helps when you’re scared to try (think question)  
> _Ember was scared to try, in case it didn’t work. Now she’s going tonight, before she can talk herself out of it._  
> _What helps you when you’re scared to try something?_  
> - Doing it quickly, before I can worry. → That’s my way. Jump before you think. It works more often than it should. That’s a fair answer. For lots of people, the waiting is the scariest part.
> - Practising first, somewhere safe. → That’s a good answer. Ember practised by the pond first, to nobody, and it helped her a lot.
> - Having a friend with me. → That’s a good answer too. It’s why Ember wants you there tonight, even if she’d never say so.
> - Telling myself it’s all right if it doesn’t work. → That’s a wise answer. Trying is brave, even when it doesn’t go right the first time.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about ways to face a scary first try (a think question with no wrong answer)._


#### `reader:s6-whistle` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-whistle)

_Ember’s kitchen after dark, with one lamp lit. Ash is in the corner, facing the wall._  
_Ember kneels a little way off, not too close. She has thought hard about the distance._  
**Ember**: If this doesn’t work, you walk me to the pond and we don’t talk. Agreed?  
**You**: Agreed.  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-2, notice): animals turn their ears to catch sounds  
> _Cats and horses turn their ears towards a sound they know. So does Ash._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that many animals move their ears towards a sound they know._

_She puts two fingers to her mouth. For a moment nothing comes out at all._  
_Then three notes. Low, high, and a small one at the end, like a question._  
_Ash’s ear moves. One ear, a very little._  
_Ember whistles it again, slower. Her hands are shaking, so it wobbles. But it’s still the right shape._  
_Ash turns round._  
_Then a sound, small and cracked: pip. wirr. a? The first sound in the register, half remembered._  
_She crosses the kitchen one careful step at a time and puts her grey head against Ember’s knee._  
**Ember**: Hello, you. It’s me. It was always me.  

**Choices**

- **Stay by the door, very quietly.**
    - You stay. Ember cries without making any noise at all. You suspect she has practised.
    - Ash lets her.
- **Let yourself out. This part is theirs.**
    - You lift the latch slowly so it doesn’t click.
    - Behind you, Ember whistles the three notes again, and again. Every time, Ash answers.

#### `reader:s6-complete` · [main](../../../side/S06.md#s6-complete)

_Next morning Ember is at your gate. She lifts it and pushes without thinking._  
_She learned Hazel’s gate trick at fifteen, the year she got Ash’s egg._  
_Ash walks so close to Ember’s boots that Ember has to walk carefully. She is still grey._  
**Ember**: Clover says the grey stays. Clover says you can only make it lighter.  
**Ember**: She ate breakfast with me in the room. That’s lighter. Not her colour. Her mornings.  
**Ember**: She answers to both now. The whistle’s hers. Ash is the one I gave her. She can keep both.  
**Ember**: This was her first ribbon. I kept it in a drawer for years, like a soppy old thing. Give it to one of yours.  
**Ember**: And this. Two fingers, like that. Now blow. No. Less spit.  
_Try one: a wet pfff. Try two: a squeak, like a door._  
_Try three: a honk. Across the pond, the very confident duck honks back._  
**Ember**: Don’t encourage it.  
_Try four: nothing. On the fifth, your whistle carries across the Grove, and three of your Nubbins come running._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, notice): mistakes are part of learning  
> _Four tries that didn’t work, and one that did. That’s how almost everyone learns to whistle._  
> _Getting it wrong while you practise isn’t failing. It’s part of learning._  
> _For grown-ups: Saw that practice and mistakes are a normal part of learning a new skill._

**Ember**: Don’t tell anyone I like you. I’ve got a reputation.  

> **Lesson** (music, do): sing a three-note pattern  
> _Your turn, for real. Whistle or hum Ash’s three notes: low, high, and a little one going up._  
> Check: `edu:hum:tune:low-high-question`
> _For grown-ups: Sang or whistled a three-note pattern from a description of its shape._


### Glossary

- **deny**: To say that something isn’t true, or that you didn’t do it.
- **vibration**: A very fast shaking, back and forth. Vibrations make sound.
- **delightful**: Very pleasant and lovely to be around.
- **receipt**: A slip of paper that shows you have been given, or paid for, something.
- **scowling**: Frowning in a cross or grumpy way.
- **pitch**: How high or low a sound is.
- **confident**: Sure of yourself, and not at all shy.
- **reputation**: What other people think someone is like.

**Notes:** Lessons: sound and vibration (kettle, humming), pitch and written notes, ears and hearing, a tune as a pattern; reading feelings from actions; multiple-meaning “record”; 41 − 23; think question on facing a scary first try. The do lessons are hum-along beats with no scoring: any attempt counts. Ember was fifteen when Hazel gave her the egg (canon). The register’s cover says only “EGGS.”; “Given away.” heads a section inside, as in the source.
