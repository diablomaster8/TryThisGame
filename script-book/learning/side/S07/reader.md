# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S7: Unverified

## S7. Unverified

- `s7-s1`: Find Byte after Moss’s talk and hear what he’s writing. · _Byte stayed behind on the low wall by the long grass, with his notebook._
- `s7-s2`: See the timer Pixel has built for Null. · _Pixel’s workbench is behind the lantern shed. Follow the ticking._
- `s7-s3`: After a week of bells, go and see how Null is doing. · _Visit Byte’s house when the journal says a week has passed. A week is seven days._
- `s7-s4`: Spend an evening with Byte and Null with the timer off. Pet Null when it comes to you, and don’t count. · _Sit still near Byte and wait. Only pet Null once it comes close on its own._

#### `reader:s7-unverified` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-unverified)

_After Moss’s talk, the Keepers drift home in twos and threes. Byte stays on the wall, writing very small and very fast._  
_Beside him sits a pale blue Nubbin with a flat, tidy ball above its head. It is watching the notebook._  
**Byte**: This is Null. Grown-up. Four years and some weeks. I know the weeks. Pixel says saying the weeks is unsettling.  
**Byte**: Moss says a Nubbin loved enough becomes an Eternal. “Enough.” That isn’t a unit.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.MD.A.1, ask): what a unit of measurement is  
> **Byte**: A unit is a fixed amount you measure with. Metres. Kilograms. Minutes.  
> **Byte**: Which of these is a real unit?  
> - ✅ A minute → Correct. A minute is always sixty seconds, everywhere, for everyone. That’s what makes it a unit.
> - Enough → That’s Moss’s word, and it’s my whole complaint. My “enough” and yours might be different. A minute is always sixty seconds.
> - A bit → A bit of what? A bit is a different size every time. A real unit, like a minute, is always the same.
> _For grown-ups: Learned what a unit of measurement is (a minute is always 60 seconds; “enough” is not a unit)._

_He shows you the page. Under everything Moss said tonight, in neat capitals: UNVERIFIED._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.2.4, ask): use the prefix un- to work out a word  
> _UN-VERIFIED. “Un” at the start of a word means “not”. To verify something means to check that it’s true._  
> _So what does Byte mean when he writes UNVERIFIED?_  
> - ✅ Not checked or proved yet. → Exactly. I’m not saying Moss is wrong. I’m saying nobody’s checked.
> - Definitely wrong. → No, that would be “false”. Unverified just means not checked yet. It might be true.
> - Very important. → Not quite. “Un” means “not”, so unverified means not checked yet. Like “unopened” means not opened.
> _For grown-ups: Used the prefix un- to work out the meaning of “unverified”._

**Byte**: When Fossil faded, everybody just sat with her. Sitting isn’t a method.  
_Fossil was Dusty’s old Nubbin. “Fade” is the Grove’s word for die. Fossil died this summer, on the hill._  
**Byte**: Enough is an amount. Amounts can be put on a schedule. Pixel’s timer runs four lanterns to the second.  
**Byte**: Before you say it: no stone. I’m not Cinder. I’m using a clock. Completely different.  
**Moss**: Your shoelace is untied.  
_Byte looks down. It is. It always is._  
_He goes very quiet._  
**Moss**: Things on a timer learn to wait for the bell, Byte. Safe is not the same as well.  
_Byte writes it down carefully. Then, next to it: UNVERIFIED._  

**Choices**

- **I’ll help. What do you need?**
    - **Byte**: Fruit, a bed and a witness. You can be the witness. Witnesses don’t touch anything.
- **Moss is usually right, Byte.**
    - **Byte**: “Usually” is a percentage. I’d like to know which percentage.
    - On the wall, Moss appears to be asleep. He is very bad at appearing to be asleep.
- **Pet Null.**
    - Null leans into your hand, surprised, and then leans harder.
    - **Byte**: Don’t, you’ll spoil the… fine. Fine. Once.

#### `reader:s7-minder` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-minder)

_Pixel’s workbench: a clock face, three little brass bells, a feeding chute and a very small bed._  
**Pixel**: Hi. So. It’s my lantern timer, basically. Four lanterns or one Nubbin, same maths, Byte says.  
**Pixel**: Bell one, fruit drops. Bell two, bed warms. Bell three, ninety seconds of petting. Byte does the petting.  
**Byte**: Ninety-one seconds was too many. I tested it.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.MD.A.1, ask): change seconds into minutes and seconds  
> **Pixel**: Ninety seconds of petting. There are sixty seconds in a minute.  
> **Pixel**: So how long is ninety seconds?  
> - ✅ 1 minute and 30 seconds → Correct. Sixty seconds make one minute, and thirty are left over.
> - 9 minutes → That would be a very long pet! Nine minutes is 540 seconds. Ninety seconds is 60 + 30: one minute and thirty seconds.
> - An hour and a half → Ha, Null would love that. But hours are much bigger. Ninety seconds is 60 + 30: one minute and thirty seconds.
> _For grown-ups: Changed 90 seconds into 1 minute and 30 seconds, using 60 seconds = 1 minute._

**Pixel**: He wanted to call it the Accelerator. I wanted the Nap Box. We settled on the Minder, which neither of us likes.  
_The clock ticks. The chute fires a plum across the shed and hits Byte right on his clipboard._  
**Pixel**: That was calibration.  
**Byte**: That was a plum.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): a push is a force that starts and stops motion  
> **Pixel**: Pushes and pulls are called forces. The chute gave that plum a big push, so it flew.  
> **Pixel**: Then Byte’s clipboard pushed back and stopped it. Too much push. I’ll turn the spring down.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a push is a force that can start something moving, and another push can stop it._

_On the next tick, a piece of fruit drops neatly into the bowl, exactly on the beat._  
**Pixel**: I built the clock. Clocks don’t care what they count down to. I’m trying to care for it.  
**Byte**: Null eats on the bell, sleeps on the bell, is loved on the bell. If enough is an amount, this is enough.  

**Choices**

- **What if Null’s hungry before the bell?**
    - **Byte**: Null won’t be. The bell’s been set just right.
    - **Pixel**: …I asked him that too. It sounded better when he said it to me.
- **Does it have an off switch?**
    - **Pixel**: Lever. Left side. I always put a lever on.
    - **Pixel**: Nobody ever asks about the lever. Thank you for asking about the lever.

#### `reader:s7-first-bell` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-first-bell)

_Byte sets Null in the little bed. The first bell rings._  
_Null looks at Byte, then at the bell, then eats._  
**Byte**: Day one. Logging begins. Three bells a day. I’ll write down every single one.  
**Pixel**: Every one? For a whole week?  
**Byte**: Come back in a week. Seven days. Not six. Not eight.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): multiply equal groups  
> **Pixel**: Three bells a day, for seven days. How many bells is that?  
> - ✅ 21 → Twenty-one. Seven groups of three. I’ll rule a column for each.
> - 10 → That’s 3 + 7, adding. But it’s three bells every day, seven times over: 3 × 7 = 21.
> - 37 → Hmm, that’s the 3 and the 7 side by side. Seven days of three bells is 3 × 7 = 21.
> _For grown-ups: Multiplied equal groups (3 bells a day for 7 days = 21) to solve a word problem._

**Pixel**: It’s going to be fine. It’s a clock. Clocks are fine.  

#### `reader:s7-week` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-week)

_A week later, Byte’s front room is all clipboards. Null sits in the little bed, perfectly upright, waiting._  
**Byte**: Best week of data I’ve ever had. Run, Swim, Power, all up. It’s never eaten so tidily.  
**Pixel**: Watch this.  
_Pixel rolls a ball across the floor, the way you would for any Nubbin. Null watches it go by and doesn’t move._  
_The bell rings, and Null eats. It doesn’t look at the fruit, only at the bell._  
_Its blue has gone thin at the edges, like a shirt washed too often. Not grey. Paler._  
**Byte**: It used to chase that ball into the stove. Why doesn’t it play?  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): why young animals play  
> **Pixel**: Young animals play all the time. Kittens pounce. Puppies chase. Lambs bounce about.  
> **Pixel**: Play is how they practise running, catching and getting along. Animal carers watch for it. It’s a sign an animal feels safe.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that young animals play to practise skills, and that play is a sign an animal feels safe._

**Pixel**: Byte. I think it’s waiting to be told.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.MD.D.10, ask): read a bar graph and compare two bars  
> _Pixel has been keeping a chart too. It shows how many times Null played each day._  
> _Day 1: ■■■■■ (5). Day 2: ■■■ (3). Day 3: ■ (1). Days 4 to 7: nothing at all._  
> _How many fewer times did Null play on Day 3 than on Day 1?_  
> - ✅ 4 → Yes. 5 take away 1 is 4. And after Day 3, the bars stop altogether.
> - 6 → That’s 5 + 1. We want the difference: 5 − 1 = 4 fewer times.
> - 1 → One is how many times it played on Day 3. The difference from Day 1 is 5 − 1 = 4.
> _For grown-ups: Read a bar graph and found the difference between two bars (5 − 1 = 4)._

_Byte turns his notebook back a week. There is Moss’s line, in his own neat capitals, and beside it: UNVERIFIED._  
**Byte**: He told me. In plain words. And I wrote “unverified” next to it.  
_He crosses it out and writes VERIFIED. Then he crosses that out too._  
_It isn’t something he wanted to be right about._  

**Choices**

- **Pull the lever.**
    - Pixel pulls it before you’ve finished saying it. The ticking stops, and the quiet is enormous.
    - **Pixel**: I always put a lever on.
- **Sit down beside Null, Byte. And wait.**
    - **Byte**: For how long?
    - **You**: Until it isn’t waiting for the bell.
    - **Byte**: That isn’t a unit of time.
    - But he reaches over and pulls the lever himself.

#### `reader:s7-same-graph` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-same-graph)

**Byte**: I wasn’t trying to own it. I was trying to keep it. Turns out those make the same graph.  
_Null sits in the bed, waiting for a bell that isn’t coming. For the first time, it looks a little lost._  
**Pixel**: A clock does the same thing every time. That’s what I love about clocks.  
**Pixel**: Null isn’t a clock. I don’t think I knew that was the problem until now.  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): how living things differ from machines (think question)  
> **Pixel**: Here’s what I keep wondering. A clock and a Nubbin both tick along, every day.  
> **Pixel**: What makes Null different from a machine?  
> - Null has feelings. A clock doesn’t. → That’s a good answer. A clock can’t be lonely, or happy, or bored. Null can.
> - Null grows and changes. Clocks stay the same. → That’s fair. Null hatched, grew up, and will change again. My clock just gets dusty.
> - Null wants things, like playing and friends. → That’s a fair answer, and I think it’s Moss’s answer too. A clock only does what it’s set to do. Null wants things of its own.
> - Maybe they’re not that different. → That’s an honest answer. Bodies do work a bit like machines: hearts pump, legs push. But a machine can’t want to chase a moth. I think that bit matters most.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how living things differ from machines (a think question with no wrong answer)._

**Byte**: Moss didn’t have a schedule or a single number. He was right anyway. I’ll have to think about that.  
**Pixel**: Tomorrow evening. No clock. We’ll all just sit there. We’re very good at sitting there.  

#### `reader:s7-no-clock` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-no-clock)

_Evening in Byte’s garden, and no bells. The Minder sits under a cloth on the step, with its lever down._  
_Byte has brought his stopwatch. He holds it the way some people hold a habit they’ve given up._  
**Byte**: I’m not timing. I’m just holding it. In case.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, notice): changing a habit happens in small steps  
> _A habit is something you do so often you hardly notice. Byte has timed everything for years._  
> _Stopping a habit is hard. Holding the stopwatch without pressing it is his first small step._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that changing a habit is hard and usually happens in small steps._

**You**: In case of what?  
**Byte**: Unverified.  
_For a long time, nothing. Null sits in the grass, looking at the place where the bell used to be._  
_Then a moth goes past, and Null’s head turns. It watches the moth all the way to the hedge._  
**Byte**: Did you see that? That isn’t on any schedule.  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): nocturnal animals  
> **Byte**: Fact: most moths fly at night. Animals that are busy at night are called nocturnal.  
> **Byte**: Bats, hedgehogs, most owls and most moths are nocturnal. That one is right on time.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the word nocturnal and some animals that are active at night._

_Null takes one step after the moth, then looks back at you, as if checking that’s allowed._  

**Choices**

- **Nod. “Go on.”**
    - Null goes. It doesn’t catch the moth, but it falls into the hedge and comes out with a leaf on its head.
    - Null, with no clock on it at all, says prrp, which Byte does not write down.
- **Put the stopwatch down, Byte.**
    - Byte looks at it for a long moment. Then he lays it face down in the grass.
    - **Byte**: It’s still running. I just can’t see it. That’s a start.

#### `reader:s7-lap` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-lap)

_Near dark, at quarter past seven, Null comes back. It climbs into Byte’s lap without being asked, and falls asleep at once._  
_Byte doesn’t move for an hour._  
_You know it’s an hour because he doesn’t check. You check, a little._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.A.1, ask): find a time one hour later  
> _Null fell asleep at quarter past seven. Byte didn’t move for one hour._  
> _What time was it when he finally moved?_  
> - ✅ Quarter past eight → Yes. One hour later, the hour hand moves on by one. 7:15 becomes 8:15.
> - Eight o’clock → Close! But we started at quarter past. One hour after 7:15 is 8:15, quarter past eight.
> - Half past seven → That’s only fifteen minutes later. One whole hour after 7:15 is 8:15.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a time one hour later (7:15 to 8:15)._

**Byte**: Its edges are bluer. Don’t tell me if I’m imagining it.  
**Pixel**: You’re not imagining it. I didn’t write it down. I just looked.  
**Byte**: Looking. Huh. Unverified. But I’ll allow it.  

#### `reader:s7-complete` · [main](../../../side/S07.md#s7-complete)

_A few evenings later, Pixel knocks on your door and says nothing, which is how you know._  
_Null has curled up under Byte’s hedge, and the air around it has begun to glow._  
_When a Nubbin’s life comes to an end, it makes a cocoon. Some fade, which means they die. Some are reborn as a new egg._  
**Byte**: End-of-life check. Four years, eleven weeks, two days. I said I wouldn’t say the days.  
_Byte sits by the cocoon with his hand on it, and doesn’t count. You can see how hard that is._  
_The cocoon doesn’t thin. It glows warm and gold, and opens on an egg: small, blue, humming._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS1-1, do): put a moth’s life cycle in order  
> **Pixel**: It’s a life cycle. Egg, young, grown-up, cocoon, egg again. Round and round.  
> **Pixel**: Moths have a life cycle too. Here are its stages, all muddled: moth, egg, pupa, caterpillar.  
> **Pixel**: Can you put them in order, starting with the egg?  
> Check: `edu:order:moth-life-cycle`
> _For grown-ups: Put the stages of a moth’s life cycle in order (egg, caterpillar, pupa, adult moth)._

**Byte**: Not Eternal. Reborn. It gets to start again, with someone who remembers it.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): two feelings at once  
> _Byte looks sad and happy at the same time._  
> _That happens. Sad to say goodbye to grown-up Null, glad it gets to start again. Two big feelings can both be true._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that it’s normal to feel two strong feelings, like sad and glad, at the same time._

**Byte**: Here. My stopwatch. Keep it, please. I’ll only look at it.  
**Pixel**: And the timer, with the bells off. It only counts lanterns now.  
_On your way home you notice Byte’s shoelace is tied. Nobody had to tell him. It won’t last._  

### Glossary

- **unit**: A fixed amount used to measure things, like a metre or a minute.
- **unverified**: Not yet checked or proved to be true.
- **calibration**: Adjusting a machine so it works or measures exactly right.
- **logging**: Writing down what happens, again and again, to keep a record.
- **data**: Facts and numbers collected so you can study them.
- **schedule**: A plan that says when things will happen.
- **habit**: Something you do so often that you do it without thinking.
- **nocturnal**: Awake and busy at night, and asleep in the day.
- **cocoon**: A soft case some creatures make around themselves while they change.
- **reborn**: Born again, to start a new life.
