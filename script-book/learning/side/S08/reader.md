# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S8: The Warren in the Hollow

## S8. The Warren in the Hollow

- `s8-s1`: Follow Nova and the fireflies past Hazel’s camp. · _Walk while the fireflies drift. Stand still when they settle. They lead on past the flat stone._
- `s8-s2`: Leave fruit at the edge of the ring, a little at a time, until the Old Girl lets you in. · _Bring twelve pieces of fruit over three evenings: four each time. Set them down at the roots and step back._
- `s8-s3`: Bring Fern to the ring of roots in the Hollow. · _Fern is at her gate next door. Walk her past the falls to the Hollow. She’ll keep up, mostly._
- `s8-s4`: Sit at the edge of the ring with Fern, and let the Old Girl come to her. · _Sit down by the roots with Fern and wait. Don’t reach in._

#### `reader:s8-further` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-further)

_You are still by the flat stone with Hazel’s notebook when Nova steps out of the trees. She doesn’t say hello._  
**Nova**: That’s the camp. It isn’t the whole of it.  
**Nova**: “Fed nineteen today,” she wrote. Did you count her bowls? Twelve.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 1.OA.A.1, ask): subtract within 20 to find what’s missing  
> **Nova**: Nineteen Nubbins fed. Twelve bowls at the camp.  
> **Nova**: How many Nubbins didn’t have a bowl here?  
> - ✅ 7 → Seven. 19 − 12 = 7. Seven mouths being fed somewhere else.
> - 31 → That’s 19 + 12. We want how many are left over: 19 − 12 = 7.
> - 9 → Count on from 12: 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19. That’s seven, not nine.
> _For grown-ups: Subtracted within 20 to find a missing amount (19 − 12 = 7)._

**Nova**: Twelve bowls, nineteen mouths. She wasn’t feeding them all here. She was carrying food further in.  
_A firefly lands on the notebook, sits a moment, and lifts off into the dark beyond the camp. Then another._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): fireflies make their own light  
> **Nova**: Fireflies aren’t really flies. They’re beetles that make their own light, in their tails.  
> **Nova**: They flash to find each other in the dark. Hardly any heat. Just glow.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that fireflies are beetles that make their own light to signal to each other._

**Nova**: They go the same way every night. I followed them once, years ago. I look in whenever I’m passing.  
**Nova**: I’ve never brought anyone. Bring fruit. Walk slowly. Slower than that.  

**Choices**

- **Lead on.**
    - **Nova**: I don’t lead. I follow fireflies. You can follow me following them.
- **Should my Nubbin wait here?**
    - **Nova**: No. They need to see you came with one that trusts you. It’s the only introduction that works.

#### `reader:s8-ring` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-ring)

_The fireflies stop above a ring of old roots, knotted out of the ground like a fence nobody built._  
_Inside sit dozens of grey Nubbins. Not in rows: in a ring, facing in. Some of them are touching._  
_A stream runs close by, and the roots make a roof against the wind._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-ESS3-1, ask): what a habitat gives the animals that live there  
> **Nova**: Hazel used to say a good home needs a few things. Look around the ring.  
> **Nova**: What does this place give the warren?  
> - ✅ Water, shelter, and each other. → Yes. The stream for water, the roots against the wind, and a ring of friends. And Hazel brought the food.
> - Nothing much. It’s just roots. → Look again. The stream gives water, the roots keep off the wind, and they have each other. That’s a lot.
> - Toys and beds. → Ha. No toys. But the roots are a kind of shelter, the stream gives water, and they keep each other warm.
> _For grown-ups: Named what a habitat provides: water, shelter, company (and food)._

_In the middle sits an old grey Nubbin whose left ear folds over at the tip. A scrap of faded ribbon is tied round it._  
**Nova**: They look lost. They aren’t. They know exactly where they are. It’s the rest of us who can never find them.  
_The old grey Nubbin gives Nova one slow look, the way a teacher looks at someone talking in assembly._  
**Nova**: That one’s in charge. I call her the Old Girl. She’s never told me what she calls herself.  
**Nova**: This is their warren. It’s been their home for longer than I’ve known it.  

#### `reader:s8-edge` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-edge)

_You take one step towards the roots. Every grey head in the ring turns at once, and you stop._  
**Nova**: Nobody crosses. Not me. You don’t walk into someone’s house just because the door is made of roots.  
**You**: Then how did Hazel feed them?  
**Nova**: She left it at the edge and stepped back. Then she came again the next night. Patient. That’s the whole trick.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): respect an animal’s space and let it choose  
> **Nova**: So. How do you show a shy animal that you’re friendly?  
> - ✅ Leave food, step back, and come again tomorrow. → That’s it. Let them choose to come to you. The choosing is what makes it trust.
> - Walk in and pick one up. → They’d run, or bite. Grabbing teaches them hands are scary. Leave food, step back, and let them choose.
> - Shout hello so they know you’re there. → Loud noises frighten shy animals. Quiet, slow and patient works better. Let them choose to come to you.
> _For grown-ups: Learned to respect an animal’s space and let it choose to come closer._

_The Old Girl watches you. Her dark ball hangs very still above her head._  
_Now and then she looks up the dark path, as if someone used to come that way._  
_Close at her side sits a grey Nubbin thinner than all the rest. Its ear folds over at the tip, just like hers._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS2-1, notice): animals in groups look after each other  
> **Nova**: Groups look after their young. In a wolf pack, the grown-ups carry food back for the pups.  
> **Nova**: Here, the strong ones leave the soft fruit for the thin one.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that animals in groups help each other survive, like wolves bringing food to their pups._

**Nova**: Her youngest, I think. Doesn’t eat much. The others leave it the soft fruit.  

**Choices**

- **Leave the softest fruit nearest the thin one.**
    - **Nova**: …That’s what Hazel did. Exactly that.
- **Put the fruit down, and sit a little way off.**
    - You sit. After a long time, a grey Nubbin creeps out, takes one piece, and carries it to the middle.
    - **Nova**: Good. Now do that for three nights.

#### `reader:s8-gap` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-gap)

_You come back three evenings running, with twelve pieces of fruit to share out between them._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, do): share into equal groups  
> _Twelve pieces of fruit, shared fairly over three evenings._  
> _Sort them into three equal piles at the roots. How many go in each pile?_  
> Check: `edu:share:fruit:12-into-3`
> _For grown-ups: Shared 12 into 3 equal groups (12 ÷ 3 = 4)._

_On the third evening the Old Girl gets up, slowly, and steps aside. There is a gap in the ring, one Keeper wide._  
**Nova**: Whenever I pass through the valley, I come and sit by these roots. She has never once done that for me.  
_Up close, her ribbon was brown once, with a leaf stitched on it. Five points, one curled stem._  
_You have seen that leaf before. It’s on Fern’s gloves, Fern’s gate, and Fern’s tea cosy._  
**Nova**: Hazel’s notebook. “The brown one let me touch her ear.” She isn’t brown. Her ribbon was.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ask): put clues together to make an inference  
> _Nova is putting clues together, like a detective._  
> _The leaf on the ribbon is Fern’s leaf. Who do you think the Old Girl might be?_  
> - ✅ A Nubbin Fern lost, long ago. → That’s what I think too. Fern sews that leaf on everything she loves.
> - Fern’s tea cosy. → Ha. No, she’s definitely a Nubbin. But the leaf is Fern’s. Fern must have tied it on, long ago.
> - Nobody special. → Maybe. But nobody sews a leaf on a ribbon for nobody. I think Fern lost her once.
> _For grown-ups: Used clues in the story to make an inference about a character._


#### `reader:s8-fern-gate` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-fern-gate)

_Fern is leaning on her gate, stitching a leaf onto a glove that already has three._  
**Fern**: You’re staring at my glove. It’s a leaf. I put them on everything.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.G.A.3, ask): find a line of symmetry  
> _Look at Fern’s leaf: five points, with a vein down the middle. Forget the curly stem for a moment._  
> _If you folded the leaf along that middle vein, would the two halves match?_  
> - ✅ Yes, they’d match. → Yes. The middle vein is a line of symmetry: each half is a mirror of the other. Lots of leaves are nearly symmetrical.
> - No, they’d be different. → Look again: two points on each side, and one at the top. Fold down the middle and they match. That fold is a line of symmetry.
> _For grown-ups: Found a line of symmetry in a leaf shape._

**Fern**: I put one on a ribbon once. Long time ago.  
_She pulls the thread tight, and keeps pulling after it’s tight._  
**Fern**: Six years ago. The Midsummer race. She went over the rail on the cliff bend. I stood at the bottom till dark.  
**Fern**: Everyone said she couldn’t have lived. Everyone was very kind about it. I hated it.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): how to help a grieving friend  
> _People were kind to Fern, but their words didn’t help. When someone is very sad, words can’t always fix it._  
> _What often helps more is listening, and staying close._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that listening and staying close can help a grieving friend more than words._


**Choices**

- **Fern. I think I’ve found her.**
    - **Fern**: Don’t.
    - **Fern**: Don’t say that unless…
    - **You**: Bring fruit. Soft fruit.
    - She is through her own gate before you’ve finished, still holding the needle.
- **Will you help me carry fruit to the Hollow tonight?**
    - **Fern**: The Hollow? At night? With you?
    - **Fern**: …Fine. Someone has to carry things badly.
    - She doesn’t ask why. You don’t tell her. Not yet.

#### `reader:s8-arrive` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-arrive)

_The fireflies drift ahead of you both, low over the path, like sparks from a slow fire._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): spot a simile  
> _“Like sparks from a slow fire.” When a writer says one thing is like another, that’s called a simile._  
> _What are the fireflies being compared to?_  
> - ✅ Sparks from a fire → Yes. Small, glowing and drifting. A simile uses “like” or “as” to paint a picture.
> - The path → The path is where they fly. The simile compares the fireflies to sparks: small, bright and drifting.
> - Fern → Ha. Fern is more like a tree-bumping machine. The fireflies are compared to sparks from a fire.
> _For grown-ups: Spotted a simile and said what it compares._

**Fern**: Is it always this dark? How did Hazel do this every night? She couldn’t see her own feet.  
_Fern walks into a tree, says sorry to it, and walks into the same tree again._  
**Fern**: I’m carrying the soft fruit. I insisted. I have sat on two of them.  
**Fern**: They’re still soft. Softer, if anything. That has to count.  
_At the stream she takes her boots off, wades across, and puts them back on the wrong feet. She doesn’t notice for a mile._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-2, notice): we need light to see; eyes adjust to the dark  
> _We see things when light bounces off them into our eyes. In the dark, there’s very little light._  
> _But eyes slowly get used to it. After about half an hour in the dark, you can see far more than at first._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that we need light to see, and that our eyes slowly adjust to the dark._

_Then the path dips, the fireflies settle low, and for the first time all night, Fern stops talking._  

#### `reader:s8-reunion` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-reunion)

_Fern stops at the roots, and every grey head turns._  
_She doesn’t notice them. She’s looking at the middle._  
_The Old Girl is already standing. Her folded ear is up. The scrap of brown ribbon hangs from it, leaf and all._  
**Fern**: Oh.  
**Fern**: Bramble? …Oh, you’ve gone grey. That’s all right, love. So have I.  
_For a long moment, nobody in the ring moves._  
_Then Bramble walks out through the gap, one careful step at a time, all the way to Fern’s boots._  
_Bramble says nothing. She puts her grey head against Fern’s shin and keeps it there._  
**Fern**: I stood at the bottom of that cliff till dark. I thought I’d left you there.  
**Fern**: I’m sorry I stopped looking. I’m so sorry I stopped looking.  
_Bramble sits down on Fern’s boots, both of them at once, and stays put._  
**Fern**: Right. Good. I’m going to cry now. Somebody hold my gloves.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): guilt, sorry and being kind to yourself (think question)  
> _Fern is saying sorry for something from six years ago. Everyone told her Bramble couldn’t have lived._  
> _Do you think Fern did something wrong by stopping looking?_  
> - No. She did her best with what she knew. → That’s a kind answer. Fern believed what everyone told her. Being kind to yourself matters too.
> - A bit, but she’s sorry, and that matters. → That’s a fair answer. Saying sorry, even late, is brave. And Bramble doesn’t seem to mind at all.
> - I’m not sure. → That’s an honest answer. Some things don’t have a clear right or wrong. Fern isn’t sure either, and that’s all right.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about guilt, saying sorry and being kind to yourself (a think question with no wrong answer)._


**Choices**

- **Hold her gloves.**
    - You hold them. Fern sits down in the mud with Bramble in her lap and cries for a long time.
    - Bramble lets her.
- **Hand Fern a ribbon from your pocket.**
    - **Fern**: You carry ribbons? Course you do. You’re Hazel’s.
    - Fern stitches a leaf onto it there and then, with shaking hands, and ties it under the old one.
    - **Fern**: She keeps both. She always was greedy.

#### `reader:s8-one-short` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-one-short)

_Later, Bramble walks back to the middle of the ring. She has a job, and she goes back to it._  
**Fern**: She always did like being in charge.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS2-1, notice): some animal groups have an experienced leader  
> **Nova**: Lots of animal groups have a leader. In an elephant herd, it’s usually the oldest female.  
> **Nova**: She remembers where to find water in a dry year, and her family follows her. Bramble’s a bit like that.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that some animal groups, like elephant herds, are led by an experienced older female._

_Bramble sits down and looks past you all, up the dark path, the way she looked the first night._  
**Nova**: The thin one. Her youngest. It isn’t at the ring tonight.  
**Fern**: Then we leave the soft fruit at the edge till it is.  
_Fern sets the softest plum down at the roots. Not one she sat on. Then she steps back, patient, like Hazel._  

#### `reader:s8-complete` · [main](../../../side/S08.md#s8-complete)

_Hazel sits on the bench by the pond, the thin grey Nubbin asleep in the crook of her arm._  
**Gran Hazel**: This one? Found her at the edge of a ring of roots, too tired to stand. So I carried her.  
**Gran Hazel**: Her mother runs the place. Ear folds over at the tip. Opinions. I gather you’ve met.  
**You**: Fern found her. Her name’s Bramble.  
**Gran Hazel**: Did she. Oh, Fern. Did she.  
**Gran Hazel**: Take this one home, then, love. Nothing’s mine. I only carry things for a bit.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, ask): think about a theme: looking after things for others  
> _“Nothing’s mine. I only carry things for a bit.”_  
> _What does Hazel mean?_  
> - ✅ We look after things for a while, then pass them on. → That’s it, love. The Grove, the Nubbins, the gate. You keep them for the next ones.
> - She doesn’t like owning things. → Ha. I own a great many teapots. No: I mean we only look after things for a while, then hand them on.
> - Her arms are tired. → They are, a bit. But I mean something bigger: we look after things for a while, then hand them on.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about a theme of the story: looking after things for others (stewardship)._

_At the ring, the thin one wakes in your arms, sees her mother, and goes stiff all over with joy._  
_Bramble washes her, ear to ear. Then she noses the old brown ribbon off her folded ear and drops it at your feet._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-LS1-2, notice): parents help their young survive  
> _Lots of animal mothers wash their babies, the way a cat licks her kittens clean._  
> _It keeps the baby clean and warm, and tells it: you’re mine, and you’re safe._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that animal parents care for their young, for example by washing them._

_Behind Bramble, three grey Nubbins from the ring stand up and look at you, as if waiting to see where you go next._  
**Nova**: That’s you invited. Come whenever you like. Bring soft fruit.

### Glossary

- **beyond**: Further away than something; on the far side of it.
- **warren**: A crowded home where lots of animals live close together, like rabbits in their tunnels.
- **huddle**: To crowd close together, often to keep warm.
- **patient**: Able to wait calmly, without getting cross or giving up.
- **stitched**: Sewn on with a needle and thread.
- **symmetry**: When one half of a shape is a mirror image of the other half.
- **simile**: A way of describing something by saying it is like something else.
- **wades**: Walks through water.
- **reunion**: When people or animals who were apart meet again.
- **opinions**: What someone thinks or believes about things.
