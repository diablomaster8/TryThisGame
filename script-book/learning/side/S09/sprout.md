# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Side story S9: The Gate That Sticks

## S9. The Gate That Sticks

- `s9-step1`: Take the old trowel to Gran. · _The trowel is by the cottage step. Tap it!_
- `s9-step2`: Go to the gate at sunset tomorrow. · _Corvin comes when the Sun goes down. Just stand by the gate._
- `s9-step3`: Be at the gate first thing in the morning. · _When the new day starts, go to the gate before anything else._

#### `sprout:s9-dusk` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-dusk)

_Evening. The gate rattles. Rattle, rattle._  
**Gran Hazel**: That’ll be him.  
_Gran doesn’t get up. Her eyes are shut._  
**Gran Hazel**: Go on. It’s your gate now.  
_Corvin is at the gate. He did a wrong thing, long ago._  
_He doesn’t look grumpy now. He looks lonely and tired._  
**Corvin Vale**: My hands were cold for years. Now they’re warm again.  
**Corvin Vale**: Getting warm hurts a bit. Nobody tells you that.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): cold hands tingle as they warm; rubbing hands warms them  
> _When cold hands warm up, they can tingle._  
> _(Rub your hands together fast. Can you feel them get warm?)_  
> Check: `edu:rub:hands:warm`
> _For grown-ups: Rubbed their hands together to feel them warm up._

_He pushes the gate. It sticks. He doesn’t know the trick._  
**Corvin Vale**: This trowel is Hazel’s. Please give it back to her.  

**Choices**

- **Why now?**
    - **Corvin Vale**: Because I can feel my hands again.
    - He walks away down the lane.
- **Do you want to come in?**
    - **Corvin Vale**: Yes. But not tonight. I haven’t asked yet.
    - He walks away. He doesn’t look back.
- **Say nothing. Take the trowel.**
    - You take it. The handle is warm.
    - **Corvin Vale**: Thank you.

#### `sprout:s9-trowel` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-trowel)

_Gran turns the trowel over in her hands._  
**Gran Hazel**: My old trowel! I wondered where it went.  
**Gran Hazel**: Corvin was my helper, long ago. Clever, and in a hurry.  
**Gran Hazel**: He tried to make my Nubbins keep very still. That was wrong.  
**Gran Hazel**: I stopped him. That night, he took some Nubbins away.  
**Gran Hazel**: In the morning, I made the gate stick.  
**You**: So he couldn’t come back in?  
**Gran Hazel**: No, love. So I’d hear him if he ever came back.  
**Gran Hazel**: A sticky gate rattles. I always hear it.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Language and Communication, do): sound words  
> _(Say the gate sounds together: rattle, creak, clack!)_  
> **Gran Hazel**: Rattle means somebody new. Clack means somebody who knows.  
> Check: `edu:say:sound-words:gate`
> _For grown-ups: Said sound words (rattle, creak, clack) out loud._


**Choices**

- **Are you still angry with him?**
    - **Gran Hazel**: I was, for a long time. Angry is heavy to carry.
    - **Gran Hazel**: I had Nubbins to look after. So I put it down.
- **Did you find the Nubbins he took?**
    - **Gran Hazel**: Most of them. I’m still sad about the rest.
- **Sit next to her. Say nothing.**
    - You sit. Gran leans on you, just a little.
    - **Gran Hazel**: There. That’s better than any words.

#### `sprout:s9-yours` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-yours)

_Gran puts the trowel down on the step._  
**Gran Hazel**: He’ll come back at the next sunset.  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-ESS1-1, do): the parts of the day in order  
> _(Say the day in order: morning, afternoon, evening, night.)_  
> **Gran Hazel**: Sunset is in the evening. Then comes night.  
> Check: `edu:order:times-of-day`
> _For grown-ups: Put the parts of the day in order and placed sunset in the evening._

**Gran Hazel**: I’m not going down there. I’ve done my part. It’s your gate now.  
**You**: What do I say to him?  
**Gran Hazel**: Nothing, to start with. Sit and wait. It works on people too.  

#### `sprout:s9-ask` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-ask)

_Sunset. Corvin is already at the gate._  
**Corvin Vale**: I practised this in the lane. A sheep heard me.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): animal sounds: sheep  
> _(What does a sheep say? Baa!)_  
> _(A baby sheep is a lamb. Say it in a little voice: baa!)_  
> Check: `edu:say:animal-sound:sheep`
> _For grown-ups: Made a sheep sound and learned that a baby sheep is a lamb._

_He puts both hands on the gate. He doesn’t open it._  
**Corvin Vale**: Long ago, I opened this gate without asking.  
**Corvin Vale**: That was wrong. I am sorry.  
**Corvin Vale**: So now I’m asking.  
**Corvin Vale**: May I come in, please?  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, notice): asking before going into someone’s home  
> _Corvin says sorry. Then he asks before he comes in._  
> _We ask first. It’s their home, and their choice._  
> _For grown-ups: Saw a character say sorry and ask permission before entering a home._

**Corvin Vale**: I’ll stand right here till morning. If it’s still shut, I’ll understand.  
_He steps back into the dark lane. He doesn’t go far._  

#### `sprout:s9-word` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-word)

_By supper, everyone knows. Corvin asked to come in._  
**Ember**: He took Ash. I’m just telling you what he did.  
**Luna**: Sable came from his garden. But she got out.  
**Fern**: I’d let him in. Then I’d worry all night!  
**Clover**: The grey stays on him too. You can only make it lighter.  
**Byte**: I’d open the gate. Don’t tell anyone.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): listening to different ideas  
> _Each friend thinks something different._  
> _It’s good to listen to everyone. Then you choose._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard several different opinions and that it helps to listen to all of them._

_Gran says nothing. But she leaves her door unlocked._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 5  
> _(Count the friends who spoke: Ember, Luna, Fern, Clover, Byte.)_  
> _(Five friends. Five ideas!)_  
> Check: `edu:count:friends:5`
> _For grown-ups: Counted five characters who shared an idea._


#### `sprout:s9-gate` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-gate)

_Morning. The pond is grey and still._  
_Gran sits by the pond with a cup of tea._  
_Corvin is at the gate. He stood there all night._  
_Frost sparkles on his coat._  

> **Lesson** (science, K-ESS2-1, notice): frost forms on very cold nights  
> _Frost is tiny bits of ice._  
> _It forms on very cold nights. When the Sun warms it, it melts._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that frost is tiny bits of ice that form on very cold nights and melt in the sun._

**Corvin Vale**: Hello, Hazel’s grandchild.  
_In the long grass, a little grey ear goes flat._  
**Corvin Vale**: I know what I took from her. I’m asking anyway.  
_Gran drinks her tea. She is listening._  

**Choices**

- **Open it for him. Don’t show him how.**
    - You lift and push. The gate opens.
    - **Corvin Vale**: Thank you. I’ll knock every time.
    - By the pond, Gran gives a tiny wave.
- **Leave it shut. Not today.**
    - You stay still. Corvin nods.
    - **Corvin Vale**: All right. That’s fair.
    - **Corvin Vale**: Thank you for taking the trowel.
    - He walks away down the lane.
    - Gran sits by the pond a little longer.

#### `sprout:s9-gate-family` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-gate-family)

_Variant of `s9-gate`._

_Morning. Gran sits by the pond with her tea._  
_Corvin is at the gate. He stood there all night._  
_Frost sparkles on his coat._  
**Corvin Vale**: Hello, Hazel’s grandchild.  
_In the long grass, a little grey ear goes flat._  
**Corvin Vale**: Things have reached me this week. From your side of the valley.  
**Corvin Vale**: Things people said. Things people found. I kept them all.  
**Corvin Vale**: I know what I took. I’m asking anyway.  
_Gran drinks her tea. She is listening._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ELOF: Language and Communication, ask): first, then: the steps of a trick  
> _Our gate has a trick. What do we do first?_  
> - ✅ Lift → Yes! First lift. Then push.
> - Push → Push first, and it sticks! First lift, then push.
> _For grown-ups: Told the two steps of the gate trick in order: first lift, then push._


**Choices**

- **Show him the trick. “Lift and push.”**
    - **You**: Lift and push. Both together.
    - Corvin lifts the gate a little. Then he pushes.
    - It swings open! Then it sticks again behind him.
    - **Corvin Vale**: Oh. That’s all it ever was.
    - He sits by the pond near Gran. She hands him her cup.
    - In the long grass, the grey ear is still flat. Not today.
- **Open it for him. Don’t show him how.**
    - You lift and push. The gate opens.
    - **Corvin Vale**: Thank you. I’ll knock every time.
    - By the pond, Gran gives a tiny wave.
- **Leave it shut. Not today.**
    - You stay still. Corvin nods.
    - **Corvin Vale**: All right. That’s fair.
    - **Corvin Vale**: Thank you for taking the trowel.
    - He walks away down the lane.
    - Gran sits by the pond a little longer.

#### `sprout:s9-complete` · [main](../../../side/S09.md#s9-complete)

_Sparks comes first, running. Then Fern. Then everyone!_  
_One by one, they lift and push. Creak, creak, creak._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): taking turns  
> _Everyone wants to come through the gate. What helps?_  
> - ✅ Take turns, one by one → Yes. One by one. Everyone gets through.
> - Everyone push at once → Bump! Squash! Taking turns, one by one, works better.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about taking turns when everyone wants the same thing._

**Gran Hazel**: Good gate, that. I never did unstick it.  
_That is how Gran says it’s all done._  

**Notes:** Corvin is “a lonely man who did a wrong thing long ago” and says sorry in s9-ask. s9-dusk’s scene-level set (s9Started) stays with the original scene. The grey ear that goes flat (Sable or a stray) keeps both sides of the gate honest. Canon: s9-dusk is Corvin’s first arrival (the gate sticks); in s9-ask he says he will stand at the gate till morning, so s9-gate and s9-gate-family find him there with frost on his coat. Gran’s stance is always “I’ve done my part. It’s your gate now.”
