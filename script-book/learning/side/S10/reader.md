# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S10: Fourteen Months

## S10. Fourteen Months

- `s10-step1`: Wish one: write your Nubbins into Hazel’s hatch register. · _The register is on the cottage bookshelf. Add a row for every Nubbin you’ve hatched: number, name, first sound, weather._
- `s10-step2`: Wish two: enter any race while Hazel watches from the back row. · _Open Races from the top bar. Any course, any level. Winning doesn’t matter._
- `s10-step3`: Wish three: walk Hazel to the other three lanterns: the stream, the old wall and the long grass. · _Keep to her pace. She stops at each post. Stop with her._
- `s10-step4`: Tell Hazel what the Grove will be. Then walk with her to the foot of the hill. · _After the final choice in chapter 12, follow Hazel across the Grove to the hill path._

#### `reader:s10-step` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-step)

_Dusk, on her first evening home. Hazel sits on the cottage step with her boots off and her feet in a bowl of warm water._  
_She is very pleased about the water. She has a blanket round her shoulders, though the evening is mild._  
**Gran Hazel**: Sit. Not there. There. I like to see the pond.  
_Gran Hazel is very poorly. The doctors can’t make her better. She knows, and now she’s going to tell you._  
**Gran Hazel**: A year, the doctor said. I’ve had fourteen months. I’m going to make him buy me a cake.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 1.OA.A.1, ask): months in a year, and finding a difference  
> _The doctor said a year. A year is twelve months. Gran has had fourteen._  
> _How many extra months has she had?_  
> - ✅ 2 → Two. Two whole bonus months, love. I spent them well.
> - 26 → That’s 14 + 12, adding. We want the difference: 14 − 12 = 2 extra months.
> - 12 → Twelve is what the doctor said. She’s had 14, so 14 − 12 = 2 extra months.
> _For grown-ups: Learned there are 12 months in a year and found a difference (14 − 12 = 2)._

_She says it the way she’d talk about the weather. Then she watches to see how you take it._  
**Gran Hazel**: Don’t be brave at me. I’ve been brave at myself for fourteen months. It’s exhausting.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): sad feelings are allowed, and sharing them helps  
> _Gran doesn’t want you to pretend to be fine. Sad, scared and cross are all allowed._  
> _Feelings you share are lighter to carry. You can tell her how you feel._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that it’s all right to show sad feelings, and that sharing them helps._

**Gran Hazel**: I want three things before I go up the hill. Small ones. I’ve written them down, so don’t argue.  
_In the Grove, people say “going up the hill” for dying. Gran means she will die soon._  
_A seed packet, from her apron pocket. Three lines on the back, in pencil. The third has a little drawing._  
**Gran Hazel**: But first, a favour. Don’t tell them. The valley. Sparks, Fern, any of them.  
**Gran Hazel**: I want them to come round and argue about races and the weather. I don’t want a week of goodbyes.  
**Gran Hazel**: So. Will you keep it? It’s a big thing to ask. I’m asking anyway.  

**Choices**

- **I’ll keep it. They’ll hear it from you, or not at all.**
    - **Gran Hazel**: Good. Thank you.
    - **Gran Hazel**: I’ll leave them something. I’m good at letters. You may have noticed.
    - She wiggles her toes in the water, and that, it seems, is the end of that.
    - Over the hedge, Sparks waves and shouts something about Saturday’s race. You wave back. It feels very heavy.
    - **Gran Hazel**: Number one. Write yours in my register. All of them. I want them in the book before I go.
- **They love you. They should get to say goodbye.**
    - **Gran Hazel**: …They’ll be dreadful about it. Clover will cry on me. Sparks will try to fix it.
    - **Gran Hazel**: Byte will bring a graph.
    - **Gran Hazel**: Fine. Tell them. Tell them to be normal about it. When they aren’t, tell them again.
    - **Gran Hazel**: Number one. Write yours in my register. All of them. I want them in the book before I go.

#### `reader:s10-register` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-register)

_Hazel reads your pages slowly, a finger under each line, the way she reads seed catalogues._  
**Gran Hazel**: Oh, that’s a terrible name. I love it.  
**Gran Hazel**: “Hatched in a hurry”? They all hatch in a hurry, love. Write the weather. You’ll want the weather, later.  

> **Lesson** (maths, do): fill in a row of a data table  
> **Gran Hazel**: A register is a table. Each row is one egg. Each column is one kind of fact.  
> **Gran Hazel**: Number, name, first sound, weather. Fill in a row for your newest Nubbin. Every box, mind.  
> Check: `edu:fill:register-row`
> _For grown-ups: Filled in a row of a data table, with a column for each kind of information._

_Under your last line she takes the pencil and writes one more, small and firm._  
_“Keeper from this page on,” it says, and then your name._  
**Gran Hazel**: There. Now it’s in the book. Things in the book stay.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): written records tell the future about the past  
> **Gran Hazel**: People have always written things down so they aren’t forgotten. Farm books, letters, diaries.  
> **Gran Hazel**: Long after we’re gone, somebody reads them and knows how we lived. Even the weather.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that written records let people in the future learn about life in the past._


#### `reader:s10-wish2` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-wish2)

**Gran Hazel**: Number two. One race. I want to sit in the stands with a terrible pie, like a normal person.  
**Gran Hazel**: Forty years, and I never once sat at the back. I was always in the gap, or down at the rail, being a nuisance.  
**Sparks**: Hazel’s coming to a race? HAZEL’S coming to a RACE?  
_Sparks has appeared from nowhere, as she does, holding a flag. Nobody knows where she got the flag._  
**Sparks**: I’m saving you a seat. Front row. I’ll fight Ember for it. I’ll lose, but I’ll fight her.  
**Gran Hazel**: Sparks, love. I want to sit at the back.  
**Sparks**: …Back row. I’ll fight Ember for the back row.  
**Gran Hazel**: Bring whichever one wants to go. Fast or slow. I don’t mind about winning. I never did, whatever they tell you.  
**Sparks**: She minded. She once threw a hat at a finish line.  
**Gran Hazel**: It was a very small hat.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): compare what a character says with what she does  
> _Hazel says she never minded about winning. Sparks says she once threw a hat at a finish line._  
> _What does the hat tell you about Hazel?_  
> - ✅ She cared about winning more than she says. → Yes! What a character does can tell you more than what she says. Hazel minded. A lot. Small hat or not.
> - She didn’t like the hat. → Ha, maybe! But you throw a hat at a finish line when you really, really want to win. Hazel minded more than she says.
> - She never minded at all. → That’s what Hazel says. But actions are clues. Throwing a hat shows she minded, at least back then.
> _For grown-ups: Compared what a character says with what she does, to understand her better._


**Choices**

- **Bring the fast one. Give her something to shout about.**
    - **Gran Hazel**: Oh, you’re cruel. I’ll have no voice left by the second bend. Good.
- **Bring the slow one. The one who stops to look at things.**
    - **Gran Hazel**: The dawdler? Oh, good. We’ll be there all afternoon. I’ll get a second pie.
    - **Sparks**: Back row’s got the best view of last place. I’ve always said so. I’ve never said so.

#### `reader:s10-cheer` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-cheer)

_The usual roar from the stands. Under it, from the back row, one voice carries over all the others._  
_Sparks, beside her, shouted herself hoarse on the first lap and has been cheering in mime ever since._  
_Win or lose, when you come off the course, Hazel is on her feet, holding half a pie, beaming._  
**Gran Hazel**: Pies are four Shells each, and I’ve had two. Worth every Shell.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.D.8, ask): a two-step money problem with Shells  
> _Hazel paid for her two pies with a 10-Shell coin._  
> _How many Shells did she get back?_  
> - ✅ 2 → Yes. Two pies at 4 Shells is 8 Shells. 10 − 8 = 2 Shells change.
> - 6 → That would be one pie: 10 − 4 = 6. But she had two: 4 + 4 = 8, and 10 − 8 = 2.
> - 8 → 8 Shells is what the pies cost. Her change is 10 − 8 = 2 Shells.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a two-step money problem with Shells (2 × 4 = 8, then 10 − 8 = 2)._

**Gran Hazel**: That one runs for you. Don’t ever spend that.  
**Gran Hazel**: You can spend Shells. You can spend time. You can spend a perfectly good pie, and I have. Never that.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, notice): recognise a metaphor  
> _Hazel talks about your Nubbin’s trust as if it were money: something you could spend, and must never spend._  
> _Talking about one thing as if it were another is called a metaphor._  
> _For grown-ups: Recognised a metaphor (trust described as something you could spend)._

_She sits down again rather suddenly, and stays sitting while the stands empty. Nobody hurries her._  

#### `reader:s10-wish3` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-wish3)

_Dusk. The last line on the seed packet has the drawing: four little posts in a row, and two stick figures._  
**Gran Hazel**: Number three. Walk the four lanterns with me. Once. Slowly. I’ll be slow whether you like it or not.  
_She takes your arm at the pond lantern. Not leaning. Holding._  
**Gran Hazel**: Moss’s post. I’ll tell you something about it, and then you’re not to make a fuss.  
**Gran Hazel**: I came here young. I didn’t know one end of a Nubbin from the other, and I was too proud to ask.  
_For a moment the pond is younger, and so is the light. There is snow on the bank._  
_A young woman sits by the frozen pond in a coat too big for her, with a bucket of feed she doesn’t know how to give._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-ESS2-3, notice): ice floats, so ponds freeze from the top  
> _Look at the frozen pond. Ice floats on water, so a pond freezes from the top down._  
> _Under the ice, the water stays liquid. Fish wait out the winter down there, slow and safe._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that ice floats, so ponds freeze from the top and fish survive in the water below._

**Young Hazel**: I don’t know what I’m doing. I don’t know what any of this is for.  
_A small, old, green-grey thing sits down a little way off. It doesn’t come closer. It doesn’t go away._  
_It sits there the next day, and the next. All that winter it sits beside her, and says nothing at all._  
_At the first thaw she looks down, and it is looking up at her._  
**Moss**: …You’ll do.  
_The light goes back to being now._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, notice): what a flashback is  
> _That was a flashback: a jump back in time, to show something from the past._  
> _Writers use flashbacks to show why a character is the way she is. Now you know where rule three came from._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned what a flashback is and why writers use them._

**Gran Hazel**: Rule three. I thought I’d made it up. I’d been on the end of it for a whole winter.

#### `reader:s10-lanterns` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-lanterns)

_At the stream lantern, she laughs at nothing at all. Then she explains that Briar would have._  
**Gran Hazel**: Briar’s. You can’t stand here and be gloomy. People have tried.  
_At the old wall, she says nothing. She puts her hand flat on the post, the way you’d touch a sleeping friend._  
**Gran Hazel**: Hush’s. For keeping. I kept a great deal here, one way and another.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): oral history: how stories carry the past  
> _Each post has a story. Gran learned them from people who learned them from people older still._  
> _The past, remembered in stories told out loud, is called oral history. It’s how the Grove remembers._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that oral history keeps the past alive through stories told aloud._

_Then the long grass. The fourth post is lit. It has burned steady since the Long Night, all on its own._  
_Hazel stops. She looks at it for a long time._  
**Gran Hazel**: Twenty-two years I came out every night and looked at this post. Flicker, flicker.  
**Gran Hazel**: I stopped hoping, after a while. I never stopped looking. They’re different things. Remember that.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): hope and patience (think question)  
> **Gran Hazel**: Hoping and looking. I say they’re different. You tell me.  
> _Can you keep looking for something even after you’ve stopped hoping?_  
> - Yes. Looking can be a way of loving. → That’s how it felt. Looking was just what I did, every night, because I loved them. That’s a thoughtful answer.
> - No. If you’re still looking, you must still hope a bit. → Maybe you’re right. Maybe a tiny bit of hope stayed, hiding under the looking. That’s a fair answer, and Gran likes it.
> - I don’t know. → Neither did I, for twenty-two years. That’s allowed. That’s an honest answer.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about the difference between hoping and keeping on (a think question with no wrong answer)._

_…_  
**Gran Hazel**: Can I ask you something? I’ve never asked anyone. There’s never been anyone to ask.  
**Gran Hazel**: Should I have gone after him? That night. Instead of wedging a gate and waiting.  

**Choices**

- **You were right to wait. He had to come back on his own.**
    - **Gran Hazel**: Maybe. It’s a very comfortable answer. I’ve been suspicious of it for twenty-two years.
    - **Gran Hazel**: But you can’t carry anyone back. I’ve tried, with Nubbins. They only ever walk.
    - **Gran Hazel**: That was the one I wanted. Thank you, love.
- **Somebody should have gone after him.**
    - **Gran Hazel**: Yes. Some nights I think so too.
    - **Gran Hazel**: I’d have dragged him home by the ear. He’d have hated me properly, and the valley might never have gone grey.
    - **Gran Hazel**: Or I’d have stood in that cold garden long enough to start liking the quiet. I don’t know. I never will.
    - **Gran Hazel**: That was the one I wanted. Thank you, love.

#### `reader:s10-night` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-night)

_You walk her home. At the cottage door she stops, with one hand on the frame._  
**Gran Hazel**: Tomorrow they’ll all come. You’ll tell them what the Grove is going to be. I want to hear you say it.  
**Gran Hazel**: After that, nobody fuss. I mean it.  
**Gran Hazel**: They still think I’m here for the holidays. Let them. It’s been lovely, being ordinary.  
_She goes in. Through the window you see her sit on the edge of the bed for a long time before she lies down._  
_You stand in the dark garden, holding the secret. It is the heaviest thing you have ever carried._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): surprises and heavy secrets; telling a trusted grown-up  
> _Some secrets are happy, like a surprise party. Some are heavy, and make your tummy hurt._  
> _If a secret ever feels too heavy, it is always all right to tell a grown-up you trust. That’s asking for help._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the difference between happy surprises and heavy secrets, and that telling a trusted grown-up is always all right._

#### `reader:s10-letter` · [main](../../../side/S10.md#s10-letter)

_Morning. Your Nubbins are up before you. The gate is stuck. You lift and push._  
_Her lantern is back on the hook by the cottage door. Somebody carried it down at first light._  
_You haven’t lit it yet._  
_Gran Hazel died up on the hill in the night. Her body stopped working, and it won’t start again._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): grief: what it is and what helps  
> _The big, heavy sadness you feel when someone you love dies is called grief._  
> _Grief can come in waves: all right one minute, very sad the next. That’s normal._  
> _What helps is people, and remembering. Gran made sure you’d have both._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the word grief, that it comes in waves, and that people and remembering help._

_On the signpost, under the stone, where the first one was: a letter in your grandmother’s writing._  
**Gran Hazel**: “If you’re reading this, I’ve gone up the hill, and this time I’m staying. Don’t be dramatic about it.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “Be a little dramatic. I’ve earned a little.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “You kept my three rules, more or less, which is more than I did. Here’s a fourth, because I can’t help myself.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “Show up. Every morning, same stuck gate, same stubborn chin. That’s all a Grove ever was. The rest is fruit.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “Tell Fern to prune the trees. Tell Sparks the hair grows back. Tell Byte his shoelaces are untied.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “Leave fruit at the edge for the ones in the long grass. You won’t always see who eats it. Leave it anyway.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “If he came to the gate, you’ll have decided about Corvin by now. Whatever you decided, I’d have done it worse.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “I’m not sorry. I’m sorry it’s now. Both are true. You knew that already.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “Love, Gran. P.S. The gate sticks. You know the rest.”  

> **Lesson** (literacy, notice): how a letter is set out  
> _Gran’s letter talks straight to you, and ends with a sign-off: “Love, Gran.”_  
> _“P.S.” is short for postscript, which means “written after”. It’s for one last thing you think of after you sign._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned how a letter is set out, including the sign-off and the P.S. (postscript)._

_Behind you, someone lifts and pushes the gate: Fern, with a basket. Then Sparks. Then everyone, one by one._  
_They have come to see why you are standing so still. You hold the letter out. You don’t have to say anything._

### Glossary

- **poorly**: Ill; not well.
- **exhausting**: Making you feel very, very tired.
- **register**: A book or list where things are written down, to keep a record.
- **column**: A line of boxes going down a table, all for the same kind of fact.
- **nuisance**: Someone or something that annoys people.
- **hoarse**: Having a rough, croaky voice from shouting or a sore throat.
- **metaphor**: Describing something by talking about it as if it were something else.
- **flashback**: A part of a story that jumps back to the past.
- **thaw**: When ice and snow melt because the weather is warming up.
- **gloomy**: Sad, and without much hope.
- **oral history**: The past as people remember it and tell it out loud.
- **ordinary**: Normal and everyday; nothing special.
- **handkerchief**: A square of cloth for wiping your nose or eyes.
- **grief**: The deep sadness you feel when someone you love dies.
- **P.S.**: Short for postscript: a note added at the end of a letter, after the name.
