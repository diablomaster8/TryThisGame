# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Side story S11: The Blight

## S11. The Blight

- `s11-step1`: Go next door. Look at Fern’s poorly plum trees. · _Go through the gap in the hedge. The poorly trees are at the bottom of the hill._
- `s11-step2`: Look at every tree with Byte’s magnifying glass. · _Look at a leaf, the bark and the fruit. Then pick a tag. You can always look again._
- `s11-step3`: Catch one of the tiny bugs on the leaves. · _Keep the sun in front of you, so your shadow stays behind you. Don’t look right at the sun._
- `s11-step4`: Help Clover let the ladybirds go in the hedge. · _Open the jar by the old dead tree. Then step back and keep still._

#### `sprout:s11-fern` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-fern)

_Creak! Fern is at your gate. It is very early._  
_She holds out a plum. It has black spots._  
**Fern**: Don’t touch it. Just look.  
_She tips her hand. Squish! The plum goes soft and dusty._  
**Fern**: Three of my trees look like this. They were fine yesterday.  
**Fern**: Hazel helped me plant these trees. Years ago.  
**Fern**: I haven’t told anyone. I’m too worried.  

> **Lesson** (sel, ELOF: Social and Emotional Development, ask): telling someone when you are worried  
> _Fern is worried. What could help her?_  
> - ✅ Tell a friend about it → You’re right. Telling someone helps. And I just told you!
> - Keep it a secret → Keep it a secret? Pop! The worry grows like a balloon. Secrets make worries feel bigger. Telling you helps already.
> - Pretend it isn’t there → Pretend it isn’t there? Hee! The plum would still sit there, spotty. I tried that. The plums are still spotty! Telling helps more.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about what helps a worried friend: telling someone they trust._


**Choices**

- **Go and look now.**
    - You go through the hedge. Fern follows, slowly.
- **Give her a hug first.**
    - **Fern**: Oh! Thank you. That helps a bit.
    - Then she marches off through the hedge.
- **Say you’ll fetch Byte.**
    - **Fern**: Byte! Yes. Byte loves a puzzle.

#### `sprout:s11-ember` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-ember)

_Three trees at the bottom are poorly._  
_Their leaves are curled up and black._  
_Ember is at the wall. Her arms are folded tight._  
**Ember**: Burn them all! Tonight! Before it spreads!  
**Fern**: All of them? There are forty trees!  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.C.6, ask): more and fewer  
> **Clover**: Lots of trees are green and well. Only three are poorly.  
> **Clover**: Are there more well trees, or more poorly trees?  
> - ✅ More well trees → Yes! Lots and lots of well trees. Only three poorly ones.
> - More poorly trees → More poorly trees? Pop, pop! Count again: only three. Let’s look again. Only three are poorly. Lots more are well!
> _For grown-ups: Compared two groups and saw that the big group of healthy trees had more._

**Clover**: Wait. We don’t know what it is yet.  
**Ember**: I waited once, and I lost my Ash.  
_Fern sits down on a box. Creak! She is very quiet._  

#### `sprout:s11-lens` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-lens)

_Thump, thump! Byte runs up. He has a magnifying glass on a string._  
**Byte**: Nobody burns anything till we look properly.  
**Ember**: You have till sunset. Then I’m doing it.  
**Byte**: This glass makes little things look big.  
_Byte ties a tag on each tree. NOT FINE. FINE. PROBABLY FINE._  
**Byte**: Look at the leaf, the bark and the fruit.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): parts of a tree: leaf, bark, fruit  
> _(Point to a leaf. Now the bark. Now a plum.)_  
> **Byte**: Leaf, bark, fruit. A plum tree has all three.  
> Check: `edu:point:tree-parts:leaf-bark-fruit`
> _For grown-ups: Named three parts of a fruit tree: leaf, bark and fruit._

**Byte**: And don’t lick anything! I have to say that.  
**Clover**: This branch looks lonely. Can I give it a bandage?  
**Byte**: It isn’t hurt. It’s a branch.  

#### `sprout:s11-map` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-map)

_By the afternoon, every tree has a tag._  
_Three trees say NOT FINE. They are all in a row._  
**Byte**: Look! The poorly trees are all where the wind blows.  
**Byte**: Something came on the wind. Something tiny.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): wind can carry light things  
> _(Hold up a tissue or a leaf. Blow gently: whoosh!)_  
> **Byte**: See? Moving air pushes light things. Wind can carry tiny bugs too.  
> Check: `edu:blow:wind:tissue`
> _For grown-ups: Blew on something light to see how wind can carry small, light things._

**You**: What is in the wind?  
**Byte**: Good question. I have a jar!  

#### `sprout:s11-jar` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-jar)

_Byte gives you a jam jar with a cloth lid._  
_Clover gives you another one. Just in case._  
_You crouch in the long grass. The sun is warm._  
_A tiny grey bug lands on a leaf._  
_Your shadow touches the leaf. Plop! The bug drops away._  
**Clover**: Shy things hide from shadows. Keep the sun in front of you.  
**Clover**: Then your shadow stays behind you. Never look right at the sun!  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): keeping still and calm; patience  
> **Clover**: Let’s be still as a stone. Count to five, slowly.  
> _(Count slowly together: one… two… three… four… five.)_  
> **Clover**: Still and slow. That’s how you catch shy things.  
> Check: `edu:keep:still:count-5`
> _For grown-ups: Practised keeping their body still and calm while counting slowly to five._

**Ember**: Or we could just burn it all.  
_But Ember stays. She watches every try._  

#### `sprout:s11-beetle` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-beetle)

_Got it! Plop! A tiny grey beetle in the jar._  
_It is no bigger than a lentil._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): insects have six legs  
> _(Look closely. Count the beetle’s legs together.)_  
> **Byte**: One, two, three, four, five, six. Six legs!  
> **Byte**: Beetles are insects. Every insect has six legs.  
> Check: `edu:count:legs:6`
> _For grown-ups: Counted a beetle’s legs and learned that all insects have six legs._

**Byte**: It’s covered in black dust. That’s the poorly stuff!  
**Byte**: The black dust rides on the beetle. Like a bus!  
**Byte**: The beetle isn’t sick. It gives the dust a ride.  
_Byte follows the wind. He finds an old dead tree, full of beetles._  
**Byte**: Only the trees near this one get poorly. The rest are safe!  
**Clover**: I have ladybirds at home. Ladybirds eat beetle eggs!  
**Clover**: Look! One tree in the poorly row is fine.  
**Fern**: That’s Grudge. Hazel’s old tree. It has the sourest plums.  
_Byte puts a new tag on the jar. It says: GUILTY._  

#### `sprout:s11-dusk` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-dusk)

_The sun goes down. Bats fly over the stream._  
_Swish! A light comes through the hedge. It’s Ember, with a burning torch._  
**Ember**: I’m sorry, Fern. I can’t wait.  
**Ember**: I waited once, for Ash. I waited too long.  
_Ember’s face looks cross. But inside, she is scared._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): naming feelings: scared can look like cross  
> _Ember looks cross. What is she really feeling?_  
> - ✅ Scared → Yes. Ember is scared of losing the trees. Scared can look cross.
> - Happy → Happy, with a burning torch and a frown? Hee! Look at her face. Not happy. She’s scared. Scared can look cross.
> - Silly → Silly? Pfft! There are no funny faces here. Not silly. She’s scared. Sometimes scared looks cross.
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that a person who looks cross may really be feeling scared._

_Byte holds the map. Clover holds Fern’s hand._  
_Everybody looks at you._  

**Choices**

- **Show her Byte’s map, kindly.**
    - You hold out the map. Ember reads it by torchlight.
    - **Ember**: Three trees. Not forty. Just three.
    - She dips the torch in the water butt. Hiss! It goes out.
    - **Ember**: Show me the old tree. I’ll help.
- **Ask the grown-ups to burn only the poorly trees, safely.**
    - Ember nods. Fern helps her. They go very carefully.
    - They burn only the three poorly trees and the old dead tree.
    - You stay far back with Clover. Fire is for grown-ups.
    - **Ember**: Not on my own this time. Not in a rush.
    - Fern brings out tea. The fire gets small, then goes out.
- _(left out in this band; plays as `map`)_

#### `sprout:s11-ladybirds` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-ladybirds)

_Clover opens her jar. Pop!_  
_Out come ladybirds! Hundreds of them, red as berries._  
**Clover**: Ladybirds eat tiny bugs that hurt plants. Gardeners love them.  
**Clover**: Off you go, ladybirds! Gobble up the beetle eggs!  
_One lands on your thumb. Then three more._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 7  
> _(Look at the ladybird on your thumb. Count its spots together.)_  
> **Clover**: One, two, three, four, five, six, seven. Seven spots!  
> **Clover**: This kind is called a seven-spot ladybird.  
> Check: `edu:count:spots:7`
> _For grown-ups: Counted seven spots and met the seven-spot ladybird, a gardener’s friend._

**Byte**: I tried to tag them all. They’re all called LADYBIRD now.  

#### `sprout:s11-complete` · [main](../../../side/S11.md#s11-complete)

_A week later, the orchard is quiet and green._  
_Three old stumps. Lots and lots of happy trees._  
_In the middle stands Grudge, full of sour purple plums._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, do): taste words: sour and sweet  
> _(Make a sour face together! Now a sweet, happy face.)_  
> **Fern**: Sour like a lemon. Sweet like a strawberry. Grudge is sour!  
> Check: `edu:make:face:sour`
> _For grown-ups: Acted out the taste words “sour” and “sweet”._

_Plop! A sour plum falls from Grudge._  
**Fern**: The beetles don’t like Grudge. Too bitter for them!  
**Fern**: This was Hazel’s. A little knife for joining trees.  

> **Lesson** (health, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): staying safe with sharp things  
> **Fern**: Who should help when we use something sharp?  
> - ✅ A grown-up → Yes. Sharp things need a grown-up. Then everyone stays safe.
> - Nobody → Nobody? A knife on its own? Ouch, that is no good! Ouch, no! Sharp things can cut. A grown-up helps every time.
> - A Nubbin → A Nubbin with a knife? Hee, hee! Its stubby hands would drop it. Ha! Nubbins have no hands for knives. A grown-up helps.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a grown-up always helps when something sharp is used._

**Fern**: It’s sharp. We’ll use it together, you and me.  
**Fern**: You saved my orchard. You’re the Orchard Warden now!  
_Byte had put a tag on Ember. It said CROSS._  
_He crosses it out. He writes: SCARED._  
**Ember**: …Yes. That’s me. Scared.  

**Notes:** Sprout leaves out s11-dusk’s “stand” (facing down a torch); defaultChoice is “map”. In “together”, grown-ups burn only the poorly trees and the child watches from far back.
