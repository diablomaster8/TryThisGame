# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S12: The Hum

## S12. The Hum

- `s12-step1`: Carry Solo home to the Thrums in the old wall. · _Hush’s old wall is the tumbled stone one on the north side of the Grove. Keep the teacup level: Solo gets seasick._
- `s12-step2`: Put your ear to the old wall at dusk and listen for the Queen-Mother. · _She lives at the heart of the wall, low down by Hush’s lantern post. Go when the hum is quietest, and keep still._
- `s12-step3`: Sit with Solo in Luna’s garden until the first warm, still morning. · _Sit on the bench by the moonflowers after dark, and don’t get up. Solo doesn’t need answers. It needs company._
- `s12-step4`: Follow the swarm by sound with Pixel’s tuning fork. · _Strike the fork and turn slowly. It buzzes hardest when you face the swarm. If you lose them, stand still and strike it again._

#### `reader:s12-solo` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-solo)

_Dusk in Luna’s night garden. The moonflowers open one by one, with a sound like someone unwrapping a sweet._  
**Luna**: Keeper. Over here. Quietly. I’ve got something in a teacup, and I don’t know what to do with it.  
_In the teacup is a beetle the size of your thumb. It is round and bronze, with a fuzzy back like a peach._  
_It is, just about, a Nubbin. Its wings buzz whenever anyone speaks._  
**Luna**: It’s a Thrum. Hundreds of them live in Hush’s old wall, in a colony. They hum. They never come out alone.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS2-1, notice): insects that live together in a colony  
> **Luna**: Thrums live a bit like honeybees. A honeybee colony can be tens of thousands of bees, all sharing one home.  
> **Luna**: Each bee has a job: cleaning, building wax, feeding babies, guarding the door, fetching nectar.  
> **Luna**: Together they survive things no single bee could. Alone, one bee doesn’t last long at all.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that honeybees live in colonies where each bee has a job, and the group helps them all survive._

_The beetle looks up at you. It clears its throat, which for a beetle is mostly wings._  
**Solo**: We are— no.  
**Solo**: I. I am. Good evening. I am sorry. I am not sure who is sorry. I think it is me.  
**Luna**: It’s been doing that for an hour. It finds it very upsetting.  
**Luna**: It needs a name, I think. Things that say “I” usually do.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.3.4, ask): the meaning of the word “solo”  
> **Luna**: In music, when one person sings on their own, it’s called a solo.  
> **Luna**: So what might “Solo” mean, as a name for a beetle?  
> - ✅ One on its own. → Exactly. A solo is one voice on its own. That’s what this beetle is, for the first time ever.
> - Very loud. → A solo can be loud, but that isn’t what the word means. It means one on its own. A solo singer sings alone.
> - A kind of beetle. → Good guess, but no. “Solo” means one on its own, like a singer singing alone.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out the meaning of “solo” from its use in music._


**Choices**

- **Call it Solo.**
    - **Solo**: Solo. Solo is… one of something. Yes. That is what I am. For now.
    - **Luna**: For now. Good. Always leave yourself a way back.
- **Give it a name of your own.**
    - You say a name. The beetle repeats it three times under its breath, like testing a step.
    - **Solo**: Nobody has ever said a thing just to me before. It is very loud.
- **Ask what it would like to be called.**
    - **Solo**: Like? I have never liked anything on my own. We like things. Together. When we all agree.
    - **Solo**: …I would like you to choose. Is that allowed? That is my first like.
    - You call it Solo. It seems to fit, the way a borrowed coat fits.

#### `reader:s12-wall` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-wall)

_The old wall hums. You feel it before you hear it. Lay a palm on the stone and it buzzes like a purring cat._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, ask): sound is made by vibration, and vibration can be felt  
> _The wall buzzes under your hand. Stone can’t hum by itself. So what are you feeling?_  
> - ✅ Hundreds of tiny wings shaking, and the shaking passing into the stone. → Yes. Fast shaking is called vibration. The Thrums’ wings vibrate, and that makes the hum. The vibration travels into the stone, so your hand can feel the sound as well as hear it.
> - The wind blowing through the cracks. → Wind can whistle, but tonight the air is still. It’s the Thrums’ wings. They shake very fast. That shaking is called vibration, and it makes the hum.
> - The stone is warm. → Warmth doesn’t buzz. A buzz is a shake. The Thrums’ wings shake very fast. That’s vibration, and vibration makes sound you can feel.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that sound is made by vibration, and that vibrations can be felt through solid things._

_The cracks smell faintly of honey. Every crack has a tiny round door, and every door has a face in it._  
**The Thrums**: Good evening, Keeper.  
**The Thrums**: We would like to say: thank you for returning the one.  
**The Thrums**: We would also like to say: the one is welcome. One moment. We are checking we all agree about “welcome”.  
_There is a long pause, full of buzzing._  
**The Thrums**: We do. Welcome. We are sorry about the delay. We are very many.  
_Solo does not get out of the teacup. Solo climbs up your sleeve instead, and stays there, humming quietly, alone._  
**Solo**: In a minute. I will go in in a minute.  
_It does not go in._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): noticing when someone isn’t ready  
> _Solo says “in a minute”, but doesn’t move. Sometimes that means: I’m not ready, and I don’t know how to say so._  
> _You don’t have to push. You can let someone stay close until they are ready._  
> _For grown-ups: Noticed that a character wasn’t ready, and that giving someone time is a kind response._


#### `reader:s12-flat` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-flat)

_Next morning Pixel is at the old wall with a tuning fork, a notebook and a very serious face._  
**Pixel**: Hi. Don’t move. Sorry. You can move. Just don’t buzz.  
**Pixel**: I tune my lantern timer to this wall. Have done for years. It hums a perfect B-flat. Nearly. Always has.  
_She taps the fork on her knee and holds it to the stone. The fork sings. The wall sings back, a tiny bit lower._  
**Pixel**: It’s gone flat. Walls don’t go flat. Walls don’t do anything. That’s their whole thing.  

> **Lesson** (music, 1-PS4-1, ask): pitch: what “flat” means in music  
> **Pixel**: A tuning fork always plays the same note. That’s what makes it useful.  
> **Pixel**: The wall’s note is now a little lower than the fork’s. So what does “flat” mean in music?  
> - ✅ A note that’s a bit lower than it should be. → Exactly. Flat means a little too low. A bit too high is called sharp. Slower vibrations make lower notes. Something in that wall has slowed down.
> - A note that’s too loud. → Loud and soft is a different thing. That’s volume. Flat is about pitch: a note that’s a little too low. Slower vibrations make lower notes.
> - A wall with no bumps. → Ha! That’s the other meaning. Same word, two jobs. In music, flat means a note a little too low. Slower vibrations make lower notes.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that “flat” means a note slightly too low, and that slower vibrations make lower notes._

**The Thrums**: Good morning. We are sorry about the flat.  
**The Thrums**: It is our Queen-Mother. She is the middle of the hum. She is getting quieter.  
**The Thrums**: When the middle goes quiet, the hum splits. Half of us leave with a new queen. Half of us stay.  
**The Thrums**: We have done it before. We do not remember it. The ones who remember always leave.  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS2-1, notice): how a honeybee colony swarms and splits  
> **Pixel**: Real honeybees do this too. It’s called swarming. When a hive gets crowded, the colony splits in two.  
> **Pixel**: In bees it’s the old queen who leaves, with about half the workers. A new queen grows up in the old hive.  
> **Pixel**: Thrums aren’t bees, so they do it their own way. But a split is how one colony becomes two.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a honeybee colony splits by swarming: the old queen leaves with about half the bees._

**Pixel**: That is the saddest thing a wall has ever said to me.  
_In your sleeve, Solo has gone completely still._  

#### `reader:s12-queen` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-queen)

_At dusk you lie in the grass with your ear against the bottom stone, where the moss is warmest._  
_Deep in the wall, under all the others, there is one slow note, like a big bee in a shut room._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, do): sound travels well through solids  
> _Press your ear right against the stone. Now lift it away, just a little. Now press it back._  
> _The deep note is clearer with your ear on the stone. Sound travels through solids, and often better than through air._  
> _It’s why you can hear a tap on a table so clearly with your ear pressed to the wood._  
> Check: `edu:listen:old-wall:ear-to-stone`
> _For grown-ups: Compared listening through stone and through air, to learn that sound travels through solid things._

**The Queen-Mother**: Ah. The Keeper. And the one in your sleeve. Yes, I can hear it. I can hear all of them.  
**The Queen-Mother**: I am going to tell you something, and you are not to tell the we.  
**The Queen-Mother**: I have said “I” for a very long time. Somebody has to, in the middle of a we. Somebody has to decide when.  
**The Queen-Mother**: It is quite lonely. Nobody tells you that about the middle.  
_Solo makes a small sound in your sleeve, like a question with no words in it._  
**The Queen-Mother**: On the first warm, still morning, half will go. The young one must choose. Go, stay, or something else.  
**The Queen-Mother**: Don’t choose for it. That would be the we all over again, only taller.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): letting others make their own choices  
> _Why does the Queen-Mother tell you not to choose for Solo?_  
> - ✅ Because Solo has only just learned to say “I”, and should get to choose. → Yes. If you chose, you’d be the we all over again: deciding for Solo. You can help someone think. The choice is still theirs.
> - Because she wants to choose herself. → She could, but she doesn’t. She says the young one must choose. Solo has just started saying “I”. Choosing for itself is part of that.
> - Because it doesn’t matter what Solo does. → It matters a lot to Solo. That’s exactly why the choice should be Solo’s. You can help someone think. The choice is still theirs.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why people should get to make their own choices, with help rather than orders._


#### `reader:s12-dark` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-dark)

_Solo won’t go into the wall and won’t come out of your sleeve. So that evening you take it to Luna’s garden._  
_Luna has raked the gravel into slow spirals. In the dark they shine a little, like the inside of a shell._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, notice): similes: comparing with “like”  
> _“Like the inside of a shell” is a simile. A simile compares two things using “like” or “as”._  
> _It helps you picture the gravel: pale, smooth, with a soft shine._  
> _For grown-ups: Spotted a simile and talked about the picture it puts in the reader’s mind._

**Solo**: In the hum I am warm on every side. Nothing is my fault. Nothing is mine. It is very restful.  
**Solo**: Out here everything is mine. The dark is mine. The cold is mine. This leaf is mine, and I did not ask for it.  
**Solo**: If I go back, I stop. Not die. Stop being the one who is frightened. I would quite like to stop.  
**Solo**: But I would also like to see what happens next. To me. Is that greedy?  
**Luna**: I garden at night because nobody else does. I liked being on my own before I liked anything else.  
**Luna**: No. That’s not true. It took years. Some nights I still don’t like it. I come out anyway.  
**Luna**: That isn’t an answer. I haven’t got one. I’ve just got a garden.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): thinking about belonging and being yourself  
> _Solo is torn. In the hum it belongs, and it’s safe. Out here it’s itself, and it’s scared._  
> _What do you think matters more: belonging to a group, or being yourself?_  
> - Belonging. Being part of something keeps you warm and safe. → That’s a fair answer. Groups look after us, and nobody does well completely alone. The Queen-Mother might add: a good group still lets you be you inside it.
> - Being yourself. You should get to think your own thoughts. → That’s a fair answer. Your own thoughts, likes and choices matter. Luna might add: being yourself can be lonely sometimes, and that’s all right too.
> - Both. You can be yourself and still belong. → That’s a fair answer, and a hard one to manage. Most people spend their whole lives working on it. Friends who like you as you are are a way of having both.
> - I don’t know yet. → That’s an honest answer. Luna doesn’t know either, and she’s had years to think about it. Some questions are worth carrying around for a while.
> _For grown-ups: Think question (no right answer): weighed belonging to a group against being yourself._


#### `reader:s12-swarm-goes` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-swarm-goes)

_You wake on the bench with dew in your hair. The air is warm and doesn’t move. It is the morning she meant._  
_Across the Grove, the old wall roars._  
_Bronze pours out of every crack at once and turns in the air, glittering, like a handful of pennies thrown at the sun._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS2-1, notice): when and how swarms leave  
> **Pixel**: Honeybees swarm on warm, calm days too. Thousands pour out at once and swirl in a big cloud.  
> **Pixel**: Swarms are usually gentle. They have no home and no honey to guard yet, so they’re busy, not cross.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that honeybee swarms leave on warm days and are usually gentle because they have no home to guard._

**The Thrums**: Goodbye. Goodbye. We are leaving. Some of us are leaving. We are deciding which.  
**The Thrums**: It is going very slowly. We are sorry. We are trying to be quick and also polite.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.NF.A.1, ask): finding half of a number  
> **Pixel**: Say there were 400 Thrums in the wall, and exactly half leave. How many are leaving?  
> - ✅ 200 → Yes. Half means two equal parts. 200 go and 200 stay. 200 and 200 make 400.
> - 100 → 100 is a quarter of 400: that’s four equal parts. Half means two equal parts. 400 split in two is 200 and 200.
> - 40 → Not quite. 40 is much smaller than half. Half of 4 is 2, so half of 400 is 200. 200 go and 200 stay.
> _For grown-ups: Found half of 400 by splitting it into two equal parts._

**Solo**: I want to see where they go. Not to go. To see. Is there a difference?  
**Pixel**: There’s a difference. I’ve got a tuning fork. Come on.  

#### `reader:s12-follow` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-follow)

_Pixel presses the fork into your hand. It is warm from her pocket and has PROPERTY OF PIXEL scratched on the handle._  
**Pixel**: Strike it on your knee. Hold it up. Turn slowly.  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, notice): a tuning fork vibrates to make its note  
> _Strike the fork and look closely. The two prongs blur. They are shaking back and forth very fast._  
> _That shaking pushes the air, and the pushes reach your ear as sound. Dip a ringing fork in water and it splashes._  
> _As the shaking dies away, the sound fades. That’s why you have to strike it again._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a tuning fork makes sound by vibrating, and the sound fades as the vibration dies away._

**Pixel**: When the fork and the swarm match, it buzzes in your fingers. That means you’re facing them.  
**Pixel**: Walk that way. Strike it again when it goes quiet.  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-2, do): using sound to find where something is  
> _Strike the fork. Turn slowly, a little at a time. Stop when it buzzes hardest in your fingers._  
> _Your ears do this all day, too. Turn your head, and a sound gets louder when you face it._  
> Check: `edu:turn:tuning-fork:loudest`
> _For grown-ups: Turned to find the direction a sound was strongest, to locate something by hearing._

**Pixel**: Don’t walk into the stream. People do.  
**Pixel**: Not me. Other people. Specific other people.  

#### `reader:s12-settled` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-settled)

_The fork leads you over the stream to Hazel’s crooked tree._  
_The swarm hangs from a branch in a great buzzing lump, the shape of a pear._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-LS2-1, notice): how a swarm finds a new home  
> _A real bee swarm does this too. It hangs in a cluster on a branch, with the queen tucked safely inside._  
> _Scout bees fly off to look for a new home, like a hollow tree._  
> _Back at the cluster, a scout does a special dance that shows the others which way to go._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a bee swarm waits in a cluster while scout bees find a new home and dance to share it._

_Pixel holds her fork to it, then towards the wall, then to it again. Then she sits down hard on the bank._  
**Pixel**: It was never one note.  
**Pixel**: It’s hundreds. All a tiny bit apart. They lean towards each other till it sounds like one. That’s all a hum is.  
**Pixel**: I’ve been tuning a clock to a crowd. For years.  

> **Lesson** (music, notice): many voices on nearly the same note sound like one  
> **Pixel**: A choir works the same way. Lots of voices, each a tiny bit different, blend into one big, rich sound.  
> **Pixel**: One voice is a solo. Lots together is a chorus. Both are music.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned how many voices singing nearly the same note blend into one sound, as in a choir._

**The Thrums**: We are here. We are a new we. We are not yet sure what we are like.  
**The Thrums**: One. Solo. Will you come into a we? This one or the old one. Either is warm.  
**Solo**: Keeper. I don’t know. Sit with me. No. Tell me. No. Both. Is that allowed?  
_You don’t choose for it. You sit down on the bank beside the teacup, and wait for Solo to decide._  

**Choices**

- **Solo chooses the hum. You nod.**
    - You carry the teacup back to the old wall. Solo looks at you for a long moment, then climbs out.
    - **Solo**: I will forget being frightened. I think I will forget this, too. Will you remember it for me?
    - It walks in through a round door in the stone. The hum goes on, a tiny bit fuller than before.
    - **Pixel**: Listen to that. B-flat. Nearly. It’s back in tune.
- **Solo chooses a home of its own. You nod.**
    - You find it a knot-hole in the gatepost, out of the wind, with a view of the pond. It inspects it for a long time.
    - **Solo**: Mine. The knot-hole is mine. I chose it. Nobody agreed.
    - **Solo**: It is very quiet in here. I will get used to it. Or I will get used to not being used to it.
    - Every dusk after that, a tiny hum comes from the gatepost. One note. Slightly flat. All its own.
- **Solo asks the hum to leave a little room. You nod.**
    - **Solo**: Lean. But leave a gap. For one. Is that allowed?
    - **The Thrums**: We will discuss it.
    - They discuss it. It takes until lunch. Pixel eats a sandwich. Then a second sandwich. Then yours.
    - **The Thrums**: We have agreed. We will be a hum with gaps in it. Ones may go out, and come in, and go out.
    - **The Thrums**: We have also agreed this was the longest meeting in the history of the wall. We are very proud.
    - **Solo**: In for supper. Out for the stars. I can do that. I can be both, slowly.

#### `reader:s12-complete` · [main](../../../side/S12.md#s12-complete)

_A week later, the Queen-Mother’s note goes quiet for good, in the night, with the whole wall humming round her._  
_In the morning there is a gift on your doorstep: a little hive of clay and wax, no bigger than a teapot._  

> **Lesson** (maths, do): hexagons, and shapes that fit together with no gaps  
> _Look inside the little hive. The wax is made of rows of tiny cells, like real honeycomb._  
> _Tap one cell and count its sides. Six sides: a hexagon. Bees build their honeycomb cells this shape too._  
> _Hexagons fit together with no gaps between them, so no space and no wax is wasted._  
> Check: `edu:tap:shape:hexagon`
> _For grown-ups: Counted the six sides of a honeycomb cell and learned that hexagons fit together without gaps._

**The Thrums**: For the Keeper. It hums. We taught it. We hope that is not rude.  
_At dusk it hums a tune you know: the bit of the Lantern Song that comes round twice._  
_Beside it stands a thimble of pale gold nectar that smells of the wall in summer._  
**The Thrums**: Hum-nectar. A Nubbin who sips it is calm for a day. Only a day. We did not want to overdo it.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, notice): ways to feel calm  
> _There’s no hum-nectar for people. But there are other ways to calm down when feelings get big._  
> _Slow breaths. A quiet hum. Sitting with someone you trust. Each of these can help, and you can try more than one._  
> _For grown-ups: Talked about real ways to calm down: slow breathing, humming, and sitting with someone trusted._

_You find you can now say things in chorus with whoever is nearby. It is a skill you didn’t know you lacked._  
**Pixel**: I’ve retuned the lantern timer. Not to B-flat. To the whole crowd. It runs a bit early now. I like it.  

### Glossary

- **colony**: A big group of animals of one kind that live together and help each other.
- **vibration**: A very fast shaking, back and forth. Vibrations make sound.
- **flat**: In music, a note that is a little lower than it should be.
- **swarm**: A big group of insects flying together, like bees leaving to find a new home.
- **simile**: A way of describing something by comparing it to another thing, using “like” or “as”.
- **scout**: Someone sent ahead to look around and find out what is there.
- **chorus**: A group of voices speaking or singing together.
- **hexagon**: A flat shape with six straight sides.
