# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Side story S13: The Mill Cellar

## S13. The Mill Cellar

- `s13-step1`: Get past the rats in the grain store. · _Share some fruit with them, or tiptoe along the wall. Then find the hatch under the sacks._
- `s13-step2`: Let the water out of the flooded room. · _Open the bottom gate first, then the middle one, then the top. Water runs downhill!_
- `s13-step3`: Find what is stopping the big wheel. · _Look in the biggest gear. Keep your hands far away from the wheel._
- `s13-step4`: Open the iron door with Tobin’s key. · _Take a lantern. It is very dark down there._
- `s13-step5`: Do what you chose with the letter. · _Keep it safe in your bag, take it to the stove with Tobin, or take it to the grave by the river._

#### `sprout:s13-tobin` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-tobin)

_Autumn. The river runs brown and loud. Whoosh!_  
_By the river leans an old mill. Moss sits on its millstone._  
**Old Tobin**: Hazel’s grandchild! You have her chin.  
**Old Tobin**: I’m Tobin. I lit the lamps in this valley for fifty-one years.  
**Old Tobin**: This was Aldous’s mill. He was Corvin’s dad. Yes, grumpy Corvin.  
**Old Tobin**: Under it is a cellar. Four floors, going down, down, down.  
**Old Tobin**: Once, in the year of the Three Hats, the wheel spun so fast—  
_He stops. He scratches his head._  
**Old Tobin**: Where was I? Cellar. Yes. Dark.  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-2, do): we need light to see  
> **Moss**: Dark means we need a lantern. We can only see when there is light.  
> _(Cover your eyes. Can you see? Now open them. Light!)_  
> Check: `edu:cover:eyes:light-dark`
> _For grown-ups: Found out that we can only see things when there is light, by covering and uncovering their eyes._

**Moss**: He never gets to the end of that story. Never once.  

#### `sprout:s13-level1` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-level1)

_Floor one: the grain store. Big sacks, all the way up._  
_It smells of old flour. And mouse._  
_Scritch, scratch. Scritch, scratch. Something is in the dark._  
_Little noses. Little whiskers. Rats!_  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 5  
> _(Count the little noses together: one, two, three, four, five.)_  
> **Old Tobin**: Five rats. And a lot of whiskers.  
> Check: `edu:count:rats:5`
> _For grown-ups: Counted five rats in the grain store._

**Old Tobin**: Mill rats. Very tidy. They never take a day off.  

**Choices**

- **Roll them a plum, and walk past while they eat.**
    - You roll a plum. Five little faces turn. Munch, munch.
    - You walk right past. Nobody looks up.
    - **Old Tobin**: Sharing food! Your grandmother’s way.
- **Cover your lantern and tiptoe along the wall.**
    - Tiptoe, tiptoe, along the wall.
    - A rat sits on your boot. It thinks you are a chair!
    - Behind you, Tobin tiptoes too. He whispers very loudly.

#### `sprout:s13-sluice` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-sluice)

_Creak! The old hoist lifts the sacks. Underneath is a hatch._  
_And three old things, dusty and forgotten._  
**Old Tobin**: Treasure! Everything in a cellar gets a grand name.  
**Old Tobin**: A ladle. The Ladle of Second Thoughts.  
**Old Tobin**: A sock. The Sock of Nobody Knows Where.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): hear words that rhyme  
> **Old Tobin**: Sock! What rhymes with sock?  
> - ✅ Rock → Sock, rock! They sound the same at the end.
> - Cheese → Cheese is lovely. But listen: sock, rock. Rock rhymes with sock!
> - Ladle → Sock, ladle… no. Sock, rock! Rock rhymes.
> _For grown-ups: Found a word that rhymes with “sock”._

**Old Tobin**: A cheese rind. The Cheese Rind of Being Brave!  
_Below the hatch, water lies still in the dark._  
**Old Tobin**: Floor two is flooded. Three gates. Open them in the right order!  
**Old Tobin**: Or we’ll be swimming. Marina could swim in soup.  

#### `sprout:s13-level2` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-level2)

_Bottom gate first. Then the middle. Then the top._  
_Glug, glug, glug! The water runs out of the room._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, notice): water runs downhill  
> **Old Tobin**: Water always runs downhill. Down, down, to the lowest place.  
> _(Run your finger down, down, like the water. Whoosh!)_  
> _For grown-ups: Saw that water runs downhill, which is why the lowest gate opens first._

_Two eels flop on the floor. They look very cross._  
_Up one wall are chalk lines. Each one a little higher._  
_Someone measured a boy here, every year, as he grew._  
_By the top line, a grown-up wrote: BIG ENOUGH TO WORK._  
**Old Tobin**: …Where was I.  
_Tobin goes quiet. He walks on down the steps._  

#### `sprout:s13-wheelpit` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-wheelpit)

_On the steps the flood left boots, a doorknob and a soggy scarf._  
**Old Tobin**: The Squeaky Boots! The Doorknob That Won’t Open!  
**Old Tobin**: And the Soggy Scarf. It still thinks it will dry.  
_Floor three: the wheel pit. The giant wheel drips. Plip, plop._  
_The river pushes it. The whole mill shakes. Rumble!_  
_Moss calls down. He is staying on his millstone, thank you._  
**Old Tobin**: Mind the wheel. It’s stuck, but it can still move.  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, ask): keeping safe near big machines  
> **Old Tobin**: Where should our hands be, near a big wheel?  
> - ✅ Far away, by our sides → Yes. Big machines can hurt fingers. We stay back.
> - On the spokes → Ooh, no. A wheel can move and pinch. Hands far away, by our sides.
> _For grown-ups: Learned to keep hands away from big moving machines._

**Old Tobin**: Find what’s jamming it. Then we step round.  

#### `sprout:s13-level3` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-level3)

_In the biggest gear is a wooden wedge. Somebody put it there._  
_A label hangs from it. It says: STOPPED. LEAVE IT._  
**You**: Somebody stopped the wheel on purpose.  
**Old Tobin**: Aldous did, in his last winter. He sat on that step.  
**Old Tobin**: He was very old. Soon after that, he died.  
**Old Tobin**: His body stopped working, and it won’t start again.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): noticing when someone is sad, and what helps  
> _Tobin is sad. He misses Aldous._  
> _What could help Tobin?_  
> - ✅ Sit next to him for a bit → Thank you. That helps. Sitting together always helps.
> - Run off and play → Tobin might feel lonely. Let’s sit with him a moment instead. Thank you. That helps.
> _For grown-ups: Heard plainly that Aldous died, noticed Tobin was sad, and chose to sit with him._

_Past the wheel, a stair goes down to an iron door. It is locked._  
**Old Tobin**: Not that door. Not yet.  
**Old Tobin**: Read Hazel’s notebook first. Then come back for the key.  

#### `sprout:s13-key` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-key)

_Later, with Hazel’s notebook in your coat, you go back._  
**Old Tobin**: Corvin was Hazel’s helper once. Before that, he was this mill’s boy.  
**Old Tobin**: Every evening I lit the lamp by the gate.  
**Old Tobin**: And every evening there was a boy at the window. Working.  
**Old Tobin**: That’s the whole story. The only one I ever finished.  
_He gives you a little iron key. It’s warm from his pocket._  
**Old Tobin**: I found more treasure. A cog: the Cog of Very Big Patience.  
**Old Tobin**: And a tiny hat, cut from a round millstone. Heavy!  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.G.A.2, do): circles, big and small  
> **Old Tobin**: A cog is round, with little teeth all round the edge.  
> _(Draw a circle in the air. Round and round!)_  
> **Old Tobin**: The hat is a circle too. Big circle, little circle. Both circles!  
> Check: `edu:draw:shape:circle`
> _For grown-ups: Drew a circle and saw that big and small round things are both circles._

**Old Tobin**: Down we go. Together.  

#### `sprout:s13-desk` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-desk)

_The bottom room is small, dry and very cold._  
_A desk. A chair. An apron on a hook, still floury._  
_A pocket watch, stopped just after five o’clock._  
**Old Tobin**: Five in the morning. That’s when the wheel started, every day.  
_Inside the big book are three rules, in chalk._  
_ONE: the wheel never stops. TWO: nobody rests. THREE: keep up!_  
_Under them, in a child’s careful writing, the same three rules._  
_Every page says work, work, work. Never once does it say rest._  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, ask): everybody needs rest and sleep  
> **Old Tobin**: Hazel’s rules were kind ones. These say: never rest.  
> **Old Tobin**: Do people need rest?  
> - ✅ Yes! Everybody needs rest and sleep → Yes. Rest and sleep help our bodies feel better and grow.
> - No, keep working → Aldous thought so. But bodies get tired. Everybody needs rest and sleep. Even millers.
> _For grown-ups: Compared a rule that said “never rest” with Hazel’s kinder rules, and learned that everybody needs rest and sleep._

_At the back is a letter. It was never sent. It says: Corvin._  
**Old Tobin**: I kept it shut away for twenty years. It got heavy, keeping it.  

#### `sprout:s13-letter` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-letter)

_You open the letter. It is short._  
**Aldous Vale**: Dear Corvin.  
**Aldous Vale**: I worked all the time. I never stopped. Not once.  
**Aldous Vale**: I was too busy to say the most important thing.  
**Aldous Vale**: I love you. I should have said it every day.  
**Aldous Vale**: If you ever want to stop, come and sit by the river.  
**Aldous Vale**: Sit as long as you like. Nobody will count.  
**Aldous Vale**: Love, your dad.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, notice): saying “I love you” to the people we love  
> _“I love you.” Three small words. They feel big when we hear them._  
> _(Ask: who could you say “I love you” to today? Wait for an answer.)_  
> _For grown-ups: Heard a kind letter from a dad to his son, and thought about who they could say “I love you” to._

**Old Tobin**: “Nobody will count.” He counted everything. And then that.  
**Old Tobin**: It isn’t mine to decide. What shall we do with it?  

**Choices**

- **Keep it for Corvin. Give it to him when his hands are warm.**
    - You fold it up and put it in your coat, by Hazel’s notebook.
    - **Old Tobin**: Yes. Cold hands can’t hold a thing like that.
    - **Old Tobin**: You’ll know when. Your grandmother always knew when.
- **Ask Tobin to burn it in the stove. Some things end with the person.**
    - **Old Tobin**: That’s a real answer. I’ve thought it too.
    - **Old Tobin**: He wrote lots of letters, and burned them. This would be the last.
- **Read it to Moss, and leave it at Aldous’s grave.**
    - **Old Tobin**: Read to old Moss, and left with Aldous. Yes.
    - **Old Tobin**: Nobody ever read Aldous a letter. This is a way to remember him.

#### `sprout:s13-letter-given` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-letter-given)

_Variant of `s13-letter`._

_You fetch the letter from its tin box. Down the lane you go._  
_Corvin is by the stile. He keeps rubbing his cold hands._  
**Corvin Vale**: Hazel’s grandchild. Twice in one evening.  
_You hold out the letter. He sees the writing and stops._  
**Corvin Vale**: That’s my father’s writing.  
_He reads it slowly. Then again, even slower._  
**Corvin Vale**: “Nobody will count.”  
_He puts it in his pocket, right over his heart._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): feeling two feelings at once  
> _Corvin’s dad said “I love you” at last. How does Corvin feel?_  
> - ✅ Sad and glad, both at once → Yes. He’s sad his dad waited so long. He’s glad he said it. We can feel two feelings at once.
> - Only cross → Maybe a little. But look: his hand is on his pocket. He’s sad and glad, both at once.
> _For grown-ups: Saw that someone can feel sad and glad at the same time._

**Corvin Vale**: Thank you. I don’t know what else. Thank you.  
_He goes on down the lane. You go back up to Hazel._

#### `sprout:s13-settled-kept` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-settled-kept)

_At home, you put the letter in the tin box under your bed._  
_Hazel’s letter is in there too._  
_Hazel’s writing leans over, like it’s walking uphill._  
_Aldous’s writing stands up straight, stiff as a soldier._  

> **Lesson** (art, ELOF: Literacy, do): straight lines and leaning lines  
> _(Draw a straight line in the air, like Aldous. Up and down!)_  
> _(Now a leaning line, like Hazel. Whee, over it goes!)_  
> Check: `edu:draw:line:straight-leaning`
> _For grown-ups: Drew straight and leaning lines in the air, the first strokes of writing._

_You check on the letter a lot. That’s part of keeping it safe._  

#### `sprout:s13-complete` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-complete)

_A week later, Tobin gives you the apron and the stopped watch._  
**Old Tobin**: Somebody meant to hand these down. He never stopped long enough.  
_Then he knocks the wedge out of the gear. Bonk!_  
_The wheel turns. Slowly. As slowly as the river likes._  
**Old Tobin**: There. Nobody’s counting.  
**Old Tobin**: Bring your seeds down. The mill makes lovely flour.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, notice): how a mill turns seeds into flour  
> **Old Tobin**: The river turns the wheel. The wheel turns the big stone.  
> **Old Tobin**: The stone squashes seeds into soft flour. Flour makes bread!  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a water mill uses the river to grind seeds into flour for bread._

**Old Tobin**: Now, the year of the Three Hats… Where was I?  
**You**: Mill. Yes. Light.  
_Tobin laughs so hard he has to sit down._  

#### `sprout:s13-settled-burned` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-settled-burned)

_Variant of `s13-complete`._

_The mill’s old stove still works. Tobin lights it._  
_You stand well back and watch. Fire is for grown-ups._  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, notice): staying safe near fire  
> **Old Tobin**: Stoves and fires are hot. Only grown-ups light them.  
> **Old Tobin**: Children stay back, a big step away. That keeps us safe.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that only grown-ups light fires and that children stay well back from stoves._

_The letter catches. Then it’s gone, quick as a blink._  
**Old Tobin**: There. The last one.  
_Tobin sits on the step with you till the stove is cold._  
_Nobody counts how long._  

#### `sprout:s13-settled-grave` · [main](../../../side/S13.md#s13-settled-grave)

_Variant of `s13-complete`._

_Aldous is buried under the trees by the river._  
_It’s autumn. A yellow leaf falls on the stone. Then another._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): autumn: leaves change colour and fall  
> _Yellow leaves are falling from the trees. What time of year is it?_  
> - ✅ Autumn → Yes! In autumn, many trees drop their leaves.
> - Summer → In summer the leaves are green, and they stay on. Falling yellow leaves mean autumn.
> - Spring → In spring, new green leaves grow. Falling yellow leaves mean autumn.
> _For grown-ups: Connected falling yellow leaves with autumn._

_Moss sits on the stone and shuts his eyes._  
_You read the letter out loud, slowly._  
**Moss**: He was never still enough to be read to. This is for remembering him.  
_You put the letter on the grave, under a flat stone._  
_Tobin takes his hat off. Then he forgets why, and puts it back on._
