# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S14: Tilly’s Tabletop

## S14. Tilly’s Tabletop

- `s14-step1`: Play the first session of Keeps & Critters: the Bog of Mild Peril. · _Roll the cardboard dice when Tilly says. Arguing with the Game Mistress makes her rulings worse, never better._
- `s14-step2`: Get your party up the Tower of the Tax Toad without going broke. · _Every Shell you pay gets taxed again. Pay with silly things the Toad hasn’t thought to tax yet, like soup or a goose._
- `s14-step3`: Find Tilly before the last session. · _Listen for a whistle through two fingers, rising at the end. It’s the supper call Hazel taught her._
- `s14-step4`: Play the last session: get past the Grey Grandmother’s geese and into the Lair. · _Three rounds of dice. You can’t lose to the geese. The worst that can happen is you get a goose._
- `s14-step5`: Sit next to the dragon. Wait. · _Don’t roll. Don’t move. Put the dice down. It takes as long as it takes._

#### `reader:s14-table` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-table)

_Rain on the kitchen window. Sparks comes in without knocking, which is how Sparks comes in, towing a small girl in big wellies._  
**Sparks**: This is Tilly. My cousin. She’s nine. It’s half-term, so she’s in charge now. Sorry.  
**Tilly**: I’m not in charge. I’m the Game Mistress. It’s different. It’s worse.  
_She unrolls a felt-tip map across Hazel’s table, right over forty years of teapot rings. It smells strongly of felt tip._  
_Then the dice: cardboard, cut out by hand, sellotaped at the corners. On every die, the six side just says “LOTS”._  

> **Lesson** (maths, ask): chance: how likely one side of a die is  
> _Each cardboard die has six sides. Only one of them says LOTS._  
> _If you roll one die, how likely is it to land on LOTS?_  
> - Certain. It always lands on LOTS. → Not quite. Five of the six sides aren’t LOTS, so most rolls won’t be. LOTS is unlikely: about one roll in every six.
> - Likely. More often than not. → Close, but there are more not-LOTS sides than LOTS sides: five against one. So LOTS is unlikely, about one roll in six.
> - ✅ Unlikely. About one roll in every six. → Yes! One side out of six. On a fair die every side has the same chance. My dice are VERY fair. I cut them with the good scissors.
> _For grown-ups: Reasoned that one side of a six-sided die comes up about 1 time in 6, so it is unlikely on any single roll._

**Tilly**: Welcome to Keeps & Critters. Rule one: the Game Mistress is always right. Rule two: see rule one.  
**Tilly**: Rule three: there are snacks, but only at the end. If you ask about the snacks, the end gets further away.  
**Byte**: Is there a rulebook? I’d like to read the rulebook first.  
**Tilly**: I’m the rulebook.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): what makes rules fair (think question)  
> _Byte wants a rulebook. Tilly says she IS the rulebook, and she can make up rules as she goes._  
> _What do you think? Is it fair if one player can change the rules any time?_  
> - No. Everyone should know the rules before they start. → That’s a fair answer. It’s why games have rulebooks and towns write their laws down: so the rules are the same for everybody.
> - Yes, if she’s fair about it. → That’s a fair answer too. Lots of games have a referee or a storyteller who decides. It works when everyone trusts them to be fair.
> - It depends if it’s still fun for everyone. → That’s a thoughtful answer. A good rule keeps a game fun and fair for all the players, not just the one in charge.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about who gets to make rules, and what makes a rule fair for everyone._

_Pixel, the inventor, is already building a dice tower out of a cereal box. Tilly watches it the way a hawk watches a vole._  
**Byte**: Twenty years married, and she still builds a tower before reading the rules.  
**Pixel**: Twenty years, and he still reads the rules.  
**Tilly**: Pick your hero. Ten seconds. Nine. Four.  

**Choices**

- **A knight with a slightly bent sword.**
    - **Tilly**: Sir You of the Bent Sword. Good. A bent sword can hit round corners.
- **A wizard who mostly does soup.**
    - **Tilly**: Soup Wizard. Your spells are hot but slow, and you have to blow on them. Don’t argue. It’s canon.
- **A rogue disguised as a hat stand.**
    - **Tilly**: Excellent. Nobody suspects a hat stand. Not even other hat stands.

#### `reader:s14-bog` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-bog)

_The Bog of Mild Peril is the pond, coloured green and labelled “BOG (MILD)”. A felt-tip frog sits in it, looking unimpressed._  
**Sparks**: I ATTACK THE BOG.  
**Tilly**: You can’t attack a bog. Roll anyway.  
**Tilly**: …A one and a one. Your sword is now a sandwich.  
**Sparks**: What KIND of sandwich?  
**Tilly**: Cheese. A sad one. You ate the good half on the way here.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 1.OA.A.1, ask): adding two dice: the smallest and biggest totals  
> _Sparks rolled a one and a one. 1 + 1 = 2. That’s the smallest total two dice can make._  
> **Sparks**: What’s the BIGGEST total? I want it. I want it for my sandwich.  
> - 6 → Six is the biggest on one die. But there are two dice! LOTS + LOTS is 6 + 6, which makes 12.
> - ✅ 12 → Yes! Two LOTS: 6 + 6 = 12. Every total from 2 to 12 is possible with two dice. TWELVE. Sandwich of DESTINY.
> - LOTS of LOTS → That’s what I’d say. In numbers, though, LOTS is 6. So LOTS + LOTS is 6 + 6 = 12.
> _For grown-ups: Added dice faces to find the smallest (2) and largest (12) totals of two six-sided dice._

**Byte**: Actually, that’s not how dice—  
**Tilly**: Rule four. Anybody who says “actually” loses a hit point.  
**Byte**: That isn’t—  
_Byte writes “HP: 8” on his character sheet, crosses it out, and writes “HP: 7 (DISPUTED)”._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, notice): how to disagree kindly  
> _A kind way to disagree is to say: “I see it differently. Can I explain?” Byte may try it. He may not._  
> _For grown-ups: Practised a respectful way to disagree with someone._

_You roll a LOTS. Tilly decides the bog likes you, and lets the whole party across. Nobody knows why, including her._  
**Tilly**: Session over. Nobody died. Somebody’s a sandwich. Same time tomorrow.  

#### `reader:s14-tower` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-tower)

_Next afternoon there’s a new tower on the map, drawn exactly where Sparks’s workshop chimney stands._  
**Sparks**: Hang on. That’s my chimney. Am I the Toad?  
**Tilly**: No comment.  
**Tilly**: The Tower of the Tax Toad. He taxes everything. He’s very rich and very damp.  
**Pixel**: Can I build a siege engine?  
**Tilly**: You can build a siege engine. Roll. …Three. Your siege engine is now a toad. It works for him.  
_Pixel looks at the cereal-box dice tower, then at Tilly, and very slowly moves it out of Tilly’s reach._  
**Tilly**: RIBBIT. That’ll be two Shells for the ribbit.  
**Tilly**: There’s a tax on hats. A tax on stairs. A tax on being taller than a toad. That’s all of you. Sorry.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): equal groups: 4 taxes of 2 Shells  
> **Tilly**: Every tax is two Shells. The ribbit, the hats, the stairs, and being taller than a toad.  
> _Four taxes, two Shells each. How many Shells does the Toad want?_  
> - 6 Shells → Count the taxes again: ribbit, hats, stairs, taller. That’s 4 groups of 2. 2, 4, 6, 8. Eight Shells.
> - ✅ 8 Shells → Yes! 4 groups of 2 is 8. 4 × 2 = 8. The Toad is pleased. The Toad is moist with joy.
> - 42 Shells → Ooh. I like that number. But no. 4 taxes of 2 Shells: 2, 4, 6, 8. Eight Shells.
> _For grown-ups: Solved 4 groups of 2 Shells as multiplication (4 × 2 = 8)._

**Byte**: And is there a tax on paying the tax?  
_Tilly’s eyes go very wide and very bright._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.1.4, ask): words that show a character’s feelings  
> _“Tilly’s eyes go very wide and very bright.” What is she feeling?_  
> - ✅ Thrilled. Byte just gave her a brilliant, terrible idea. → Yes. Wide, bright eyes show excitement. A tax on the tax! Byte will regret this.
> - Scared of the Toad. → Scared eyes can go wide too. But “bright” is a happy, excited word. And look: she says thanks SO much. She’s thrilled.
> - Sleepy. → Sleepy eyes droop and close. Hers go wide and bright. She’s wide awake and thrilled with Byte’s idea.
> _For grown-ups: Used describing words (wide, bright eyes) to work out how a character feels._

**Tilly**: …Yes. Thanks, Byte. Thanks SO much.  
**Pixel**: Twenty years married, and Byte still hands people ideas.  
**Byte**: You married someone who asks questions. It was in the vows.  

#### `reader:s14-toad` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-toad)

**Byte**: The tax on the tax is a tenth of it. Then a tenth of that. It shrinks and shrinks, and never quite stops.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.OA.C.5, ask): a number pattern with a rule: divide by ten each time  
> **Byte**: Let’s cut each Shell into a hundred tiny bits. So the first tax is 200 bits. The tax on that is a tenth: 20 bits.  
> **Byte**: The rule is: divide by ten each time. 200, 20, then…?  
> - ✅ 2 → Yes! A tenth of 20 is 2. Add them up: 200 + 20 + 2 = 222 bits. Two Shells, and then two, and two. And the next tax is a tenth of a bit, and the next a tenth of that. More twos, forever. That’s called recurring.
> - 10 → That would be halving. Byte’s rule is a tenth: 20 shared into ten parts is 2. So the next tax is 2 bits.
> - 0 → It gets very small, but not that fast! A tenth of 20 is 2. After that the taxes are smaller than a bit, but never quite zero.
> _For grown-ups: Continued a number pattern by applying the rule “divide by ten each time” (200, 20, 2)._

**Tilly**: The Toad doesn’t do maths. The Toad does vibes.  
**Byte**: Actually—  
**Tilly**: Six.  
_Byte changes his hit points again, in very small, very cross handwriting._  
_Then Pixel’s Nubbin, asleep on the bread bin all afternoon, wakes up, climbs down onto the map, and eats the Tax Toad._  
_The Toad is cardboard. The Nubbin doesn’t seem to mind. The Toad may have opinions, but nobody will hear them now._  
**Tilly**: …  
**Tilly**: The Tax Toad has been eaten by a god. That’s canon now. Everyone’s taxes are cancelled.  
**Sparks**: BEST GAME. BEST GAME EVER MADE.  
_Tilly laughs so hard she has to lie on the floor. Then she stops, quite suddenly, and looks at the top corner of the map._  
_That corner is coloured grey. The felt tip was pressed so hard it went through the paper, into Hazel’s table._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): reading a character’s actions for hidden feelings  
> _Why do you think Tilly pressed so hard on the grey corner?_  
> - ✅ Something about that corner gives her a big feeling. → That’s good reading. People press hard when they feel something strongly. And she stopped laughing the moment she looked at it. Keep an eye on that corner.
> - Her felt tip was running out. → Maybe! But she stopped laughing very suddenly when she saw it. That’s a clue the corner means something big to her.
> - She really doesn’t like grey. → Could be part of it. But look how suddenly she went quiet. That’s a clue the corner is about a big feeling, not just a colour.
> _For grown-ups: Used a character’s actions (pressing hard, going quiet) to infer a hidden feeling._


#### `reader:s14-sparks` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-sparks)

_The morning of the last session, Sparks is waiting at your gate, quietly. You didn’t know Sparks had a quiet setting._  
**Sparks**: The dragon in that corner’s got bigger. She colours it in again every night. Last night it got a cardigan.  
**Sparks**: It’s Hazel. Obviously it’s Hazel.  
**Sparks**: Hazel went off at the end of the winter. She left Tilly a note, with just “Tilly” on the front. She won’t open it. It’s in her welly.  
**Sparks**: She won’t say the name. Just “the dragon”.  
**Sparks**: Tilly’s gone off somewhere. She does that. You go and find her. She likes you. You’re not a sandwich.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): missing someone, and different ways of carrying a big feeling  
> _Missing someone very much is a kind of grief. It can feel sad, or cross, or both at once._  
> **Sparks**: When I miss someone I get louder. Tilly goes into a game and makes them a dragon.  
> _Talking about it, playing it out, drawing it: all of these are ways to carry a big feeling._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that missing someone is a kind of grief, and that people carry it in different ways, including play._


#### `reader:s14-gate` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-gate)

_You hear her before you see her: a whistle through two fingers, rising at the end. Every Nubbin in the Grove looks up._  

> **Lesson** (science, 1-PS4-1, notice): a whistle is vibrating air  
> _A whistle is air blown hard through a tiny gap. The air shakes back and forth very fast._  
> _That shaking is called a vibration, and it’s what you hear._  
> _The Nubbins have learned that this sound means food, the way a dog learns the sound of its lead._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a whistle’s sound comes from vibrating air, and that animals can learn what a sound means._

_Tilly is sitting on the gate that sticks, kicking it. It doesn’t swing. It hardly ever does._  
**Tilly**: They think it’s supper. It isn’t supper. I just do it.  
**Tilly**: Hazel taught me. She said if you whistle it properly, anyone who loves you comes running.  
_The Nubbins arrive, hopeful, and sit in a row in front of her wellies._  
**Tilly**: See. It works on them.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): why we keep doing things a missing person taught us (think question)  
> _It isn’t supper, but Tilly keeps whistling the supper call. Why do you think she does it?_  
> - It makes her feel close to Hazel. → That’s a kind answer. Doing something a person taught us can feel like holding their hand from far away.
> - She’s hoping someone will come running. → That’s a thoughtful answer. Remember what Hazel told her: anyone who loves you comes running. Maybe Tilly is testing it.
> - She just likes whistling. → That’s a fair answer. Sometimes we do things without knowing why. Feelings don’t always come with labels.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why someone who misses a person might keep doing the things that person taught them._


**Choices**

- **Ask her about Hazel.**
    - **Tilly**: I’m not talking about her.
    - A long pause. A Nubbin licks her welly.
    - **Tilly**: She went without saying goodbye. You’re supposed to say goodbye. That’s the RULE.
    - She takes the note out of her welly. It’s still folded, damp at one corner. She looks at it. She puts it back.
    - **Tilly**: Come and play. I’ve got a dragon to do.
- **Ask her about the dragon instead.**
    - **Tilly**: She’s the Grey Grandmother. She’s very old and very rude.
    - **Tilly**: She flew away without saying goodbye. Her treasure hoard is mostly biscuit tins.
    - **Tilly**: She’s the final boss. Final means you can’t have another one after. Come on.

#### `reader:s14-lair` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-lair)

_The last session. Tilly has drawn the curtains and put a torch under a mixing bowl for “atmosphere”. It mostly lights the bowl._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-1, notice): opaque and transparent materials  
> **Pixel**: It’s a metal bowl. Metal is opaque: light can’t get through it. So the light stays trapped inside.  
> **Pixel**: A glass bowl is transparent. The light would shine straight through. Less spooky. More salad.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the difference between opaque materials (block light) and transparent ones (let light through)._

**Tilly**: The Lair. Beyond this door is the Grey Grandmother. First you have to get past her geese.  
**Tilly**: Her geese are very rude. Rude geese, rude dragon. It runs in the family.  
**Sparks**: I HIT THE GOOSE WITH MY SANDWICH.  
**Tilly**: Roll. …LOTS. The goose eats the sandwich and is now your friend. You have a goose.  
**Pixel**: My toad and I would like to help.  
**Tilly**: Your toad worked for the Tax Toad. The Tax Toad got eaten. Your toad is having a hard time. Roll anyway.  
**Byte**: Do I still have six hit—  
**Tilly**: Four. You said “do” in an “actually” voice.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 1.OA.A.1, ask): take away: hit points from 6 to 4  
> _Byte had six hit points. Now Tilly says four. How many did he just lose?_  
> - 1 → Count back from 6: 5, 4. That’s two steps. 6 − 2 = 4. He lost two.
> - ✅ 2 → Yes. 6 − 2 = 4. Two hit points for one syllable. I want that recorded.
> - 4 → Four is what he has left. He started with 6. 6 − 4 would leave 2. He lost two: 6 − 2 = 4.
> _For grown-ups: Solved a take-away problem (6 − 2 = 4) by counting back._


#### `reader:s14-dragon` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-dragon)

_Tilly lifts an upside-down cereal box off the top corner of the map. Under it is the dragon._  
_She is cut from a biscuit-tin lid and coloured grey, with a felt-tip cardigan and a tiny whistle on a thread._  
**Tilly**: The Grey Grandmother. She flew away without saying goodbye. She’s the boss.  
**Sparks**: Right. I roll to hit the—  
_Tilly snatches the dice off the table so fast that one of them tears._  
**Tilly**: No.  
**Tilly**: Nobody hits her. She’s immune. To swords and sandwiches and toads and geese. To everything.  
_It is suddenly very quiet in the kitchen. The rain has stopped. Nobody noticed when._  
**Tilly**: I don’t know how it ends. I don’t know how you end it if nobody’s allowed to hit her.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): a character’s motive  
> _Why won’t Tilly let anyone hit the dragon?_  
> - ✅ Because the dragon is really Hazel, and Tilly loves her. → Yes. Tilly made the dragon out of missing Hazel. She can be cross with her and still never want her hurt.
> - Because it’s against the rules. → She did just make it a rule. But why? The cardigan and the whistle are clues. The dragon is Hazel, and Tilly loves her too much to let anyone hit her.
> - Because she wants to win. → Look again: she isn’t playing to win. She doesn’t even know how it ends. The dragon is Hazel, and Tilly loves her.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a character’s motive from clues in the story._


**Choices**

- **“My hero puts the sword down and sits next to the dragon.”**
    - **Tilly**: That’s not a move.
    - **Tilly**: …What for?
    - **You**: Hazel’s third rule. When you don’t know what to do, sit down next to them and wait.
- **Roll to see if your hero is allowed to sit down.**
    - You roll the torn die. It lands on the torn side, which has no number at all.
    - **Tilly**: That’s… not a number.
    - **You**: Then the Game Mistress decides. My hero would like to sit down next to the dragon.
    - Tilly looks at the torn die for a long time.
    - **Tilly**: The dice says… the dragon allows it.
- **Ask the Game Mistress what the dragon wants.**
    - **Tilly**: She doesn’t WANT anything. She just WENT.
    - You don’t say anything. Tilly glares at the dragon. The dragon, being cardboard, glares back.
    - **Tilly**: …She wants someone to sit with her. Fine. FINE.

#### `reader:s14-wait` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-wait)

_You move your counter to the dragon’s square, and leave it there. Your hero is a button._  
**Tilly**: She doesn’t do anything. She just sits there being a dragon.  
**You**: That’s all right. We’re waiting.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): calm breathing while you wait with someone  
> _Waiting is easier with slow breaths. Breathe in while you count to four. Breathe out while you count to four._  
> _Do it three times, with your button next to the dragon. No rolling. No rush._  
> Check: `edu:breathe:slow:3`
> _For grown-ups: Practised slow, counted breathing as a way to stay calm and patient beside someone who is upset._

_Sparks moves a button next to yours. Then Pixel. Then Byte, after looking round for a rule against it._  
**Byte**: Is there a rule against it?  
**Tilly**: …No.  
_Four buttons round a biscuit-tin dragon. At the window, a Nubbin taps on the glass, hoping it might be supper._  

#### `reader:s14-tears` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-tears)

**Tilly**: The dragon says—  
_She stops. She picks up the dragon and holds it in both hands._  
**Tilly**: The dragon says she had something important to do. Grown-ups always say that.  
**Tilly**: She says she didn’t say goodbye because she’s rubbish at goodbyes. She’d have cried and made it everyone’s problem.  
**Tilly**: She says—  
**Tilly**: I don’t know what she says. I don’t KNOW. She didn’t SAY.  
_Tilly cries the way nine-year-olds cry: all at once, with her whole face._  
_Sparks is round the table before anyone else can move, and says nothing at all. It is the loudest thing Sparks has ever done._  
_Pixel quietly passes a tea towel. Byte quietly passes a better tea towel._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): crying is allowed, and what helps  
> _Crying is allowed. It’s one of the ways a body lets a big feeling out._  
> _Afterwards, lots of people feel a little lighter. It helps to have someone close by, even if nobody says anything._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that crying is a normal way to let out big feelings, and that having someone nearby helps._

_You keep your button where it is._  

#### `reader:s14-complete` · [main](../../../side/S14.md#s14-complete)

**Tilly**: Session over. Everyone gets snacks. That’s a rule. It was always a rule.  
_The snacks are crisps, Fern’s slightly burnt biscuits, and a bowl of grapes the Nubbins have already found._  
_Tilly eats with the dragon in her lap. She doesn’t let go of it once._  
_Now and then Tilly pats her left welly, to check the note is still there. It is._  
**Tilly**: The Grey Grandmother isn’t a boss any more. She’s a character. She lives in the game and you can visit her.  
**Tilly**: And Byte gets all his hit points back. For the tea towel.  
**Byte**: Thank you. I’d like that in writing. Actually—  
**Tilly**: Don’t push it.  
_She puts her cardboard crown on your head. The front says GAME MISTRESS. The back, smaller, says “(OR KEEPER)”._  
**Tilly**: You keep the map. And the dice. Not the torn one. I’m keeping the torn one.  
_When they’ve gone, you find the map on Hazel’s table. Someone has coloured the grey corner yellow._  
_“HERE BE HAZEL. SHE IS ALOWED.”_  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ask): spelling “allowed”, and the homophone “aloud”  
> _Tilly wrote ALOWED. Which spelling means “it’s OK, she’s let in”?_  
> - ✅ allowed → Yes: allowed, with a double L. “Aloud” sounds just the same but means out loud, like reading aloud. Words that sound the same but mean different things are called homophones.
> - aloud → Good ear: it sounds the same! But “aloud” means out loud, like reading aloud. Tilly means “allowed”, with a double L.
> - alowed → That’s Tilly’s spelling, and it’s a brave one. The real word has a double L: allowed.
> _For grown-ups: Corrected a spelling (allowed) and learned the homophone pair allowed / aloud._

_Out by the gate, faintly, someone is practising the supper call. Every Nubbin in the Grove goes to look._  
_Tilly still misses Hazel. But now the missing has somewhere to live: a yellow corner of a map, where you can visit._  

> **Lesson** (art, do): making your own story place on a map  
> _Tilly turned a sad corner into a story place. Make one of your own._  
> _Draw a corner of a map. Label it “HERE BE…” and finish it with someone or something you love or miss._  
> Check: `edu:draw:map-corner:here-be`
> _For grown-ups: Drew and labelled a story map corner for someone or something they love or miss, as Tilly did._


### Glossary

- **canon**: The official facts of a story: the things that really happen in it.
- **disputed**: Argued about, because not everyone agrees it is true.
- **siege engine**: A big machine built long ago for attacking castle walls.
- **vibes**: A slangy word for the feeling something gives you.
- **setting**: One of the levels a machine can be switched to, like loud or quiet.
- **hoard**: A big, secret pile of treasure, kept safe.
- **atmosphere**: The feeling a place has, like spooky, cosy or exciting.
- **opaque**: Not letting light through, so you can’t see through it.
- **immune**: Safe from something, so it can’t hurt you.
- **homophone**: A word that sounds the same as another but means something different, like allowed and aloud.
