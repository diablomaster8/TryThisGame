# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Side story S15: Lucky Eggs

## S15. Lucky Eggs

- `s15-step1`: Help Byte count what comes out of the Mystery Eggs. · _Stand by the wagon and tap Do it. Plain egg or sparkly egg?_
- `s15-step2`: Tiptoe to the wagon and talk to Jackpot the parrot. · _Dashwood is having a nap. Go along the stream stones. Don’t touch the wagon, or the bells ring!_
- `s15-step3`: Shine a light through the special egg to see inside. · _Go somewhere dim, like under the bridge. Hold the egg in front of the lantern._
- `s15-step4`: Make an honest egg stall with Byte. · _Put the stall by the bridge or by your gate. Byte writes the true numbers on the board._

#### `sprout:s15-wagon` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-wagon)

_A wagon rolls down the lane. Ding-a-ling! Bells on the roof!_  
_A man in a yellow waistcoat sits on top. That’s Dashwood Prance._  
_A brass parrot sits on his shoulder. Tick, tick, tick._  
**Dashwood Prance**: Mystery Eggs! Every egg a winner!  
**Jackpot**: MOST EGGS ARE PLAIN.  
**Dashwood Prance**: Thank you, Jackpot.  
_A big sign says: ONE IN THREE IS SPARKLY!_  
**The children**: Sparkly! Sparkly! Sparkly!  
_A little girl from the village jingles her purse. That’s Pip._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): hearing rhymes  
> **Pip**: Egg, egg, I love an egg!  
> **Pip**: What rhymes with egg?  
> - ✅ leg → Yes! Egg, leg. They rhyme!
> - bell → An egg and a bell? Ding, dong! They sound a little alike. Bell rhymes with shell! Egg rhymes with leg.
> - wagon → Egg and wagon? Rattle, rattle! They don’t sound the same. Egg… wagon. No! Egg rhymes with leg.
> _For grown-ups: Listened for a rhyme: egg and leg._

**Byte**: Is that true? Let’s count every egg and see.  

**Choices**

- **Buy an egg yourself. Let’s see!**
    - **Dashwood Prance**: A Keeper! Choose with your heart!
    - You choose one. It’s plain. Jackpot says nothing, very loudly.
    - **Byte**: That’s one egg. Let’s keep counting.
- **Ask Pip to wait before she spends it all.**
    - **Pip**: Wait? But the sparkly ones might run out!
    - **Jackpot**: THEY WILL NOT RUN OUT.
- **Ask Dashwood how he knows.**
    - **Dashwood Prance**: I know it in my heart, friend!
    - **Byte**: Numbers don’t live in hearts. They live in counting.

#### `sprout:s15-tally` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-tally)

**Pip**: I saved my Shells! I can buy four eggs!  
_By lunchtime, Byte’s card is full._  
**Byte**: Lots and lots of plain eggs. Only three sparkly ones.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.C.6, ask): more and fewer  
> **Byte**: Plain eggs or sparkly eggs: which are there more of?  
> - ✅ Plain eggs → Yes! Many more plain eggs than sparkly ones.
> - Sparkly eggs → More sparkly eggs? Ooh, a glitter storm! Look again. Look again. Only three sparkly. Lots and lots of plain. More plain!
> _For grown-ups: Compared two groups and saw there were more plain eggs than sparkly ones._

**Byte**: That’s not one in three. The sign is wrong.  
_Plop! Pip sits on the bridge with four plain eggs._  
_She is trying hard to be brave._  
**Pip**: The plain ones are nice too. They’re warm.  

#### `sprout:s15-sneak` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-sneak)

**Byte**: Jackpot always tells the truth. Let’s ask him.  
_After lunch, Dashwood has a nap. Snore… snore…_  
_Jackpot sits in the window. Tick, tick, tick._  
**Byte**: Don’t touch the wagon. All the bells will ring!  
_There are stepping stones by the stream._  

> **Lesson** (health, ELOF: Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development, do): moving slowly and quietly  
> _(Let’s tiptoe! Five tiny, quiet steps together.)_  
> _(Count them: one, two, three, four, five.)_  
> **Byte**: Quiet feet! Like a cat.  
> Check: `edu:tiptoe:steps:5`
> _For grown-ups: Controlled their body to take five slow, quiet tiptoe steps._

_One stone wobbles. One stone is a frog! Ribbit! Splash!_  

#### `sprout:s15-jackpot` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-jackpot)

**Jackpot**: HELLO. I CANNOT LIE. IT IS MY SPRINGS.  
**You**: How many eggs are sparkly, Jackpot?  
**Jackpot**: TEN EGGS. ONE SPARKLY. NINE PLAIN. HE WROTE IT DOWN.  
_Byte holds up ten fingers. He folds down nine._  
**Byte**: And the big sign?  
**Jackpot**: HE SAYS THE SIGN IS A POEM. A POEM IS NOT A NUMBER.  
**Jackpot**: ALSO THE SPECIAL EGG IS NOT SPECIAL. OOPS.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Responsible decision-making, ask): honesty: telling the truth is fair  
> **Byte**: The sign says something that isn’t true. Is that fair?  
> - ✅ No, it isn’t fair → Right. The truth is fair. Then people can choose.
> - Yes, it’s fine → A sign that fibs is fine? Hmm. Pip’s Shells are gone. Hmm. Pip spent her Shells because of that sign. Not fair to Pip.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about why telling the truth is fair to other people._

_The snoring stops. Creak! You freeze! Then it starts again._  

#### `sprout:s15-legendary` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-legendary)

_Dashwood wakes up. He shines the special egg._  
**Dashwood Prance**: Lovely, isn’t she? Hold her for one Shell a minute!  
**Byte**: Here are three Shells. Three minutes, please.  
_Under the bridge, it is dim and green._  
_Pop! Byte lights a little lantern._  
**Byte**: Light behind an egg shows what’s inside. Hazel taught us.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, do): light can shine through thin things  
> _(With a grown-up, shine a torch through your fingers.)_  
> _(Look! Your fingers glow pink. Light shines through thin things.)_  
> **Byte**: Eggshells are thin too. Let’s look!  
> Check: `edu:shine:light:fingers`
> _For grown-ups: Shone a torch through their fingers to see that light passes through thin things._


#### `sprout:s15-painted` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-painted)

_The light shines through the egg. Glitter sparkles everywhere._  
_But the egg inside is plain pink. Something little is moving!_  
**Byte**: It’s a plain egg with paint on! There’s a big thumbprint.  
**Byte**: It’s a healthy egg, though. It will hatch in a week.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.4, do): a week is seven days; show 7  
> _(A week is seven days. Hold up seven fingers together.)_  
> **Byte**: Seven days. Then, crack! A baby Nubbin.  
> Check: `edu:show:fingers:7`
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a week is seven days and showed seven on their fingers._

_Glitter gets on your fingers. It stays for days!_  

#### `sprout:s15-verdict` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-verdict)

_Dashwood sees the glitter on your hands._  
_He sits down on the wagon step. Creak!_  
**Dashwood Prance**: When I was little, I bought a golden egg.  
**Dashwood Prance**: I carried it home in my hat. I loved it.  
**Dashwood Prance**: It hatched brown. It had been painted.  
**Dashwood Prance**: But I loved that brown baby anyway. So much.  
**Jackpot**: HE MEANS IT. I CANNOT LIE.  
_The children wait. Pip holds her four plain eggs._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Civ.8.K-2, ask): fairness: putting things right, kindly  
> **Byte**: The sign wasn’t true. How can we make it fair?  
> - ✅ Tell the truth, kindly → Yes. Kind and true. That fixes things best.
> - Shout at Dashwood → Shout at Dashwood? Shouty-shout! All the wagon bells would jangle. Shouting makes people sad and cross. Kind and true works better.
> - Do nothing → Do nothing? Then four plain eggs sit sadly in Pip’s lap. Then Pip loses her Shells. Let’s be kind and true.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how to make an unfair thing fair by being truthful and kind._


**Choices**

- **Show everyone the count and the painted egg.**
    - You hold up the painted egg. Byte reads the count.
    - **The children**: One in TEN? That’s not fair!
    - **Dashwood Prance**: It was only a poem. I’m sorry.
    - Dashwood drives away before dark.
    - He leaves a table, a box of eggs, and Jackpot.
    - **Jackpot**: I DID NOT WANT TO GO. I CANNOT LIE.
- **Ask him to give the Shells back and fix the sign.**
    - **Dashwood Prance**: Give them back? Oh… all right.
    - He counts the Shells back into small hands. One by one.
    - He changes the sign. Now it says ONE IN TEN.
    - **Pip**: I’m keeping one egg. I don’t mind if it’s plain!
    - Jackpot stays with you. Everybody agrees.
- **Buy his wagon. Run an honest stall yourself.**
    - **Dashwood Prance**: Buy Marigold? My wagon? Hmm. How much?
    - Byte does the sums. Byte loves doing sums.
    - **Dashwood Prance**: Polish her once a year. And keep her away from goats!
    - He walks off down the lane, whistling.

#### `sprout:s15-odds-board` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-odds-board)

_Thump! Byte puts up a board. He writes in big chalk letters._  
_PLAIN: 9 IN 10. SPARKLY: 1 IN 10._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.OA.A.1, do): 9 and 1 make 10  
> _(Hold up ten fingers. Fold one down for the sparkly egg.)_  
> **Byte**: How many are still up? Nine! Nine plain, one sparkly. Ten eggs.  
> Check: `edu:show:fingers:9-and-1`
> _For grown-ups: Used ten fingers to see that 9 and 1 make 10._

_Underneath, smaller: ALL OF THEM ARE WARM._  
**Pip**: Why do I like this board better?  
**Byte**: Because it’s true. True feels good.  
_Some children buy eggs. Some just come to read the board._  

#### `sprout:s15-complete` · [main](../../../side/S15.md#s15-complete)

_The last egg is wrapped in a red spotty hanky._  
_You unwrap it. It glows all over, like a pearl!_  
**Jackpot**: THAT ONE IS REAL. I CHECKED. TWICE.  
_A card says: For the Keeper. This one really is a winner._  
_Jackpot hops onto your shoulder. Boing! Tick, tick._  
**Jackpot**: I WOULD LIKE TO LIVE WITH YOU. I CANNOT LIE.  
_Dashwood gave Pip the painted egg as he left._  
_Pip takes the painted egg home in her hat._  
_A week later, it hatches. It is brown as a boot._  

> **Lesson** (art, do): naming and finding a colour: brown  
> _(Can you find something brown near you? Point to it!)_  
> _Brown like a boot. Brown like Pip’s new Nubbin._  
> Check: `edu:find:colour:brown`
> _For grown-ups: Named the colour brown and found something brown nearby._

_Pip loves it more than anything._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): loving friends just as they are  
> _Pip’s Nubbin isn’t sparkly. Pip doesn’t mind one bit._  
> _We love our friends just as they are._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that friends are loved for who they are, not for being fancy._


**Notes:** All choices kept; “expose” is softened (the children are cross, nobody kicks anything). The Circuit sticker and the bulk buyer up the valley are left out for this band.
