# Learning Grove · Little Sprouts (ages 3–6, read aloud): Side story S18: The Grove Museum

## S18. The Grove Museum

- `s18-step1`: Dig up three fossils with Dusty. Put them in order, oldest first. · _The deepest layer is the oldest, like the bottom blanket on a bed._
- `s18-step2`: Wait quietly by Luna’s moth sheet for three night finds. · _Keep very still. The stiller you are, the faster the finds come._
- `s18-step3`: Find three creatures in Marina’s rock pools. · _Look under the weed and the stones. Always put each stone back the same way up._
- `s18-step4`: Find the Pole Star with Nova. Then wait for the Moon. · _Find the seven stars of the Plough. The two end stars point to the Pole Star._
- `s18-step5`: Fill the middle case. Then talk to Professor Delve at the door. · _Put something special in the big case in the middle of the barn._

#### `sprout:s18-barn` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-barn)

_Hazel’s old barn smells of hay. Swallows’ mud nests line the roof._  
_A mole in a tweed waistcoat stands by a barrow of glass boxes._  
**Professor Delve**: Hello! I’m Professor Delve. I want to make a museum!  
**Professor Delve**: Four rooms. Twelve treasures. And every sign must be true!  
_A little brown moth lands on his nose._  
**Professor Delve**: Oh! A mo… a mo… a MOTH!  
_Professor Delve faints, very neatly, into the barrow._  
**Professor Delve**: I’m all right. I’m just lying down.  
_Dusty is at the door, holding a trowel._  
**Dusty**: I heard “fossils”. My hands want a job. Can I help?  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, ask): first sounds: d  
> _Delve and Dusty start with the same sound. Which one?_  
> - ✅ “d”, like dig → D-d-Dusty. D-d-Delve. And d for dig!
> - “m”, like mole → Mole starts with m. Delve and Dusty start with d-d-d!
> - “s”, like sun → Listen: d-d-Dusty, d-d-Delve. They start with d!
> _For grown-ups: Heard the same first sound, “d”, in two names._


#### `sprout:s18-fossils` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-fossils)

_The cliff has stripes, like a cake. Lots of layers._  
**Dusty**: Deeper is older. Like blankets on a bed.  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): first and last: the deepest rock layer is the oldest  
> **Dusty**: When you make a bed, which blanket goes on first?  
> - ✅ The bottom one → Yes! Bottom first. So the bottom rock is the oldest.
> - The top one → The top one goes on last! Bottom first. The bottom rock is oldest.
> _For grown-ups: Used first and last to see why the deepest layer of rock is the oldest._

_Fossils are signs of plants and animals from long ago, kept in stone._  
_You find a curly shell, a flat fern and a little stone bug._  
**Dusty**: My Nubbin was called Fossil. Like these! She always slept while I dug.  
**Dusty**: Fossil died, not long ago. She was my best friend.  
**Dusty**: It’s very quiet without her snoring.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): missing someone who died  
> _When someone dies, their body stops, for always._  
> **Dusty**: I miss her. Missing someone means you loved them.  
> _Dusty feels sad. It’s all right to feel sad._  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that missing someone who died is a sign of love, and that sadness is all right._

_Dusty does Fossil’s snore. Zzz-pff. Then Dusty smiles, a little._  
**Professor Delve**: A trilobite! A sea animal from long, long ago.  
**Professor Delve**: A fern! Ferns have no flowers.  
**Professor Delve**: An ammonite! A curly-shelled sea animal. A cousin of the octopus.  
**Professor Delve**: True, true and true! Hooray!  

#### `sprout:s18-night` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-night)

_Luna hangs a white sheet between two trees._  
_A lantern shines on it. It glows like a sail._  
**Luna**: A moth trap. Nobody gets hurt. We look, we draw, they go home.  
**Professor Delve**: I shall watch from far away. Very far away.  
_He sits at the end of the garden, under a blanket._  
**Luna**: Now, the hard part. We wait. And we don’t talk.  
**Luna**: …That was me talking. Starting now.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): waiting quietly  
> _(Be as quiet as a moth. Your grown-up counts to ten.)_  
> _(One… two… three… all the way to ten. Shhh.)_  
> **Luna**: Lovely waiting! You stayed so still.  
> Check: `edu:wait:quietly:count-10`
> _For grown-ups: Practised waiting quietly and still while a grown-up counted to ten._


#### `sprout:s18-night-finds` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-night-finds)

_A moth lands on the sheet. It’s green and black._  
_It’s hard to see! It looks just like old tree bark._  

> **Lesson** (science, ELOF: Scientific Reasoning, ask): camouflage: colours that help animals hide  
> **Luna**: Why is the moth so hard to see on the bark?  
> - ✅ Its colours match the bark → Yes! Its colours match. It hides in plain sight.
> - It’s invisible → Not invisible! Its colours just match the bark. That’s how it hides.
> - It’s asleep → Maybe! But mostly its colours match the bark. That’s how it hides.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that some animals hide because their colours match where they rest._

**Luna**: Its name means “Marvel of the Day”. But it flies at night!  
_Far away: thump. Professor Delve has fainted again._  
_By morning: a moth drawing, a spider web and an owl pellet._  
**Professor Delve**: Owls swallow little meals whole. They cough up the fur and bones.  
**Professor Delve**: Spiders make silk inside their bodies. They spin it into webs.  

#### `sprout:s18-shore` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-shore)

_Low tide. Marina’s rock pools shine like little mirrors._  
**Marina**: Rule one. Turn a stone, then turn it back the same way.  
**Marina**: That stone is somebody’s roof.  

> **Lesson** (science, K-ESS3-3, ask): looking after animals’ homes  
> **Marina**: Why do we put the stone back the same way?  
> - ✅ It’s a little animal’s home → Yes. Little crabs live under there. We keep their homes safe.
> - So it looks nice → It does look nice! But mostly, it’s somebody’s home.
> - It doesn’t matter → It does! Little animals live under it. It’s their home.
> _For grown-ups: Learned to put stones back in rock pools to look after the animals that live there._

**Marina**: And the tide always comes back. Even if you’re not ready!  
_A little crab walks past Professor Delve’s boot._  
**Professor Delve**: Not a moth! What a good day.  

#### `sprout:s18-shore-finds` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-shore-finds)

_You find an orange sea star. One arm is short. It’s growing back!_  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 5  
> _(Count the sea star’s arms together.)_  
> _One, two, three, four, five. Five arms. One is little!_  
> Check: `edu:count:arms:5`
> _For grown-ups: Counted the five arms of a sea star._

_You find a limpet, stuck tight to its rock._  
_And a hermit crab, moving into a bigger shell!_  
**Professor Delve**: A sea star can grow back an arm it lost.  
**Professor Delve**: A hermit crab moves to a bigger shell when it grows.  
**Marina**: Look, it left its old shell. A smaller crab might want it.  
**Professor Delve**: Or a museum might want it. I’m only saying.  

**Choices**

- **Take the empty shell for the museum.**
    - **Marina**: Fair. There are lots of shells.
    - **Professor Delve**: It shall have a little cushion!
- **Leave the shell for the next crab.**
    - **Marina**: Good. Somebody’s old house is somebody’s new one.
    - **Professor Delve**: Then we’ll have a drawing instead.

#### `sprout:s18-stars` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-stars)

_Night on the hill. Nova lies in the cold grass._  
**Nova**: Clear night. Best sky in the valley.  
**Nova**: I brought you something. It’s a rock. It’s not a big thing.  
_A black stone, heavy and bumpy. Like burnt toast._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): saying thank you for a gift  
> _Nova gives you a present. What can you say?_  
> - ✅ “Thank you!” → …You’re welcome. Shh. Look at the stars. Nova smiles at the sky. Just a little.
> - “Is that all?” → That might hurt Nova’s feelings. “Thank you” is kinder.
> - Say nothing → A “thank you” lets Nova know you love it.
> _For grown-ups: Practised saying thank you for a present._

_A Nubbin sniffs the rock that looks like toast. It is not toast!_  
**Nova**: It fell from the sky. I walked two days to find it.  
**Professor Delve**: A rock from space! And it isn’t a moth!  
**Nova**: Now. Find me north.  

#### `sprout:s18-star-finds` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-star-finds)

_Look for seven stars in the shape of a pan. That’s the Plough._  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.CC.B.5, do): count to 7  
> _(Count the stars of the Plough together.)_  
> _One, two, three, four, five, six, seven!_  
> Check: `edu:count:stars:7`
> _For grown-ups: Counted the seven stars of the Plough._

_The two end stars point to the Pole Star._  
_The Pole Star stays still. The other stars turn slowly round it._  
**Nova**: That star helped me find my way home. Lots of times.  
_Late at night, the Moon comes up. Orange, then white._  
**Professor Delve**: The Moon always shows us the same face.  
**Professor Delve**: A shooting star isn’t a star. It’s space rock or dust, burning up.  
_Whoosh! A shooting star. Professor Delve ducks under his hat._  
**Professor Delve**: It isn’t a moth. I just like to be safe.  

#### `sprout:s18-heart` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-heart)

_Twelve cases are full. The big middle case is empty._  
**Professor Delve**: Every museum has a middle case. It holds the most special thing.  
_Dusty comes in, holding a bent card._  
**Dusty**: Fossil’s card. From the hill, by her flower.  
**Dusty**: I don’t want it in a drawer. I want people to see it.  
**Professor Delve**: A museum helps everybody remember.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): remembering someone we love  
> _How can we remember someone we love?_  
> - ✅ Tell stories about them → Yes. Telling stories keeps her close.
> - Hide all their things away → Then I might forget. I want to see her card and remember.
> _For grown-ups: Talked about remembering someone who died by sharing stories about them._

**Professor Delve**: Or there’s Hazel’s egg book. Every egg Hazel ever hatched is in it.  
**Dusty**: That’s a good thing. It just isn’t Fossil.  

**Choices**

- **Put Fossil’s card in the middle case.**
    - **Dusty**: Thank you. She’d hate the glass. She’d sit on top of it!
    - **Professor Delve**: And Hazel’s book stays at the cottage. It’s still being written.
- **Put Hazel’s egg book in the middle case.**
    - **Dusty**: Can Fossil’s card go inside it? Like a bookmark?
    - **Professor Delve**: A bookmark is allowed. A bookmark is very welcome!
    - **Professor Delve**: The book is only borrowed. When you want it back, I’ll carry it home.
- **Leave the case empty, for whoever comes next.**
    - You put a card inside: For whoever we remember next.
    - **Professor Delve**: An empty case! People will stand and think.
    - **Dusty**: Good. Fossil’s card can go beside it.

#### `sprout:s18-door` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-door)

_The barn is swept. Twelve signs shine._  
**Professor Delve**: One last thing. The door.  
**Professor Delve**: The roof has a hole. Drip, drip! Two Shells a visit would mend it.  

> **Lesson** (maths, K.OA.A.2, ask): adding 2 and 2  
> **Professor Delve**: Two Shells for you. Two Shells for a friend. How many?  
> - ✅ 4 → Yes! Two and two make four.
> - 3 → Let’s count on fingers: one, two… three, four. Four!
> - 2 → That’s just one visit. Two and two more make four!
> _For grown-ups: Added two and two to make four._

**Professor Delve**: Or it could be free. Then I’ll mend the roof with my hat.  
_He looks at his hat. His hat looks worried._  

**Choices**

- **Two Shells a visit, to keep it mended.**
    - **Professor Delve**: Sensible. Places need mending.
    - **Professor Delve**: Anyone shorter than the door handle goes free!
- **Keep it free for everyone.**
    - **Professor Delve**: Free. Yes. It belongs to the whole valley.
    - **Professor Delve**: I’ll put out a jar. Just a hopeful one.

#### `sprout:s18-complete` · [main](../../../side/S18.md#s18-complete)

_Opening day! Half the valley comes._  
_Children press their noses on the trilobite case._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.His.2.K-2, notice): museums keep things from long ago  
> **Professor Delve**: A museum keeps old things safe.  
> **Professor Delve**: Then children can learn about long ago.  
> _For grown-ups: Heard that museums keep old things safe so we can learn about the past._

_Sparks reads every sign out loud. Very loud._  
**Professor Delve**: You did this, Curator. That means you look after the museum. Well done!  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RF.K.2, do): clapping syllables  
> _(Clap it together: Cu-ra-tor. How many claps?)_  
> _Three claps! Cu-ra-tor._  
> Check: `edu:clap:syllables:curator`
> _For grown-ups: Clapped the three syllables in “Curator”._

_He gives you his old magnifying glass, on a ribbon._  
**Professor Delve**: It makes little things look big. I’d like it to keep looking.  
_Then a moth flies in. The Curator catches the professor!_  

**Notes:** All choices kept. Fossil’s death is said plainly (s18-fossils). Delve’s plaque facts are kept true and simplified for reading aloud.
