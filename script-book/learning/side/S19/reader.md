# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S19: Cinder’s Last Light

## S19. Cinder’s Last Light

- `s19-step1`: Ask Marina about the path up to the high pass. · _She’s at the tide pools, breaking the ice. Go and find her there._
- `s19-step2`: Climb the snowfield to the high pass. · _Step in Marina’s footprints. When the wind gusts, crouch low and wait for it to pass._
- `s19-step3`: Sit beside the red light, and wait. · _Don’t move and don’t speak. It can take a long time. That’s the point._
- `s19-step4`: Stay with her until her light goes. · _Stay sitting. Don’t leave the hollow. It won’t be long._

#### `reader:s19-warmth` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-warmth)

_Two days after the Long Night. The valley is white to the doorsteps, and very quiet._  
_Moss sits on the cottage step, facing north, where the snowfield climbs to the high pass. He has sat like that since dawn._  
_He is thinner than he was. On a bright morning you can see the step through him._  
**Moss**: There is something warm up there.  
**Moss**: There shouldn’t be. Nothing has been warm on the high pass since the White Year. Nothing.  
_Up there, the snow is the blue of skimmed milk, and quite empty._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): the higher up a mountain, the colder the air  
> _High up, the air is colder. The higher you climb a mountain, the colder it usually gets._  
> _That’s why snow stays on a high pass long after the valley has thawed. The tops of very high mountains keep snow all year._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that air usually gets colder the higher up a mountain you go, so snow lasts longer up high._

**Moss**: I felt it on the night we unmade the stone. Like a candle someone forgot, in a room you thought was dark.  
**Moss**: I am too thin to climb. I am going anyway. You may carry me, if you would. In your coat. I won’t talk much.  
**Moss**: That last part was a lie. Marina knows the path. Ask her.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): use a character’s words to understand his personality  
> _Moss says, “That last part was a lie.” Which part does he mean?_  
> - ✅ “I won’t talk much.” → Yes! Moss is joking about himself. He knows he’ll chat the whole way up. Being able to laugh at yourself is a lovely trait.
> - “I am going anyway.” → He means that bit: he’s very determined. The lie is “I won’t talk much”. He’s joking about how chatty he is.
> - “Marina knows the path.” → That one’s true. He tells you to go and ask her. The lie is “I won’t talk much”, a joke about himself.
> _For grown-ups: Used a character’s joke about himself to understand his personality._


#### `reader:s19-warmth-sable` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-warmth-sable)

_Variant of `s19-warmth`._

_Two days after the Long Night. The valley is white to the doorsteps, and very quiet._  
_Sable hasn’t eaten her breakfast. She sits at the top of the garden, facing north, where the snowfield climbs to the high pass._  
_She has sat like that since dawn. When you come near, she doesn’t move away. She doesn’t look round, either._  
_Beside her floats the pale light that was Moss’s. It stayed when Moss faded at the Long Night, when he died._  
_The pale light leans north too, like a candle flame in a draught._  
_Up there, the snow is the blue of skimmed milk, and quite empty. Then, for a moment, it isn’t: a speck of red, like a forgotten coal._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): the higher up a mountain, the colder the air  
> _High up, the air is colder. The higher you climb a mountain, the colder it usually gets._  
> _That’s why snow stays on a high pass long after the valley has thawed. The tops of very high mountains keep snow all year._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that air usually gets colder the higher up a mountain you go, so snow lasts longer up high._

_Sable stands. She looks at you, then at the pass, then at you, the way she looks at a gate she wants opened._  
**You**: All right. All right. We’ll ask Marina.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): read what a character wants from her actions  
> _Sable never says a word. How do you know she wants to go up to the pass?_  
> - ✅ She looks from you to the pass and back, like at a gate she wants opened. → Yes. Her eyes do the talking. Characters can show what they want through what they do, not only what they say.
> - She tells you. → Sable doesn’t speak. But she looks from you to the pass and back, the way she asks for a gate. Her actions tell you.
> - You can’t know. → You can, from clues! She skips breakfast, faces north, and looks from you to the pass and back. Her actions say it.
> _For grown-ups: Inferred what a non-speaking character wanted from her actions._

_You are at the cottage window with a cup of tea when you see it: high on the pass, a speck of red, like a forgotten coal._  
_You look away, and look back. It is still there, steady, in all that white._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): the higher up a mountain, the colder the air  
> _High up, the air is colder. The higher you climb a mountain, the colder it usually gets._  
> _That’s why snow stays on a high pass long after the valley has thawed. The tops of very high mountains keep snow all year._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that air usually gets colder the higher up a mountain you go, so snow lasts longer up high._

_Nobody else is looking. Moss would have been. But Moss faded at the Long Night, which means he died._  
_You still look round for him. Before you have quite decided anything, you are reaching for your boots._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): notice how missing someone shows up in small moments  
> _When someone we love has died, we often miss them most in small moments: a seat they always sat in, a thing they would have spotted first._  
> _Looking round for them is part of grief. Doing what they would have done is one way of carrying them with you._  
> _For grown-ups: Talked about how grief shows up in small, everyday moments, and how doing what a loved one would have done can help._

**You**: Marina. Marina knows the path.  

#### `reader:s19-marina` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-marina)

_Marina is knee-deep in a tide pool, with the ice broken round her and her sleeves rolled up, perfectly happy._  
**Marina**: The high pass? In midwinter? Lovely. Bracing. You’ll hate it.  
**Marina**: All the water in this valley comes down off that snowfield. Somebody has to know where it starts. That’s me.  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-ESS2-3, ask): snow melts into water that flows to the sea (the water cycle)  
> **Marina**: Here’s a riddle for you. How does snow on top of a mountain end up in my tide pools?  
> - ✅ It melts, and the water runs down in streams and rivers to the sea. → Exactly. Snow is frozen water. When it warms up, it melts, and the water runs downhill all the way to the sea. Then the sun warms the sea, water rises into the sky as vapour, makes clouds, and falls again as rain or snow. Round and round.
> - Somebody carries it down in buckets. → Ha! That would be a lot of buckets. No: snow is frozen water. It melts, and the water runs downhill to the sea. Then it rises as vapour, makes clouds, and falls as snow again. That’s the water cycle.
> - It doesn’t. It stays up there forever. → Some of it stays a long time. But when snow warms up, it melts, and the water runs downhill to the sea. Then it rises, makes clouds, and falls again. That’s the water cycle.
> _For grown-ups: Explained how melting snow flows to the sea, and how water cycles back up into clouds._

**Marina**: Nobody else goes up there. There’s nothing up there but wind and the view.  
**Marina**: Boots, rope, two pairs of socks. First light tomorrow. Don’t be brave. Be careful.  
**Marina**: Two pairs. I will check.  

> **Lesson** (health, notice): how layers keep you warm, and going out in snow safely  
> **Marina**: Layers keep you warm because they trap air between them, and trapped air holds your body’s heat in.  
> **Marina**: And a mountain in winter is only for going up with a grown-up who knows the way. Today, that’s me.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned why layers keep you warm, and that snowy mountains are only for going up with a grown-up who knows the way._


#### `reader:s19-climb` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-climb)

_First light. Marina ropes you to her. Then, after some thought, she ropes Moss to a button of your coat._  
_He rides inside your coat, against your chest, with just his face out. He is facing you, not the mountain._  
_He weighs about as much as a held breath._  
**Moss**: Left.  
**Marina**: Right.  
**Moss**: Her right. My left. We are agreeing, in different directions.  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): left and right depend on which way you face  
> _Marina faces up the mountain. Moss faces you, so he faces the opposite way to her._  
> _When Marina says “right”, which way is that for Moss?_  
> - ✅ His left. → Quite so. When two people face opposite ways, one person’s right is the other’s left. That’s why maps use north, south, east and west. They stay the same whichever way you face.
> - His right too. → Try it with a friend: face each other and both point right. You point opposite ways! So Marina’s right is Moss’s left. That’s why maps use north, south, east and west instead.
> - Straight ahead. → Right is always to the side. But Moss faces the opposite way to Marina, so her right is his left.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out that left and right depend on which way you face, and why maps use compass directions._

_The snowfield is crusted on top and soft beneath. Every step goes through with a crunch like biting toast._  
**Marina**: Lovely, isn’t it? That wind. Takes your face right off.  
**Moss**: Your heart is very loud. I am not complaining. It’s warm.  

> **Lesson** (health, notice): why your heart beats faster when you exercise  
> _Climbing is hard work. Your muscles need more oxygen, so your heart beats faster to pump more blood to them._  
> _Your breathing speeds up too. When you rest, both slow down again._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned why the heart and breathing speed up during exercise and slow down with rest._


#### `reader:s19-climb-sable` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-climb-sable)

_Variant of `s19-climb`._

_First light. Marina ropes you to her. Sable will not be roped._  

> **Lesson** (health, notice): staying safe together on a snowy mountain  
> **Marina**: On a snowy mountain, people rope together. If one slips, the others can hold them.  
> **Marina**: Only ever go up with a grown-up who knows the mountain, and stay close.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned why climbers rope together and stay close to a grown-up who knows the way._

_She goes ahead, a grey shape on the white, as if she knows the way._  
_Moss’s pale light drifts along at her shoulder, where he used to walk._  
_The snowfield is crusted on top and soft beneath. Each of your steps goes through with a crunch like biting toast._  
**Marina**: Lovely, isn’t it? That wind. Takes your face right off.  
_Sable is light enough not to break the crust. She leaves a line of small prints, neat as stitches._  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): spreading weight helps you stay on top of snow  
> _Your boots break through the crust. Sable’s paws don’t. Why not?_  
> - ✅ She’s lighter, and her weight is spread over four paws. → Yes. Less weight pressing on each bit of snow means the crust holds. That’s why snowshoes are wide: they spread a person’s weight out, so they sink less.
> - She’s floating. → Not quite! She’s much lighter than you, and her weight is spread over four paws, so the crust holds her.
> - Her paws are warmer. → Warm paws would melt snow, not help her stay up! It’s about weight: she’s light, and spread over four paws.
> _For grown-ups: Explained that lighter, spread-out weight sinks less into snow (why snowshoes work)._

**Marina**: She’s not stopping. Is she always like this?  
**You**: Only when it matters.  

#### `reader:s19-climb-alone` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-climb-alone)

_Variant of `s19-climb`._

_First light. Marina ropes you to her, checks the knot twice, and then checks your socks._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.1, ask): multiplication as equal groups  
> **Marina**: Two pairs of socks, I said. A pair is two socks. So how many socks should I find on you?  
> - ✅ 4 → Four. Two pairs, two in each pair: 2 × 2 = 4. Two on each foot. You pass.
> - 2 → That’s one pair. I said two pairs. Two groups of two: 2 × 2 = 4 socks.
> - 3 → Three socks would be a very odd foot. Two pairs is two groups of two: 2 × 2 = 4.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out 2 × 2 = 4 by thinking of pairs as equal groups._

_The snowfield is crusted on top and soft beneath. Every step goes through with a crunch like biting toast._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-4, notice): melting and freezing again can make a hard crust on snow  
> **Marina**: That crust is sun and frost taking turns. By day the sun melts the top of the snow a little.  
> **Marina**: At night it freezes again, harder. Melting and freezing, over and over, and you get a lid on the snow.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that snow can melt in the sun and refreeze at night, making a hard crust._

**Marina**: Lovely, isn’t it? That wind. Takes your face right off.

#### `reader:s19-hollow` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-hollow)

_Near the top, the rock leans out over the snow like a hand held over a cup. Under it is a hollow, out of the wind._  
_There is no snow in the hollow. There hasn’t been for a very long time, you think. The bare rock is dry, and faintly warm._  

> **Lesson** (science, 2-PS1-4, ask): snow melts when it warms above freezing  
> _Snow melts when it warms above 0 °C (32 °F). All around the hollow is deep snow. Inside, there is none._  
> _Why do you think there’s no snow in the hollow?_  
> - ✅ The rock is warm, so snow melts, and the overhang keeps most snow off. → Good thinking. Warm rock melts any snow that lands, and the rock roof shelters the hollow from falling snow. Melting can be undone, too: if it got cold enough, the water would freeze again.
> - Snow doesn’t like rock. → Snow lands on rock all over the mountain. This rock is warm, so the snow melts, and the overhang keeps most snow off.
> - Someone swept it away. → Nobody comes up here. The rock is warm, so snow melts when it lands, and the overhang shelters the hollow.
> _For grown-ups: Explained why warm rock and shelter keep a place free of snow (melting above 0 °C)._

_In the middle of it, on a flat stone, is a light. Red. Smaller than a fingernail. Almost nothing._  
**Marina**: I’ll be on the lip. Shout if you want the rope.  
_Marina goes back up to the lip without being asked. She turns her back, and looks at the view very hard._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, ask): notice when someone is giving others privacy  
> _Marina turns her back without being asked. Why do you think she does that?_  
> - ✅ To give you privacy for something important. → Yes. Marina can tell this moment matters. Stepping back so others can have it is a quiet kind of kindness.
> - She’s bored of the hollow. → She says nothing about being bored. She went “without being asked”, and looks hard at the view. She’s giving you privacy.
> - She’s cross with you. → Nothing she says sounds cross. She offers you the rope, then steps away. She’s giving you privacy, which is kind.
> _For grown-ups: Inferred that a character was giving others privacy, and why that is kind._

_Moss has gone completely still in your coat._  
**Moss**: …Cinder.  
_The red light doesn’t answer. It is too small to answer. It has kept itself barely alight for longer than the cottage has stood._  
**Moss**: She can’t hear us. Not yet. We speak only to those who sit still long enough. She taught me that, once.  
**Moss**: Let me out. I’ll sit by her. You sit by me. We’ll wait.

#### `reader:s19-sit` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-sit)

_You sit on the dry rock. The wind goes over the overhang with a sound like a long breath out._  
_Below you lies the whole valley: the falls frozen white, smoke from the cottage, and four lantern posts too small to see._  
_You wait. Nobody speaks. The red light stays very small._  
_Your feet go cold._  
_You wriggle your toes inside your two pairs of socks, the way Marina showed you, and they warm up a little._  

> **Lesson** (health, notice): notice cold fingers and toes and warm them up  
> _In real cold, fingers and toes get cold first. If they hurt or start to go numb, tell a grown-up and warm up straight away._  
> _Wriggling them, dry socks, gloves and a warm drink all help._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned to notice cold fingers and toes, warm them up, and tell a grown-up if they hurt or go numb._

_You keep waiting. Waiting is the whole job today._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): slow breathing to help you sit still and calm  
> _Sitting still is easier when you breathe slowly. Try it with the wind._  
> _(Breathe in slowly through your nose. Then breathe out, long and slow, like the wind over the rock. Do it five times.)_  
> Check: `edu:breathe:slow:5`
> _For grown-ups: Practised five slow breaths to stay calm and still while waiting._


#### `reader:s19-question` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-question)

_After a long time, the light grows a little. Not brighter. Bigger, the way a coal does when you breathe on it._  
**Cinder**: Are they safe?  
**Moss**: No. They’re well.  
_The light is quiet for a long time. You watch it understand. It takes the whole of her._  
**Cinder**: …Well. Well is better, isn’t it. I didn’t know there was a better.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, ask): find the big idea (theme) of a story  
> _Cinder asked if the Nubbins were “safe”. The answer was “well”. Those words aren’t the same._  
> _What is the difference?_  
> - ✅ Safe means nothing can hurt you. Well means you’re free to live, and grow, and be happy. → Yes. Cinder kept them safe by keeping them still. But still isn’t living. That is the big idea of the whole Grove story: safe is not the same as well. A big idea like that is called a theme.
> - They mean exactly the same. → They can sound alike. But a Nubbin frozen still is safe from harm, and not well at all. Well means free to live and grow. That difference is the story’s theme.
> - A well is a hole you get water from. → Ha! Different well. Here “well” means healthy and happy, free to live. Safe is not the same as well. That’s the story’s theme.
> _For grown-ups: Identified the story’s theme: being kept safe is not the same as being well._

**Cinder**: I loved them. It was so cold, and I couldn’t carry them all. So I made a way to carry them all.  
**Cinder**: I was so frightened. I held them so tight. And they were so quiet, after. I told myself it was resting.  
**Cinder**: Do they still say my name, down there?  
**You**: Cinder held them close and tight, kept them warm one winter night;  
**You**: when the morning came to call, they did not move at all.  
**Cinder**: That’s fair. Do they skip well?  
**Moss**: Badly. With great enthusiasm.  
**Moss**: Cinder. I forgave you the first spring. I’ve carried it about ever since. Here. It’s yours.  
**Cinder**: …Thank you, Moss. You’re the Keeper. You live down there, with the grey ones in the grass, and what I did.  
**Cinder**: Am I forgiven? By the ones who have to live with it?  
**Moss**: Don’t look at me. It isn’t mine to give. It never was.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, ask): think about who can forgive, and for whom  
> _Before you answer her, think about this._  
> _Can one person forgive someone on behalf of other people? Or can only the ones who were hurt forgive?_  
> - Only the ones who were hurt can really forgive. → That’s a thoughtful answer. Many people believe forgiveness belongs to those who were hurt. You can still remember someone truthfully: the good they meant, and the harm they did.
> - Someone who cares for them can forgive for them. → That’s a kind answer, and a fair one. A Keeper speaks for the Grove in lots of ways. Maybe this is one of them.
> - I’m not sure. It’s hard. → Not being sure is honest. Grown-ups argue about this too. Whatever you say next, say it kindly.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about whether we can forgive on behalf of others (a think question with no single right answer)._


**Choices**

- **“You’re forgiven.”**
    - **You**: You’re forgiven.
    - The light says nothing for a while. Then it turns the colour of a hearth when the door is shut and the kettle is on.
    - **Cinder**: Oh.
    - **Cinder**: I didn’t know it would be so light. I thought being forgiven would be heavy. I thought I’d have to carry it.
    - **Moss**: No. That’s the other thing about it. This one you just put down.
- **“It isn’t mine to forgive. But you’re remembered.”**
    - **You**: It isn’t mine to forgive. But you’re remembered.
    - The light holds very still.
    - **Cinder**: Remembered.
    - **Cinder**: That’s more than I asked for. It’s what I should have asked for.
    - **Cinder**: Not the rhyme. Me. Will you tell them the rest? That I loved them?
    - **You**: All of it.
    - **Moss**: We are made of being remembered. I used to say that to her. She used to roll her eyes.

#### `reader:s19-after` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-after)

_The light is getting smaller. It isn’t going out. It’s going, the way the last of the day goes off a hill._  
**Cinder**: I kept myself alight so long. Just a little. Just in case there was news.

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): explain a character’s motive  
> _Why did Cinder keep herself alight for so long?_  
> - ✅ She was waiting to hear whether the Nubbins were all right. → Yes. “Just in case there was news.” She needed to know they were well. Now she knows, she can stop.
> - She liked being cold. → She kept herself warm, not cold. She was waiting “just in case there was news” about the Nubbins.
> - She forgot to stop. → She kept going on purpose: “just in case there was news”. She wanted to know the Nubbins were all right.
> _For grown-ups: Explained why a character acted as she did, using her own words as evidence._

_You understand what she means. Cinder is dying. When her light goes, it won’t come back._  
**Cinder**: Will you stay? You don’t have to do anything. Just stay.

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): being with someone who is dying  
> _When someone is dying, you don’t have to fix anything. Being there with them is a real kindness._

> _For grown-ups: Learned that simply being with someone at the end of their life is a real kindness, and that all feelings are allowed._


#### `reader:s19-fade` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-fade)

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): explain how a simile changes the feeling of a moment  
> _The light going is compared to “someone walking away up a long road, who turns round once to wave”._

#### `reader:s19-complete` · [main](../../../side/S19.md#s19-complete)

_Where the light was, the flat stone is still warm. Not hot. Warm, like a hand that has only just let go of yours._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, notice): stone warms and cools slowly, and heat moves from warm to cool  
> _Stone warms up slowly and cools down slowly. That’s why a stone can stay warm for a while after the heat has gone._  
> _When you touch it, heat moves from the warm stone into your cooler hand. Heat always moves from warmer to cooler._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that stone holds heat and cools slowly, and that heat moves from warmer things to cooler ones._

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): read a character’s real feelings behind her words  
> _Marina says her red eyes are from the wind. What do you think is really going on?_  
> - ✅ She’s been crying, and doesn’t want a fuss. → That’s what I think too. She turned her back and looked hard at the view. Some people prefer to cry quietly.
> - It really is the wind. → Cold wind can make eyes water. But she turned her back at the saddest moment, and says “Lovely up here” sadly. She’s been crying, quietly.
> - She has a cold. → Nobody mentions a cold. Look at her clues: back turned, eyes red, a sad voice. She’s been crying, quietly.
> _For grown-ups: Used clues to see the feeling a character was hiding behind her words._

### Glossary

- **pass**: A low gap between mountains where a path can cross over.
- **bracing**: Cold and fresh in a way that wakes you right up.
- **crusted**: Covered with a hard top layer.
- **overhang**: A part of a rock or roof that sticks out over the space below it.
- **enthusiasm**: Being really keen and excited about something.
- **theme**: The big idea a story is about, underneath what happens.
- **grief**: The deep sadness you feel when someone you love dies.
- **draught**: A stream of cool air that moves through a room or space.
