# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S21: CL-4NK

## S21. CL-4NK

- `s21-step1`: Show Clank round the Grove. · _He follows you on one wheel. Go round things, not over them. He can’t do steps, kerbs or roots, though he believes he can._
- `s21-step2`: Undo Clank’s helping before he helps anything else. · _Dry the cat, prop up the tree, then try to catch Clank before he reaches the step. In that order, if you can. You won’t._
- `s21-step3`: Find Clank. · _Follow the wheel-track through the thyme. He’s hiding somewhere he thinks he fits in._
- `s21-step4`: Help Byte build a ramp for the cottage step. · _Byte measures; you saw. Measure twice. Byte will measure eleven times. Let him._
- `s21-step5`: Stay in the Grove with Clank until dusk. · _Feed the Nubbins first. Then sit by the cottage step and do nothing at all. It’s harder than it sounds._

#### `reader:s21-unveil` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-unveil)

_Something is coming up the lane, squeaking. It’s Pixel, pushing a wheelbarrow with a tea towel over it._  
**Pixel**: Morning. Don’t be alarmed. Be a bit alarmed. A normal amount.  
**Pixel**: After Sparks fixed the lantern posts, there was a whole box of parts left over. I couldn’t just leave them in a box.  
_Off comes the tea towel. Underneath: a lantern hood, a glass chimney, a brass wick-winder for a nose, and one wheel._  
**Pixel**: Garden helper. Watering, weeding, carrying. In theory.  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): what a robot is  
> **Pixel**: A robot is a machine that can sense things, follow instructions, and move or do jobs.  
> **Pixel**: The instructions are called a program. People write them. I wrote his. Mostly in capital letters, it turns out.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that a robot is a machine that senses, follows a program of instructions written by people, and acts._

_Pixel turns the wick-winder. Something inside warms up, then whistles, rising, like a kettle that has just heard good news._  
**Clank**: HELLO! Hello hello HELLO. I am CL-4NK! The four is silent! So is the dash!  

> **Lesson** (literacy, ask): numbers standing in for letters in a name  
> _Clank says the four in CL-4NK is silent. But look at its shape. Which letter is the 4 pretending to be?_  
> - ✅ A → Yes! CL-A-NK. A 4 looks a bit like a pointy A. People sometimes swap in numbers that look like letters. So the four isn’t silent at all. It’s an A in disguise. Don’t tell him.
> - B → B would make CL-B-NK, which sounds like a sneeze. A 4 looks more like a pointy A: CL-A-NK. Clank!
> - F → CL-F-NK? Tricky to say! The 4 is shaped a bit like a pointy A: CL-A-NK. Clank!
> _For grown-ups: Decoded a name where a number (4) stands in for a similar-looking letter (A)._

**Clank**: Call me Clank! You are my BEST FRIEND!  
**You**: We’ve only just—  
**Clank**: Best friend. Decided. Announcement: I HAVE A BEST FRIEND!  
_He rolls forward to shake your hand, rolls straight off the cottage step, and lands on his hood in the thyme._  
**Clank**: Announcement! I have found the step!  
**Clank**: The step is very good. The step is also my best friend.  
**Pixel**: That’s one. I gave it a week before he met the step. I was being optimistic.  

#### `reader:s21-tour` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-tour)

**Clank**: Announcement: this is a POND! Announcement: this is a GATE! It sticks! Best friend!  
**Clank**: Announcement: I am announcing!  
_You go round roots and kerbs, not over them. On the flat path his one wheel whizzes. At the smallest bump, he wobbles._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): what wheels are good at, and what they can’t do  
> _A wheel rolls instead of dragging, so on smooth, flat ground it needs only a small push._  
> _But a wheel can’t roll up something that sticks straight up, like a step. That’s why prams, wheelchairs and suitcases love a ramp._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that wheels roll easily on smooth ground but struggle with steps and bumps._

_By the foot of the hill, every Nubbin in the Grove is following him in a line, like ducklings behind a teapot._  
_One climbs onto his hood. Then three. He turns his chimney-light down low for them, and they purr._  
**Clank**: They like me. Announcement: they LIKE me.  
**Clank**: I didn’t know that was available.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): what a simile tells you  
> _“Like ducklings behind a teapot.” What does that simile help you picture?_  
> - ✅ The Nubbins follow him in a neat little line, and he’s round and warm like a teapot. → Yes! Two pictures in one: a wobbly line of followers, and a round, warm, whistling leader.
> - The Nubbins are actually ducks. → Ha! They’re still Nubbins. The simile says they follow him LIKE ducklings: in a neat little line behind a round, warm teapot.
> - Clank is making tea. → Not today. The simile compares him to a teapot because he’s round and warm, with a line of little followers behind.
> _For grown-ups: Explained what a simile helps the reader picture._


#### `reader:s21-help` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-help)

_You are woken at dawn by a whistle going up and up, and an announcement right outside your bedroom window._  
**Clank**: GOOD MORNING, BEST FRIEND! I have done SO MUCH HELPING!  
_Fern is in her garden in her dressing gown, holding her cat at arm’s length. Mustard is soaked, and furious about it._  
**Fern**: He watered Mustard. He watered my cat. In fairness, Mustard was in the beans. And he is a bit bean-coloured.  
**Clank**: Cats are plants that run! I looked it up! In my head!  

> **Lesson** (science, K-LS1-1, ask): how plants and animals get food  
> _Clank says cats are plants that run. Is he right?_  
> - ✅ No. A cat is an animal. It eats its food and moves about. → Right. Plants make their own food, using sunlight, air and water. Cats have to eat theirs. Both need water, which is the one thing Clank got right. Just not poured on the outside.
> - Yes. Cats are plants. → Mustard would like a word. Plants make their own food from sunlight, air and water. Cats eat theirs. So a cat is an animal.
> - A cat is a kind of bean. → He is bean-coloured. I’ll give you that. But beans are plants that make their own food from sunlight. Cats eat theirs. Mustard is an animal.
> _For grown-ups: Explained a difference between plants (make their own food) and animals (eat food), while noting both need water._

_Behind her, Fern’s one good apple tree has been pruned. Carefully. Thoroughly. Into the shape of a duck._  
**Fern**: That’s the wrong tree. The ones I’m keeping alive, badly, have never looked better. That one was my good one.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Social awareness, notice): helping by asking first  
> _Clank really wanted to help. But helping works best when you ask first: “Would you like me to…?”_  
> _Then you help with what the person actually needs. Fern needed the beans watered. Not the cat._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that real helping starts with asking what someone needs._

_Then you notice the western hill. The small old shape that is always on top of it isn’t there._  
**Clank**: Also! I rescued a little old one! He was stranded up the hill! For HOURS! He wasn’t even MOVING!  
_Speeding round the pond at what can only be called full kettle is Clank._  
_He is holding the little grey watcher from the hill out in front of him, like a pie._  

#### `reader:s21-moss` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-moss)

_You catch up with Clank at the cottage. So does the step._  
_Clank goes over the edge. The little grey one leaves his hands, turns once in the air, and lands on his feet, as if he’d planned the morning._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): balanced and unbalanced forces: why things fall off edges  
> **Pixel**: On flat ground, gravity pulls him down and the ground pushes back up. The pushes balance, so he stays put.  
> **Pixel**: Off the edge, nothing pushes up. The forces are unbalanced. Gravity wins. Down he goes. Every time.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that balanced forces keep an object still, and unbalanced forces (like gravity with nothing pushing back) make it move._

**Clank**: Announcement… I have found the step again.  
_The little grey one looks at you for a long time. It’s the look of something very old that has been carried round a pond like a pie._  
_Then he turns and walks back up the hill, very dignified. It takes him the rest of the morning. He doesn’t look back once._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): reading a silent character’s body language  
> _The little grey one doesn’t say a word. What do his long look and his slow walk away tell you?_  
> - ✅ He wasn’t pleased, but he’s far too dignified to make a fuss. → Good reading. A long look and a walk with no looking back can say more than shouting. He has been carried like a pie, and he has feelings about it.
> - He loved the ride and wants another go. → If he loved it, he might stay! He walks all the way back without looking round. That says: not pleased, but too dignified to fuss.
> - He’s lost. → He knows exactly where he’s going: back to the top of his hill. The slow, proud walk says he wasn’t pleased.
> _For grown-ups: Inferred a silent character’s feelings from his actions and body language._

**Pixel**: That’s two.  

#### `reader:s21-log` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-log)

_That afternoon Byte is at the gate with a clipboard, a pencil behind each ear, and the face of someone who has made a table._  
**Byte**: Incident log. Since breakfast: one wet cat. One duck-shaped tree. One old Nubbin, moved at about eleven miles an hour.  
**Byte**: Two falls down the step. Fourteen announcements of best friendship. One of them to a bucket.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 2.MD.D.10, do): making and reading a tally chart  
> **Byte**: Help me turn my log into a tally chart. One row each: wet cats, duck trees, carried Nubbins, falls, best friends.  
> **Byte**: Tally marks go in fives: four lines, then one across. Which row is longest?  
> Check: `edu:make:tally-chart:incident-log`
> _For grown-ups: Made a tally chart from a list of events (1, 1, 1, 2, 14) and found the largest category._

**Byte**: He’s a kettle on a wheel who wants to help. That is exactly how accidents introduce themselves.  
**Byte**: Next time it’s a Nubbin he waters. Or drops. Pixel should switch Clank off. Tonight. Before he hurts someone.  
**Pixel**: Twenty years married, and you still want everything switched off at night.  
_Behind you, a small whistle starts to rise, and stops halfway._  
_Clank is at the top of the cottage step. He has heard all of it. His chimney-light has gone very dim._  
_He reverses. He goes off the step backwards, lands in the thyme, and doesn’t announce anything at all._  
**Pixel**: …That’s three.  
_By the time you reach the step he’s gone. There’s just a wheel-track through the herbs, heading for the lantern shed._  
**Byte**: I didn’t know he could hear. I didn’t know he could… mind.  

> **Lesson** (science, ask): what makes something alive; can a robot be a friend (think question)  
> _Byte didn’t know Clank could mind. What do you think? Is Clank alive? Can a robot be a real friend?_  
> - He’s a machine, so he isn’t alive. But he can still be a friend. → That’s a fair answer. Scientists say living things grow, need food and water, and make young. Clank does none of those. But being a friend is about how you treat each other. He’s very good at that part.
> - He might be alive. He has feelings. → That’s a fair answer. Clank seems to mind things, and that matters. A scientist would say he doesn’t grow, eat or make young. Nobody is quite sure what his feelings are made of. Not even Pixel.
> - He’s a tool, like a watering can. → That’s a fair answer. He was built to do jobs. But a watering can doesn’t hide when its feelings are hurt. Maybe Clank is something new: a tool who wants to be a friend.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about what makes something alive (growing, needing food and water, making young) and whether a robot can be a friend._


**Choices**

- **Tell Byte he had a point. Clank does need to learn.**
    - **Byte**: I did have a point. I hate that I had a point. It’s much worse having one when it hurts.
    - **Pixel**: Something between “on” and “kettle”. That’s all he needs. I can build that.
- **Tell Byte that wasn’t fair. He’s only days old.**
    - **Byte**: Days old. I didn’t count that in.
    - **Byte**: I was days old once. I’m told I bit a vicar.

#### `reader:s21-shed` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-shed)

_The lantern shed smells of paraffin and old string. Clank is on the back shelf among the spare chimneys, pretending to be a lantern._  
_He is very bad at it. Lanterns don’t shake._  
**Clank**: Announcement. I’m a lantern now. Lanterns are useful. Nobody switches off a lantern.  
**Clank**: Except in the morning. They do switch them off in the morning.  
**Clank**: Byte’s right. I watered a cat. I made a tree into a duck. I carried the old one too fast and he didn’t say one single thing.  
**Clank**: Am I a friend? Or am I a thing that does jobs?  
**Clank**: Because if it’s jobs, I can do jobs better. I can learn. But if I stop being useful…  
**Clank**: If I stop being useful, will you still…  
_He stops. Something inside him clicks, and clicks again, looking for a word it hasn’t got._  
**Clank**: I don’t have the word. Pixel didn’t put it in.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, ask): putting a word to a worry  
> _“If I stop being useful, will you still…” Which word do you think Clank is looking for?_  
> - …like me? → That could be it. Clank is worried: scared about something that might happen. Finding the word for a worry makes it easier to share.
> - …want me? → That could be it. Clank is worried he only matters when he’s doing jobs. Saying a worry out loud is the first step to someone helping.
> - …love me? → That could be it. It’s a big word for a small kettle. Clank is worried. Naming a worry is brave, even if you need help finding the word.
> _For grown-ups: Helped a character find words for a worry, and learned that naming a worry makes it easier to share._


**Choices**

- **Sit down below the shelf, and wait.**
    - You sit on the shed floor under the shelf. It isn’t comfortable. You stay.
    - **Clank**: What are you doing? Is it a job?
    - **You**: No. I’m just here.
    - **Clank**: Just here for what?
    - **You**: Nothing. That’s the whole thing.
    - After a long time Clank comes down off the shelf, very carefully, and parks beside you. He doesn’t announce it.
- **Teach him the step: it’s all right to ask for help.**
    - **You**: Do you know why you keep falling down the step?
    - **Clank**: Because I am brave?
    - **You**: Because you never ask anyone to help you down it.
    - **Clank**: But then I’d be needing. Helpers don’t need. Needing is the opposite of a job.
    - **You**: Everybody needs a hand with a step sometimes. Fern does. Hazel did. I do, on bad days.
    - Clank thinks about this for so long that his whistle goes off by itself, very softly.

#### `reader:s21-ramp` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-ramp)

_Next afternoon Byte comes up the lane with a plank, a saw, and the face of a sum that won’t come out._  
**Byte**: I measured the step. Nineteen centimetres. The safe height for one wheel is, as far as I can find, zero.  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.MD.A.1, ask): choosing a sensible unit of length  
> _Byte says the step is nineteen… what? Which unit makes sense for a step?_  
> - ✅ Centimetres → Yes. 19 cm is about the length of a pencil. A good size for a step, and a bad size for one wheel.
> - Metres → 19 metres is taller than a house! That would be quite a step. For a step, centimetres make sense: 19 cm.
> - Kilometres → 19 kilometres would take about four hours to walk! For a step, centimetres make sense: 19 cm.
> _For grown-ups: Chose centimetres as the sensible unit for a step’s height, comparing it with metres and kilometres._

**Byte**: I measured it eleven times. It was nineteen every time. I’m still a little suspicious.  
**Byte**: So it isn’t him. Statistically, it’s the step.  
**Byte**: I said he should be switched off. That was a conclusion. I hadn’t finished collecting the data.  
**Byte**: The data now includes eight Nubbins out of eight, following him everywhere. That is a significant result.  

> **Lesson** (science, K-2-ETS1-1, notice): defining the real problem before fixing it  
> _Byte has changed the problem. Not “Clank keeps falling”, but “the step is too high for a wheel”._  
> _Engineers and scientists work like this. Say exactly what the problem is, look at all the data, and change your mind when the data changes._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that solving a problem starts with defining it clearly, and that good thinkers change their minds when new evidence arrives._

**Clank**: Byte! Byte! Does that mean you’re my best—  
**Byte**: Provisionally.  

#### `reader:s21-down` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-down)

_The ramp is finished: small, sanded, very well made. Byte calls it an inclined plane. Everyone else calls it a ramp._  
**Clank**: Announcement. I am going down the step.  
**Clank**: Announcement. Would somebody… come with me? The first time?  
_Byte puts one hand on his hood. You put one on the other side. Pixel holds the wheelbarrow, for reasons._  
_He goes down the ramp at the speed of a snail with doubts. He reaches the bottom. Nothing falls._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, ask): why a ramp (inclined plane) is gentler than a step  
> _Why can Clank go down the ramp safely, when the step always tipped him over?_  
> - ✅ The ramp spreads the drop over a long, gentle slope, so he goes down a little at a time. → Yes. Gravity still pulls, but the slope means it pulls him gently along instead of straight down all at once. A steeper ramp would make him go faster. Byte checked. Eleven times.
> - Ramps switch gravity off. → Gravity never switches off! The ramp spreads the drop out over a long slope, so gravity pulls him down gently, a little at a time.
> - It’s magic. → It feels like magic! But it’s a simple machine: a slope spreads the drop out, so gravity takes him down gently, a bit at a time.
> _For grown-ups: Explained that a ramp (inclined plane) spreads a drop over a longer distance, so gravity moves an object down more gently._

**Clank**: I DID THE STEP! I did the step and I didn’t find it!  
_He does a little dance: forward, back, a spin on his one wheel. You copy it. Byte copies it, and later denies it._  
**Pixel**: He did. He danced like that at our wedding. His lace came undone then too.  
**Pixel**: That’s… none. I’ll take none.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, notice): asking for help is brave  
> _That might be the bravest thing Clank has done. He asked for help._  
> _Asking isn’t the opposite of being helpful. It’s how everybody gets down hard steps. Robots included._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that asking for help is a brave and normal thing to do._


#### `reader:s21-evening` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-evening)

_The afternoon goes gold. Clank parks at the bottom of the new ramp and doesn’t water anything, which costs him a great deal._  

> **Lesson** (science, notice): why evening sunlight looks golden  
> _Why does the afternoon go gold? When the Sun is low, its light travels through much more air to reach you._  
> _The air scatters away lots of the blue light on the way. What gets through looks warm and golden._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that low evening sunlight passes through more air, which scatters blue light, so it looks golden._

**Clank**: Announcement: I am not helping. I am just… here.  
**Clank**: Is that allowed? Is just here allowed?  
**You**: Just here is allowed.  
**Clank**: Announcement: just here is allowed.  
_He twitches towards a dandelion. He stops himself. It is the hardest thing he has done all week._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): being still and noticing  
> _Be “just here” with Clank. Sit still and listen._  
> _Can you hear three different sounds? Maybe a bird, the pond, and a very small kettle-whistle trying not to happen._  
> Check: `edu:listen:sounds:3`
> _For grown-ups: Practised sitting still and noticing three sounds, a simple calming exercise._


#### `reader:s21-complete` · [main](../../../side/S21.md#s21-complete)

_At dusk the Nubbins come in from the long grass one by one, and climb onto Clank, because he’s warm, like a kettle that’s just been on._  
_Seven of them. Eight. One on his wheel. One perched on his lantern hood, like a hat._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-PS3-2, notice): heat moves from warmer things to cooler things  
> _Heat moves from warmer things to cooler things. As dusk cools the air, Clank’s warmth flows into the Nubbins snuggled on him._  
> _That’s why they choose him. He’s the warmest seat in the Grove._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that heat moves from warmer objects to cooler ones._

_One Nubbin licks Clank’s brass nose. It is not supper. Clank announces it anyway._  
**Clank**: Announcement.  
**Clank**: I am being very still.  
_He is. For an hour, the chattiest thing in the valley doesn’t move a bolt. Not even to announce that he isn’t moving._  
_You sit beside him. That’s all. It turns out that’s plenty._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.MD.A.1, ask): a time interval: one bean a minute  
> _Clank waters one bean a minute. There are 30 beans in the row. How long will the whole row take?_  
> - ✅ 30 minutes → Yes! One a minute, 30 beans: 30 minutes. That’s half an hour of very loud watering.
> - 3 minutes → That would be ten beans a minute, and Clank is a one-bean-a-minute robot. 30 beans takes 30 minutes: half an hour.
> - 30 hours → He’s slow, but not that slow! One bean a minute means 30 beans take 30 minutes: half an hour.
> _For grown-ups: Worked out a time interval (30 beans at one a minute takes 30 minutes, half an hour)._

**Pixel**: Keep him. He’s yours now. He’ll water things, slowly. Mostly plants.  
_Pixel drops a spare lantern hood on your head. It fits, as if it was waiting to._  
_Next morning he is out among the beans with a watering can, one bean a minute._  
_Byte’s log has a new line: “Best friends: fifteen. One of them still a bucket.”_  
_Mustard watches from Fern’s wall, from very far away._  

### Glossary

- **optimistic**: Hoping and expecting that things will turn out well.
- **announcement**: Something said out loud so that everyone knows it.
- **pruned**: Cut back, to trim a plant or tree into shape.
- **stranded**: Stuck somewhere with no way to get back.
- **dignified**: Calm and proud, even when something silly happens.
- **incident**: Something that happens, often something that goes wrong.
- **paraffin**: A kind of oil that is burned in lamps and lanterns.
- **data**: Facts and measurements collected so you can find something out.
- **provisionally**: For now, until it is checked or decided for sure.
- **inclined plane**: A flat surface set at a slope: a ramp. It is one of the simple machines.
