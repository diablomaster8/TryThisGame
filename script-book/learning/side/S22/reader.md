# Learning Grove · Grove Readers (ages 7–10): Side story S22: The Climb

## S22. The Climb

- `s22-step1`: Climb the Scree with Nova. · _On loose stones keep moving: three quick steps, then rest on a big rock. Never stop on the small ones._
- `s22-step2`: Climb the Chimney: brace and push, one side to the other. · _Push with your back, step with your feet. Move one hand or foot at a time, and keep three on the rock._
- `s22-step3`: Cross the Ridge. · _Keep low in the gusts. Hand on the rock, eyes on the next step, not the drop._
- `s22-step4`: Plant the summit flag on top of the Tooth. · _It’s in Nova’s bag, under the oatcakes. Wedge the pole into the crack on the very top._

#### `reader:s22-foot` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-foot)

_The Tooth stands over the valley like something the valley bit and didn’t finish. There’s already snow on its tip._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, L.4.5, ask): understand a name that works as a metaphor  
> _Why do you think the people of the valley call this mountain the Tooth?_  
> - ✅ Its sharp, pointed top looks like a tooth. → Yes. Calling the mountain a tooth because of its shape is a metaphor: it says one thing is another, to help us picture it.
> - Because it bites people. → Mountains don’t bite! The name is a metaphor. Its sharp, pointed top looks like a tooth.
> - Because it is white, like a tooth. → Only its snowy tip is white. Mostly it’s the sharp, pointed shape that looks like a tooth. That kind of name is a metaphor.
> _For grown-ups: Explained a place name as a metaphor based on shape._

_Nova is at the foot with a coil of rope, a bag of oatcakes, and the look of someone who has been here before._  
**Nova**: Three times. I’ve tried it three times. I got as far as the Ridge every time. Then I came down. I always come down.  
**Nova**: That’s me. I come, I look, and I leave before anywhere can start to matter.  
**Nova**: I asked you for help once, in the Hollow. It went well. I thought I’d try it again before I lose my nerve.  
_On the rock beside her sits something the size of a cat, made of shadow. It is exactly Nova’s shape._  
**Nova’s Worry**: You’re going to fall.  
_Nova goes very still. She looks at you._  
**Nova**: …You can see it.  
**Nova**: Nobody can see it. I’ve carried that about my whole life and nobody has ever once—  
**You**: Is it dangerous?  
**Nova**: It’s mine. It’s my Worry. It’s talked to me since I was small. It’s mostly right, which is the annoying part.  
**Nova’s Worry**: Mostly.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-awareness, notice): name a worry and understand what it is for  
> _Everybody has worries. A worry is your mind trying to keep you safe, by warning you about what might go wrong._  
> _Nova’s Worry is a feeling, shown as a character. Sometimes it’s useful. Sometimes it’s too loud. Noticing it and giving it a name is a good first step._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned that worry is the mind’s way of warning us, and that naming a worry is a helpful first step._

**Nova**: Right. Scree first. I’ll sing. It helps. It doesn’t help anyone else.  

#### `reader:s22-scree` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-scree)

_The scree slides under every step, with a sound like someone pouring out a sack of teacups._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-ESS2-1, notice): frost breaks rock into scree (weathering)  
> **Nova**: Scree is made by frost. Rain gets into cracks in the cliff, then freezes at night.  
> **Nova**: Ice takes up more room than water, so it pushes the crack a little wider. Bit by bit, stones break off and tumble down here.  
> _Breaking rock into pieces like this is called weathering._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned how freezing water widens cracks and breaks rock into scree (weathering)._

**Nova**: ♪ Little stones, little stones, sliding out from under my bones, ♪  
**Nova**: ♪ I would like to go up but I’m going down, which is the opposite of up, little stones. ♪  
_It doesn’t rhyme. It doesn’t fit the beat. She sings it four times._  
**Nova’s Worry**: Last time you slipped here and cut your knee. You’ve still got the scar.  
**Nova**: It’s right. It’s always right about the facts. It’s the next bit it gets wrong.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): tell a fact from a worried guess  
> **Nova**: My Worry says two kinds of things. Facts, and guesses about what’s going to happen.  
> **Nova**: Which of these is a fact, not a guess?  
> - ✅ “You slipped here last time.” → Right. That really happened. “You’re going to fall” is only a guess. Spotting the difference makes a worry a bit smaller.
> - “You’re going to fall.” → That one sounds very sure. But it’s a guess about the future. Worries often dress up guesses as facts.
> - “It will go wrong again.” → Nobody knows that yet. It’s a guess about the future. The fact is, I slipped last time. That’s all.
> _For grown-ups: Practised telling a fact from a worried guess about the future, a way of calming worries._


**Choices**

- **“It is right. And you got up and kept going.”**
    - **Nova**: …I did, didn’t I. I bled on my boot all the way to the Chimney.
    - **Nova’s Worry**: That’s… also true.
    - **Nova**: Ha.
- **“That was last time. This time there are two of us.”**
    - **Nova**: Two of us. Huh.
    - **Nova’s Worry**: Three.
    - **Nova**: It can count. It’s always been able to count.

#### `reader:s22-chimney` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-chimney)

_The Chimney is a crack in the rock just wide enough for a person and a doubt._  
_You go up it with your back pressed on one side and your boots on the other._  

> **Lesson** (health, notice): climbing safety: three points of contact  
> **Nova**: Climber’s rule: three points of contact. Two hands and a foot, or two feet and a hand, on the rock at all times.  
> **Nova**: Move only one at a time. And real climbing is only ever with a grown-up, a helmet and the right gear.  
> _For grown-ups: Learned the climber’s “three points of contact” rule and that real climbing needs a grown-up and safety gear._

**Nova**: ♪ I’m in a chimney, I am not a soot, I am a Nova, and this is my foot. ♪  
**Nova**: ♪ This is my other foot. ♪  
_Far below, a buzzard turns and turns in circles, watching you with what feels like professional interest._  
_It hardly flaps its wings at all._  

> **Lesson** (science, 4-LS1-1, notice): how buzzards soar on rising warm air  
> _Buzzards ride on rising warm air, called a thermal. They circle round and round inside it, going higher without flapping._  
> _Their long, broad wings are made for it. They save their energy for hunting._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned how a buzzard’s broad wings let it soar on rising warm air (thermals)._


#### `reader:s22-chimney-top` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-chimney-top)

_Halfway up the Chimney, Nova stops. The Worry is wedged in above her, looking down._  
**Nova’s Worry**: Everywhere you stay, you lose something. The last place you stayed, you were nine. Remember?  
_Nova doesn’t move. Her knuckles are white on the rock._  
**Nova**: It’s true. That’s the thing. That one’s true.  
**Nova**: Somebody left. So I learned to be the one who leaves first. It hurts less.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.3.3, ask): explain a character’s motive  
> _Why does Nova always leave places first?_  
> - ✅ Someone left her when she was young, so she leaves first to avoid being hurt again. → Yes. Her habit of leaving comes from an old hurt. Knowing why a character acts helps us understand them, and be kind.
> - She gets bored easily. → She says why herself: “Somebody left. So I learned to be the one who leaves first.” It’s about an old hurt.
> - She doesn’t like anyone. → She likes you enough to ask for help! She leaves first because somebody once left her, and it hurt.
> _For grown-ups: Explained a character’s behaviour using what she says about her past._

**Nova**: It’s quicker.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, do): calm your body with counted breathing  
> _Nova is frozen by a big feeling. Help her calm her body first._  
> _(Breathe in slowly while you count to four. Breathe out while you count to four. Do it three times with Nova.)_  
> **Nova**: …Better. My hands feel like mine again.  
> Check: `edu:breathe:count:4-in-4-out`
> _For grown-ups: Practised counted breathing (in for 4, out for 4) as a way to calm a big feeling._


**Choices**

- **“It’s true. It still isn’t a reason to let go.”**
    - **Nova**: …No. It isn’t.
    - She moves one hand. Then one foot. Then the other hand. Very slowly, like someone learning how.
    - **Nova’s Worry**: I wasn’t telling you to let go. I was telling you it was a long way down.
    - **Nova**: I know. You’re very bad at saying what you mean. I wonder where you get it.
- **“Not here. Not now. Look at me, not down.”**
    - She looks at you. Not at the drop. Not at the Worry. At you.
    - **Nova**: …Right. Right. Hands.
    - She climbs. The Worry climbs too, right behind her, muttering about loose rock. It’s right about the loose rock.

#### `reader:s22-ridge` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-ridge)

_The Ridge is a knife-edge of rock with the whole sky on both sides. The wind comes over it in long, cold shoves._  
_Nova checks her map. It is covered in wiggly brown lines, bunched up tight around the Ridge._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, ask): read contour lines on a map  
> **Nova**: The wiggly lines are contour lines. Each one joins up places that are the same height.  
> **Nova**: Where the lines are squashed close together, what is the land like?  
> - ✅ Very steep. → Yes. Close lines mean the land goes up a lot in a short distance: steep. Lines far apart mean gentle slopes. So the Ridge is steep on both sides. Which we knew.
> - Flat. → Flat land has contour lines far apart, or none at all. Close-together lines mean it climbs fast: steep.
> - Wet. → Water is usually shown in blue. Brown contour lines show height. Close together means steep.
> _For grown-ups: Learned that contour lines join places of equal height, and that close lines mean steep ground._

_This is where she turned back before. You can see the place: a little cairn of three stones, one for each try._  
**Nova**: Here. This is where I always… yes.  
**Nova’s Worry**: Go back. It’s windy. It’s high. They’ll wonder where you are. You can come another day.  
**Nova**: Every true thing it says makes me smaller. I fight it, and fight it, and up here I lose. Every time.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): decide what to do with a worry that tells the truth  
> _Here is a hard question. Nova’s Worry is telling the truth: it really is windy and high._  
> _What do you think you should do with a worry that tells the truth?_  
> - Listen to it, then decide for yourself. → That’s a wise answer. A worry can give useful facts, like “hold on tight”. But it doesn’t have to make every choice for you.
> - Fight it until it goes away. → Lots of people try that, and it’s a fair idea. Nova has, for years. Sometimes fighting a worry just makes it louder. Let’s see what she tries next.
> - Let it decide, and go home. → Sometimes that really is the right call. If something is truly dangerous, staying safe matters most. The trick is telling real danger from a worry that is just loud.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about how to respond to a worry that is telling the truth (a think question with no single right answer)._


#### `reader:s22-ridge-cross` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-ridge-cross)

_In the middle of the Ridge, Nova stops. The Worry is beside her. It has always been beside her._  
_She looks at it for a long time. Then she holds out her hand._  
**Nova**: Come on, then.  
**Nova’s Worry**: …What?  
**Nova**: You’re coming anyway. You always come. So come with me. Not at me.  
_The Worry looks at her hand. Then it takes it._  
_It’s smaller, with its hand in hers. Not gone. Smaller._  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.4.2, ask): find the theme of a story  
> _The Worry didn’t vanish. It got smaller when Nova walked with it instead of fighting it._  
> _What is the story telling us?_  
> - ✅ You can’t always make a fear go away, but it can shrink when you stop fighting it and keep going. → Yes. That’s the theme: the big idea underneath. Fear can come along without being in charge.
> - Worries always disappear if you hold their hand. → Look again: “Not gone. Smaller.” The Worry stays. It just stops being in charge. That’s the theme.
> - You should never have worries. → Everyone has worries, even Nova. The story says a worry can come along without being the boss.
> _For grown-ups: Identified the theme: a fear can shrink when you accept it and keep going._

_They cross the Ridge together, keeping low in the gusts. Nova starts the stones song again, and a second voice joins in, horribly flat._  

> **Lesson** (science, 3-PS2-1, notice): keeping low helps you balance against a push  
> _Wind pushes on everything in its way. A tall, upright body is easy to push off balance._  
> _Crouching low makes you steadier, so your grip and your weight can balance the wind’s push._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned why crouching low helps you stay balanced against a strong push like the wind._

**Nova’s Worry**: ♪ …little stones, which are very loose, and we should mind them… ♪  
**Nova**: You can SING?  
**Nova’s Worry**: I’ve always been able to sing. You never asked.  

#### `reader:s22-scramble` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-scramble)

_The last bit is a scramble on hands and knees, up to a point of rock the size of a kitchen table._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 4.MD.A.2, ask): solve a height problem by subtracting  
> _Nova’s map says the foot of the Tooth is 300 metres above sea level. The top is 900 metres._  
> _How high have you climbed today?_  
> - ✅ 600 metres → Yes. 900 − 300 = 600. Think of it as 9 hundreds take away 3 hundreds: 6 hundreds.
> - 1,200 metres → That’s 900 + 300. We want the difference between the top and the foot: 900 − 300 = 600 metres.
> - 300 metres → 300 metres is where you started. The climb is the difference: 900 − 300 = 600 metres.
> _For grown-ups: Subtracted hundreds (900 − 300) to find how far a climb went up._

_Nova unpacks the flag from under the oatcakes. The pole comes in two halves that screw together._  
**Nova**: Give me the flag. No, you do it. No. Together.  
**Nova**: Fine. Together.  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Relationship skills, notice): agree by compromising  
> _When two people both want to do something, sharing it is a good way to agree._  
> _Finding an answer that works for both of you is called a compromise._  
> _For grown-ups: Learned what a compromise is, using Nova’s “together” as an example._

_You both take hold of the pole. There’s a crack in the very top of the rock, just the right size._  
_The Worry holds the corner of the flag so it doesn’t flap in your faces. It’s surprisingly helpful._  

#### `reader:s22-summit` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-summit)

_From the top of the Tooth you can see everything at once._  
_The falls are a long white thread. There’s Dusty’s cliff. Marina’s pools are like spilled coins. The Circuit looks square and small._  
_The Hollow is dark in its fold of trees. And the Grove: the pond, the cottage roof, and four lantern posts no bigger than pins._  

> **Lesson** (social-studies, D2.Geo.2.K-2, do): draw a simple map with symbols and a key  
> **Nova**: From up here the valley looks like a map. A map is a view from above, drawn smaller, with symbols for real things.  
> **Nova**: Let’s make one. Mark the falls, the pools, the Circuit, the Hollow and the Grove. Give each a symbol, and explain them in a key.  
> _(Draw your map of the valley. Any symbols are fine, as long as your key explains them.)_  
> Check: `edu:draw:map:valley-view`
> _For grown-ups: Drew a map of a landscape seen from above, using symbols and a key._

_Nova sits down. The Worry sits beside her, holding her hand. Neither of them says anything for a while._  
**Nova**: I’d like to stay for a while.  
_She says it to the valley first. Then, more quietly, to the Worry. Then, last of all, to you._  
**Nova**: Not up here. Down there. I’d like to stay for a while.  

> **Lesson** (literacy, RL.2.3, ask): describe how a character changes  
> _At the start, Nova said she always leaves before anywhere can matter. What has changed?_  
> - ✅ She wants to stay in the valley now, even though it’s frightening. → Yes. She has changed from someone who always leaves to someone who chooses to stay. That’s a big change for a character.
> - She wants to live on top of the mountain. → She says, “Not up here. Down there.” She means the valley. She has chosen to stay.
> - She’s going to leave tomorrow. → It’s the opposite! For the first time, Nova wants to stay. That’s how she has changed.
> _For grown-ups: Described how a character changed from the beginning of the story to the end._

**Nova’s Worry**: It’ll be frightening.  
**Nova**: Yes. You’ll come too.  

#### `reader:s22-complete` · [main](../../../side/S22.md#s22-complete)

_Going down is quicker and much worse, as it always is. Nova sings the whole way. So does the Worry._  
_At the bottom she unpacks the oatcakes, which have been sat on. There are nine. She shares them out in three._  

> **Lesson** (maths, 3.OA.A.3, ask): share equally into groups (division)  
> _Nine squashed oatcakes, shared fairly between Nova, you and the Worry. How many each?_  
> - ✅ 3 each → Yes. 9 ÷ 3 = 3, because 3 × 3 = 9. Even the Worry gets three.
> - 6 each → Three people with 6 each would need 18 oatcakes. 9 ÷ 3 = 3, so it’s 3 each.
> - 4 each → 4 + 4 + 4 = 12, which is too many. 3 + 3 + 3 = 9, so it’s 3 each.
> _For grown-ups: Shared 9 into 3 equal groups (9 ÷ 3 = 3)._

**Nova**: Keep the flag. Put it somewhere you’ll see it. I’ll know where it is. I’ll come and look at it.  
**Nova**: And have my boots. They’re my spare pair. I won’t need spares. I’m not going anywhere.  
**Nova**: You can see the whole valley from up there now, whenever you like. In case you ever need to find me. I’ll be in it.  
_The Worry waves. Small. Only you can see it._  

> **Lesson** (sel, CASEL: Self-management, ask): choose a way to cope with worry  
> _Nova tried lots of ways to climb with her Worry. Which one would you like to try next time you feel worried?_  
> - Sing a silly song. → Good choice. Singing helps you breathe steadily, and it’s hard to feel quite so scared while being silly.
> - Breathe slowly and count. → Good choice. Slow, counted breaths tell your body it’s safe to calm down.
> - Sort the facts from the guesses. → Good choice. Asking “Is that a fact, or a guess?” can make a worry much smaller.
> - Tell a friend, and do it together. → Good choice. Worries feel lighter when you share them. That’s exactly what Nova did with you.
> _For grown-ups: Chose a coping strategy to try when feeling worried (all choices are good ones)._


### Glossary

- **nerve**: The courage to do something scary. If you “lose your nerve”, you suddenly feel too scared to do it.
- **scree**: A slope covered in small, loose stones that have broken off the rock above.
- **weathering**: The slow breaking-up of rock by things like frost, rain and wind.
- **professional**: Done the way an expert would do it, as their job.
- **thermal**: A rising current of warm air that birds can ride upwards.
- **cairn**: A pile of stones built as a marker, often on hills and mountains.
- **contour line**: A line on a map that joins places of the same height.
- **gusts**: Sudden, strong rushes of wind.
- **compromise**: An answer two people agree on, where each gets some of what they want.
- **sea level**: The height of the sea’s surface. Mountain heights are measured from it.
- **symbol**: A small picture or mark that stands for something real.
