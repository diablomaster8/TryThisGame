# Ori's Alef-Bet, Hebrew (LH1–LH6)

## LH1. Shalom, Ori!

*Original: a beginner Hebrew course for the Learning Grove (greetings)* · audience: all · where: Learning Grove only · when: Any morning once the Learning Grove is open: Nova brings Ori to the gate.

**Cast:** Ori

### Step 1: Greet Ori at the gate.

Hint: Walk to the gate and tap Ori. He says שָׁלוֹם (shalom), so say it back. · check: `lang:he:greet:ori`

#### <a id="lh1-s1"></a>Scene `lh1-s1`

_The gate rattles. Somebody on the other side is lifting and pushing, and losing._  
**Nova**: Lift AND push, Ori. Both at once. It’s a Grove thing.  
_The gate gives. In tumbles a boy in a football shirt, hugging a biscuit tin like it’s a trophy._  
**Ori** _(happy)_: שָׁלוֹם (shalom, “hello”)! Sorry. I had a fight with your gate. Your gate won.  
**Nova**: This is Ori. He’s staying with me for three weeks. He’s ten, he lives in Israel, and he has already told me forty star names.  
**Ori**: Forty-two. You stopped listening at forty.  
**Ori** _(think)_: In Hebrew, shalom means hello. It also means goodbye. And peace. It’s a very hard-working word.  
**Ori**: אֲנִי (ani, “I”)… קוֹרְאִים לִי… (korim li, “my name is…”) Ori. Ani Ori. That’s me. Now you.  
_Over the wall, a small round wild Nubbin hears “shalom”, waves at Ori with both arms, and falls over._  
**Ori**: To ask how you are, I say מָה שְׁלוֹמְךָ? (ma shlomkha?) to a boy, or מָה שְׁלוֹמֵךְ? (ma shlomekh?) to a girl.  
**Ori**: The ending changes. Hebrew likes to know who it’s talking to. Which one should I ask you?  

**Choices**

- **Ask me “ma shlomkha?”**
    - **Ori**: Ma shlomkha?
    - **You**: בְּסֵדֶר (beseder, “fine”). I think?
    - **Ori**: Beseder! That means “OK, fine”. Short and strong, like a header into the top corner.
- **Ask me “ma shlomekh?”**
    - **Ori**: Ma shlomekh?
    - **You**: בְּסֵדֶר (beseder, “fine”). I think?
    - **Ori**: Beseder! That means “OK, fine”. Short and strong, like a header into the top corner.
- **Either one. Just say shalom!**
    - **Ori**: Shalom, then! And if anyone asks how you are, say בְּסֵדֶר (beseder). It means “OK, fine”.
    - **You**: Beseder.
    - **Ori**: Perfect. You sound like my uncle. That’s a compliment. Mostly.

#### <a id="lh1-s1-done"></a>Done `lh1-s1-done`

**Ori** _(happy)_: You said it! A proper shalom. Ten out of ten. Well, nine. The gate gets a point for defending.  
_The round Nubbin waves at Ori again, topples, and lies there looking pleased with itself._  
**Ori**: I’m calling that one the Wobbler. It’s my favourite already. Don’t tell the others.  

### Step 2: Teach three Nubbins to wave when they hear שָׁלוֹם (shalom).

Hint: Stand by a Nubbin, say shalom and wave. When it waves back, say תּוֹדָה (toda, “thank you”). · check: `lang:he:teach:nubbins:shalom:3`

#### <a id="lh1-s2"></a>Scene `lh1-s2`

**Ori**: Right. Nubbin school. I’m the coach. You’re the assistant coach. The Wobbler is team captain, obviously.  
_Ori sits cross-legged on the grass, facing three Nubbins: the Wobbler, a shy one behind a dandelion, and one who is mostly asleep._  
**Ori**: Coaching rule one: always say thank you. In Hebrew, that’s תּוֹדָה (toda, “thank you”).  
**Ori**: Rule two: be polite. בְּבַקָּשָׁה (bevakasha) means “please”. It also means “you’re welcome”. Hebrew words like to keep busy.  
**Ori**: When one of them waves at shalom, we cheer טוֹב (tov, “good”)! Big tov. Stadium tov.  
_The shy Nubbin peeks out from behind the dandelion, looks at Ori, and ducks back._  
**Ori**: Will she wave? כֵּן (ken, “yes”)? Or לֹא (lo, “no”)?  
**Ori**: Lo. Not yet. That’s fine. A coach is patient. I’m not patient. You be the patient one.  
_The Wobbler waves at nobody in particular, falls over, and gets up to wave again._  

**Choices**

- **Sit down near the shy one and wait.**
    - You sit and wait. After a long time, one tiny paw lifts, just a little.
    - **Ori**: Ken! KEN! Toda! (That was me whispering. I’m bad at whispering.)
- **Whisper “shalom” very softly.**
    - You whisper it, and the shy Nubbin tilts her head as if the word tickled. One paw lifts, just a little.
    - **Ori**: Tov! Very tov! Quiet shalom works on quiet Nubbins. Write that down, coach.

#### <a id="lh1-s2-done"></a>Done `lh1-s2-done`

_The shy one waves, and the sleepy one waves in its sleep. The Wobbler waves, falls over, and waves from the ground._  
**Ori** _(happy)_: Three Nubbins! Toda, toda, toda!  
**You**: Bevakasha.  
**Ori**: Bevakasha! You used the “you’re welcome” one. Assistant coach is now head coach. I’m demoted. Beseder.  

### Step 3: Say לַיְלָה טוֹב (laila tov, “good night”) to everyone.

Hint: Find Fern, Clover and Nova before they go indoors, and tap each one to say laila tov. · check: `lang:he:say:laila-tov`

#### <a id="lh1-s3"></a>Scene `lh1-s3`

_The sun slides behind the western hill and the lanterns blink on. Ori yawns, and pretends he didn’t._  
**Ori**: Hebrew has a “good” for each part of the day. Morning is בֹּקֶר טוֹב (boker tov, “good morning”).  
**Ori**: Evening is עֶרֶב טוֹב (erev tov, “good evening”). Like now. Erev tov, lanterns!  
**Ori**: And at bedtime, לַיְלָה טוֹב (laila tov, “good night”). Tov, tov, tov. Everything’s good. Hebrew is very optimistic.  
**Nova**: Go round and say laila tov to everyone before they go in. Fern’s still in the orchard.  
**Ori**: If you won’t see someone till tomorrow, say לְהִתְרָאוֹת (lehitra’ot, “see you”).  
**Ori**: Or just shalom. Shalom works for goodbye too. Shalom does everything. Shalom should have its own football team.  
_At the word “shalom”, the Wobbler waves goodbye, falls over, and goes to sleep where it landed._  

#### <a id="lh1-s3-done"></a>Done `lh1-s3-done`

**Fern**: Laila tov to you too! I think. Did I say it right? I’ve been practising on the plum tree.  
**Ori**: Perfect. Fern, תּוֹדָה רַבָּה (toda raba, “thank you very much”). That’s the big thank-you. For people who try.  
_All three Nubbins are asleep in a heap by Ori’s tent, like dropped socks._  
**Ori**: Lehitra’ot, everybody. Laila tov. Toda raba for today. Tomorrow: letters.  

### Complete

#### <a id="lh1-complete"></a>Complete `lh1-complete`

_Morning. Somebody has hung a sign on the gate that says שָׁלוֹם in neat, slightly wonky paint._  
**Ori** _(happy)_: Boker tov! I made you a sign. Shalom for people coming in, shalom for people going out. Very efficient.  
**Ori**: Quick-fire round. I say it, you say it back. Boker tov! Toda! Bevakasha! Ken! Lo! Laila tov!  
**Ori**: And I made up a chant. Don’t laugh. Actually, you can laugh. Laughing is tov.  
**Ori**: “Boker tov, the sun is out! Shalom, shalom, wave and shout! Erev tov, the lanterns glow, laila tov, and off we go!”  
_The Wobbler hears “shalom”, waves with both arms, wobbles hard and, for the first time, stays upright._  
**Ori** _(shock)_: It stayed up! Historic! Lehitra’ot, gravity!  
_The shalom sign stays on the gate, and you can wave “shalom” to any Nubbin who has learned it._  

**Rewards**

- שָׁלוֹם welcome sign (decor): A hand-painted sign for the gate that says שָׁלוֹם (shalom): hello for people coming in, goodbye for people going out. Nubbins who know the word wave at it.
- “Shalom” wave emote (cosmetic): A big two-handed wave. Nubbins who learned “shalom” wave back.

## LH2. Right to Left

*Original: a beginner Hebrew course for the Learning Grove (the alef-bet)* · audience: all · where: Learning Grove only · when: The morning after LH1 is complete. · requires: LH1

**Cast:** Ori

### Step 1: Find the four letters of שָׁלוֹם (shalom) hidden round the garden, and trace each one.

Hint: Ori painted them on pebbles: look by the pond, the gate, the orchard and his tent. Trace them starting from the right. · check: `lang:he:find:letters:shalom`

#### <a id="lh2-s1"></a>Scene `lh2-s1`

**Ori**: Today: the alef-bet. That’s our alphabet. It’s named after its first two letters, alef and bet.  
**Ori**: Rule one: Hebrew goes right to left. Start on the right side of the page and travel left. Like a winger who only uses his left foot.  
_The Wobbler hears “right to left”, thinks about it very hard, and starts walking backwards._  
**Ori**: I hid the letters of שָׁלוֹם round the garden, painted on pebbles. Four letters.  
**Ori**: ש is shin. It says “sh”. Three arms reaching up, like a goalie celebrating a save.  
**Ori**: ל is lamed. It says “l”. It’s the tall one: it sticks up above all the others, like it’s trying to see over a fence.  
**Ori**: ו is vav. A stick with a little hook. Here it makes an “o”. Vav can do v, o or u. Very flexible. A real midfielder.  
**Ori**: And mem, which says “m”. At the end of a word it changes shape and closes up like a box: ם. Five letters do that.  
**Ori**: Find shin, lamed, vav and final mem. Then line them up from the RIGHT.  

#### <a id="lh2-s1-done"></a>Done `lh2-s1-done`

_Four pebbles sit on the path, lined up from right to left: ש ל ו ם._  
**Ori** _(happy)_: Shin, lamed, vav, mem. Shalom! The little dots and lines in שָׁלוֹם are the vowels. Children’s books use them. Grown-ups mostly leave them out.  
**Ori**: Without the dots it’s still shalom. You just have to know. Like a goalkeeper just knows.  
_The Wobbler arrives, backwards, and sits proudly on the vav._  

### Step 2: Trace alef (א) and bet (ב), the letters the alef-bet is named after, then gimel, dalet and hey.

Hint: Start each letter from the right side. Ori draws the first one; you go over it with a stick. · check: `lang:he:trace:alef-bet`

#### <a id="lh2-s2"></a>Scene `lh2-s2`

_By the pond, the mud has dried into a smooth patch of sand. Ori flattens it with the side of his trainer._  
**Ori**: Now the first letters. The ones the alef-bet is named after.  
**Ori**: א is alef. It’s silent. It just holds a vowel, like a cup holds tea. A letter that says nothing. I could never.  
**Ori**: ב is bet. With a dot inside it says “b”. Without the dot it says “v”, and we call it vet. One letter, two sounds.  
**Ori**: Then ג gimel, “g”. ד dalet, “d”. ה hey, “h”. That’s five letters. A whole five-a-side team.  
**Ori**: Hebrew has no capital letters, by the way. No big versions, no small versions. Very fair.  
**Nova**: I tried learning these once, on a long boat trip. I got as far as gimel. The boat got as far as seasick.  
_The Wobbler, still walking backwards, reverses into the pond with a soft plop. It climbs out, delighted._  
**Ori**: Your stick starts on the right and travels left. Which one first?  

**Choices**

- **Alef first. The silent one.**
    - **Ori**: Alef first. Respect. The quiet ones go first in this game.
- **Bet first. The one that makes two sounds.**
    - **Ori**: Bet first! Bold. Rule-breaker. I like it. Alef straight after, though.

#### <a id="lh2-s2-done"></a>Done `lh2-s2-done`

_In the sand: א ב ג ד ה, a bit wobbly and very proud._  
**Ori** _(happy)_: Alef and bet! One letter speaks, one letter stays quiet. And if bet loses its dot, it turns into vet.  
**Ori**: Next is the most important word in the whole alef-bet. My name.  

### Step 3: Write Ori’s name, אוֹרִי, on his tent.

Hint: Four letters, from the right: alef, vav, resh, yod. Tap the paintbrush on the tent to start. · check: `lang:he:write:ori`

#### <a id="lh2-s3"></a>Scene `lh2-s3`

_Ori’s tent stands by the cottage. It is green, slightly crooked, and has a football scarf tied to the pole._  
**Ori**: My name in Hebrew is אוֹרִי. Ori. Four letters: alef, vav, resh, yod.  
**Ori**: You know alef. You know vav, the stick with the hook. New ones: ר is resh, “r”. It looks like a bent elbow.  
**Ori**: And י is yod. It says “y”, or sometimes “i”. Yod is the smallest letter. Tiny, but you can’t spell me without it.  
**Ori**: Here’s the good bit. אוֹר (or) means “light”. Add a yod and you get Ori: “my light”.  
**Ori**: My parents named me “my light”. No pressure. I’ve only been going out to look at stars every night since I was four.  
**Nova** _(soft)_: His savta told me that once, in a letter I carried for her. A long time ago now.  
_The Wobbler watches with enormous attention. It is facing the wrong way._  
**Ori**: Korim li Ori, and now you can write it too. Paint it on the tent. Start on the right. Alef first.  

**Choices**

- **Paint it big, across the door.**
    - **Ori**: Big! You’ll be able to see it from the hill. Maybe from space.
- **Paint it small and neat, by the zip.**
    - **Ori**: Small and neat. Like a signature. A famous person’s signature.
- **Paint it, then add a star next to it.**
    - **Ori**: A star! You get me. A light next to “my light”. That’s poetry, that is.

#### <a id="lh2-s3-done"></a>Done `lh2-s3-done`

_On the tent, in fresh paint: אוֹרִי._  
**Ori** _(happy)_: That’s me. Alef, vav, resh, yod. You wrote my name in Hebrew. Nobody here has done that before.  
_The Wobbler finally turns round, sees the name the right way up, and waves at it._  

### Complete

#### <a id="lh2-complete"></a>Complete `lh2-complete`

_At dusk, Ori sets flat stones along the path to his tent. Each one has a letter painted on it._  
**Ori**: Stepping stones! Alef, bet, gimel, dalet, hey. Then the shalom ones. Then the ones from my name, אוֹרִי.  
**Ori**: Quick-fire. I point, you answer. The tallest letter? Lamed! The smallest? Yod! The one that holds a vowel and says nothing? Alef!  
**Ori** _(happy)_: Hebrew has twenty-two letters. You’ve met eleven. That’s half. It’s half-time!  
_You step from stone to stone, right to left. The Wobbler follows you backwards and doesn’t fall in the pond once._  
**Ori**: The alef-bet stones are yours. Walk them every day and the letters walk into your head.  

**Rewards**

## LH3. Counting Plums

*Original: a beginner Hebrew course for the Learning Grove (numbers)* · audience: all · where: Learning Grove only · when: Any day after LH2 is complete, when Fern’s plums are ripe. · requires: LH2

**Cast:** Ori

### Step 1: Help Fern count plums in Hebrew, from אַחַת (achat, 1) to עֶשֶׂר (eser, 10).

Hint: Tap each plum as it goes in the basket and say its number. Ori teaches one to five; for six to ten, copy him. · check: `lang:he:count:plums:10`

#### <a id="lh3-s1"></a>Scene `lh3-s1`

_In Fern’s orchard the plum trees are heavy. Fern is under one with two baskets and the face of someone who has lost count._  
**Fern**: Ten plums for each basket, for the market. I’ve counted these four times and got four different numbers.  
**Ori**: Let me. I count in Hebrew. It’s faster. It isn’t faster. It sounds faster.  
**Ori**: אַחַת (achat, 1). שְׁתַּיִם (shtayim, 2). שָׁלוֹשׁ (shalosh, 3).  
**Ori**: אַרְבַּע (arba, 4). חָמֵשׁ (chamesh, 5). Chamesh has the throaty “ch”, like the end of “loch”.  
**Fern**: Achat… shtayim… I keep wanting to sneeze it.  
**Ori**: Sneezy is close! SHTAI-yim. Say it like you mean it.  
**Ori**: Six to ten, I’ll call them and you copy. Copying counts today. Tomorrow you do it alone.  
_The Wobbler sits under the tree, catching every plum that falls. Mostly with its face._  

**Choices**

- **I’ll count. You check.**
    - **Ori**: Deal. If you’re wrong, I say lo. If you’re right, I say ken. I expect to say ken a lot.
- **Let Fern count. We’ll cheer.**
    - **Fern**: No pressure, then.
    - **Ori**: Huge pressure. Stadium pressure. Achat!

#### <a id="lh3-s1-done"></a>Done `lh3-s1-done`

**Fern** _(happy)_: Ten! Ten plums. That’s… עֶשֶׂר?  
**Ori**: Eser! Ten. Two baskets of eser makes twenty, but twenty is a different lesson. Don’t get greedy.  
_The Wobbler has a plum balanced on its head. Nobody mentions it._  

### Step 2: Play Ori’s “How Many?” football game: count the goals in Hebrew.

Hint: Watch each shot. After every goal, tap the right Hebrew number. Get it right and you win the ball. · check: `lang:he:minigame:count-goals`

#### <a id="lh3-s2"></a>Scene `lh3-s2`

_Ori sets up four jumpers, a goal at each end, and bounces his football on his knee. He says it’s a world record; it isn’t._  
**Ori**: The game is called “How Many?” I shoot. You count the goals, in Hebrew. Right answer, you get the ball.  
**Ori**: You need the big numbers now. שֵׁשׁ (shesh, 6). שֶׁבַע (sheva, 7). שְׁמוֹנֶה (shmone, 8).  
**Ori**: תֵּשַׁע (tesha, 9). And עֶשֶׂר (eser, 10). Ten is the best number. It’s my shirt number.  
_The Wobbler is in goal. It has decided that being in goal means waving at the ball._  
**Ori** _(think)_: Warning. I am a very good footballer and a very bad loser. Ask my savta. She hid all our board games.  
**Ori**: Ready? Achat, shtayim, shalosh, GO!  

**Choices**

- **Go easy on me.**
    - **Ori**: Lo. Never. A coach never goes easy. …Beseder, a bit easy. For the first shot.
- **Bring it on.**
    - **Ori**: Ken! Now you’re talking like a footballer!

#### <a id="lh3-s2-done"></a>Done `lh3-s2-done`

_Final score: Ori ten, the Wobbler one. The Wobbler waved, the ball bounced off its tummy, rolled the whole length of the grass and into Ori’s goal._  
**Ori** _(shock)_: That’s… that’s one. Achat! It scored ACHAT. Best of three. Best of shesh. Best of sheva. Best of eser!  
**Nova**: Ori.  
**Ori** _(sad)_: …Beseder. Toda for the game. Well played, Wobbler. (He means it about halfway.)  

### Step 3: Count ten stars with Ori, in Hebrew.

Hint: Go up the western hill after dark. Tap a star to count it; Ori says each number with you. · check: `lang:he:count:stars:10`

#### <a id="lh3-s3"></a>Scene `lh3-s3`

_Night. Ori drags you up the western hill with a blanket, a torch and the biscuit tin, which is full of snacks for once._  
**Ori**: Best sky I’ve ever seen. No city lights. At home I have to squint.  
**Ori**: A star is כּוֹכָב (kochav). Every one of those is a kochav. Each one is a sun, just really, really far away.  
**Ori**: And that’s the יָרֵחַ (yareach, “moon”). The yareach isn’t a kochav. It makes no light of its own. The sun lights it up.  
_Something on a branch says “hoo”. Two round eyes blink at you._
