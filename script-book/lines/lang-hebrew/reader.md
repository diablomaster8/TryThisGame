> - I’m not sure. Maybe it just grew that way. → That’s an honest answer. Words grow and change over a long time, and people don’t always know why.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why a single word, shalom, can mean hello, goodbye and peace._

### Glossary

- **שָׁלוֹם (shalom)**: Hello, goodbye and peace, all in one word (say it: sha-LOME).
- **אֲנִי (ani)**: The Hebrew word for “I” (say it: ah-NEE).
- **קוֹרְאִים לִי… (korim li…)**: How you say “my name is…”; it means “they call me” (say it: kor-EEM lee).
- **מָה שְׁלוֹמְךָ? (ma shlomkha?)**: “How are you?” when you ask a boy or man (say it: mah shlom-KHA).
- **מָה שְׁלוֹמֵךְ? (ma shlomekh?)**: “How are you?” when you ask a girl or woman (say it: mah shlo-MEKH).
- **בְּסֵדֶר (beseder)**: OK, fine (say it: beh-SEH-der).
- **תּוֹדָה (toda)**: Thank you (say it: toe-DAH).
- **בְּבַקָּשָׁה (bevakasha)**: Please, and also “you’re welcome” (say it: beh-vah-kah-SHAH).
- **טוֹב (tov)**: Good (say it: TOVE).
- **כֵּן (ken)**: Yes (say it: KEN).
- **לֹא (lo)**: No (say it: LOW).
- **בֹּקֶר טוֹב (boker tov)**: Good morning (say it: BO-ker TOVE).
- **עֶרֶב טוֹב (erev tov)**: Good evening (say it: EH-rev TOVE).
- **לַיְלָה טוֹב (laila tov)**: Good night (say it: LIE-la TOVE).
- **לְהִתְרָאוֹת (lehitra’ot)**: See you, for when you’ll meet again later (say it: leh-heet-rah-OTE).
- **תּוֹדָה רַבָּה (toda raba)**: Thank you very much: the big thank-you (say it: toe-DAH rah-BAH).

> **Lesson** (literacy, ask): why vowel points are often left out (think question)  
> _Grown-ups mostly leave the vowel dots out. Why might they do that?_  
> - It’s quicker to write without them. → That’s a fair answer. Fewer marks means faster writing.
> - They know the words, so they don’t need the help. → That’s a thoughtful answer. Once you know a word well, you can read it from its letters alone.
> - I think they should keep them! → That’s a fair point too. The dots help learners, which is why children’s books keep them.
> _For grown-ups: Thought about why Hebrew vowel points (niqqud) are used in children’s books but often left out by adults._
