# Ori’s Alef-Bet: word list and script primer

Hebrew is written right to left. It has 22 letters and no capital letters. Vowels are small dots and lines (niqqud): children’s books use them, adults mostly leave them out. Five letters have a different shape at the end of a word: ך ם ן ף ץ. Stress usually falls on the last syllable; exceptions are marked in sayIt. “Ch” and “kh” are the throaty sound in Scottish “loch”. Numbers are the forms used when counting aloud. Colours are the masculine forms used to name a colour. Feelings and “friend” have a boy form and a girl form; “how are you?” changes for a boy or a girl. `native` is the vetted spelling with niqqud and is the only form used in scenes. `plain` is the no-niqqud spelling as adults write it, using the usual full spelling (extra vav or yod); plain forms were not in the vetted list, so have the native-speaker reviewer confirm them when recording audio. Every `audio` clip (he/<slug>) is a native-speaker recording. Do not synthesise. Nubbin reactions once a word is learned: shalom (wave), toda (little bow of the head), laila tov (yawn), kochav (look up), yareach (look up and sway), the colour words (point at something that colour), sameach/smecha (big smile), atsuv/atsuva (droop), ayef/ayefa (yawn and sit), the animal words (act them out at the LH6 party). The script primer: show the 22 letters right to left in rows, with final forms (alt) beside their letters. Tracing goes right to left.
