# Hana's Hiragana, Japanese (LJ1–LJ6)

## LJ1. Konnichiwa, Hana!

*Original: a beginner Japanese questline for the Learning Grove* · audience: all · where: Learning Grove only · when: A summer morning once the Learning Grove is on: Nova arrives at the gate with Hana.

**Cast:** Hana

### Step 1: Greet Hana at the gate in Japanese.

Hint: Tap Hana and choose Greet. Say “konnichiwa”, and bow if you like. · check: `lang:ja:greet:hana`

#### <a id="lj1-s1"></a>Scene `lj1-s1`

_Someone is racing up the lane. The gate sticks, as it always does, so she climbs it._  
_A girl in running shoes drops down on your side, sketchbook first. Nova follows at a walk, carrying both bags._  
**Nova** _(happy)_: This is Hana. She’s staying with me for the summer. She raced me from the bridge.  
**Hana**: I won by eleven seconds. Nova says she let me win. Nova is a terrible liar.  
**Hana**: こんにちは (konnichiwa, “hello”)!  
_She bows: a small, quick dip from the waist, then straight back up._  
**Hana**: That’s おじぎ (ojigi), bowing. At home we bow to greet, to thank and to say sorry. A deeper bow shows more respect.  
**Hana**: はじめまして (hajimemashite, “nice to meet you”). You only say it the first time you meet someone. One go. No pressure.  
**Hana**: わたしは はな です (watashi wa Hana desu, “I am Hana”). Your own name goes in the middle, like a sandwich.  
**Hana**: Then finish with よろしくおねがいします (yoroshiku onegaishimasu). It means “please treat me kindly”, or “pleased to meet you”.  
**Nova** _(happy)_: I practised that one all the way here on the train.  
**Hana**: She said it to the ticket man, a cow and a postbox.  
**Hana** _(happy)_: Ready? You say hello. I say はい (hai, “yes”). Today はい is my answer to everything.  
**Nova**: Everything?  
**Hana**: はい.  

**Choices**

- **Bow and say “konnichiwa”.**
    - You bow. Hana bows back, slightly deeper, which she seems to think means she won.
    - **Hana**: はい! That’s “yes, exactly”. You meant that bow.
- **Wave and say “konnichiwa”.**
    - **Hana**: A wave works too. Friends wave all the time.
    - **Hana**: Next time, add a tiny bow. Like this. See? Easy.
- **Try the lot: “Hajimemashite. Watashi wa… desu. Yoroshiku onegaishimasu.”**
    - **Hana**: You did the whole sandwich!
    - **Hana**: It took Nova three days to get that right.
    - **Nova**: Two days.
    - **Hana**: No. Three.

#### <a id="lj1-s1-done"></a>Done `lj1-s1-done`

_Hana is already drawing you. She drew Nova, the gate and a passing bee while you were saying hello._  
**Hana**: Done. Your nose is very good. Your ears are a bit much. That’s my fault, not yours.  
_Behind the water butt, three wild Nubbins have watched the whole thing. One tries a bow, tips forward and rolls into the herbs._  
**Hana** _(happy)_: They want to learn! Tomorrow we teach them. Properly.  
**Hana** _(think)_: Also, is that a frog?  
**Nova**: Are you scared of frogs, Hana?  
**Hana** _(think)_: いいえ (iie, “no”)! Not even slightly. I’m just saying. In case it comes up.  

### Step 2: Teach three Nubbins to bow when you say “konnichiwa”.

Hint: Tap a Nubbin, choose Say, then “konnichiwa”. Do it a few times; Nubbins learn by copying. · check: `lang:ja:teach:nubbins:konnichiwa:3`

#### <a id="lj1-s2"></a>Scene `lj1-s2`

_Morning. Dew on everything. Hana was up before you, and mentions it twice._  
**Hana** _(happy)_: おはよう (ohayō, “good morning”)! That’s for friends and family.  
_Fern comes over the fence with a bucket of plums and a slightly crumpled face._  
**Hana**: おはようございます (ohayō gozaimasu), Fern! That’s the polite one. For grown-ups, teachers and people with buckets.  
**Fern**: Is it? Well. Good morning. Have a plum. I’ve been up since five, apologising to the trees.  
**Hana**: ありがとうございます (arigatō gozaimasu, “thank you”, the polite way). With friends it’s just ありがとう (arigatō).  
**Hana**: Right. The plan. We teach them こんにちは first, because it works most of the day.  
**Hana**: Not now, strictly. Now is おはよう. But one word at a time. They’re Nubbins.  
_She sprints at the herb bed, misjudges it, and bumps a small round Nubbin clean off its feet._  
**Hana** _(shock)_: ごめんなさい (gomen nasai, “I’m sorry”)! Sorry, sorry!  
_She bows low to the Nubbin, because she means it. The Nubbin blinks, copies her, and lands on its face._  
**Fern**: That one’s Dumpling. He eats things. Bows aren’t food, Dumpling.  
**Hana** _(happy)_: But that was a good おじぎ. Very sincere.  
**Hana**: すみません (sumimasen, “excuse me”), everybody. Can you come a bit closer? That’s how you ask nicely.  
**Hana**: Who shall we start with?  

**Choices**

- **The shy one under the bench.**
    - You crouch and say こんにちは, very softly. The shy one peeks out and dips its head the smallest amount.
    - **Hana**: That counts. Small bows count.
- **The bouncy one on the wall.**
    - You say こんにちは. The bouncy one bows so hard it bounces off the wall and has to start again.
    - **Hana**: Enthusiastic. I respect it.
- **The sleepy one in the sun.**
    - You say こんにちは. The sleepy one bows, very slowly, and does not come back up for some time.
    - **Hana**: Is it still bowing? Or asleep?
    - **Fern**: Bit of both.

#### <a id="lj1-s2-done"></a>Done `lj1-s2-done`

_Three Nubbins in a row. You say こんにちは. Three small bows, nearly together._  
**Hana** _(happy)_: ありがとう, everyone! That was beautiful. I’m drawing it.  
_A fourth bow, from the end of the row: Dumpling, late, copying the others and facing the wrong way._  
**Hana**: Other way, Dumpling.  
_Dumpling turns round, bows again, bumps into the water butt, and looks extremely satisfied._  

### Step 3: Say “oyasumi” (good night) to everyone before bed.

Hint: At dusk, tap each Nubbin, Nova and Hana and choose Say, then “oyasumi”. · check: `lang:ja:say:oyasumi`

#### <a id="lj1-s3"></a>Scene `lj1-s3`

_Evening. The light goes gold, then orange, then the colour of weak tea._  
_Luna comes up the path with a trowel and a jar of moths, on her way to the night garden._  
**Hana**: こんばんは (konbanwa, “good evening”), Luna!  
**Luna** _(soft)_: Konbanwa. Did I say it right? Nova taught me last summer. I’ve been saving it.  
**Hana**: Perfect. Fun fact: the last letter of こんばんは and こんにちは looks like “ha” but says “wa”. Japanese likes to surprise you.  
**Fern**: I’m off to bed. Goodbye, all.  
**Hana**: Not さようなら (sayōnara)! That’s goodbye for a long time. You’ll see us in the morning.  
**Hana**: Say またね (mata ne, “see you”). Or, at bedtime, おやすみ (oyasumi, “good night”).  
**Fern** _(happy)_: Mata ne, then. Oyasumi.  
**Hana**: For grown-ups you want to be extra nice to, it’s おやすみなさい (oyasuminasai). The polite one is longer. It usually is.  
**Hana**: Now you. Everybody gets an oyasumi before bed. Nubbins, Nova, Dumpling, me. That’s the rule.  
_Dumpling is already asleep, upright, in the herb bed._  

**Choices**

- **Start with the Nubbins.**
    - You go along the row. Oyasumi, oyasumi, oyasumi. Three sleepy bows and one snore.
    - **Hana**: The snore was Dumpling. It still counts.
- **Start with Nova.**
    - You say おやすみなさい to Nova, with a little bow.
    - **Nova**: Oyasuminasai. The polite one! I feel very grand.
- **Start with Hana.**
    - **Hana**: Me first? Oyasumi! I’m first to bed. I win.
    - **Nova**: It’s not a race.
    - **Hana**: Everything is a race.

#### <a id="lj1-s3-done"></a>Done `lj1-s3-done`

_You say oyasumi to everyone. Even the herb bed, just in case._  
**Nova** _(soft)_: おやすみなさい. You’re better at this than I was after a week.  
_Dumpling bows to the moon. The moon does not bow back, but it seems to appreciate it._  
_Hana is asleep on the bench with her pencil still moving, drawing a line off the edge of the page._  

### Complete

#### <a id="lj1-complete"></a>Complete `lj1-complete`

_Next morning a paper lantern hangs on the gate. Nova painted it last night, in wobbly brush letters: こんにちは._  
**Nova** _(happy)_: A welcome sign. For everyone who comes through, whatever they speak.  
**Hana**: I made up a song on the train. An おじぎ song. Bow at the end of every line.  
**Hana**: おはよう (ohayō), up comes the sun! こんにちは (konnichiwa), hello, everyone!  
**Hana**: ありがとう (arigatō), thanks for the fun! こんばんは (konbanwa), the day is done!  
**Hana**: おやすみ (oyasumi), down goes the light. Bow, bow, and say good night!  
_Three Nubbins bow at the end of every line. Dumpling bows one line late every time, and has to lie down afterwards._  
**Hana** _(happy)_: はい. You can bow now. Properly. I’m giving you that.  
**Fern** _(think)_: Is there a polite version of the song?  
**Hana**: いいえ. It’s already perfect.  

**Rewards**

- Paper-lantern welcome sign (decor): A paper lantern for the gate, painted with こんにちは (konnichiwa, “hello”). Nubbins who pass it give a little bow.
- Bow emote (cosmetic): Your Keeper bows. Any Nubbin that has learned “konnichiwa” bows back.

## LJ2. The Hiragana Pond

*Original: a beginner Japanese questline for the Learning Grove* · audience: all · where: Learning Grove only · when: The morning after LJ1: Hana has been painting on the stepping stones in the pond. · requires: LJ1

**Cast:** Hana

### Step 1: Find あ い う え お on the stepping stones, in order, and trace each one.

Hint: Look across the pond for painted stones. Start with あ, nearest the bank, and follow its numbered strokes. · check: `lang:ja:find:kana:vowels`

#### <a id="lj2-s1"></a>Scene `lj2-s1`

_Hana has been up since dawn, painting the flat stones in the pond. Marina watches, the way she watches anyone touching water._  
**Marina**: She asked you first. Mostly.  
**Hana** _(happy)_: These are hiragana, one of the ways we write Japanese. There are forty-six basic ones.  
**Hana**: Today we need five: あ い う え お. Say them a, i, u, e, o. Like “ah, ee, oo, eh, oh”.  
**Hana**: They come first in the hiragana chart. Nearly every other hiragana ends in one of those five sounds.  
_She has hidden the five painted stones among plain ones, all the way across. Some face up. Some are sideways._  
**Hana**: Find them in order, あ first. Step on the wrong stone and you have to say “sorry, stone”.  
**Hana**: Then we spell words. うえ (ue) means “up”. いえ (ie) means “house”. あお (ao) means “blue”.  
**Marina** _(think)_: And if somebody falls in?  
**Hana**: Nobody falls in. I’m very fast.  
_On the お stone sits a frog. It is fat, wet and completely uninterested in hiragana._  
**Hana** _(think)_: That’s fine. That is a normal frog and I am being normal about it.  

#### <a id="lj2-s1-done"></a>Done `lj2-s1-done`

_あ, い, う, え, お. You hop across in order. On the お stone, the frog moves exactly one centimetre._  
**Hana**: Now spell うえ. Up!  
_You jump onto う, then え, then up onto the bank. Hana claps._  
**Hana** _(happy)_: いえ next, then あお. And nobody mention the frog.  
**Marina**: Nobody mentioned the frog.  

### Step 2: Trace か き く け こ in the sand by the pond.

Hint: Tap the sand patch. Follow the numbered strokes in order; there is no rush. · check: `lang:ja:trace:ka-row`

#### <a id="lj2-s2"></a>Scene `lj2-s2`

_Dusty has raked a patch of sand flat by the pond. They lean on the rake like it’s an old friend._  
**Dusty**: Smooth as I can get it. There was a spoon under here. And another spoon. I don’t know whose spoons these are.  
**Hana**: Now we put a “k” in front of the five. You get か き く け こ. Ka, ki, ku, ke, ko.  
**Hana**: Every hiragana has its strokes in the same order, every time. Mostly top to bottom, left to right.  
_She traces く with one finger: a single stroke, like a bird’s beak. Then こ: two short lines, one above the other._  
**Hana**: く is one stroke. こ is two. か has a little dash off to one side, like it’s waving.  
**Hana**: Words! いけ (ike) is “pond”, like this one. かお (kao) is “face”. あか (aka) is “red”.  
**Dusty** _(happy)_: So I’ve been raking next to an いけ all morning and didn’t know it.  
_The frog from the お stone has followed you. It sits in the middle of the sand, exactly where く should go._  
**Hana** _(think)_: It’s not in the way. I’ll just write round it. Frogs are fine. It’s fine.  

**Choices**

- **Gently move the frog back to the pond.**
    - You scoop it up in both hands and set it at the water’s edge. Hana breathes out, quietly.
    - **Hana**: I was fine. But ありがとう.
- **Write round the frog.**
    - You draw く in a curve round the frog. It looks like the frog is getting a hug.
    - **Hana**: That’s actually a really good drawing.
- **Ask Hana if she’s scared.**
    - **Hana**: Me? いいえ. No. Not at all.
    - She is standing on one leg, as far from the frog as a person can be while still holding a stick.

#### <a id="lj2-s2-done"></a>Done `lj2-s2-done`

_か き く け こ, in a neat row in the sand. Dusty steps back to admire it and treads on the け._  
**Dusty**: Sorry. ごめんなさい. I’ll rake it.  
**Hana**: Now write かお, and I’ll put a face in it.  
_She draws two eyes in the お and a smile under the か. It looks a lot like Dusty._  
**Dusty** _(shock)_: That’s me, isn’t it.  

### Step 3: Write はな (Hana) on Hana’s sketchbook.

Hint: Open the sketchbook and trace は, then な. You can write across or down. · check: `lang:ja:write:hana`

#### <a id="lj2-s3"></a>Scene `lj2-s3`

_Hana lays her sketchbook open on the grass. The first page is blank, which, for Hana, is extremely rare._  
**Hana** _(happy)_: Last two for today: は (ha) and な (na). Put them together: はな. That’s me. Hana.  
**Hana**: はな also means “flower”. I prefer to think the flowers were named after me.  
**Hana**: There’s another way to write it: 花. That’s kanji. Kanji came from Chinese writing, and each one carries a meaning.  
**Hana**: So Japanese has three scripts. Hiragana, like はな. Kanji, like 花. And katakana, mostly for words borrowed from other languages.  
**Hana**: Like ピンク (pinku). Say it.  
_You say it. It sounds a lot like “pink”._  
**Hana**: Exactly. Japanese borrowed it, and it came out a bit different.  
**Hana**: And は is sneaky. Mostly it says “ha”. But in わたしは it says “wa”, because it’s pointing at who the sentence is about.  
**Hana**: Write はな on my sketchbook. Big. You can go across in a row, or down in a column. Japanese can do both.  

**Choices**

- **Write it across, left to right.**
    - You write は, then な, side by side across the page.
    - **Hana**: Like a name on a door. Good.
- **Write it down, top to bottom.**
    - You write は at the top and な underneath, in a column.
    - **Hana**: Very fancy. When a page is in columns, you read them from right to left.

#### <a id="lj2-s3-done"></a>Done `lj2-s3-done`

_はな, in your best handwriting. The な has a slightly wobbly loop. Hana studies it for a long time._  
**Hana** _(soft)_: That’s the first time anyone here has written my name the way my family writes it.  
**Hana**: I’m keeping this page forever. I won’t even draw on it. Much.  
_The frog hops onto the sketchbook and sits on the は. Hana makes a very small noise that is definitely not a scream._  

### Complete

#### <a id="lj2-complete"></a>Complete `lj2-complete`

_Marina has helped Hana paint the rest of the stones. Twelve kana cross the pond now, and a Nubbin waits on the bank._  
**Hana**: Quick-fire! I call a word, the Nubbin hops on the letters. Go! うえ!  
_う, え. The Nubbin hops twice and points up._  
**Hana**: いえ! いけ!  
_A house, a pond. The Nubbin points at your cottage, then at the pond, very pleased with itself._  
**Hana**: かお! あか! あお!  
_Face, red, blue. The Nubbin pulls a face, goes pink in the cheeks, then sits down, overwhelmed._  
**Hana**: Last one: はな.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: I’d keep the stones. Better than plain ones.  
**Hana** _(happy)_: Hiragana stepping stones! Now anyone who crosses this pond is reading Japanese, whether they like it or not.  

**Rewards**

- Hiragana stepping stones (decor): Twelve stepping stones for the pond, painted あ い う え お, か き く け こ, は and な. Step on them and Hana’s recording says each sound.

## LJ3. Counting Koi

*Original: a beginner Japanese questline for the Learning Grove* · audience: all · where: Learning Grove only · when: After LJ2: Marina arrives at the pond with two buckets of koi. · requires: LJ2

**Cast:** Hana

### Step 1: Count the new koi into the pond in Japanese, from ichi to jū.

Hint: Tap each fish as it swims in, and say the number. Hana calls six to ten first; copy her. · check: `lang:ja:count:fish:10`

#### <a id="lj3-s1"></a>Scene `lj3-s1`

_Marina is at the pond with two big buckets. Something inside flashes orange and white._  
**Marina**: New fish. A friend upriver had too many. Like people with courgettes.  
**Hana** _(happy)_: こい (koi)! They’re koi! I’ve drawn about a thousand of them.  
**Hana**: You say koi in English too. Same word. Japanese lent it to English.  
**Marina**: We count them in, so we know nobody’s missing later. Hana says we’re counting in Japanese.  
**Hana**: Hold up your fingers. いち (ichi), one. に (ni), two. さん (san), three.  
**Hana**: よん (yon), four. ご (go), five. One whole hand.  
_A koi slides out of the bucket and into the pond. Then another. They do not wait for anyone to be ready._  
**Hana** _(shock)_: Quick! いち! に!  
**Marina**: There are ten. Five in this bucket, five in that one.  
**Hana**: After ご, I’ll call the next numbers and you copy me. Don’t blink.  

#### <a id="lj3-s1-done"></a>Done `lj3-s1-done`

**Hana**: After ご came ろく (roku), six. なな (nana), seven. はち (hachi), eight.  
**Hana** _(happy)_: Then きゅう (kyū), nine, and じゅう (jū), ten. Ten koi! Ten fingers! Nobody fell in!  
**Marina**: A little dramatic. But correct.  
**Hana**: That’s counting out loud. In a sentence, fish get their own counting word. Japanese has one for nearly everything.  
**Marina**: And for Hanas?  
**Hana** _(happy)_: There’s only one Hana. No word needed.  
_Ten koi drift in a slow orange circle. Dumpling sits on the bank, counting them with his head._  

### Step 2: Race Hana round the pond, counting the laps in Japanese.

Hint: Open the race from the lap counter. Say each lap number as you pass the post. · check: `lang:ja:minigame:race-count`

#### <a id="lj3-s2"></a>Scene `lj3-s2`

_Sparks arrives at speed, the way Sparks arrives everywhere. Pixel follows with a board on a stick._  
_Byte sets four stopwatches in a row on the grass._  
**Pixel**: He brings four to everything. He brought four to our wedding.  
**Byte** _(think)_: The vows ran long. I have the figures.  
**Sparks** _(happy)_: I HEAR SOMEONE HERE THINKS SHE’S FAST.  
**Hana**: I don’t think I’m fast. I know I’m fast.

## LJ6. Floating Lanterns

*Original: a beginner Japanese questline for the Learning Grove* · audience: all · where: Learning Grove only · when: After LJ5, in late summer: Hana is sitting by the stream, not drawing. · requires: LJ5

**Cast:** Hana

### Step 1: Ask Hana why she is sitting by the stream.

Hint: Hana is sitting by the stream. Tap her and choose Talk. · check: `talk:hana:toro-nagashi`

#### <a id="lj6-s1"></a>Scene `lj6-s1`

_Late summer. The evenings come earlier now. Hana sits by the stream, not drawing, which is how you know something is up._  
**Hana**: At home, it’s おぼん (Obon) this week. It’s a time in summer when families remember the people in their family who have died.  
**Hana**: At Obon, my family floats a paper lantern down the river for ojiichan. It’s called とうろうながし (tōrō nagashi).  
**Hana**: Lots of places do it, often at the end of Obon. Every place does it a bit differently, and some don’t do it at all.  
**Hana**: A river is かわ (kawa). This stream is more of a little かわ. But I think it would work.  
**Hana** _(soft)_: The lantern has a light inside. ひかり (hikari), light. You watch it float away. It’s the quietest thing I know.  
**Hana**: Last year I floated one with obāchan. I wrote ojiichan’s name on it.  
**Hana** _(soft)_: This year I’m here, not at the river. Could I float his lantern on your stream? You can come, if you want.  

**Choices**

    - **Hana**: He taught me to run. He said always race people, because it makes them faster too.

    - **Hana**: ありがとう. That helped.
