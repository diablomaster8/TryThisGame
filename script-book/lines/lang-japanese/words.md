# Hana’s Hiragana: word list and script primer

Japanese uses three scripts: hiragana for Japanese words and grammar (46 basic characters); katakana mostly for borrowed words, like ピンク (pinku); and kanji, characters that came from Chinese and carry meaning, like 木 (tree), 山 (mountain) and 川 (river), which began as pictures. Romanisation is Hepburn with macrons (ō, ū). Say-its write long vowels doubled. Many Japanese words have no strong stress; the capitals are a rough guide only. In こんにちは and こんばんは, and in わたしは, the は is read “wa”. Politeness: friendly forms (おはよう, ありがとう, おやすみ) for family and friends; polite forms (おはようございます, ありがとうございます, おやすみなさい) for grown-ups and people you don’t know well. Counting games use yon, nana and kyū; shi, shichi and ku are the other readings of 4, 7 and 9. Every vocab item needs a native-speaker recording (VocabItem.audio). No TTS. Font: Noto Sans JP or similar. Seven say-its are not in the vetted list (ike, ue, ie, kao, and the culture items ojigi, Obon, tōrō nagashi); they are written from the kana (the culture items evenly, with no capitals) and flagged in each item’s notes for checking against the recordings.
