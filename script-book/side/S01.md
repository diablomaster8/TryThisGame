# Side story S1: The Wardens’ Walk

## S1. The Wardens’ Walk

*Hazel’s Grove original: the Ages of the Grove (story bible, the Four Wardens)* · audience: all · where: The Grove, by the pond lantern · when: Chapter 4, straight after c4-close, once the Long-Grass lantern has flickered out.

**Cast:** Briar, Hush, Cinder, Young Hazel

### Step 1: Walk beside Moss to the stream lantern, at his pace.

Hint: Moss walks very slowly. Stay next to him; if you get ahead, he stops and waits for you to notice. · check: `walk:moss:lantern-stream`

#### <a id="s1-ask"></a>Scene `s1-ask`

_The Keepers drift home with their empty cups. Three lanterns burn; the fourth post, by the long grass, is dark._  
_Moss hasn’t gone back up the hill. He is sitting by your boot, looking at the dark post._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Twenty-two years, that post has been dark.  
**You**: That’s a long time.  
**Moss** _(think)_: Not tonight. Ask me something else. Ask me anything else.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: I told you the tale. A tale is the part that fits in a song. The rest doesn’t fit anywhere.  
**Moss**: At each post there’s one small thing I keep that isn’t in the tale. Four posts. Four small things.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Hazel used to walk round them with me every Lantern Night. Slowly. I would like to do it again.  
**Moss**: Very slowly. I am older than the trees, and the trees are not in a hurry either.  

**Choices**

- **Walk beside him.** — sets `{"s1-pace":"walk"}`
    - You walk, and it takes a long time to cross the grass. Halfway, you realise that is the point.
    - **Moss**: Good. You didn’t sigh. Hazel sighed for the first ten years.
- **Offer to carry him.** — sets `{"s1-pace":"offered"}`
    - **Moss**: Certainly not. I have legs. They are very short and very old, but they are mine.
    - **Moss**: Ask me again another year. I may surprise us both.
- **Ask which post comes first.** — sets `{"s1-pace":"asked"}`
    - **Moss**: Briar’s, by the stream. She hated waiting more than anything, except being sad.
    - **Moss**: She was very good at not being sad. It was most of what she did.

#### <a id="s1-briar"></a>Done `s1-briar`

_The stream lantern hisses softly in the damp. Moss looks at it for a while before he speaks._  
**Moss** _(happy)_: Briar. Joy, the songs call her. That’s a big word. She laughed. That was mostly it.  
**Moss**: She laughed at frogs. At weather. At me, constantly. When she was frightened she laughed loudest of all.  
**Moss**: When it was her time she went up the hill laughing. She said she’d thought of a joke and wanted to tell the view.  
_For a moment the lantern light goes old and gold, and you hear a voice that isn’t his._  
**Briar** _(happy)_: If I’m not down by supper, eat mine. If I’m not down by spring, plant something nice.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Nobody had to plant anything. Something yellow came up where she sat. The first memorial flower.  
**Moss** _(happy)_: Typical. She always got the last word.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: I never found out what the joke was. Some evenings I sit up there and try to guess.  

### Step 2: Play Briar’s game: float a lit leaf-boat from the stream lantern all the way to the pond.

Hint: Tap the water behind the boat to nudge it on. Keep it off the reeds or the candle goes out. Leaves are free. · check: `float:leaf-boat:stream-pond`

#### <a id="s1-boats-ask"></a>Scene `s1-boats-ask`

**Moss** _(happy)_: She had a game. Of course she had a game. Leaf-boats, a candle stub in each, from her post down to mine.  
**Moss**: If yours reached the pond still lit, you had to laugh out loud. Those were the rules. She wrote them.  
**Moss** _(think)_: There were eleven rules. Nine of them were about laughing.  
_He produces a dock leaf from somewhere, folded very precisely, with a stub of candle stood in it._  
**Moss** _(think)_: I have never once got one to the pond. Not in all the years. She said I watched it too hard.  
**Moss**: I have been watching it less hard, on purpose, for a very long time. It hasn’t helped.  

**Choices**

- **Fold yours wide and flat.** — sets `{"s1-boat":"flat"}`
    - **Moss**: Briar’s way. Flat as a pancake and twice as smug.
- **Give yours a sail.** — sets `{"s1-boat":"sail"}`
    - **Moss**: A sail. Nobody has ever thought of a sail.
    - **Moss**: …I don’t know if that’s allowed. She’d have allowed it. She’d have been furious she didn’t think of it.

#### <a id="s1-boats"></a>Done `s1-boats`

_The stream takes the boats as if it has been waiting all year. Moss’s tips over at once, with enormous dignity._  
**Moss** _(think)_: Every year.  
_Yours wobbles past the stepping stones, spins once at the reed bend, and slides out onto the pond. The candle is still lit._  
_Your Nubbin, who has run beside it the whole way along the bank, sits down hard in astonishment._  
**Moss**: Well? Rules are rules.  
_You laugh. It comes out louder than you meant, and it carries right across the water._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: …There. That’s the sound. I haven’t heard it at this post in a very long time.  
_Behind you, Moss’s boat drifts back to the bank upside down, as if it would quite like another go._  
**Moss** _(think)_: I have a theory about Briar’s joke, you know. That my boat never reaches the pond.  
**Moss**: She folded my first one. I’ve used her fold ever since. I’m beginning to suspect the fold.  
_He looks at the upside-down boat with deep suspicion._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Next Lantern Night. And the one after. Somebody ought to.  

### Step 3: Walk with Moss to the lantern by the old wall.

Hint: Hush’s post is on the north side of the Grove, by the tumbled stone wall. Go on a quiet evening. · check: `walk:moss:lantern-wall`

#### <a id="s1-wall-ask"></a>Scene `s1-wall-ask`

_A few evenings later Moss is waiting at the gate. He has clearly been waiting a while, and would like you to know it._  
**Moss**: The old wall next. Hush’s. Feed them first. I’ll wait. I am, you’ll have noticed, good at it.  
_Byte is leaning on the wall with a notebook, being casual in a way that took planning._  
**Byte** _(think)_: Is he talking? Right now? Ask him something only he would know. For verification.  
**Moss**: Tell the pale one it’s the other shoe this time.  
_Byte looks down at both shoes. Byte goes very quiet, and leaves._  
**Moss** _(think)_: Hush was the quiet one. People think quiet is restful. Hush wasn’t resting. Hush was listening.  
**You**: Listening to what?  
**Moss**: Whatever you weren’t saying. Come on. Mind the step.  

#### <a id="s1-hush"></a>Done `s1-hush`

_The lantern by the old wall burns lower than the others, steady and quiet, like a candle in an empty room._  
**Moss** _(sad)_: After the White Year, some of the stilled ones never moved again. They sat where they were set down.  
**Moss**: The rest of us were busy. Mending. Hatching. Getting the valley warm. Hush sat with the still ones.  
**Moss**: Every one. Every day. She didn’t try to wake them. She kept them company, so they weren’t alone in it.  
_Moss stops. When he goes on, his voice is smaller._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: The last of them faded on a winter morning, under this wall, with Hush beside it.  
_The memory comes up slowly, like breath on cold glass._  
**Hush** _(soft)_: That’s the last of them, Moss. Somebody kept them. I think I’d like to be kept now.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: She called grief a kind of keeping. You hold it, so it doesn’t get dropped all over the valley.  
**Moss**: She held it a very long time. Then she put it down. I’ve never thought that was weakness.  

### Step 4: Walk with Moss to his own lantern, by the pond.

Hint: The pond post is Moss’s own. Go at dusk; he likes the light then. · check: `walk:moss:lantern-pond`

#### <a id="s1-pond-ask"></a>Scene `s1-pond-ask`

_Moss is on the hill when you look for him, and by the pond when you stop looking. That seems to be how it works._  
**Moss** _(think)_: Mine. I’ve been putting it off. Not because it’s sad. Because it’s mine, and I talk too much about myself.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Sit. No, closer. There. That’s where she sat.  
**You**: Hazel?  
**Moss**: Hazel. Before she was anybody’s gran. Before she was anybody’s anything.  

**Choices**

- **Sit where she sat.** — sets `{"s1-sat":true}`
    - The grass there is worn, very slightly, in the shape of someone who came back to the same spot for forty years.
    - **Moss**: She’d say you were sitting in it wrong. You aren’t.
- **Ask him why he chose her.** — sets `{"s1-asked-why":true}`
    - **Moss**: Patience. I’m getting there. Rush me and I’ll start from the Spring Age, and we’ll be here till May.

#### <a id="s1-hazel"></a>Done `s1-hazel`

_The pond lantern burns clean and warm. Moss looks at the water, not at you._  
**Moss** _(happy)_: She came here young. Clever, cross, and in a terrible hurry. She wanted the Nubbins to love her by Tuesday.  
**Moss**: They didn’t. So she sat by this pond every day and complained to the nearest thing that would listen. Me.  
**Moss** _(happy)_: I didn’t say a word. All winter. She fidgeted the whole time, and never once asked me to.  
_The light goes old and gold. Snow, in the memory, and a young woman in a coat too big for her, sitting on a frozen bank._  
**Young Hazel** _(sad)_: I don’t know what I’m doing. I don’t suppose you do either.  
**Moss** _(happy)_: And at the first thaw, I looked up at her and said, “You’ll do.”  
**Moss**: She laughed so hard she fell in the pond. It was only ankle-deep. She has never let me forget it.  
**Young Hazel** _(soft)_: You sat with me all winter. Why?  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Because you kept coming back. That’s all it ever takes. Most people don’t.  
_The memory thins. The pond is a pond again, with your face in it, and his._  
**Moss** _(happy)_: Her third rule. Sit down next to them and wait. She thinks she invented it. I’ve never had the heart to say.  

### Step 5: Moss won’t visit the Long-Grass post while it stands empty. He’ll tell you when.

Hint: This story finishes in chapter 9. Carry on with the campaign. · check: `chapter:9:end`

#### <a id="s1-wait"></a>Scene `s1-wait`

_You look across the Grove at the fourth post, by the long grass. Moss doesn’t._  
**Moss** _(sad)_: Not that one. Not while it’s empty.  
_Over the lane, somebody’s children are skipping, chanting in the flat sing-song children keep for old things._  
_“Cinder held them close and tight, / kept them warm one winter night;”_  
_“when the morning came to call, / they did not move at all.”_  
**Moss** _(soft)_: They’ve got the words right. They’ve got her wrong.  
**Moss**: Everybody remembers her winter. Somebody should remember the rest of her. I’ll tell you. When it’s home.  
**You**: When what’s home?  
**Moss** _(think)_: Feed them. Go to bed. Ask me again when I ask you.  

#### <a id="s1-cinder"></a>Done `s1-cinder`

_The evening after you buried the tin. Frost reaches out of the long grass in thin white fingers._  
_Moss is waiting at your door. He is so faint you can see the doorstep through him._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: All four home. I said I’d tell you. You may carry me. I’m told it’s an honour. Don’t make a face.  
_You carry him to the fourth post. He weighs almost nothing, like a bird’s nest with opinions._  
**Moss**: Everyone remembers Cinder in the snow. I’m going to tell you about the day before.  
**Moss**: Hot. The hottest day anyone could remember. The Nubbins lay flat in the long grass like dropped gloves.  
**Moss** _(happy)_: Cinder spent the whole day carrying them into the shade, one at a time. They kept wandering back out.  
_The light goes old and gold, then red at the edges. There is a smell of hot grass, and a voice, fierce and fond._  
**Cinder** _(happy)_: Out of the sun, you silly things. You’ll burn. Come here. Come here. I’ve got you.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: She was never gentle. But she was never once tired of them. Not once, in all the years.  
**Moss** _(sad)_: That night the first snow came. In summer. And it didn’t stop.  
_The memory goes white, and is gone. Frost glitters at the foot of the post._  
**Moss** _(sad)_: Cinder-love, they call it now. A love that holds too tight. It’s fair. It isn’t all of her.  

### Complete

#### <a id="s1-complete"></a>Complete `s1-complete`

**Moss** _(soft)_: There. Now two of us remember her warm. That’s twice as many as yesterday.  
_He nudges something across the frost to your boot: an acorn on a loop of faded string, worn smooth as a pebble._  
**Moss**: Hazel tied that on me, the spring after our first winter. First thing anyone had given me in a very long time.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Wear it. Then something of hers is walking about the Grove, and something of mine.  
_Hanging on the post is a soft hat with a little lantern stitched into the brim, and a note in big loud letters._  
_“SAW YOU TWO DOING THE ROUNDS IN THE DARK, SO I MADE YOU THIS. IT PROBABLY DOESN’T EXPLODE. —SPARKS”_  
_That night you write it all down: four posts, four small things. You call the pages The Tale of the Four._  
_Moss falls asleep on your windowsill before you finish, which he has never done before._  

**Rewards**

- “The Tale of the Four” journal pages (item): Four pages in your journal, in your own hand: Briar’s laugh, Hush’s grief, Moss choosing young Hazel, and Cinder’s last warm day. Readable any time.
- Lantern hat (cosmetic): A soft hat with a small lantern stitched into the brim, made by Sparks. It glows after dark. It probably doesn’t explode.
- Moss’s acorn charm (cosmetic): An acorn on a loop of faded string that young Hazel once tied on Moss. Your Keeper wears it at the neck.
