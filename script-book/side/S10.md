# Side story S10: Fourteen Months

## S10. Fourteen Months

*The Grey Havens in The Lord of the Rings, and Spiritfarer: a farewell that is prepared for, not sprung* · audience: all · where: The cottage step, at dusk · when: Chapter 11, Hazel’s first evening home (after c11-open). Ends in chapter 12, after the final choice in c12-open.

**Cast:** Young Hazel

### Step 1: Wish one: write your Nubbins into Hazel’s hatch register.

Hint: The register is on the cottage bookshelf. Open it and add every Nubbin you’ve hatched. · check: `write:hatch-register:your-nubbins`

#### <a id="s10-step"></a>Scene `s10-step`

_Dusk, her first evening home. Hazel is on the cottage step with her boots off and her feet in a bowl of warm water._  
_She is very pleased about the water. She has a blanket round her shoulders, though the evening is mild._  
**Gran Hazel**: Sit. Not there. There. I like to see the pond.  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: A year, the doctor said. I’ve had fourteen months. I’m going to make him buy me a cake.  
_She says it the way she’d say the weather. Then she watches to see how you take it._  
**Gran Hazel** _(think)_: Don’t be brave at me. I’ve been brave at myself for fourteen months. It’s exhausting.  
**Gran Hazel**: I want three things before I go up the hill. Small ones. I’ve written them down, so don’t argue.  
_A seed packet, from her apron pocket. Three lines on the back, in pencil. The third has a little drawing._  
**Gran Hazel** _(think)_: But first, a favour. Don’t tell them. The valley. Sparks, Fern, any of them.  
**Gran Hazel**: I want them to come round and argue about races and the weather. I don’t want a week of goodbyes.  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: So. Will you keep it? It’s a big thing to ask. I’m asking anyway.

#### <a id="s10-register"></a>Done `s10-register`

_Hazel reads your pages slowly, a finger under each line, the way she reads seed catalogues._  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: Oh, that’s a terrible name. I love it.  
**Gran Hazel**: “Hatched in a hurry”? They all hatch in a hurry, love. Write the weather. You’ll want the weather, later.  
_Under your last line she takes the pencil and writes one more, small and firm._  
_“Keeper from this page on,” it says, and then your name._  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: There. Now it’s in the book. Things in the book stay.  

### Step 2: Wish two: enter any race while Hazel watches from the back row.

Hint: Open Races from the top bar. Any course, any tier. Winning doesn’t matter. · check: `race:enter:any:hazel-watching`

#### <a id="s10-wish2"></a>Scene `s10-wish2`

**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: Number two. One race. I want to sit in the stands with a terrible pie, like a normal person.  
**Gran Hazel**: Forty years, and I never once sat at the back. Always in the gap, or down at the rail, being a nuisance.  
**Sparks** _(shock)_: Hazel’s coming to a race? HAZEL’S coming to a RACE?  
_Sparks has appeared from nowhere, as she does, holding a flag. Nobody knows where she got the flag._  
**Sparks** _(happy)_: I’m saving you a seat. Front row. I’ll fight Ember for it. I’ll lose, but I’ll fight her.  
**Gran Hazel**: Sparks, love. I want to sit at the back.  
**Sparks**: …Back row. I’ll fight Ember for the back row.  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: Bring whichever one wants to go. Fast or slow. I don’t mind about winning. I never did, whatever they tell you.  
**Sparks** _(think)_: She minded. She once threw a hat at a finish line.  
**Gran Hazel**: It was a very small hat.  

**Choices**

- **Bring the fast one. Give her something to shout about.** — sets `{"s10-racer":"fast"}`
    - **Gran Hazel**: Oh, you’re cruel. I’ll be hoarse by the second bend. Good.
- **Bring the slow one. The one who stops to look at things.** — sets `{"s10-racer":"slow"}`
    - **Gran Hazel**: The dawdler? Oh, good. We’ll be there all afternoon. I’ll get a second pie.
    - **Sparks**: Back row’s got the best view of the last place. I’ve always said so. I’ve never said so.

#### <a id="s10-cheer"></a>Done `s10-cheer`

_The usual roar from the stands, and under it, from the back row, one voice that carries over all the others._  
_Sparks, beside her, shouted herself hoarse on the first lap and has been cheering in mime ever since._  
_Win or lose, when you come off the course Hazel is on her feet, holding half a pie, beaming._  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: That one runs for you. Don’t ever spend that.  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: You can spend Shells. You can spend time. You can spend a perfectly good pie, and I have. Never that.  
_She sits down again rather suddenly, and stays sitting while the stands empty. Nobody hurries her._  

### Step 3: Wish three: walk Hazel to the other three lanterns: the stream, the old wall, the long grass.

Hint: Keep to her pace. She stops at each post; stop with her. · check: `walk:lanterns:three:hazel`

#### <a id="s10-wish3"></a>Scene `s10-wish3`

_Dusk. The last line on the seed packet has the drawing: four little posts in a row, and two stick figures._  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: Number three. Walk the four lanterns with me. Once. Slowly. I’ll be slow whether you like it or not.  
_She takes your arm at the pond lantern. Not leaning. Holding._  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: Moss’s post. I’ll tell you something about it, and then you’re not to make a fuss.  
**Gran Hazel**: I came here young. I didn’t know one end of a Nubbin from the other, and I was too proud to ask.  
_For a moment the pond is younger, and so is the light. There is snow on the bank._  
_A young woman sits by the frozen pond in a coat too big for her, with a bucket of feed she doesn’t know how to give._  
**Young Hazel** _(sad)_: I don’t know what I’m doing. I don’t know what any of this is for.  
_A small, old, green-grey thing sits down a little way off. It doesn’t come closer. It doesn’t go away._  
_It sits there the next day, and the next. All that winter it sits beside her, and says nothing at all._  
_At the first thaw she looks down, and it is looking up at her._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: …You’ll do.  
_The light goes back to being now._  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: Rule three. I thought I’d made it up. I’d been on the end of it for a whole winter.

#### <a id="s10-letter"></a>Complete `s10-letter`

_Morning. Your Nubbins are up before you. The gate is stuck. You lift and push._  
_Her lantern is back on the hook by the cottage door. Somebody brought it down at first light._  
_You haven’t lit it yet._  
_On the signpost, under the stone, where the first one was: a letter in your grandmother’s hand._  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: “If you’re reading this, I’ve gone up the hill, and this time I’m staying. Don’t be dramatic about it.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: “Be a little dramatic. I’ve earned a little.”  
**Gran Hazel**: “You kept my three rules, more or less, which is more than I did. Here’s a fourth, because I can’t help myself.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: “Show up. Every morning, same stuck gate, same stubborn chin. That’s all a Grove ever was. The rest is fruit.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: “Tell Fern to prune the trees. Tell Sparks the hair grows back. Tell Byte his shoelaces are untied.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: “Leave fruit at the edge for the ones in the long grass. You won’t always see who eats it. Leave it anyway.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(think)_: “If he came to the gate, you’ll have decided about Corvin by now. Whatever you decided, I’d have done it worse.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(soft)_: “I’m not sorry. I’m sorry it’s now. Both are true. You knew that already.”  
**Gran Hazel** _(happy)_: “Love, Gran. P.S. The gate sticks. You know the rest.”  
_Behind you, someone lifts and pushes the gate: Fern, with a basket. Then Sparks. Then everyone, one by one._  
_They have come to see why you are standing so still. You hold the letter out. You don’t have to say anything._  
_Byte looks down. His shoelaces are untied._  
_He leaves them._
