# Side story S11: The Blight

## S11. The Blight

*Warcraft III: the culling of Stratholme (theme only: a good person about to do a terrible thing to stop a plague)* · audience: all · where: The Grove gate, then Fern’s south orchard next door · when: Chapter 5, any morning after c5-open (once Ember has told her story of Ash), but not the morning S5 opens. Closes before chapter 8 begins.

### Step 1: Go next door and look at the sick plum trees in Fern’s south orchard.

Hint: Go through the gap in the hedge past Fern’s water butt. The sick trees are at the bottom of the slope. · check: `inspect:tree:s11-sick-plums`

#### <a id="s11-fern"></a>Scene `s11-fern`

_Fern is at your gate before breakfast, holding a plum out on her flat palm as if it might bite._  
**Fern** _(sad)_: Don’t touch it. Just look at it. Then tell me I’m imagining it.  
_The skin is freckled black. When she tilts her hand, the plum caves in and pours out a soft grey dust._  
**Fern**: Three trees at the bottom of the south orchard. Fine yesterday. Today they’re… that.  
**Fern** _(think)_: I know. “Keeping the trees alive, badly.” Yes. I said it first. I’ve said it for years.  
_Through the hedge, the orchard hums with wasps drunk on windfalls. The whole slope smells of hot jam._  
**Fern** _(soft)_: Hazel grafted half those trees for me, the year I moved in. She whitewashed every trunk herself, every spring.  
**Fern** _(sad)_: I haven’t told anyone. If I tell someone, it’s real.  

**Choices**

- **Go and look now.** — sets `{"s11-first":"look"}`
    - You go through the hedge. Fern follows two steps behind, as if the trees might hear her coming.
- **Put an arm round her first.** — sets `{"s11-first":"comfort"}`
    - **Fern**: Oh, don’t. I’ll go soggy. I’ve got a whole orchard to be soggy about.
    - She leans on you for exactly one second, then marches off through the hedge.
- **Say you’ll fetch Byte.** — sets `{"s11-first":"byte"}`
    - **Fern**: Byte. Yes. Byte will put it in a chart, and then it’s a problem instead of a tragedy.

#### <a id="s11-ember"></a>Done `s11-ember`

_Three trees at the bottom of the slope._  
_Their leaves have curled into little black fists. Dust lies under them like spilt flour._  
_Someone else has seen. Ember stands at the orchard wall with her arms folded so tight her knuckles are white._  
**Ember** _(angry)_: Burn it. All of it. Tonight, before it spreads.  
**Fern** _(shock)_: All of it? Ember, there are forty trees.  
**Ember** _(angry)_: There’ll be none if you wait. I know how waiting goes. You’re polite, you wait, and then it’s too late.  
_Clover has come up behind you with a basket of bandages, which is what Clover brings to everything._  
**Clover** _(sad)_: You can’t burn forty trees for three. You don’t even know what it is yet.  
**Ember** _(angry)_: I didn’t know what he was either, till he had Ash. Ask me how that went.  
_Fern sits down on an upturned crate between them, and doesn’t say anything at all._  
**Clover** _(soft)_: That’s cinder-love, that is. Holding on so tight you’d burn the lot. Don’t tell her I said so.  

### Step 2: Map the orchard with Byte’s hand lens: check every tree and label it.

Hint: Look at a leaf, the bark and the fruit of each tree under the lens, then pick a label. Note the wind as you go. · check: `minigame:clear:s11-orchard-map`

#### <a id="s11-lens"></a>Scene `s11-lens`

_Byte arrives at a jog with a hand lens on a string, a roll of luggage labels and a pencil behind each ear._  
**Byte** _(think)_: Nobody burns anything until it’s been looked at. That isn’t a rule. That’s just being a person.  
**Ember** _(angry)_: You’ve got till dusk. Then I’m doing it, with or without your little labels.  
**Byte** _(angry)_: They’re standard labels. They’re not little. They’re regular.  
_He ties the first label to the first sick tree. In capitals: NOT FINE._  
_The next gets PROBABLY FINE. The next gets FINE (ACCORDING TO FERN). The water butt gets DO NOT DRINK._  
**Byte**: Forty trees, one lens. You look, I write. Clover, stop bandaging that branch. It isn’t injured. It’s a branch.  
**Clover** _(soft)_: It looked lonely.  
**Byte** _(think)_: Leaves for spots, bark for holes, fruit for dust. And don’t lick anything. I have to say that. I’ve met people.  
_On his way past the wall he labels Ember, very small and very fast: VOLATILE. She doesn’t notice. Yet._  

#### <a id="s11-map"></a>Done `s11-map`

_By mid-afternoon the orchard flutters with labels like a washing line. You spread the map on Fern’s crate._  
_Three trees NOT FINE, in a crooked row at the bottom. Six INCONCLUSIVE, SMELLS. The rest are fine._  
**Byte** _(think)_: It isn’t random. Look. Every sick one is in the line of the wind that comes up off the falls.  
**Byte**: A thing you catch by touching goes tree to tree. This one skipped trees. It came through the air.  
**You**: So what’s in the air?  
**Byte** _(happy)_: Good question. I have a jar.  

### Step 3: Catch one of whatever is riding the wind into the bottom row.

Hint: Keep your shadow behind you. Whatever it is drops off the leaf the moment a shadow touches it. · check: `catch:bug:s11-carrier`

#### <a id="s11-jar"></a>Scene `s11-jar`

_Byte hands you a jam jar with a muslin lid. Clover hands you a second one, in case the first gets lonely._  
**Byte**: Something is landing on the sick side. If we catch it, we can stop guessing.  
_You crouch in the long grass by the bottom row._  
_The sun is hot on your neck. Dust lifts off the black plums like smoke._  
_Something small and grey lands on a leaf. The moment your shadow touches the leaf, it folds its legs and drops._  
**Clover** _(soft)_: Keep the sun in your face, so your shadow’s behind you. Shy things hide from shadows. I would.  
**Ember** _(angry)_: Or we could burn the lot and not crawl about in the grass.  
_She says it, but she doesn’t leave. She stands at the wall and watches every catch you miss._  

#### <a id="s11-beetle"></a>Done `s11-beetle`

_In the jar: a beetle no bigger than a lentil, grey as pencil dust, wings folded like a paper fan._  
**Byte** _(shock)_: Under the lens it’s covered in spores. Black ones. It isn’t the disease. It’s the postman.  
_Byte follows the wind upslope with his pencil, past the three sick trees, to a dead wild plum in the hedge by the stream._  
_The dead tree is grey and crawling with them. Each breath of wind off the falls lifts a few and carries them down the row._  
**Byte** _(happy)_: They can’t fly into the wind. Only the trees downwind of that stump get them. The rest are safe. It can be contained.  
**Clover** _(happy)_: Ladybirds eat beetle eggs. I’ve a jar of them at home. They’re very rude about it.  
**Clover** _(shock)_: That one’s clean. Right in the middle of the sick row. Look.  
**Fern** _(think)_: Grudge. Hazel’s old damson. Sourest fruit in the valley. I’ve been meaning to cut it down for years.  
_Byte ties a new label to the jar. It says: GUILTY._  

### Step 4: Help Clover let her ladybirds go in the hedge where the beetles breed.

Hint: Open Clover’s jar by the dead wild plum, or where it stood, then step back and keep still. · check: `release:ladybirds:s11-orchard`

#### <a id="s11-dusk"></a>Scene `s11-dusk`

_The sun goes down behind the western hill. Bats come out over the stream._  
_Then a light comes through the hedge, low and orange._  
_Ember, with a pitch torch. In the firelight her face isn’t angry. It is very, very frightened._  
**Ember** _(sad)_: I’m sorry, Fern. I’m not waiting. I waited once.  
**Fern** _(sad)_: Ember…  
**Ember** _(sad)_: When Ash went, I kept thinking: tomorrow. I’ll go and get her back tomorrow. Then tomorrow was years.  
**Ember** _(angry)_: Fast is the only thing I’ve got. Don’t take it off me.  
_Byte holds the map. Clover holds Fern’s hand. Everybody looks at you._  

**Choices**

- **Stand between her and the trees.** — sets `{"s11-dusk":"stood"}`
    - You step in front of the first healthy tree. The torch is close enough to warm your face.
    - **Ember**: Move.
    - **You**: No.
    - For a long moment nobody breathes. Then the torch drops, slowly, until it points at the grass.
    - **Ember**: You’d better be right. You’d better be right, or I’ll never forgive you.
    - She doesn’t leave. She helps Fern fell the three sick trees and the dead stump by hand, in the dark, without a word.
- **Hand her the map. Let the evidence speak.** — sets `{"s11-dusk":"map"}`
    - You hold out Byte’s map. She doesn’t want to take it. She takes it.
    - She reads it by her own torchlight: the line of the wind, the dead plum, the jar marked GUILTY.
    - **Ember**: Three. Not forty.
    - **Ember**: Three.
    - She pushes the torch into the water butt. It goes out with a long hiss, like a held breath.
    - **Ember**: Show me the stump. I’ll do the stump.
- **Take the torch with her. Burn only the three sick trees, together.** — sets `{"s11-dusk":"together"}`
    - You put your hand on the torch, next to hers. She looks at you. Then she lets you hold it too.
    - You burn the three sick trees and the dead wild plum, and nothing else.
    - It takes a long time. Neither of you lets go.
    - **Clover**: Goodbye, trees. You were very good plums.
    - **Ember**: That’s how it should’ve gone. With Ash. Not on my own. Not in a rush.
    - Fern brings out tea. Ember drinks hers standing up, watching the fire, until it’s only warm.

#### <a id="s11-ladybirds"></a>Done `s11-ladybirds`

_Clover unscrews her jar. Out pour ladybirds, hundreds of them, red as rosehips, and settle along the hedge._  
**Clover** _(happy)_: Off you go. Every egg you can find. Be as rude as you like.  
_One lands on your thumb and won’t get off. Then three more. After a while they are, more or less, yours._  
**Byte** _(think)_: I tried to label them. It isn’t possible. They’re all called LADYBIRD now.  

### Complete

#### <a id="s11-complete"></a>Complete `s11-complete`

_A week later the orchard is quiet. Three black stumps at the bottom of the slope; thirty-seven green trees above them._  
_In the middle of the bare row one tree stands untouched: Grudge, the old damson, heavy with sour purple fruit._  
**Fern** _(happy)_: Byte says the beetles hate the bark. Too bitter. Hazel used to say Grudge was too stubborn to die.  
**Fern** _(soft)_: She always told me to graft off it. I never did. Who wants sour plums? Here. She left this at mine.  
_A short curved knife with a plum-wood handle wound in green twine. The edge is still good._  
**Fern**: Bind a cutting of Grudge to a young tree, and the young tree grows up stubborn too. Her trick. Yours now.  
**Fern** _(happy)_: You saved my orchard. That makes you its warden, whether you like it or not.  
_Ember comes past the wall. She has found Byte’s label in her pocket. She reads VOLATILE, and looks at him._  
_Byte takes it, crosses it out, and writes something else: FRIGHTENED. Then, underneath: (FAIR)._  
**Ember** _(soft)_: …Fair.  

**Rewards**

- Hazel’s grafting knife (feature): Take a cutting from Grudge, the old damson in Fern’s orchard, and graft it onto a young fruit tree. Grafted trees never catch the blight.
- Orchard Warden (title): Given by Fern for saving her orchard. Shown under your name.
- Ladybird petpets (cosmetic): A small, rude cloud of Clover’s ladybirds that rides on your Keeper’s hat and shoulders. They are all called LADYBIRD.
