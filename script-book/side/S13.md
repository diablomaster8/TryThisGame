# Side story S13: The Mill Cellar

## S13. The Mill Cellar

*Diablo: the descent through the cellars under Tristram’s cathedral, the hero who carries the evil inside himself to keep it from others, and the old storyteller who knows the lore (themes only)* · audience: all · where: The old mill below the falls · when: From chapter 7 (autumn). Levels 1–3 any time from then; the bottom level only after chapter 8’s notebook. Closes before chapter 12.

**Cast:** Old Tobin (Diablo’s old storyteller who knows the lore (theme only; an original character)), Aldous Vale

### Step 1: Get past the rats in the grain store and find the hatch to the next level.

Hint: Feed the rats or creep past them along the wall. The hatch is under the sacks; the old hoist will lift them. · check: `explore:mill-cellar:level-1`

#### <a id="s13-tobin"></a>Scene `s13-tobin`

_Autumn. Below the falls the river runs brown and loud, and the old mill leans over it like a man trying to hear something in the water._  
_Its wheel hasn’t turned in twenty years. Moss sits on the old millstone by the door, the only thing here he approves of._  
_A very old man in a lamplighter’s coat is unlocking the door with a key the size of a ladle._  
**Old Tobin**: Hazel’s grandchild. Thought so. You’ve got her chin, and her habit of standing exactly where I’m about to swing a door.  
**Old Tobin** _(happy)_: Tobin. I lit the lamps in this valley for fifty-one years. Every lane, every bridge, every dusk.  
**Old Tobin**: Then they put in the new ones that light themselves, and I was free to tell stories full time. Nobody’s thanked them for that.  
**Old Tobin** _(think)_: This was Aldous Vale’s mill. Corvin Vale’s father. Yes, that Corvin. Him with the cold garden.  
**Old Tobin**: There’s a cellar under it. Four levels, going down. I’ve kept it shut twenty years.  
**Old Tobin**: The river’s getting in now. By spring it’ll have the lot. Somebody ought to see what’s down there before the river decides.  
**Old Tobin** _(happy)_: There was a winter, this would be the year of the Three Hats, when the wheel ran so hard the floor smoked, and—  
_He stops. He looks at the door. He looks at you._  
**Old Tobin** _(think)_: Where was I? Cellar. Yes. Dark.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: He’s been telling that one since before you were born. He has never once reached the end.  
**Old Tobin**: I heard that, Moss. I reach the end all the time. I just don’t bring anyone with me.  

#### <a id="s13-level1"></a>Done `s13-level1`

_Level one: the grain store. Sacks to the ceiling, gone hard as stone. The air tastes of old flour and mouse._  
_Something scrabbles in the dark. Then a great many somethings._  
**Old Tobin** _(think)_: Rats. Mill rats. Very organised. Aldous used to say they were the only staff who never asked for a day off.  

**Choices**

- **Roll them some fruit, and walk past while they eat.** — sets `{"s13-rats":"fed"}`
    - You roll a plum into the dark. Forty small faces turn to it at once, like a choir to a conductor.
    - You walk straight past. Nobody so much as looks up.
    - **Old Tobin**: Bribery. Your grandmother’s method. Works on rats and parish councils.
- **Shade your lantern and creep along the wall.** — sets `{"s13-rats":"crept"}`
    - You go along the wall in the dark, one step at a time. A rat sits on your boot for a while, and decides you are furniture.
    - Behind you, Tobin creeps too, whispering a story about a much bigger rat, very loudly.

### Step 2: Drain the flooded sluice room by opening its three gates in the right order.

Hint: Water runs downhill. Open the lowest gate first, then the middle, then the top, or the room fills again. · check: `explore:mill-cellar:level-2`

#### <a id="s13-sluice"></a>Scene `s13-sluice`

_The hoist lifts the sacks with a shriek. Under them: a hatch, and three things nobody has touched in twenty years._  
**Old Tobin** _(happy)_: Loot. That’s the proper word for it. I had it off a pedlar.  
**Old Tobin**: A ladle. Plain, that. Look at it. The Gently Used Ladle of Second Thoughts.  
**Old Tobin**: A sock. Also Plain. The Sock of Uncertain Origin. Don’t ask it where it’s been.  
**Old Tobin** _(happy)_: And the cheese rind the rats were guarding. Oh, that’s Fine, that is. The Rat-Nibbled Cheese Rind of Courage.  
**You**: Does everything get a name?  
**Old Tobin**: Everything found in a cellar gets a name. It’s the rule. I made it up just now, and it’s very old.  
_Below the hatch you can hear water. Not running. Just lying there in the dark, breathing._  
**Old Tobin** _(think)_: Level two’s the sluice room. Flooded since Aldous went. Three gates. Get them in the wrong order and you’ll be swimming.  
**Old Tobin**: Marina could swim it. Marina could swim a soup.  

#### <a id="s13-level2"></a>Done `s13-level2`

_The last gate groans open. The water goes out of the room with a long sigh, like a bath let out after twenty years._  
_On the wet floor: two eels, very annoyed. And up one wall, a row of chalk marks, measuring a boy._  
_The marks stop at about the height of your shoulder. Beside the last one, in a square adult hand: FIT FOR THE SLUICE._  
**Old Tobin**: …  
**Old Tobin** _(sad)_: Where was I.  
_He doesn’t say the rest. He goes on down the steps ahead of you, and doesn’t look at the wall._  

### Step 3: Find what is jamming the great wheel, and get past it to the bottom stair.

Hint: Watch which gear shudders when the river pushes. Never stand between the spokes. · check: `explore:mill-cellar:level-3`

#### <a id="s13-wheelpit"></a>Scene `s13-wheelpit`

_Drying on the steps, where the flood left them: a pair of boots, a doorknob with no door, a scarf like pond weed._  
**Old Tobin** _(think)_: Squeaky Boots of Announcing Yourself. Fine. The Damp Doorknob of Not Quite Opening. Also Fine.  
**Old Tobin** _(happy)_: And that’s Rare. The Soggy Scarf of Lingering Optimism. Wring it out all you like. It still thinks it’ll dry.  
_Level three is the wheel pit. The great wheel sits half in the cellar and half in the river, dripping._  
_It doesn’t turn. Each time the river leans on it the whole mill shudders, and dust comes down like flour._  
_Up through the floorboards you hear Moss call down that he is staying on his millstone, thank you, being far too old for underneath._  
**Moss**: Mind the third stair. It has opinions.  
**Old Tobin** _(think)_: It was a perfectly good stair until you said that.  
**Old Tobin**: Mind the wheel. It’s stuck, not dead. There’s a difference, and the difference is what takes your fingers off.  
**Old Tobin**: The stair to the bottom’s past the gears. Find what’s jamming them, and step round it.  

#### <a id="s13-level3"></a>Done `s13-level3`

_In the biggest gear, driven in hard, is a wooden wedge. Not something that fell. Something put there._  
_A tag hangs from it on a bit of string, in the same square hand: STOPPED. LEAVE IT._  
**You**: Somebody wedged it on purpose.  
**Old Tobin** _(sad)_: Aldous. His last winter. They found him sat on that step, there. First time anyone ever saw him sit.  
_Past the wheel, a narrow stair goes down to an iron door. It is locked. The lock has been oiled._  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: Not that one. Not yet.  
**Old Tobin**: There’s a thing you ought to know first, about Aldous’s boy and your grandmother. It’s hers to tell, not mine.  
**Old Tobin**: She wrote most things down. When you’ve read it, come back, and I’ll find the key. I know exactly where it is. That’s the trouble.  

### Step 4: Unlock the iron door and go down to the bottom of the cellar with Tobin.

Hint: Use Tobin’s key on the iron door past the wheel pit. Take a lantern; there’s no light below. · check: `explore:mill-cellar:level-4`

#### <a id="s13-key"></a>Scene `s13-key`

_After the Hollow, with Hazel’s notebook still in your coat, you go back down to the mill. Tobin is on the step, waiting._  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: You know now. About Corvin and her. I can see it on you. You’ve the look of someone who read the last page first.  
**Old Tobin**: He was her apprentice. Before that, he was this mill’s boy.  
**Old Tobin** _(sad)_: I lit the lamp at that gate every dusk for thirty years. There was always a boy at the window. Working.  
**Old Tobin** _(sad)_: That’s the whole story. Short, isn’t it. It’s the only one I’ve ever finished.  
_He holds out a small iron key on a bootlace. It is warm from his pocket._  
**Old Tobin**: I named the last of the stair loot while you were away. Couldn’t help it. A cog: the Cog of Tremendous Patience. Rare.  
**Old Tobin**: My old lamp hook, which I lent Aldous one winter and never got back. The Lamplighter’s Hook of Talking Too Long. Storied, that.  
**Old Tobin**: And a little hat cut from a broken grindstone. Somebody meant to hand that down, so it’s Heirloom. Millstone Hat of Considerable Weight.  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: Down we go. I’ll come with you. I’ve never been in. I’ve only ever kept it shut.  

#### <a id="s13-desk"></a>Done `s13-desk`

_The bottom room is small, dry and very cold._  
_A desk. A chair pushed in square. A lamp with the wick trimmed._  
_On the hook behind the door: a miller’s apron, still floury, and a pocket watch, stopped at four minutes past five._  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: Five in the morning. That’s when the wheel started, every day of his life.  
_On the desk, a ledger. Inside the cover, in chalk, three rules._  
_ONE. THE WHEEL DOES NOT STOP._  
_TWO. NOTHING IDLES._  
_THREE. WHAT CANNOT KEEP UP IS LET GO._  
_Under them, copied out again in a child’s careful hand, ruled in pencil so the letters stay straight: the same three rules._  
_You turn the pages. Sacks, hours, men, wheel speed._  
_Every year the hours go up. The word “rest” never appears._  
_Near the back: “Boy, 12, put on the night shift. New method: the wheel never stops. Output up a third.”_  
_Folded inside the back cover is a letter. Addressed. Never sent. Corvin._  
**Old Tobin** _(sad)_: I found it the day they found him. I read it. I shouldn’t have. Then I shut the door on it, so nobody else had to carry it.  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: Twenty years. It got heavy, keeping it from people. I didn’t know it would.  

### Step 5: Do what you decided with Aldous’s letter.

Hint: Keep it safe in your bag, burn it in the mill’s old stove, or take it to the grave under the alders by the race. · check: `resolve:item:mill-letter`

#### <a id="s13-letter"></a>Scene `s13-letter`

_The paper is good paper, the kind you’d buy for something that mattered. You unfold it._  
**Aldous Vale**: Corvin.  
**Aldous Vale**: I have started this nine times. The other eight went in the stove. You’d say that was waste. You’d be right. I taught you that.  
**Aldous Vale**: They say in the valley you did a wrong thing at the old woman’s Grove, and went in the night. I don’t know what. I know how.  
**Aldous Vale**: Quickly. In the dark. Without once sitting down to think. I know whose hands those were. They were mine first.  
_…_  
**Aldous Vale**: I can tell you the weight of every sack this mill ground the year you were born. I can’t tell you what you laughed at.  
**Aldous Vale**: Your mother used to sit by the race of an evening with her feet in the water. I called it idling.  
**Aldous Vale**: I would give the mill to sit there once with her now.  
**Aldous Vale**: I won’t say sorry. I don’t know how, and you’d hear it as one more order. You always heard me that way. I made sure of it.  
_…_  
**Aldous Vale**: I’ve stopped the wheel. It’s very quiet. I think you’d hate it. I think you’d get used to it.  
**Aldous Vale**: If you ever stop, stop here. You could sit as long as you liked. Nobody would count.  
**Aldous Vale**: Your father.  
_…_  
**Old Tobin** _(sad)_: “Nobody would count.” He counted everything. His own boy’s hours, in a book. And then that.  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: It isn’t mine to decide. It never was. I only held the door shut.  

**Choices**

- **Keep it for Corvin. Give it to him when he can feel again.** — sets `{"millLetter":"kept"}`
    - You fold it along its old folds and put it inside your coat, next to Hazel’s notebook.
    - **Old Tobin**: When he can feel again. Yes. Cold hands can’t hold a thing like that. It would go straight through them.
    - **Old Tobin**: You’ll know when. Your grandmother always knew when. It was infuriating.
- **Burn it. Some things should end with the person.** — sets `{"millLetter":"burned"}`
    - **Old Tobin**: …That’s a real answer. I’ve thought it myself, most winters.
    - **Old Tobin**: He wrote nine and burned eight. You’d only be finishing what he started.
- **Read it to Moss, and leave it on Aldous’s grave.** — sets `{"millLetter":"grave"}`
    - **Old Tobin**: Read to the old one, and left with the man. Yes.
    - **Old Tobin**: Nobody ever read Aldous anything. He hadn’t the time to listen. He has now.

**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: Hazel’s grandchild. Twice in one evening. People will talk.  
_You hold out the letter. He sees the writing on the front before he takes it, and doesn’t take it._

_He takes it. His fingers are clumsy with the cold coming out of them. It takes him three goes to unfold it._  
_He reads it by the last of the light. Then again, slower._  
_You look at the hedge, so he doesn’t have to be looked at._  
_…_  
**Corvin Vale** _(soft)_: “Nobody would count.”

_He goes on down the lane. You walk back up to the cottage, where Hazel is still on the step._

### Complete

#### <a id="s13-complete"></a>Complete `s13-complete`

_A week later Tobin brings the apron and the stopped watch up from the bottom room, and gives them to you._  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: Heirlooms. Somebody meant to hand them down. He just never stopped long enough to do it.  
_Then he knocks the wedge out of the great gear with a mallet and a short speech._  
_The wheel turns. Slowly. Not Aldous’s speed: the river’s._  
_Water drips off each paddle as it comes round, taking its time._  
**Old Tobin** _(happy)_: There. It goes as fast as the river feels like, and not a turn faster. Nobody’s counting.  
**Old Tobin**: Bring your seeds down. It grinds a lovely flour. Nubbins go mad for a flour treat. Don’t give them six, like I did.  
**Old Tobin** _(soft)_: And come and sit, when you like. I’ve a story about the year of the Three Hats. I’ll get to the end of it one day.  
**Old Tobin** _(think)_: Where was I?  
**You**: Mill. Yes. Light.  
_Tobin looks at you for a long moment. Then he laughs until he has to sit down on the millstone._

**Notes:** THEME. We carry what our parents carried until someone sets it down. Tobin kept the cellar shut twenty years “so nobody else had to carry it”; Aldous’s three mill rules (in s13-desk) are the dark mirror of Hazel’s three rules, and the boy copied them out. That is where Corvin learned “the method”. The real question: is a letter like this a gift or a weight, and who gets to decide? All three answers are defensible. RUNNING GAG.
