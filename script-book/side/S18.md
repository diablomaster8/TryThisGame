# Side story S18: The Grove Museum

## S18. The Grove Museum

*Inspired by Animal Crossing’s museum and its owl curator who fears bugs, and Stardew Valley’s community centre (themes only)* · audience: all · where: Hazel’s old barn · when: Chapter 6, the day after c6-close (after Fossil’s farewell). Closes in chapter 11.

**Cast:** Professor Delve

### Step 1: Dig three fossils from the cliff layers with Dusty, and sort them oldest to youngest.

Hint: Lower layers were laid down first, so they are older. Note how deep each find was, then sort them. · check: `minigame:dig:s18-cliff-layers`

#### <a id="s18-barn"></a>Scene `s18-barn`

_Hazel’s old barn smells of hay, apples and long-ago rain. Swallows’ nests line the rafters like little clay cups._  
_In the middle of the floor stands a mole in a tweed waistcoat, beside a wheelbarrow full of empty glass cases._  
**Professor Delve** _(happy)_: Ah. The grandchild. Professor Delve, archivist. I wrote to your grandmother about a museum. She said yes.  
**Professor Delve**: In principle. Eleven years ago. Moles are patient. I have come to collect the principle.  
**Professor Delve**: Four wings. Three exhibits each. Fossils, night creatures, shore life, the heavens. Twelve. A tidy number.  
**Professor Delve** _(think)_: Every exhibit gets a plaque, and every plaque must be true. Not nearly true. Not true-ish. True.  
_A small brown moth drifts down from the rafters and lands on the end of his nose._  
**Professor Delve** _(shock)_: Oh. Oh dear. Oh, no, not one of—  
_Professor Delve faints, quite neatly, into the wheelbarrow._  
**Professor Delve** _(sad)_: I’m perfectly all right. I’m simply horizontal.  
_A muddy figure in the doorway: Dusty, two days after the rain on the hill, holding a trowel with nothing to use it on._  
**Dusty** _(soft)_: I heard “fossils”. From the lane. My hands want something to do. Can they do this?  

#### <a id="s18-fossils"></a>Done `s18-fossils`

_The cliff is striped like a cake: sand, then red rock, then grey shale, then a dark band just above the beach._  
**Dusty** _(think)_: Deeper’s older. Rock goes down in layers, like blankets on a bed. Nobody puts the bottom blanket on last.  
_A coiled shell near the top. A fern pressed flat in the red rock._  
_And deep in the dark band, a little armoured bug._  
**Dusty** _(happy)_: Fossil used to sit right there while I dug. Supervising. She was terrible at it. She slept.  
**Dusty** _(soft)_: …It’s quiet without the snoring.  
_Back at the barn, Professor Delve reads each plaque aloud before he’ll let it go on the wall._  
**Professor Delve**: “Trilobites were sea animals with jointed shells, relatives of insects and crabs. They lived for over 250 million years.”  
**Professor Delve**: “Ferns have no flowers and no seeds. They spread by spores, which usually form under their leaves.”  
**Professor Delve**: “Ammonites were sea animals related to squid and octopus. They died out about 66 million years ago.”  
**Professor Delve** _(happy)_: True, true and true. Wing one. Somebody check my nose for moths.  

### Step 2: Run the moth trap in Luna’s garden and wait quietly for three night finds.

Hint: Sit still near the sheet: every time you move, finds come slower. At dawn, look under the ash tree. · check: `minigame:moth-trap:s18-luna-garden`

#### <a id="s18-night"></a>Scene `s18-night`

_Luna hangs a white sheet between two apple trees and sets a lantern under it. The sheet glows like a sail at sea._  
**Luna** _(soft)_: A moth trap. Nothing gets hurt. They land on the sheet, we look, we draw, and at dawn they go home.  
**Luna**: The museum gets the drawings. Living things aren’t exhibits. They’re neighbours.  
**Professor Delve** _(think)_: I shall supervise from a safe distance. Of about a mile.  
_He sits under a blanket at the far end of the garden, holding a tin of smelling salts like a cup of tea._  
**Luna** _(soft)_: Now the hard part. We wait, and we don’t talk.  
**Luna** _(happy)_: …That was me talking. Starting now.  

#### <a id="s18-night-finds"></a>Done `s18-night-finds`

_Near midnight a moth lands on the sheet, green and black like lichen on old bark. You lose it twice while looking at it._  
**Luna** _(happy)_: Merveille du Jour. “Marvel of the day”, and it flies by night. Somebody had a sense of humour.  
_From the far end of the garden comes a soft thump. Professor Delve has fainted from a mile away, on principle._  
_By dawn you have a drawing of the moth, a spider’s web lifted whole onto black card, and an owl pellet from under the ash._  
**Professor Delve**: “The Merveille du Jour is a moth that flies in autumn. Its green and black wings hide it on lichen-covered bark.”  
**Professor Delve**: “Owls swallow small prey whole. The fur and bones they can’t digest are pressed into a pellet and coughed up.”  
**Professor Delve**: “Garden spiders spin webs from silk made in their own bodies. They often eat the old web before spinning a new one.”  
**Professor Delve** _(think)_: Recycling. Admirable. Somebody turn the moth drawing to face the wall whenever I walk past.  

### Step 3: Find three shore creatures in Marina’s rock pools before the tide turns.

Hint: Look under the weed and the flat stones, and put each stone back the way you found it. When the water reaches the barnacles, the tide is turning. · check: `minigame:rock-pool:s18-low-tide`

#### <a id="s18-shore"></a>Scene `s18-shore`

_Low tide past the ridge. Marina’s rock pools are full of sky, and the weed smells of salt and pennies._  
**Marina** _(think)_: Rules. Turn a stone, turn it back the same way up. That’s somebody’s roof.  
**Marina**: Living things go back before the tide turns. The museum gets drawings, and whatever they’ve finished with.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: And the tide comes back whether you’re ready or not. It’s very rude like that.  
**Professor Delve**: I have brought a bucket, a net and a deep respect for things with pincers.  
_A small crab sidles past the professor’s boot. He doesn’t faint. He looks extremely pleased about it._  
**Professor Delve** _(happy)_: Not a moth. A good day.  

#### <a id="s18-shore-finds"></a>Done `s18-shore-finds`

_A sea star the colour of a ripe apricot, one arm short and growing back. A limpet, clamped in its own worn hollow._  
_And a hermit crab halfway between houses, hurrying out of a cramped winkle shell into a roomy whelk._  
**Professor Delve**: Drafts for the plaques. “A sea star can regrow an arm it has lost. Many have a simple eye at the tip of each arm.”  
**Professor Delve**: “A limpet grazes on the rock when the tide is in. When the tide goes out, it returns to its own spot, its home scar.”  
**Professor Delve**: “A hermit crab keeps its soft body inside an empty snail shell. When it outgrows one, it moves to a bigger one.”  
**Marina** _(think)_: Look. It’s left the old shell behind. Perfect, that. Somebody else will want it. A smaller crab.  
**Professor Delve** _(think)_: Or a museum. A museum would want it very much. I am only saying.  

**Choices**

- **Take the empty shell for the museum.** — sets `{"s18-shell":"taken"}`
    - **Marina**: Fair. There are plenty of shells. There’s only one museum.
    - **Professor Delve**: It shall have a velvet cushion. A small one. The crab would want it small.
- **Leave the shell for the next crab.** — sets `{"s18-shell":"left"}`
    - **Marina**: Good. That’s how pools work. Somebody’s old house is somebody’s new one.
    - **Professor Delve**: Then we shall have a drawing. Drawings never need a bigger house.

### Step 4: On a clear night on the hill, find the Pole Star with Nova, then wait up for the Moon.

Hint: Find the seven stars of the Plough. The two at the end of the pan point towards the Pole Star. The Moon rises late, in the east. · check: `minigame:stargaze:s18-hill`

#### <a id="s18-stars"></a>Scene `s18-stars`

_Nova is on the western hill at nightfall, flat on her back in the cold grass beside the memorial flowers._  
**Nova**: Clear night. No moon till late. Best sky in the valley. I’ve slept under most of them, so I’d know.  
**Nova** _(think)_: I brought you something for the museum. I don’t give people things. It’s a rock. Don’t make it a moment.  
_She drops a stone the size of a walnut into your palm: black, heavy for its size, with a crust like burnt toast._  
**Nova**: I watched it come down over the moor, years back. Walked two days to find it. Carried it ever since.  
**Professor Delve** _(happy)_: A meteorite! Oh, that is wonderful. And it isn’t a moth. A very good day.  
**Nova**: Now. Find me north. Then you can have the other two exhibits as well.  

#### <a id="s18-star-finds"></a>Done `s18-star-finds`

_The Pole Star isn’t the brightest. It’s just the one that stays put while the whole sky wheels slowly round it._  
**Nova** _(soft)_: That one got me home more times than I’ve told anyone. Which is none.  
_Near midnight the Moon comes up over the ridge, low and orange, and goes white as it climbs._  
**Nova** _(soft)_: Same face. Always the same face. Hazel looked at that face. So did whoever was here before her.  
**Professor Delve**: “Polaris, the Pole Star, sits almost directly above Earth’s North Pole, so it hardly moves across the night sky.”  
**Professor Delve**: “The Moon always shows Earth the same face. Its pull, together with the Sun’s, raises the tides.”  
**Professor Delve** _(happy)_: “A shooting star isn’t a star. It is space rock or dust burning up in Earth’s air. A piece that lands is a meteorite.”  
**Nova** _(happy)_: You read plaques to the sky, Professor.  
**Professor Delve** _(soft)_: The sky has never once corrected me. I find that very restful.  

### Step 5: Fill the centre case, then talk to Professor Delve at the barn door.

Hint: Place the centre-case exhibit in the middle of the barn, then speak to the professor by the door. · check: `place:decor:s18-centre-case`

#### <a id="s18-heart"></a>Scene `s18-heart`

_Twelve cases full, and one more. The big one in the middle of the barn, under the empty swallows’ nests, is bare._  
**Professor Delve** _(think)_: The centre case. Every museum has one. It holds the thing the rest of the museum is for.  
_Dusty comes in out of the cold, turning something over in their hands: a card, bent at one corner._  
**Dusty** _(soft)_: Fossil’s card. From the hill. The rain got at it, so I made a new one for the flower and kept this.  
**Dusty** _(sad)_: I don’t want it in a drawer. I don’t want it rained on. I want it somewhere people come.  
**Professor Delve** _(soft)_: That is exactly what a museum is for. A museum is a place where a valley remembers.  
**Professor Delve**: Though I wonder. Your grandmother kept a hatch register. Every egg in forty years, and its first sound.  
**Professor Delve** _(think)_: That is not one Nubbin remembered. That is all of them. It could go in the middle. On loan, naturally.  
**Dusty** _(sad)_: …That’s fair. That’s a good thing. It just isn’t Fossil.  

**Choices**

- **Put Fossil’s card in the centre case.** — sets `{"s18-centre":"card"}`
    - **Dusty**: Thank you. She’d hate the glass. She’d sit on top of it.
    - **Professor Delve**: And the register stays in the cottage, where it is still being written. Quite right. It isn’t finished.
- **Put Hazel’s hatch register in the centre case.** — sets `{"s18-centre":"register"}`
    - **Dusty**: Can her card go in it? Tucked in the front. Like a bookmark.
    - **Professor Delve**: A bookmark is permitted. A bookmark is, in fact, encouraged.
    - **Professor Delve**: On loan. It goes home the moment the family wants it. I’ll carry it myself.
- **Leave the case empty, for whoever comes next.** — sets `{"s18-centre":"empty"}`
    - You prop a card in the empty case: “For whoever this valley remembers next.”
    - **Professor Delve**: An empty case. Most irregular. People will stand in front of it for a very long time.
    - **Dusty**: Good. Let them. Fossil’s card can go on the ledge beside it, where people put their hands.

#### <a id="s18-door"></a>Done `s18-door`

_The barn is swept. Twelve plaques shine._  
_The swallows are long gone south and have left their clay cups behind._  
**Professor Delve** _(think)_: One last matter. The door. Glass needs polishing. Plaques come loose. Archivists need… worms.  
**Professor Delve**: Two Shells a visit would keep the place standing. Or it could be free, and we would manage. Somehow. Probably.  

**Choices**

- **Charge two Shells, for upkeep.** — sets `{"s18-entry":"shells"}`
    - **Professor Delve**: Sensible. A place that costs nothing is a place nobody remembers to mend.
    - **Professor Delve**: Anyone shorter than the door handle goes free. I insist. I have measured.
- **Keep it free.** — sets `{"s18-entry":"free"}`
    - **Professor Delve**: Free. Hm. A place where a valley remembers ought to belong to the valley. I agree.
    - **Professor Delve**: I shall put out a jar. Nobody need put anything in it. The jar is merely hopeful.

### Complete

#### <a id="s18-complete"></a>Complete `s18-complete`

_On opening day half the valley comes, and most of them stand at the centre case longer than they meant to._  
_Children press their noses to the trilobite. Sparks reads every plaque aloud, loudly, to people who are reading them._  
**Professor Delve** _(happy)_: You did this, Curator. I only had the principle.  
_He gives you a brass magnifying glass on a ribbon, worn smooth where a great many thumbs have held it._  
**Professor Delve** _(soft)_: It was mine. I can’t see much through it any more. I’d like it to keep looking at things.  
_Then a moth comes in through the open door, and the Curator’s first official act is to catch the professor._
