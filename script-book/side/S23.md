## S23. Three Visitors

*A Christmas Carol (Charles Dickens, 1843): Marley’s warning, the three spirits, Fezziwig’s ball, Belle, Fred’s guessing game, Ignorance and Want, the unmourned grave and the vow. Public domain; lifted lines from the 1843 text (Project Gutenberg #46), listed in the file header* · audience: main · where: A note under your door; then Corvin Vale’s cottage up the valley, and wherever the visitors walk · when: The morning after c9-close, on every chapter 9 route (the Collector partner route included). ### Step 2: Sit up with Corvin until midnight.

Hint: Take the second chair. Don’t light the stove: he’d rather you didn’t. · check: `sit:s23-second-chair:midnight`

#### <a id="s23-chairs"></a>Scene `s23-chairs`

_Inside, the cottage is clean and silent and nearly empty. The stove is laid, and not lit._  
_In front of it are two chairs, set square, a little apart. One has a cushion that has never been sat on._  
**Corvin Vale**: You came. Good. Sit. The one with the cushion. I bought it for visitors. There haven’t been any.  
**Corvin Vale**: The stove is laid. I don’t light it. I considered offering you tea. It was close.  
_He sits in the other, his left hand flat on his coat over the breast pocket. The pocket is empty._  
_He keeps his hand there anyway. Now and then he presses, as if to check._  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: Last night, at midnight, something knocked. Not at the front.  
**Corvin Vale**: At that one.  
_He nods at a second door in the back wall. It is nailed shut, and the nails have rusted into the frame._  
**Corvin Vale**: That door hasn’t opened in twenty-two years. It faces the valley. I didn’t care for the view.  
**Corvin Vale**: I didn’t answer. I sat here, and I felt nothing. I’d like that confirmed by somebody else.  
_Your Nubbin climbs onto his boots and settles there, the way it sits on yours. He looks down at it._  
_Corvin looks at the Nubbin on his boots for a long time. He does not move his feet._  
_His feet have gone to sleep, and he lets them._  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: It’s warm. I can see that it’s warm. I can’t feel that it is. Do you understand the difference?  

### Step 3: Wait for the first of the three.

Hint: Wherever you chose to be, stay there. One o’clock comes on its own. · check: `wait:s23-first-night:one-oclock`

#### <a id="s23-caller"></a>Scene `s23-caller`

_Midnight. The clock strikes, and the nailed door doesn’t open, and someone comes in through it anyway._  
_Small and round, gold at the edges the way old memories are, and grey all through. You can see the nails through him._  
**Corvin Vale** _(shock)_: …Thimble.  
**Corvin Vale** _(soft)_: Hazel’s. One of hers. He was the first one I—  
_He doesn’t finish. Thimble crosses the floor without a sound and sits on Corvin’s other boot._  
_Then he goes to the window and touches the frosted glass: once, twice, three times. Three small prints._  
_He looks at you, in the second chair, as if making sure the witness is counted. Then at Corvin._  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: Three.  
**Corvin Vale**: And I’m to sit up for them.  
_When you look back at the window, Thimble has gone. The three prints are still there._  
_Corvin takes out a ledger and a pencil, and writes down the time. Under it, in the same square hand: “Nothing felt.”_  
**Corvin Vale**: Thank you. You’ve witnessed it. You can go home now.  
_He says it kindly, and he means it. It is the first thing he has asked of you that isn’t a price._

### Step 4: Walk with Briar and Corvin into what was.

Hint: Keep close to her light. Visions don’t wait, but they don’t hurry either. · check: `walk:s23-briar:what-was`

#### <a id="s23-briar"></a>Scene `s23-briar`

_One o’clock. A small warm light comes up the lane, bobbing, with a laugh in it somewhere._  
_It doesn’t matter where you were: lane, step or chair. When it comes in at the nailed door, you are in the room to meet it._  
_It is a Nubbin, or the shape of one, made of morning. Light springs up off the crown of her head like water from a spring._  
**Corvin Vale**: Who are you?  
**Briar** _(happy)_: Briar. What was.  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: Long ago?  
**Briar**: No. Your past.  
_She laughs, not unkindly: the laugh of someone who has just noticed how serious everybody is being._  
**Corvin Vale**: You’re the one in the song. The stream lantern. You’ve been gone longer than this cottage has stood.  
**Briar** _(happy)_: Gone. Yes. Up you get, Corvin Vale. You too, witness.  
_She takes his hand, and his hand doesn’t notice. Her other paw she holds out to you, and it is like holding a patch of sun._  
_The back wall of the cottage goes thin as paper, and then it isn’t there at all._

### Step 5: Watch Lantern Night, twenty-two years ago.

Hint: Stay by the pond. When the fiddle starts, nobody minds where you stand. · check: `watch:s23-lantern-night:reel`

#### <a id="s23-lantern-night"></a>Scene `s23-lantern-night`

_Then it is summer, all at once: the Grove at dusk, twenty-two years ago, on Lantern Night._  
_The gate opens at a touch, like any gate. You have never once seen it do that._  
_A young man stands just inside it in a thin coat, as if he isn’t sure he’s allowed any further._  
_A woman comes down the path with a lantern in each hand, laughing at someone behind her. She isn’t old yet._  
**Young Hazel** _(happy)_: Corvin. Hold this. No, by the handle, you’ll have your eyebrows off.  
_She gives him her lantern, and he holds it as if it might go off. Nobody has ever handed him anything lit before._  
_Then there is a fiddle, and a reel on the grass by the pond, and the whole valley dancing it badly._  
_Corvin dances it worst. He goes left when everyone goes right, and treads on Hazel twice._  
_She laughs. Not at him: with him, until he laughs too, helplessly, bent over with his hands on his knees._  
**Briar** _(happy)_: Such a little thing. A field, some candles, a man with a fiddle.  
_Beside you, the older Corvin watches his younger self laugh. His own face doesn’t move._  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: It cost her nothing. Some candles. A field she already had.  
**Corvin Vale**: The happiness she gives, is quite as great as if it cost a fortune.  
_He says it flat, like a clerk reading back a sum. As if he has found it written down in someone else’s hand._  

#### <a id="s23-reel"></a>Done `s23-reel`

_The reel comes round again. Briar tugs at your sleeve until you join in at the bottom of the set._  
_You learn it the way everyone did: wrong, then less wrong, then not caring. Young Hazel spins past without seeing you._  
_Corvin doesn’t dance. He stands where his younger self stood, by the gate, holding nothing._  

### Step 6: See the winter that ended it.

Hint: There is nothing to do but watch. That’s the hard part. · check: `watch:s23-winter:gate`

#### <a id="s23-cold-one"></a>Scene `s23-cold-one`

_The music stops, and the light goes cold and blue. It is winter in the Grove, and very quiet._  
_Too quiet: the Nubbins sit in the frosted grass in a row, not touching. None of them looks up._  
_The smallest sits on young Corvin’s boot, the way yours sat on his tonight. It is Thimble, and the grey is coming up through him like damp through paper._  
_Young Corvin doesn’t look down. His hand is flat on his coat, over a pocket that isn’t empty yet._  
_Hazel is in the cottage doorway, with snow on her shoulders. She doesn’t shout; she is past shouting._  
**Young Hazel** _(sad)_: Another idol has displaced me.  
**Corvin, at twenty-two**: What idol?  
**Young Hazel** _(soft)_: A cold one.  
**Young Hazel** _(soft)_: You keep them so safe they’ve stopped being well.  
_That is all. She goes in and shuts the door, and the snow keeps coming._  
_Later, in the dark, the gate opens. That is all the vision shows: a gate, opening, and the dark._  
_Corvin claps his hand over Briar’s light, hard, the way you’d put out a candle. It shines straight through his fingers._  
**Briar** _(soft)_: Your lip is trembling. And what is that upon your cheek?  
_Nothing: there is nothing on his cheek. His face is perfectly dry._  
_That is the worst of it._

### Step 7: Follow Hush into what is.

Hint: She only shows tonight. Everything you see is happening right now, somewhere in the valley. · check: `walk:s23-hush:what-is`

#### <a id="s23-hush"></a>Scene `s23-hush`

_The second night. Corvin is in his chair before you arrive, with the ledger open on his knee._  
_Neither of you says anything for three hours. It isn’t comfortable, and it isn’t meant to be._  
_Your Nubbin falls asleep on Corvin’s boots and snores. He writes the snore down in the ledger, with the time._  
_At one o’clock the cottage goes quiet. Not silent: quiet, as if every sound has been gently put away._  
_She is in the corner, though nobody saw her come. Tall for a Nubbin, moon-pale, wrapped in her own grey like a cloak._  
_It is the quietest thing you have ever seen. Even the clock seems to tick more carefully._  
**Corvin Vale** _(soft)_: Hush.  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: The old wall. The one who sat with the still ones.  
**Hush** _(soft)_: Look upon me!  
_She says it so quietly it is hardly more than a breath. Then nothing more._  
_He looks, and so do you. There isn’t much to see: a pale shape, a grey cloak, patience all the way down._  
_Then she turns to the nailed door, and it lets her through, and both of you after her, into tonight._  

### Step 8: See what is happening in Ember’s kitchen tonight.

Hint: Hush takes you in through the wall. Ember can’t see you. Don’t try to help. · check: `watch:s23-ember:stove`

#### <a id="s23-stove"></a>Scene `s23-stove`

_Ash lies by the stove, grey, facing the corner._  
_Ember puts a bowl down beside her. Ash doesn’t turn, and Ember doesn’t wait to see if she will._  
**Ember** _(angry)_: I hope he’s cold.  
**Ember** _(angry)_: I hope he’s sitting up there in that clean house, cold to the bone, and nobody comes.

_Ember stares at the stove for a long time._  
**Ember** _(soft)_: …No.  
**Ember** _(soft)_: I hope he’s cold exactly as long as it takes.  
_She doesn’t say as long as what takes. She gets up and puts another log on, which wasn’t needed._  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: That’s fair. That’s more than fair.

### Step 10: Go with Hush to the Hollow.

_Hush doesn’t say._  
**Corvin Vale**: Will it live?  
_Hush only looks at the root._  
_In his own garden Corvin would have known exactly what to do with a thin one. He would have made it quiet._
