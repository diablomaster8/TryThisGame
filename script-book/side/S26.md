## S26. The Walled Garden

### Step 1: Find out what the robin on the old wall post wants from you.

#### <a id="s26-robin"></a>Scene `s26-robin`

_Late summer. The rain has stopped at last, and Fossil’s flower is a few days old on the hill._  
_Dusty hasn’t been down from the cliff since._  
_A robin has taken the old wall lantern post. It sits on the top like a lid._  
_After that, wherever you go, it goes: the pond, the woodpile, the cottage step. It never comes closer than a spade’s length._  
_It looks at you the way a landlord looks at a tenant._  
**Fern** _(think)_: It’s decided you owe it something. Robins do that. One followed me for a whole spring over a raisin.  
**Fern**: I never found out which raisin.  
_Your Nubbin sits down under the post and stares up at it. The robin stares back, and neither of them blinks first._  

#### <a id="s26-robin-fed"></a>Done `s26-robin-fed`

### Step 3: Follow the robin along the old wall with Linnet.

Hint: Watch where the robin scratches. Leave the ground round the lantern post alone; you know why. · check: `find:s26-door`

#### <a id="s26-key"></a>Scene `s26-key`

_Next morning Linnet is waiting at the top of the lane: early, cross, and slightly out of breath. Tobin is not with her._  
**Linnet**: He thinks I’m reading. I am. I’m reading the valley.  
_The old wall runs along the north side of the Grove, tumbled and deep in ivy. The robin goes ahead, post to bush to stone._

**Linnet**: Dig. Go on. It’s pointing.  
_You kneel, and lift out an iron key, black with age, its bow as wide as your palm._

**You**: Nothing. Something old. It’s asleep.  
**Linnet** _(think)_: Everything here is old and asleep.

### Step 4: Fetch Dusty to the hidden garden. They’ll know if anything in it is alive.

Hint: Dusty is up on their cliff. Bring them along the old wall to the ivy door. · check: `escort:dusty:s26-garden`

#### <a id="s26-inside"></a>Scene `s26-inside`

_The door opens inwards, slowly, scraping a fan in a hundred years of leaves._  
_Three stone steps lead down from the doorway into a garden walled on all four sides._  
_Everything in it is grey: grey tangles, grey canes, bare roses climbing the walls and falling off them again. In the middle, a stone bench._  
**Linnet** _(soft)_: It’s so quiet in here.  
**Linnet** _(soft)_: I bet nobody’s said a single word in here for a hundred years.  
_Nothing moves. Even the robin lands without a sound._  
**Linnet**: …Is it dead? All of it?  
**You**: I don’t know. I know someone who would.  
**Linnet** _(think)_: Then fetch them. I’ll wait here. I’m very good at the tops of things.  
_She folds her arms in the doorway at the top of the three steps, and guards a garden nobody has wanted for a hundred years._

### Step 6: Wait in the garden at dusk and catch whoever has been pruning the roses.

#### <a id="s26-pruned"></a>Scene `s26-pruned`

_Then Linnet spots a rose by the far wall that has been cut back. Badly, but not long ago._  
**Linnet** _(think)_: Look. Somebody’s been in here. That cut’s from this summer. The wood’s still pale.  
**Dusty** _(think)_: She’s right. And whoever did it held the shears upside down.  
**Linnet**: So somebody’s got another key. Or somebody comes over the top.

### Step 8: Sit on the stone bench with Moss and hear the garden’s story.

Hint: The bench in the middle of the garden. Sit down, and let him take his time. · check: `sit:s26-hush-bench`

#### <a id="s26-bench"></a>Scene `s26-bench`

_That evening the light goes long and gold across the garden, and Moss comes down off the wall at last, the slow way._  
_He climbs onto the stone bench and sits, as if he has sat there before. He has._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: It was Hush’s. The bench, the roses, the wall. All of it.  
**Linnet**: Who’s Hush?  
**Moss** _(soft)_: A friend of mine. The quiet one. She kept the griefs nobody else had room for.  
_Linnet puts her brake on, and Dusty sits down on the path. You sit on the end of the bench, and wait._  

#### <a id="s26-hush"></a>Done `s26-hush`

**Moss** _(sad)_: Briar wanted the stilled ones up on the hill, in the light, where the valley could see them. Hush said the wind would have them.  
**Moss**: So she carried them in here herself, one at a time, all that winter. She was not big. It took her till the thaw.  
**Moss**: Then she planted roses up every wall. Not for herself. A still thing can still be looked at, she said, and should have something back.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: The last of them went in the second winter, here, with the roses bare above it. She said bare was still something.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: You heard the rest at Lantern Night. The door, and the key. I will not tell that twice in one year.  
_Nobody says anything. The robin turns round twice on the back of the bench, and settles._  
**Linnet** _(soft)_: Not even Hazel knew? About in here?  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Hazel had enough doors.  
_Then Moss looks down at the patch of bare earth beside the bench. He looks at it for a long time, the way he looks at the hill._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: If I ever have a flower, I’d like a bit of it here.  
**Moss** _(think)_: Don’t go telling people. They’d make a speech.  
_Linnet sets a white pebble on the bare patch to mark it, and says nothing at all. For Linnet, that is a speech._
