# Side story S27: Some Nubbin

## S27. Some Nubbin

*Charlotte’s Web (E. B. White, 1952): a spider who saves a small life with words written in her web, and dies in her season. Adapted as an original Grove story; two short approved quotes.* · audience: all · where: Corvin’s Circuit: the old lantern shed, the runners’ pens and the finishing straight (the spring scene is in the Grove) · when: Opens in chapter 5, on the first visit to the Circuit after c5-close (and c5-sold, if it plays). The Show waits for chapter 7; the egg sac for wisp or chapter 8. Closes before chapter 9 opens.

**Cast:** Spindle (Charlotte A. Cavatica (Charlotte’s Web, E. B. White); she writes and never talks), Hob (Lurvy, the hired man (Charlotte’s Web), loosely: Corvin’s stable lad), Mr Tull (An original character: the dealer from over the pass (the book’s threat, made a businessman)), Wisp (Templeton the rat (Charlotte’s Web), in this story only: the hoarder who carries the egg sac), Clank (Claptrap (Borderlands), reference only)

### Step 1: Fetch a coil of wire for Sparks from the Circuit’s old lantern shed.

Hint: The shed is behind the finishing straight. Bottom drawer of the cabinet under the window. · check: `find:s27-wire-coil`

#### <a id="s27-wire"></a>Scene `s27-wire`

_High summer at the Circuit. The sand is hot enough to fry on, and the railings are furred with frost, which nobody up here mentions._  
_Sparks is on her back under the start lamp, with a screwdriver in her teeth._  
**Sparks** _(angry)_: Start lamp’s gone again. I need wire. Copper, thin, a coil about so big.  
**Sparks**: There’s a cabinet of mine in the old lantern shed, behind the straight. Bottom drawer. Be a love.  
**Sparks** _(think)_: He doesn’t like people in there. He’s never been in there himself, mind. Not once in eleven years.  
**Sparks**: It’s the only place up here with dust in it. I love it. Don’t tell him.  
_Your Nubbin climbs into your coat pocket for the walk. It doesn’t like the cold rails either._  

#### <a id="s27-evening"></a>Done `s27-evening`

_The lantern shed smells of paraffin and warm wood. Dust turns slowly in the light from one small window._  
_Under the window stands Sparks’s cabinet, every drawer labelled in her square capitals: FUSES, WICKS, SPARE, EVENING LAMPS._  
_You find the wire. When you stand up, there is a web across the corner of the window that wasn’t there a moment ago._  
_It isn’t a new web; you just hadn’t looked. And in the middle of it, very small, is a word._  
_EVENING._  
_A grey-brown spider no bigger than a fingernail sits beside it. She lifts two front legs at you, politely._  
_It is eleven in the morning. You find, to your own surprise, that you lift a hand back._  

### Step 2: Ask the stable lad about the little grey runner at the end of the row.

Hint: Hob does the buckets by the pens. He’s the only one up here who whistles. · check: `talk:s27-tam`

#### <a id="s27-row"></a>Scene `s27-row`

_Through the shed window, past the spider and her word, you can see the runners’ pens._  
_One pen to each runner, in a straight row, every rail white with frost. Every runner grey, and none of them moving._  
_At the very end of the row is the smallest. It is the only one facing the wrong way._  
_It isn’t looking at anything in particular. It is just looking out, down the valley, the way you might look at weather coming._  
_There is a number painted on its side in neat white paint. 9._  
_Outside, a lad with two buckets goes along the pens, whistling. He stops whistling at the end of the row._

**Hob**: I’m Hob. I do the buckets. I’m not meant to whistle. I forget.

**Hob** _(sad)_: Autumn Show’s at the first frost that isn’t his. Anything that doesn’t place goes over the pass.  
**Hob**: There’s a dealer comes for them. Mr Tull. He’s all right, as dealers go. They don’t come back, is all.  
**Hob** _(sad)_: Nine’s never placed. Not once.  
_In the shed window behind you, the spider has gone very still in the middle of her web._  

### Step 3: Sit down by the shed window, and wait with the spider.

Hint: Sit on the upturned crate by the window and don’t do anything. It takes as long as it takes. · check: `sit:s27-shed-window`

#### <a id="s27-wait"></a>Scene `s27-wait`

_The next time you come up to the Circuit, the first thing you do is go to the shed. You didn’t decide to._  
_EVENING has gone. In its place, in the middle of the web, is a new word._  
_WAIT._  
_The spider sits at the edge of the web with her legs drawn in. She isn’t looking at you; she is thinking._  
_You know that kind of thinking. You’ve watched someone in your family do it on a cottage step for hours._  
_There is an upturned crate under the window. It is exactly the right height for sitting next to someone._  

#### <a id="s27-spindle"></a>Done `s27-spindle`

_You sit, and the spider thinks, and the dust turns in the light. Your Nubbin falls asleep in your pocket._  
_After a long time, the door bangs open, and Sparks comes in to find out where her wire went._  
**Sparks** _(shock)_: There you are. I’ve been holding a lamp together with my— oh.  
_She looks at you, and the crate, and the web, and the word. She sits down on the floor without being asked._  
**Sparks** _(soft)_: She writes. Course she writes. She’s been living on my drawer labels for years. Look at her, the little clerk.  
**Sparks**: She wants a name. Anyone who can spell wants a name. Spindle. All legs and she winds things. Spindle.  
_The spider considers this. Then she lifts two legs, politely, once at Sparks and once at you._  
**Sparks** _(happy)_: That’s a yes. That’s definitely a yes. I’m going to cry and then I’m going to fix the start lamp.  

### Step 4: Ask Fern, Clover, Luna and Byte for one word each, and bring them back to Spindle.

Hint: Fern is in her orchard, Clover by the pond, Luna in her night garden and Byte wherever there is a clipboard. · check: `collect:s27-words:4`

#### <a id="s27-labels"></a>Scene `s27-labels`

_Over the next few days Spindle practises. FUSES appears in the web, and then WICKS, and then SPARE._  
_They are very good, and the S’s in particular are beautiful. They are also no use to anybody._  
_On the fourth day she writes MISC, and looks very pleased with herself._  
**Sparks** _(shock)_: That’s my worst drawer. She’s been through my worst drawer.  
**Sparks**: That’s the trouble with learning off a cabinet. She can tell you where everything is, but not how anyone feels.  
_Spindle spins a short line, and then cuts it, and then sits looking at the gap where a word ought to go._  
_Then she looks through the window at the end of the row, and back at you, and lifts one leg. Just one._  
**Sparks** _(think)_: She wants words. Proper ones. And she can’t exactly go down the valley asking.  
**Sparks**: Ask round. Everyone’s got one word they’d save for something that mattered. Bring them up here.  

#### <a id="s27-words"></a>Done `s27-words`

_Fern is up a ladder in her orchard, losing an argument with a plum tree._  
**Fern**: A word? For a spider? Oh, I’ve got one. Somebody’s. As in, this one is somebody’s.  
**Fern** _(sad)_: When Bramble went, people said “it’s only a Nubbin.” She wasn’t only. She was mine. Somebody’s.  
_Clover listens with her head on one side, the way she listens to the shy ones._  
**Clover** _(soft)_: Warm. You can’t make a grey one not grey. But you can make it warm. Warm is allowed.  
_Luna is in her night garden at noon, asleep in a deckchair. She answers without opening her eyes._  
**Luna** _(soft)_: Home. Everything in my garden grows towards it in the dark. Even the moonflowers. Especially them.  
_Byte takes the question very seriously, and goes away, and comes back with a sheet of paper._  
**Byte** _(think)_: “Statistically above average.” It’s accurate. It’s kind. It’s the nicest thing I know how to say about anybody.  
_You read the four words to the shed window. Spindle listens to each, legs folded, exactly like a judge._  
_At Byte’s she goes very still for a long moment. Then she points one leg, delicately, at the length of the rail._  
**Sparks** _(happy)_: She’s saying it won’t fit. I’d tell him gently. He’s sensitive about his adjectives.  
_Spindle spins three short threads and pulls them tight, the way someone squares a pile of letters on a desk._  
_Then she does something she hasn’t done before. She points at you, and then at the end of the row._  
**Sparks** _(think)_: She’s asking what it’s called. Nine isn’t a name. Nine’s a number. You can’t save a number.  
_The little grey runner is still looking down the valley. It has never had a name to look up at._

### Step 5: Be at the pens at first light, to see what Spindle wrote in the night.

Hint: Come up the valley before dawn. Stand by the shed: the frost shows the silk only for the first hour. · check: `watch:s27-pen-railing:dawn`

#### <a id="s27-first-night"></a>Scene `s27-first-night`

_At dusk Spindle comes to the edge of the web and waits, quite plainly, to be carried._  
_You hold out a matchbox. She walks into it the way a duchess gets into a carriage._  
_You carry her across the yard in the blue light to the last pen in the row, and open the box on the rail._  
_The rail is so cold it smokes. Spindle steps out onto it without a moment’s fuss._  
_Inside the pen the little grey runner watches the spider. It is the first thing you have seen it watch._  
_You go home. It is the hardest part, it turns out: going home and leaving someone to work._  

#### <a id="s27-first-word"></a>Done `s27-first-word`

_First light. Your Nubbin keeps watch with you from your pocket, nose out, and your breath hangs in the air._  
_The Circuit’s summer frost has come down in the night as it always does. Every rail is white._  
_And on the rail of the last pen, every thread of Spindle’s web has caught the frost, and stands out white as chalk._  
_It isn’t a number. It is a name, in letters the height of your hand: its name._  
_Fern has come up the valley with you to see. Fern reads it out first, loudly, and then again, louder._  
**Fern** _(happy)_: That’s its NAME. Look. It’s got a NAME. Everybody, it’s got a—  
_Inside the pen, the little grey runner turns round. For the first time, it is facing the right way._  
_Corvin’s runners have numbers. This one has a name, and now everybody knows it._  

### Step 6: Corvin wants a word with you by the pens.

Hint: He’s at the end of the row, by the rail with the name on it. · check: `talk:corvin:s27-pens`

#### <a id="s27-odd-things"></a>Scene `s27-odd-things`

_Corvin comes out of the house at seven, as he always does, with his ledger under his arm._  
_He stops at the end of the row. He looks at the rail for a long time._  
**Corvin Vale** _(think)_: Frost does odd things.  
_He writes something in the ledger, and goes in._  
_By noon there are people at the Circuit fence who have never come up the valley before._  
_Two children from the lane, holding hands. A man with a bicycle, who says he was only passing, and stays all afternoon._  
_By then the frost is long gone from the rail. They have come to look at a small grey runner whose name they know._  
_Corvin comes out again and looks at the fence. Then he looks across the yard, at you._

**Corvin Vale**: Somebody wrote on my rail in the night. I don’t care for things written on my rails. Was it you?  
_Behind you, through the shed window, you can feel a very small person holding very still._

### Step 7: At dusk, carry Spindle to the main railing by the finishing straight.

Hint: Bring the matchbox. The main railing runs along the straight, opposite the stands. · check: `carry:s27-spindle:main-railing`

#### <a id="s27-second-night"></a>Scene `s27-second-night`

_Spindle has been working on the second word all day. You can tell from the look of the web: well used, a little ragged._  
_She points one leg, quite firmly, past the pens and out at the finishing straight._  
**Sparks** _(shock)_: The main rail? With the stands right there? She’s not shy, is she. I love her. I’m terrified.  
_Somewhere in the house a lamp is lit in an upstairs window. It stays lit._  
_At dusk, Spindle walks into her matchbox. You put it in your inside pocket, against your shirt, where it’s warm._  
_You cross the yard to the straight in the last of the light, as if you were only going for a walk._  

#### <a id="s27-somebodys"></a>Done `s27-somebodys`

_Dawn. All along the main rail, opposite the empty stands, in white: SOMEBODY’S._  
_Fern’s word. Fern, at the fence, has both hands over her mouth._  
_That morning a cart comes over the pass a week early. Mr Tull gets down and walks along the rail, reading it._  
**Mr Tull** _(think)_: Mr Vale. I heard you had a runner with a name. I didn’t think you held with names.  
**Corvin Vale**: I don’t. It’s the frost. It does odd things up here.  
**Mr Tull**: So I see. It’s a lot of odd, for one rail.  
_He doesn’t stay. He climbs back onto his cart and looks at the fence, and the people at it, and frowns all the way up the pass._  
_By noon, the little grey runner has been moved out of the row to a pen behind the house, where the public can’t see._  

### Step 8: Carry Spindle in Sparks’s toolbox to the inside pen behind the house.

Hint: Walk slowly past the house. Nobody stops someone carrying a toolbox. · check: `carry:s27-spindle:inside-pen`

#### <a id="s27-toolbox"></a>Scene `s27-toolbox`

**Sparks** _(angry)_: Behind the house? That’s the coldest pen up here. The tap’s frozen in August. He’s hiding it.  
**Sparks**: Right. Nobody looks twice at a toolbox. Toolboxes are invisible. I’ve walked into the judges’ tent with one.  
_She empties the top tray of her toolbox and lines it with a clean rag, which is the first clean rag you have ever seen her own._  
_Spindle walks in. She settles between a spool of fuse wire and a very small spanner, as if she has always travelled this way._  
_You go past the house at dusk with the toolbox swinging. The lamp in the upstairs window is still lit._  
_Nobody stops you. Sparks was right: nobody looks twice at a toolbox._  

#### <a id="s27-warm"></a>Done `s27-warm`

_Behind the house the frost is thickest. The inside pen’s rail is so cold your breath crackles on it._  
_At first light, across it, the third word: WARM._  
_Corvin comes round the corner of the house in his shirtsleeves, as if he had been waiting up._  
_He stands at the rail. Then he lays his bare hand flat on the word._  
_You wait for the frost to melt under his palm. It doesn’t, not a single letter._  
_He takes his hand away and looks at it. Then he touches his breast pocket, once, and goes back inside._  

### Step 9: Go up to the Circuit for the Autumn Show.

Hint: The Show is on the first autumn morning. Spindle rides in your hat brim. Don’t bend down suddenly. · check: `watch:s27-autumn-show`

#### <a id="s27-ready"></a>Scene `s27-ready`

_The first autumn dawn. The frost in the Grove is real frost now, and the leaves have started to let go._  
_Clover comes up the valley with you, and helps you brush the little grey runner through the rail of its pen._  
**Clover** _(soft)_: Gently. Grey ones are thin-skinned. Long strokes, and stop when it leans in. That means more.  
_It leans in. Clover stops brushing and lets it._  
_Byte is at the rail with his clipboard. His newest stopwatch has a very small web across the strap, and in it, one word: ABOVE._  
**Byte** _(think)_: It isn’t accurate. There’s no data. I’m keeping it.  
**Byte** _(think)_: Pixel says I’m to stop timing things that aren’t racing. I’ve timed how long she’s been saying it.  
**Byte**: I’ve worked out its chances of placing. To three decimal places.  
**Byte** _(sad)_: I’m not going to tell you the number.  
_Spindle will not be left in the shed. She makes this extremely clear by walking up your sleeve._  
_In the end she rides in the brim of your hat, which is the best seat at the Circuit, and she knows it._  

#### <a id="s27-show"></a>Done `s27-show`

_The Autumn Show. Flags, a brass band of three, and more people at the fence than the Circuit has ever had._  
_Most of them aren’t here for the racing. They are here to see a small grey runner with a name._  
_Corvin’s champion, the big grey Number One, wins as it always does. It doesn’t look at the crowd; it never has._  
_The little grey runner comes last. It isn’t slow, exactly: it spends the whole race looking at the people who came._  
_It doesn’t place. The judge pins the three rosettes on three grey runners who don’t look down at them._  
_Mr Tull’s cart is waiting by the gate. He is eating a sandwich, and looks as if he wishes he weren’t there._  
_In your hat brim, Spindle sits very still. Then she taps the brim twice, which you have learned means: tonight._  

### Step 10: Hold the lantern low for Spindle while she writes her last word.

Hint: Stay by the finishing straight and keep the light steady. She’ll tell you when she’s done. · check: `wait:s27-spindle:home-word`

#### <a id="s27-home-word"></a>Scene `s27-home-word`

_That night Spindle doesn’t walk into the matchbox. She walks onto your hand, and stays there._  
_You carry her to the very start of the finishing straight. She looks down the whole length of the rail._  
_Then she starts: not one letter, but one stroke of one letter, from the top rail to the bottom._  
_It is going to be the biggest word she has ever written. It is going to take all night._  
_You hold the lantern low, so she can see, and walk beside her down the rail, a step at a time._  
_Your Nubbin falls asleep in your pocket, and wakes, and sleeps again. The lantern hisses._  

#### <a id="s27-midnight"></a>Done `s27-midnight`

_At midnight the house door opens. You shutter the lantern and step back into the shed doorway, out of the light._  
_Corvin comes down the straight in his coat, with a lamp. He is looking for something, and he finds it._  
_Halfway along the rail, in the lamplight, a grey-brown spider is halfway through a letter._  
_He stands over her for a long time. Then he lifts his hand, the cold one, over the web._  
_He holds it there, and you don’t breathe. Spindle doesn’t stop working, not for a single thread._  
_He lowers his hand._  
**Corvin Vale** _(soft)_: Spell it right, then.  
_He goes back to the house, and the door shuts quietly. It is the first right thing you have seen him do._  
_You come out of the shed, and open the lantern, and hold it low again. She works on until nearly dawn._

### Step 11: Walk the little grey runner home to the Grove.

Hint: Go slowly. It has never walked further than the end of the straight. · check: `escort:s27-nine:grove`

#### <a id="s27-letgo"></a>Scene `s27-letgo`

_By breakfast half the valley is at the Circuit fence. Nobody arranged it; people just came._  
_Along the whole finishing straight, in letters as tall as a Nubbin, the frost says: HOME._  
**Fern** _(happy)_: HOME. Look at the size of it. She must have run out of rail.  
_Mr Tull is at the gate with his cart. He reads the rail, and then the faces along the fence._  
**Mr Tull** _(think)_: Morning, Mr Vale. I’ve come for whatever didn’t place. But I’ll not take that one.  
**Mr Tull**: Its name’s been up on your rails all summer. I’d have half this valley at my gate by Sunday, asking after it.  
**Mr Tull**: I sell runners. I don’t sell the ones people ask after. Good day to you.  
_He tips his hat to the fence, quite politely, and drives away over the pass. His cart is lighter by one._  
_Corvin stands at the end of the straight with his hands in his pockets. He reads the rail one more time._  
_Hob is at the pen behind the house. He opens his mouth to say “Nine”, and stops, and says its name instead._  
_He says it carefully, like a word in another language. Corvin pretends not to hear._  
**Corvin Vale**: Take it. It’s no use to me famous.  
_He says it to the rail, not to you. Then he goes back to the house without looking round._  
_The little grey runner looks at the open pen for a long time. Then it follows you to the road, three steps behind._  

#### <a id="s27-gate"></a>Done `s27-gate`

_At the Grove gate you lift and push. The little grey runner stops on the threshold._  
_It looks at the gate as if a gate is a thing that might shut on it._  
_Your Nubbin hops out of your pocket, goes through, and sits on the far side, waiting._  
_After a long while, the runner comes through too. It sits down by the gatepost and looks at everything._
