## S28. Please Look After This Nubbin

### Step 1: Take the stray from the carrier’s cart to Byte’s house.

Hint: Walk slowly and it will follow you. Byte’s house is the one with the very straight hedge, next to the glasshouse. · check: `escort:s28-parcel:byte-house`

#### <a id="s28-cart"></a>Scene `s28-cart`

_The morning after the Hollow. You slept badly, with Hazel’s notebook under your pillow._  
_Hooves in the lane. The carrier’s cart pulls up at the gate, and the carter leans down without getting off._  
**The carter**: Morning. Got a passenger for this end of the valley. Wouldn’t give its name.  
_On a crate in the back sits a small brown Nubbin, very upright, with a basket on its knees._  
_It sees you, and bows. Not a nod: a proper bow, from the middle._  
_Round its neck, on a piece of string, hangs a luggage tag._  
_“Please look after this one. Thank you.”_  
_Underneath, smaller: “For Byte. H.”_  
**The carter**: Never saw who put it on. It was sat on the milestone at the ford, with a plum on its head for the fare.  
**The carter** _(happy)_: Plum was very fair. I’ve had worse off people.  
_You know that handwriting. You have read a letter in it so many times the folds have gone soft._  
_The ink has run a little, in some old rain. It doesn’t say where she is, or how she is._  
_The Nubbin climbs down off the cart with great care, one step at a time, and waits by your boot._  

#### <a id="s28-for-byte"></a>Done `s28-for-byte`

_Byte opens his door before you knock. He has a pencil behind each ear, in case one runs out._  
**Byte** _(shock)_: That’s a Nubbin. Why have you brought me a Nubbin? I didn’t order a Nubbin.  
_The Nubbin bows to him, from the middle. After a long moment Byte bows back, very slightly, in case it’s a trick._  
_He reads the tag, then the back of the tag, which is blank, then the front again._  
**Byte** _(think)_: “For Byte. H.” Who’s H.?

### Step 5: Follow Parcel when it sets off at eleven with half a sandwich.

Hint: Walk behind Parcel, not beside it: it likes to lead. It goes towards the foot of the hill. · check: `walk:s28-parcel:low-wall`

#### <a id="s28-sandwich"></a>Scene `s28-sandwich`

_A week on, Parcel has a routine. Byte has written it down, with times._  
**Byte** _(think)_: Up at seven. Bows to the kettle. Marmalade. Tidies its basket. Bows to the kettle again.  
**Byte**: And at eleven it makes a sandwich, cuts it in half, and goes out with one half. Comes back without it.  
**Byte** _(think)_: I’ve followed it twice. It loses me at the wall. I think it does it on purpose.

#### <a id="s28-elevenses"></a>Done `s28-elevenses`

_The low wall at the foot of the hill, in the thin autumn sun. Moss is sitting on it, and these days you can see a little of the wall through him._  
_Parcel climbs up beside him, sets the half sandwich on the stone between them, very precisely, and bows._  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Elevenses.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: It brings one every morning. I don’t eat. It knows that. It brings one anyway. Somebody raised it properly.  
**Moss** _(happy)_: The robins have them, after. We sit, and I tell it things. The winter the pond froze with a duck in it. It bows at the end.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: Plenty have come down that road with a label and a small case, you know. Most of the good ones did.  
**Moss** _(soft)_: People think precious things shine. Mostly they’re a bit worn. They’ve been held a lot.  
_Footsteps. Byte comes round the end of the wall at four minutes past eleven, out of breath._  
**Moss**: His lace.  
_You look at Byte. You don’t say anything; you don’t have to._  
**Byte** _(think)_: It’s untied, isn’t it. He’s said it. You’ve got the face.  
_Byte ties it. Moss watches him tie it with great interest._  
**Moss**: Other one.
