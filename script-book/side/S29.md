# Side story S29: The Gates of Dawn

## S29. The Gates of Dawn

*Kenneth Grahame, The Wind in the Willows (Methuen, 1908; public domain): the river, the Wild Wood, Badger’s kitchen, Dulce Domum, The Piper at the Gates of Dawn and Wayfarers All* · audience: all · where: The stream below the Grove; later Marina’s river beyond the ridge, the Wild Wood under the Circuit and Dusty’s cliff · when: Opens after c4-close (the week after Lantern Night). Steps are gated by chapter (see notes) and run to chapter 7. Closes when chapter 8 opens.

**Cast:** Granny Brock (Mr Badger (Kenneth Grahame, The Wind in the Willows, 1908), recast as an old charcoal-burner), The seafarer (the Sea Rat ("Wayfarers All", The Wind in the Willows, 1908), recast as a person), Valley children (the field-mice carol singers ("Dulce Domum", The Wind in the Willows, 1908))

### Step 1: Help Marina carry her mended boat down to the water.

Hint: The boat is upside down on the bank below the Grove. Take one end; Marina takes the other, and tells you which is which. · check: `carry:s29-boat:stream`

#### <a id="s29-digging"></a>Scene `s29-digging`

_The week after Lantern Night. Down by the stream the air smells of cut grass and warm mud._  
_Marina is on the bank with her boat upside down on two trestles, painting its bottom blue and humming._  
_A few yards off, the turf heaves. It heaves again and splits, and a pair of earthy hands comes up through it._  
_Then a head, covered in soil, blinking at the sun as if the sun had done it on purpose._  
**Dusty** _(shock)_: Bother! O blow!  
_Dusty climbs out, shakes off about a bucketful of earth, and looks back down the hole with real dislike._  
**Dusty**: I’ve been clearing out the burrow since March. Every time I finish a room, the room next door falls in.  
**Dusty** _(angry)_: Hang digging!  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Well. Nobody in this valley ever thought they’d hear you say that.  
**Dusty**: Nobody in this valley ever heard me mean it. Morning. What are you doing to that boat?  
**Marina** _(think)_: Mending her. She’s done. Come on, both of you, one at each end. You’ve never been in a boat, have you, Dusty?  
**Dusty**: I’ve been in a hole. Is it much different?  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Opposite, mostly.  

#### <a id="s29-launch"></a>Done `s29-launch`

_The boat goes into the stream with a smack and a wobble, and sits there looking pleased with itself._  
_Your Nubbin is in before anyone else, and takes the very front: the little pointed seat._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: That’s the bow. And that’s a Nubbin who knows what’s what.  
**Dusty** _(shock)_: It’s moving. It’s just sitting there, and it’s moving. Is it meant to do that?  
**Marina**: That’s the whole idea, love.  

### Step 2: Go down the stream with Marina to the backwater, and have the picnic.

Hint: Sit still in the middle while Marina rows. Leaning over the side to look at fish does not count as sitting still. · check: `explore:s29-river:backwater`

#### <a id="s29-boats"></a>Scene `s29-boats`

**Marina** _(think)_: Vests on. Cork, see? Nobody gets in my boat without one. Not you, not Dusty, not me, not the King.  
_The vests are old and stiff, and smell faintly of the sea._  
_Dusty puts theirs on back to front, then the right way round, then back to front again._  
_Marina pushes off. The bank slides away, and the stream takes the boat as if it had been waiting all morning._  
**Dusty** _(think)_: So what is it you actually do? In a boat? All day?  
_Marina stops rowing. She looks at Dusty very seriously, as if she has waited years for someone to ask._  
**Marina** _(soft)_: Believe me, my young friend, there is nothing—absolute nothing—half so much worth doing as simply messing about in boats.  
**Dusty**: That’s not an answer.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: It’s the only answer. Duck. Low branch.  
_In the bow, your Nubbin leans out over the water with its ears blown flat, like a figurehead that has seen something wonderful._  
_Dusty tries to swap seats with it, once. It gives them a look, and they sit back down._  

#### <a id="s29-picnic"></a>Done `s29-picnic`

_The stream runs into the river, and the river turns off into a backwater: still and green, with a willow leaning over it._  
_Marina ties up to the willow, hauls out a wicker basket, and starts unpacking it onto a blue-and-white rug._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Cold chicken. Ham. Two loaves, one for now and one for later. Three cheeses. Gherkins. Seed cake. Ginger beer. Lemonade…  
**Dusty** _(shock)_: O stop, stop! This is too much!  
**Marina**: It’s a picnic, Dusty. Too much is the amount.  
_Out in the middle of the backwater a line of bubbles comes up. Then a flat brown head, whiskered, watching you._  
_Then a second head beside it, much smaller and much rounder. It isn’t watching you at all; it is watching the gherkins._  
**Marina** _(soft)_: Otters. Keep still. That’s the old one, and that’s his kit. I call him Portly. You’ll see why.  
_The old otter looks at you a long time, and sinks without a ripple. The kit doesn’t sink; the kit comes ashore._  
_He walks straight up the rug, takes the biggest gherkin from the jar, and goes, dripping, before anyone can move._  
**Dusty** _(shock)_: He took a gherkin. He looked me in the eye and he took it.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: He does that. Last summer it was a whole pork pie. He couldn’t lift it. He tried for an hour.  

### Step 3: Fish the picnic out of the river and get everyone back to the bank.

Hint: Your Nubbins can swim; let them. Grab whatever floats: the basket, the rug, the oars. Leave the gherkins to the otters. · check: `collect:s29-picnic:river`

#### <a id="s29-oars"></a>Scene `s29-oars`

_After lunch, with the sun gone soft and the basket much lighter, Marina rows you back out of the backwater._  
**Dusty**: Let me have a go.  
**Marina** _(think)_: Not today. Next time, when you’ve watched a bit more.  
**Dusty**: I’ve watched loads. You just pull. Anyone can pull. I pull roots out of cliffs.  
_Before she can stop them, Dusty has grabbed both oars, stood up, and dug them hard into the river._  
_One oar bites. The other skims the top of the water and sends Dusty over backwards, boots in the air._  
**Marina** _(shock)_: You’ve caught a crab. That’s what it’s called. Sit down. SIT—  
_Dusty sits up, grabs the side, and the whole boat turns over, quite slowly, as if it has thought about it first._  
_…_  
_Green water, bubbles, a great deal of noise. Then the cork vests do exactly what they’re for, and you bob up like three corks._  
_Your Nubbins paddle past you towards the bank in a neat line, unbothered, as if they’d planned to swim home all along._  

#### <a id="s29-fire"></a>Done `s29-fire`

_Marina gets everything ashore: you, Dusty, the basket, the rug, both oars. Then she swims back for the boat._  
_She lights a fire in a ring of stones and sits Dusty in front of it, wrapped in the rug._  
**Dusty** _(sad)_: I’m so sorry. Your boat. Your lovely picnic. I’m an idiot.  
**Marina** _(soft)_: Never mind. Honestly. Never mind.  
**Dusty**: The cheese is in the river.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Never mind the cheese. The fish will have a lovely afternoon. Never mind.  
_She says it until Dusty believes it. It takes most of an hour, and a whole pot of tea._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Tomorrow I’ll teach you properly. Both of you. Then at least you’ll fall in the right way.  

### Step 4: Take Marina’s rowing lesson, then find something to give Portly.

Hint: Pull with your back, not your arms, and look where you’ve been, not where you’re going. Portly will be close by. He always is. · check: `give:s29-portly:gift`

#### <a id="s29-lessons"></a>Scene `s29-lessons`

**Marina** _(think)_: Lesson one: sit down. Lesson two: stay sat down. Most people never get past two.  
**Marina**: Feet braced. Pull with your back. Blades in, pull, out, and turn them flat. Look behind you. Rowing is all looking behind you.  
**Dusty**: That seems like a design fault.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Take it up with the river.  
_By the afternoon you can both go in a straight line, mostly. Dusty only catches one more crab, and keeps their seat._  
**Marina** _(soft)_: And one for the little one. Cut it down from an old one. Arms up.  
_Marina buckles a tiny cork vest onto your Nubbin. It stands very still, deeply proud, then marches up and down so everyone can see._  
_All afternoon there has been a small brown head in the water behind the boat. Wherever you row, it rows too, more or less._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Portly. He’s adopted you. He does that, then he forgets, then he does it again.  
**Marina**: Give him something, if you like. Not food; he’ll only want more food. Something he can keep.  

**Choices**

- **A blue glass bead from your pocket.** — sets `{"s29-gift":"bead"}`
    - You hold it out on a flat palm. Portly comes up the bank, sniffs it, and takes it in his teeth very carefully, like an egg.
    - He rolls it about on a flat stone for ten minutes, watching the light go through it.
    - **Marina**: Magpie. He’ll have that till he’s grey.
- **A red ribbon off the picnic basket.** — sets `{"s29-gift":"ribbon"}`
    - You lay it on the bank. Portly takes one end and swims off with it trailing behind him like a flag.
    - **Dusty**: Well, we’ll see him coming a mile off now.
    - **Marina**: Half a mile. That’ll do.
- **A tiny brass bell off an old collar.** — sets `{"s29-gift":"bell"}`
    - Marina threads it on a loop of soft cord, loose enough to slip off if it catches. Portly lets you put it over his head.
    - He shakes, and it rings. He shakes again, astonished, and then can’t stop.
    - **Marina**: Well. We’ll always know where he is now. Whether we want to or not.

#### <a id="s29-lessons-done"></a>Done `s29-lessons-done`

_Portly takes his present home along the bank in the evening light, very slowly, stopping to look at it._  
**Dusty** _(happy)_: He’s never stolen anything off you. Have you noticed? That’s how you can tell he likes you.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Same time next week. If either of you stands up in my boat again, I’ll sink you myself.  

### Step 5: Go into the Wild Wood with Marina and find Dusty.

Hint: Keep to the path and keep talking. If you lose the path, stand still and listen for Dusty instead. · check: `find:s29-dusty:wild-wood`

#### <a id="s29-wildwood"></a>Scene `s29-wildwood`

_August, and there is frost on the grass at dusk, and nobody can say why. It seems to come down off the slope below the Circuit._  
_Marina is at your gate with a lantern and a stout stick. She doesn’t lift and push; she shouts._  
**Marina** _(angry)_: Dusty’s gone into the Wild Wood. On their own. At teatime. They’ve been like a cat in a thunderstorm all week.  
**Marina**: I said don’t, it’s getting dark. They said they only wanted a look. Nobody only has a look in the Wild Wood.  
_Later, Dusty will tell it like this, and nobody will laugh._  
_It was quiet under the first trees, and cold. The path went in, the light went out, and the frost crunched._  
_Then the faces began._  
_Little ones, in the holes in the banks and between the roots. Gone whenever you looked straight at them._  
_Then the pattering: behind, ahead, either side, like rain that hadn’t decided where to fall._  
_Dusty ran, which is the wrong thing. They ran until they fell into the hollow of an old beech, and stayed there._  
**Marina** _(think)_: Rules of the Wood. Keep to the path. Don’t run. Keep talking. If you run out of things to say, say anything.  
**Marina**: Shopping lists. Times tables. Anything. Come on.  

#### <a id="s29-beech"></a>Done `s29-beech`

_You go in talking. Marina names every rock pool on the coast from west to east, and you do your times tables, badly._  
**Marina** _(think)_: Seven eights?  
_“Fifty-four?” you say._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Keep going. The Wood can’t tell.  
_The faces, if they are faces, keep to the edge of the lantern light. The pattering keeps pace with you, and stops when you stop._  
_Your Nubbin rides inside your coat with only its eyes showing. It doesn’t like the Wood, and it doesn’t make a sound._  
_At last, in the hollow of a great beech, something says “Ow” very quietly. Dusty is curled in the dead leaves, white with frost._  
**Dusty** _(sad)_: I only wanted a look.  
**Dusty**: I know. I know. Don’t say it.  
**Marina** _(soft)_: Wasn’t going to. You’re frozen. Up you get.  
_But the frost has thickened while you searched. The path has gone under it, and the lantern is guttering._  
**Marina** _(think)_: We’re not getting home tonight. Not that way.  

### Step 6: Find shelter in the Wild Wood before the lantern goes out.

Hint: Look for anything people made: a step, a scraper, a door. Old stones mean old houses. · check: `find:s29-brock-door:wild-wood`

#### <a id="s29-door"></a>Scene `s29-door`

_You go on slowly, holding on to one another’s sleeves, with Dusty in the middle._  
_Dusty trips over something, hard, and sits down in the frost holding their shin._  
**Dusty** _(angry)_: Ow. Something with an edge. In the middle of a wood. Who leaves an edge in a wood?  
_Marina scrapes the frost away with her stick. It is an iron boot-scraper, very old, set in a stone step._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: A scraper means a step. A step means a door. Dig, Dusty. You’re good at that.  
**Dusty**: Fine. Yes. Digging. I take it all back.  
_Under the frost there is a doormat, and beyond the doormat a little green door set into a bank under old stones._  
_Marina finds a bell-pull and pulls it. Somewhere deep in the bank, a long way off, a bell clanks._  
_Nothing. Then, very slowly, bolts going back: one, two, three, and a fourth, rusty, that takes some persuading._  

#### <a id="s29-kitchen"></a>Done `s29-kitchen`

_The door opens a crack. An old woman with a candle glares out: a quilted dressing gown, and a soot-black apron over it._  
**Granny Brock** _(angry)_: Who’s ringing my bell at this hour? Nobody rings it at any hour. That’s why it’s there.  
**Marina**: Granny Brock. It’s Marina, from the river. We’re lost, and this one’s frozen.  
**Granny Brock** _(think)_: Marina. Hm. And a frozen one. And somebody’s grandchild, by the chin on it. And a… what is that, in your coat?  
_Your Nubbin looks out at her, and she looks back. Neither of them blinks first, and something is settled._  
**Granny Brock**: In. All of you. Boots off. Not you, you’ve not got boots. In.  
_The passage runs back into the bank and opens into a kitchen older than the Wood: stone flags, a red fire, hams on hooks._  
_The walls are carved with bits of words nobody can read now. It is the ruin of a house older than anyone’s grandmother._  
**Granny Brock**: Sit. Eat. Then talk, if you must. Better if you mustn’t.  
_She feeds you soup, and bread, and more soup. She puts Dusty nearest the fire and drops a blanket on them without looking._  
**Granny Brock** _(think)_: The Wood? It looks. That’s all it does. It looks at folk who don’t belong to it, and they run, and that’s their lookout.  
**Granny Brock**: My people burned charcoal in this wood before your grandmothers were born. There was a town here once. Now there’s me.  
**Granny Brock**: People come and go. The Wood stays. So do I.  
_Dusty listens to every word, very hard, the way you listen to someone old when someone at home is getting old too._  
_When the fire gets low, Granny Brock puts another log on without being asked. Nobody goes to sleep for a long time._

### Step 7: Follow Granny Brock out of the Wood the back way.

Hint: Keep one hand on the wall and your head down. The old culvert comes out at the edge of the fields. · check: `walk:s29-culvert:fields`

#### <a id="s29-culvert"></a>Scene `s29-culvert`

_First light. Granny Brock is up before any of you, in boots and a coat older than her door._  
**Granny Brock** _(think)_: Not back through the Wood. Not with that one. The Wood’s had its look at them already.  
**Granny Brock**: There’s an old way. A drain from the old town. Goes under the lot and comes out by the fields. Mind your heads.  
_She takes a lantern and a ring of keys off a nail and leads you down a passage, then down some steps, then down some more._  
_The culvert is low and round and dry, made of old brick. Your Nubbin runs ahead into the dark, then runs back to check you’re coming._  
**Granny Brock** _(think)_: That one’s sensible. Goes ahead, comes back. More than I can say for some.  

#### <a id="s29-fields"></a>Done `s29-fields`

_Daylight, round and white at the end of the tunnel. You come out among nettles at the edge of a hayfield, blinking._  
_Behind you the Wood stands dark and quiet, and doesn’t look at all as if it was looking at anyone._  
**Granny Brock** _(think)_: Go home. Don’t come in after dark. Come in the day, if you like. Bring cake. Don’t ring the bell.  
_She goes back into the culvert and the dark takes her. Somewhere inside, a gate shuts, and four bolts._  
**Dusty** _(soft)_: I’m a river person now. I’ve decided. Rivers are flat. You can see what’s coming. Nothing looks at you from a river.  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Otters do.  
**Dusty**: Otters are allowed.  

### Step 8: Row up the river to the weir pool with Marina and Dusty.

Hint: Take an oar each. Above the backwater the river narrows; where it’s too shallow to row, everyone gets out and carries. · check: `minigame:s29-rowing:weir`

#### <a id="s29-low-river"></a>Scene `s29-low-river`

_Summer’s end. Since the rain on the hill, Dusty has been sleeping in Marina’s boathouse, and nobody has said a word about it._  
_Marina wants to show you the top of the river before the weather turns. Dusty takes an oar without being asked, and sits down first._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Look at that. Sitting down. I’ve made a sailor of you.  
_Above the backwater the river is wrong. The banks stand high and dry, and weed lies flat on stones that ought to be under water._  
**Marina** _(think)_: A hand too low. It’s been a hand too low all summer. Something upstream’s having it.  
_Halfway up, the boat grounds on gravel. There is nothing for it: out, all of you, and carry._  
_The water round your ankles is cold enough to make your teeth hurt._  
**Dusty** _(shock)_: Cold. In August.  
**Marina** _(sad)_: It was cold in July, too. Nobody listened then either.

### Step 9: Catch Marina up on the river path and tell her what’s wrong.

Hint: She’s gone on ahead towards the boathouse. She walks fast when she’s hungry. Run. · check: `talk:marina:s29-dusk-path`

#### <a id="s29-dusk"></a>Scene `s29-dusk`

_Dusk along the river path. Above you rises a cliff striped like a cake: brown, red, grey, brown._  
_Marina walks ahead, fast, talking about supper. Halfway along, Dusty stops dead, as if somebody has called their name._  
**Dusty** _(shock)_: Oh.  
_They are looking up the cliff, at a small round door in the rock and a window with no light in it._  
**Dusty** _(sad)_: That’s mine. I can smell it. Earth, and old tea, and… it’s mine. I haven’t been back. Not since the rain.  
**Dusty**: Marina! Marina, wait—  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Come on, you two! Supper! I’m starving, and it’s getting dark!  
_Marina hasn’t heard; she’s already round the bend. Dusty looks after Marina, and up at the door, and after her again._  
_Then they sit down on an old stump by the path, put their face in their hands, and cry._  

#### <a id="s29-turnback"></a>Done `s29-turnback`

_You catch Marina up at the bend. You don’t have to say much: you say “Dusty”, and point._  
_She turns round at once, in the middle of a word, and goes back down the path at a run._  
**Marina** _(soft)_: Dusty. Oh, Dusty. What is it?  
**Dusty** _(sad)_: It’s the house. It’s only the house. I can’t go in. Everything in it is hers.  
**Dusty**: And I can’t walk past it either. I tried. My feet won’t.  
**Marina** _(sad)_: Then we won’t walk past it. I’m an idiot. I was thinking about my supper.  
**Marina** _(soft)_: Right. Up we go. All of us. I’m not leaving you on a stump.  

### Step 10: Fetch dry kindling for Dusty’s stove.

Hint: The woodpile is round the side of the cliff house. The top layer is wet; dig down. · check: `collect:s29-kindling:cliff-house`

#### <a id="s29-cliffhouse"></a>Scene `s29-cliffhouse`

_Inside, Dusty’s house is cold, dusty and dark: a round room cut from the rock, a stove, two chairs and a great many trowels._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Look at it. A proper little house, this. Snug as an egg. And you’ve been sleeping in my boathouse with the nets.  
**Dusty** _(sad)_: It’s not snug. It’s cold. There’s nothing to eat.  
**Marina** _(think)_: There’s always something to eat. You: kindling, the dry stuff, round the side. Dusty: sit. I’ll do the cupboards.  
_Dusty sits. Marina goes through the cupboards like weather, opening everything and talking to all of it._  
**Marina** _(happy)_: Biscuits. Ancient. They’ll do. Tea. A jar of… pickled onions? They’ll do. Candles!  

#### <a id="s29-supper"></a>Done `s29-supper`

_You come back with your arms full. Soon the stove is roaring, the room has gone gold, and even the dust looks cosy._  
_Marina lays out biscuits, onions and tea on an upturned crate as if it were a banquet. Your Nubbin sits as close to the stove as it dares._  
_Beside the stove there is a round basket with a cushion in it, flattened in the middle. Nobody sits on that side of the stove._  
**Dusty** _(soft)_: Fossil’s. She liked it there. Twenty years by that stove, and she never once let me have the warm side.  
**Dusty**: I couldn’t come in with it there. And I couldn’t move it. So I didn’t come in.  
**Marina** _(soft)_: Leave it where it is. It’s hers. It can be hers and still be in your house.  
_Outside: feet on the cliff path, whispering, and a great deal of shushing. A lantern on a pole bobs up past the window._  
_Dusty opens the door. Half the valley’s children are on the step in their wellies, red-nosed, holding the lantern up high._  
**Valley children**: We heard you were home. It’s not Lantern Night, we know. We practised one verse anyway.  
**Valley children** _(soft)_: Light one for the ones who went up the rise  
**Valley children** _(soft)_: with the morning sun in their sleeping eyes;  
**Valley children** _(soft)_: we are made of the ones who remember us here,  
**Valley children** _(soft)_: so we gather, we gather, we gather each year.  
**Dusty** _(soft)_: …Thank you. Thank you. Come in, all of you. There’s biscuits. They’re very old.  
_That night Dusty sleeps at home, in their own bed, with the stove ticking and the basket where it always was._
